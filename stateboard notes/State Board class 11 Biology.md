> **Subject:** Biology  
> **Class:** 11  
> **Chapter:** The Living World

---

# Introduction

The Earth contains millions of organisms with different forms, structures and lifestyles. The study of living organisms, their identification, classification and relationships is called biology.

This chapter covers:

- Characteristics of living organisms
- Biodiversity
- Need for classification
- Taxonomy and systematics
- Taxonomical hierarchy
- Binomial nomenclature
- Five kingdom classification
- Three-domain system

---

# PART A: THE LIVING WORLD

---

# 1. Characteristics of Living Organisms

Living organisms show certain basic characteristics that distinguish them from non-living things.

---

# 1. Growth

## Definition

Increase in size, mass or number of cells is called growth.

---

Examples:

- Increase in height of plants
- Growth of animals

---

In living organisms:

Growth occurs by:

- Cell division
- Increase in cell size

---

# 2. Reproduction

## Definition

The process by which organisms produce new individuals of their own kind is called reproduction.

---

Types:

## Asexual reproduction

Single parent involved.

Example:

- Bacteria
- Amoeba

---

## Sexual reproduction

Two parents involved.

Example:

- Humans
- Flowering plants

---

# 3. Metabolism

## Definition

The sum total of all chemical reactions occurring inside a living organism is called metabolism.

---

Types:

### Anabolism

Building up of complex molecules.

Example:

Photosynthesis

---

### Catabolism

Breakdown of complex molecules.

Example:

Respiration

---

# 4. Cellular Organisation

All living organisms are made up of cells.

---

Levels of organisation:

\[
\boxed{
Cell \rightarrow Tissue \rightarrow Organ \rightarrow Organ\ system \rightarrow Organism
}
\]

---

# 5. Consciousness

## Definition

The ability of an organism to sense and respond to its surroundings is called consciousness.

---

Examples:

- Plants respond to light.
- Humans respond to stimuli.

---

# PART B: BIODIVERSITY

---

# 6. Biodiversity

## Definition

The variety of living organisms present on Earth is called biodiversity.

---

Biodiversity includes:

1. Genetic diversity
2. Species diversity
3. Ecosystem diversity

---

# 7. Genetic Diversity

Variation in genes among individuals of the same species is called genetic diversity.

---

Example:

Different varieties of rice.

---

# 8. Species Diversity

The variety of different species present in an area is called species diversity.

---

Example:

Different animals in a forest.

---

# 9. Ecosystem Diversity

The variety of ecosystems in a region is called ecosystem diversity.

---

Examples:

- Forests
- Deserts
- Oceans

---

# PART C: NEED FOR CLASSIFICATION

---

# 10. Classification

## Definition

The process of grouping organisms based on similarities and differences is called classification.

---

# 11. Importance of Classification

Classification helps:

1. Identify organisms easily.
2. Study relationships among organisms.
3. Understand evolution.
4. Organise biological information.

---

# PART D: TAXONOMY AND SYSTEMATICS

---

# 12. Taxonomy

## Definition

The science of identification, naming and classification of organisms is called taxonomy.

---

Father of taxonomy:

\[
\boxed{\text{Carolus Linnaeus}}
\]

---

# 13. Systematics

## Definition

Systematics is the study of diversity of organisms and their evolutionary relationships.

---

It includes:

- Identification
- Classification
- Nomenclature
- Evolutionary relationships

---

# PART E: CONCEPT OF SPECIES

---

# 14. Species

## Definition

A group of similar organisms that can interbreed and produce fertile offspring is called a species.

---

Example:

\[
\boxed{\textit{Homo sapiens}}
\]

(Human species)

---

# PART F: TAXONOMICAL HIERARCHY

---

# 15. Taxonomic Categories

The classification system consists of different levels called taxonomic categories.

---

Hierarchy:

\[
\boxed{
Kingdom
\rightarrow
Phylum
\rightarrow
Class
\rightarrow
Order
\rightarrow
Family
\rightarrow
Genus
\rightarrow
Species
}
\]

---

# 16. Kingdom

The highest taxonomic category.

---

Example:

Animalia

---

# 17. Phylum

Group of related classes.

---

Example:

Chordata

---

# 18. Class

Group of related orders.

---

Example:

Mammalia

---

# 19. Order

Group of related families.

---

# 20. Family

Group of related genera.

---

Example:

Felidae

---

# 21. Genus

Group of closely related species.

---

Example:

Homo

---

# 22. Species

The basic unit of classification.

---

Example:

sapiens

---

# PART G: BINOMIAL NOMENCLATURE

---

# 23. Binomial Nomenclature

## Definition

The scientific naming system using two names is called binomial nomenclature.

---

Introduced by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Carolus Linnaeus}}
\]

---

# 24. Rules of Binomial Nomenclature

Scientific name contains:

1. Genus name
2. Species name

---

Example:

Human:

\[
\boxed{\textit{Homo sapiens}}
\]

---

Rules:

- Genus starts with capital letter.
- Species starts with small letter.
- Names are written in italics.
- Latin language is used.

---

# PART H: FIVE KINGDOM CLASSIFICATION

---

# 25. Five Kingdom Classification

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{R.H. Whittaker (1969)}}
\]

---

Five kingdoms:

1. Monera
2. Protista
3. Fungi
4. Plantae
5. Animalia

---

# PART I: THREE DOMAIN SYSTEM

---

# 26. Three Domain Classification

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Carl Woese}}
\]

---

Domains:

1. Bacteria
2. Archaea
3. Eukarya

---

# 27. Domain Bacteria

Characteristics:

- Prokaryotic organisms
- True bacteria

Example:

E. coli

---

# 28. Domain Archaea

Characteristics:

- Ancient prokaryotes
- Live in extreme environments

Examples:

- Methanogens
- Halophiles

---

# 29. Domain Eukarya

Characteristics:

- Cells with true nucleus
- Membrane-bound organelles present

Includes:

- Protists
- Fungi
- Plants
- Animals

---

- Confusing taxonomy and systematics.
- Writing species name with capital letter.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Living organisms show growth, reproduction, metabolism and consciousness.
- Biodiversity refers to the variety of life forms.
- Classification helps organise and study organisms.

# Chapter 2: Biological Classification

> **Subject:** Biology

---

# Introduction

Biological classification is the scientific arrangement of organisms into groups based on similarities and differences.

This chapter covers:

- Kingdom Monera
- Kingdom Protista
- Kingdom Fungi
- Lichens
- Viruses
- Viroids

---

# PART A: KINGDOM MONERA

---

# 1. Monera

## Definition

Kingdom Monera includes all prokaryotic organisms that lack a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

---

Examples:

- Bacteria
- Cyanobacteria
- Mycoplasma

---

# 2. General Characteristics of Monera

- Unicellular organisms.
- Prokaryotic cell structure.
- Nuclear membrane absent.
- Membrane-bound organelles absent.
- Cell wall usually present.
- Reproduce mainly by binary fission.

---

# PART B: CLASSIFICATION OF MONERA

Monerans are classified into:

1. Archaebacteria
2. Eubacteria
3. Cyanobacteria
4. Mycoplasma

---

# 3. Archaebacteria

## Characteristics

- Primitive bacteria.
- Live in extreme environments.
- Cell wall composition is different from true bacteria.

---

Types:

### 1. Methanogens

Found in:

- Marshy areas
- Cattle intestine

Produce:

\[
\boxed{\text{Methane gas}}
\]

---

### 2. Halophiles

Live in:

\[
\boxed{\text{High salt environments}}
\]

---

### 3. Thermoacidophiles

Live in:

- Hot springs
- Acidic environments

---

# 4. Eubacteria (True Bacteria)

## Characteristics

- Common bacteria.
- Cell wall made of peptidoglycan.
- May be autotrophic or heterotrophic.

---

# Shapes of Bacteria

---

## 1. Cocci

Spherical bacteria.

Example:

Staphylococcus

---

## 2. Bacilli

Rod-shaped bacteria.

Example:

Bacillus

---

## 3. Spirilla

Spiral-shaped bacteria.

---

## 4. Vibrios

Comma-shaped bacteria.

---

# 5. Nutrition in Bacteria

Bacteria may be:

---

## Autotrophic

Prepare their own food.

Types:

### Photosynthetic bacteria

Use sunlight.

---

### Chemosynthetic bacteria

Use chemical energy.

---

## Heterotrophic

Depend on other organisms.

Types:

- Saprophytic
- Parasitic
- Symbiotic

---

# 6. Reproduction in Bacteria

Main method:

\[
\boxed{\text{Binary fission}}
\]

---

Other methods:

- Fragmentation
- Spore formation

---

# 7. Cyanobacteria

## Definition

Photosynthetic bacteria containing chlorophyll are called cyanobacteria.

---

Examples:

- Nostoc
- Anabaena

---

Importance:

- Fix atmospheric nitrogen.
- Improve soil fertility.

---

# 8. Mycoplasma

## Characteristics

- Smallest living cells.
- Lack cell wall.
- Can survive without oxygen.
- Some cause diseases.

---

Example:

Mycoplasma pneumoniae

---

# PART C: KINGDOM PROTISTA

---

# 9. Protista

## Definition

Kingdom Protista includes unicellular eukaryotic organisms.

---

Characteristics:

- True nucleus present.
- Mostly aquatic.
- Mostly unicellular.
- Reproduce sexually and asexually.

---

Examples:

- Amoeba
- Paramecium
- Euglena

---

# PART D: CLASSIFICATION OF PROTISTA

---

# 10. Chrysophytes

Includes:

- Diatoms
- Golden algae

---

Characteristics:

- Aquatic organisms.
- Cell wall contains silica.

---

Importance:

Diatom deposits form:

\[
\boxed{\text{Diatomaceous earth}}
\]

---

# 11. Dinoflagellates

Characteristics:

- Mostly marine.
- Have two flagella.
- Some produce toxins.

---

Example:

Gonyaulax

---

Cause:

\[
\boxed{\text{Red tide}}
\]

---

# 12. Euglenoids

Characteristics:

- Freshwater organisms.
- No cell wall.
- Have pellicle.
- Mixotrophic nutrition.

---

Example:

Euglena

---

# 13. Slime Moulds

Characteristics:

- Saprophytic organisms.
- Form plasmodium.
- Produce spores.

---

# 14. Protozoans

Characteristics:

- Animal-like protists.
- Heterotrophic.
- Mostly unicellular.

---

Types:

---

## Amoeboid Protozoans

Example:

Amoeba

Movement:

\[
\boxed{\text{Pseudopodia}}
\]

---

## Flagellated Protozoans

Example:

Trypanosoma

Movement:

\[
\boxed{\text{Flagella}}
\]

---

## Ciliated Protozoans

Example:

Paramecium

Movement:

\[
\boxed{\text{Cilia}}
\]

---

## Sporozoans

Example:

Plasmodium

Causes:

\[
\boxed{\text{Malaria}}
\]

---

# PART E: KINGDOM FUNGI

---

# 15. Fungi

## Definition

Fungi are eukaryotic, heterotrophic organisms that obtain nutrition by absorption.

---

Examples:

- Mushroom
- Yeast
- Moulds

---

# 16. General Characteristics of Fungi

- Eukaryotic.
- Mostly multicellular.
- Cell wall made of chitin.
- Lack chlorophyll.
- Reproduce by spores.

---

# 17. Classification of Fungi

Based on reproduction:

1. Phycomycetes
2. Ascomycetes
3. Basidiomycetes
4. Deuteromycetes

---

# 18. Phycomycetes

Characteristics:

- Aquatic or parasitic.
- Asexual spores called zoospores.

Example:

Rhizopus

---

# 19. Ascomycetes

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Sac fungi}}
\]

---

Produce:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ascospores}}
\]

---

Examples:

- Yeast
- Penicillium

---

# 20. Basidiomycetes

Produce:

\[
\boxed{\text{Basidiospores}}
\]

---

Examples:

- Mushroom
- Rust fungi

---

# 21. Deuteromycetes

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Imperfect fungi}}
\]

---

Sexual reproduction absent.

Example:

Alternaria

---

# PART F: LICHENS

---

# 22. Lichens

## Definition

Lichens are symbiotic associations between algae and fungi.

---

Components:

Algae:

Provides food by photosynthesis.

Fungus:

Provides shelter and minerals.

---

# 23. Importance of Lichens

- Indicators of air pollution.
- Help in soil formation.

---

# PART G: VIRUSES

---

# 24. Virus

## Definition

Viruses are acellular infectious particles made of nucleic acid surrounded by a protein coat.

---

Characteristics:

- Non-cellular.
- Can reproduce only inside host cells.
- Show both living and non-living characteristics.

---

# 25. Structure of Virus

Components:

1. Genetic material
2. Protein coat (capsid)

---

Genetic material may be:

- DNA
- RNA

---

# 26. Examples of Viruses

| Virus           | Disease                |
| --------------- | ---------------------- |
| HIV             | AIDS                   |
| Influenza virus | Flu                    |
| TMV             | Tobacco mosaic disease |

---

# 27. Bacteriophage

## Definition

A virus that infects bacteria is called bacteriophage.

---

# PART H: VIROIDS

---

# 28. Viroids

## Definition

Viroids are infectious agents made only of RNA without a protein coat.

---

Discovered by:

\[
\boxed{\text{T.O. Diener}}
\]

---

Example:

Potato spindle tuber disease

---

# Important Differences

## Virus vs Viroid

| Virus                                | Viroid                |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------- |
| Has nucleic acid and protein coat    | Only RNA present      |
| Larger                               | Smaller               |
| Infects animals, plants and bacteria | Mainly infects plants |

---

## Archaebacteria vs Eubacteria

| Archaebacteria           | Eubacteria              |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Live in extreme habitats | Common habitats         |
| Different cell wall      | Peptidoglycan cell wall |
| Primitive bacteria       | True bacteria           |

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Smallest living cell is:

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Mycoplasma}}
\]

---

## Example 2

Lichen is an association between:

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Algae and fungi}}
\]

---

## Example 3

Malaria is caused by:

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\textit{Plasmodium}}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing viruses with living organisms.
- Forgetting fungi lack chlorophyll.
- Mixing bacteria and archaea.
- Forgetting lichens are symbiotic associations.
- Confusing viroids with viruses.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Monera includes prokaryotic organisms.
- Protista includes unicellular eukaryotes.
- Fungi are heterotrophic organisms with chitin cell walls.
- Lichens show mutual association between algae and fungi.
- Viruses are acellular infectious particles.
- Viroids contain only RNA.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Classification helps us understand the diversity and relationships of organisms.}
}
\]

# Chapter 3: Plant Kingdom

> **Subject:** Biology  
> **Class:** 11  
> **Chapter:** Plant Kingdom

---

# Introduction

The plant kingdom includes all organisms that are mainly autotrophic and capable of photosynthesis. Plants show great diversity in their structure, reproduction and life cycles.

This chapter covers:

- Classification of plant kingdom
- Algae
- Bryophytes
- Pteridophytes
- Gymnosperms
- Angiosperms
- Life cycle patterns in plants

---

# PART A: CLASSIFICATION OF PLANT KINGDOM

---

# 1. Basis of Classification

Plants are classified based on:

1. Body differentiation
2. Presence of vascular tissues
3. Seed formation
4. Nature of reproductive structures

---

# 2. Major Groups of Plants

Plant kingdom is divided into:

1. Algae
2. Bryophyta
3. Pteridophyta
4. Gymnosperms
5. Angiosperms

---

# PART B: ALGAE

---

# 3. Algae

## Definition

Algae are simple, chlorophyll-containing, autotrophic organisms mainly found in aquatic environments.

---

Characteristics:

- Mostly aquatic.
- Body is not differentiated into root, stem and leaf.
- Called thallus.
- Photosynthetic.
- Reproduce by vegetative, asexual and sexual methods.

---

# 4. Classification of Algae

Algae are classified into:

1. Chlorophyceae (Green algae)
2. Phaeophyceae (Brown algae)
3. Rhodophyceae (Red algae)

---

# 5. Chlorophyceae (Green Algae)

Pigments:

- Chlorophyll a
- Chlorophyll b

---

Stored food:

\[
\boxed{\text{Starch}}
\]

---

Examples:

- Chlamydomonas
- Volvox
- Spirogyra

---

Characteristics:

- Freshwater organisms.
- Green colour due to chlorophyll.

---

# 6. Phaeophyceae (Brown Algae)

Pigments:

- Chlorophyll a
- Chlorophyll c
- Fucoxanthin

---

Stored food:

\[
\boxed{\text{Laminarin and mannitol}}
\]

---

Examples:

- Sargassum
- Fucus

---

Characteristics:

- Mostly marine.
- Large-sized algae.

---

# 7. Rhodophyceae (Red Algae)

Pigment:

\[
\boxed{\text{Phycoerythrin}}
\]

---

Stored food:

\[
\boxed{\text{Floridean starch}}
\]

---

Examples:

- Polysiphonia
- Gelidium

---

# Importance of Algae

- Produce oxygen.
- Used as food.
- Source of agar.
- Used in industries.

---

# PART C: BRYOPHYTA

---

# 8. Bryophytes

## Definition

Bryophytes are small terrestrial plants that require water for reproduction.

---

Called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Amphibians of plant kingdom}}
\]

---

Characteristics:

- Non-vascular plants.
- No true roots, stems and leaves.
- Dominant gametophyte generation.
- Found in moist places.

---

# 9. Classification of Bryophytes

Types:

1. Liverworts
2. Mosses

---

# 10. Liverworts

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Marchantia}}
\]

---

Characteristics:

- Thalloid body.
- Reproduce by gemmae.

---

# 11. Mosses

Examples:

- Funaria
- Sphagnum

---

Characteristics:

- Leafy structure.
- Sporophyte develops on gametophyte.

---

# Importance of Bryophytes

- Help in soil formation.
- Prevent soil erosion.
- Sphagnum forms peat.

---

# PART D: PTERIDOPHYTA

---

# 12. Pteridophytes

## Definition

Pteridophytes are vascular plants that reproduce through spores.

---

Characteristics:

- First vascular plants.
- Have true roots, stems and leaves.
- No seeds.
- Sporophyte is dominant.

---

Examples:

- Fern
- Selaginella
- Equisetum

---

# 13. Life Cycle of Pteridophytes

Two generations:

1. Sporophyte
2. Gametophyte

---

# Importance of Pteridophytes

- Used as ornamental plants.
- Ancient pteridophytes contributed to coal formation.

---

# PART E: GYMNOSPERMS

---

# 14. Gymnosperms

## Definition

Gymnosperms are seed-producing plants with naked seeds.

---

Meaning:

\[
\text{Gymno = Naked}
\]

\[
\text{Sperma = Seed}
\]

---

Characteristics:

- Seeds are not enclosed in fruits.
- Vascular plants.
- Mostly evergreen.
- Sporophyte dominant.

---

Examples:

- Cycas
- Pinus
- Gnetum

---

# 15. Features of Gymnosperms

- Roots, stems and leaves are well developed.
- Male and female cones present.
- Pollination occurs by wind.

---

# PART F: ANGIOSPERMS

---

# 16. Angiosperms

## Definition

Angiosperms are flowering plants in which seeds are enclosed within fruits.

---

Characteristics:

- Produce flowers.
- Seeds enclosed in fruits.
- Double fertilisation occurs.
- Most advanced plants.

---

Examples:

- Mango
- Rose
- Wheat

---

# 17. Classification of Angiosperms

Angiosperms are divided into:

1. Monocotyledons
2. Dicotyledons

---

# 18. Monocots

Characteristics:

- One cotyledon.
- Parallel venation.
- Fibrous roots.
- Floral parts in multiples of three.

---

Examples:

- Rice
- Wheat
- Maize

---

# 19. Dicots

Characteristics:

- Two cotyledons.
- Reticulate venation.
- Tap roots.
- Floral parts in multiples of four or five.

---

Examples:

- Pea
- Mango
- Rose

---

# PART G: ALTERNATION OF GENERATIONS

---

# 20. Alternation of Generations

## Definition

The occurrence of two phases in plant life cycle is called alternation of generations.

---

Two phases:

## Gametophyte

Produces gametes.

---

## Sporophyte

Produces spores.

---

# Important Differences

## Bryophytes vs Pteridophytes

| Bryophytes                 | Pteridophytes                |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Non-vascular               | Vascular                     |
| No true roots/stems/leaves | True roots, stems and leaves |
| Gametophyte dominant       | Sporophyte dominant          |

---

## Gymnosperms vs Angiosperms

| Gymnosperms     | Angiosperms              |
| --------------- | ------------------------ |
| Naked seeds     | Seeds enclosed in fruits |
| No true flowers | Flowers present          |
| Example: Pinus  | Example: Mango           |

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Bryophytes are called:

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Amphibians of plant kingdom}}
\]

---

## Example 2

Plants with naked seeds are:

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Gymnosperms}}
\]

---

## Example 3

Stored food in green algae is:

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Starch}}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing bryophytes and pteridophytes.
- Forgetting gymnosperms have naked seeds.
- Mixing monocots and dicots.
- Forgetting algae have thallus body.
- Confusing gametophyte and sporophyte generations.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Plant kingdom is classified into algae, bryophytes, pteridophytes, gymnosperms and angiosperms.
- Algae are simple photosynthetic organisms.
- Bryophytes require water for reproduction.
- Pteridophytes are the first vascular plants.
- Gymnosperms have naked seeds.
- Angiosperms are flowering plants with enclosed seeds.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Plant diversity shows the evolutionary progression from simple to complex forms.}
}
\]

# Chapter 4: Animal Kingdom

> **Subject:** Biology  
> **Class:** 11  
> **Chapter:** Animal Kingdom

---

# Introduction

The animal kingdom includes multicellular, eukaryotic organisms that are generally heterotrophic and capable of movement.

Animals are classified based on:

- Level of organisation
- Body symmetry
- Germ layers
- Body cavity
- Segmentation
- Presence or absence of notochord

---

# PART A: BASIS OF CLASSIFICATION

---

# 1. Levels of Organisation

Animals show different levels of organisation.

---

## A. Cellular Level

Cells are arranged loosely.

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Porifera}}
\]

---

## B. Tissue Level

Similar cells form tissues.

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Cnidaria}}
\]

---

## C. Organ Level

Different tissues form organs.

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Platyhelminthes}}
\]

---

## D. Organ System Level

Organs form organ systems.

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Higher animals}}
\]

---

# 2. Body Symmetry

---

## Asymmetrical

Body cannot be divided into equal halves.

Example:

Sponges

---

## Radial Symmetry

Body parts arranged around a central axis.

Example:

Hydra

---

## Bilateral Symmetry

Body can be divided into two equal halves.

Example:

Humans

---

# 3. Germ Layers

---

## Diploblastic Animals

Body develops from two germ layers:

1. Ectoderm
2. Endoderm

Example:

Cnidaria

---

## Triploblastic Animals

Body develops from three germ layers:

1. Ectoderm
2. Mesoderm
3. Endoderm

Example:

Platyhelminthes onwards

---

# 4. Body Cavity (Coelom)

---

## Acoelomate

Body cavity absent.

Example:

Platyhelminthes

---

## Pseudocoelomate

False body cavity present.

Example:

Aschelminthes

---

## Coelomate

True body cavity present.

Example:

Annelida onwards

---

2. Dendrites
3. Axon

---

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Transmit signals}}
\]

A protective covering around axons is called myelin sheath.
