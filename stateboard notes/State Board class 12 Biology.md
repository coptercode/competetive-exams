# Chapter 1: Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

> **Subject:** Biology  
> **Class:** 12  
> **Chapter:** Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

---

# Introduction

Sexual reproduction in flowering plants involves the formation of male and female gametes, their fusion, and the development of seeds and fruits.

The reproductive structure of flowering plants is the **flower**.

This chapter covers:

- Structure of flower
- Male gametophyte development
- Female gametophyte development
- Pollination
- Fertilization
- Seed and fruit formation
- Apomixis
- Parthenocarpy
- Polyembryony

---

# PART A: STRUCTURE OF FLOWER

---

# 1. Flower

## Definition

A flower is a modified reproductive structure of angiosperms responsible for sexual reproduction.

---

# 2. Parts of a Flower

A typical flower consists of four whorls:

1. Calyx
2. Corolla
3. Androecium
4. Gynoecium

---

# 3. Calyx

Made up of:

\[
\boxed{\text{Sepals}}
\]

Function:

- Protects the flower bud.

---

# 4. Corolla

Made up of:

\[
\boxed{\text{Petals}}
\]

Functions:

- Attracts pollinators.
- Helps in pollination.

---

# 5. Androecium

Male reproductive part of flower.

---

Contains:

\[
\boxed{\text{Stamens}}
\]

Each stamen consists of:

1. Filament
2. Anther

---

# 6. Anther Structure

A typical anther is:

- Bilobed.
- Ditecous.
- Contains four pollen sacs.

---

Each pollen sac contains:

\[
\boxed{\text{Microsporangium}}
\]

---

# 7. Gynoecium

Female reproductive part of flower.

---

Consists of:

1. Stigma
2. Style
3. Ovary

---

Inside ovary:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ovules}}
\]

are present.

---

# PART B: DEVELOPMENT OF MALE GAMETOPHYTE

---

# 8. Microsporogenesis

## Definition

Formation of male gametes through development of pollen grains is called microsporogenesis.

---

Occurs inside:

\[
\boxed{\text{Anther}}
\]

---

# 9. Structure of Anther Wall

Layers:

1. Epidermis
2. Endothecium
3. Middle layers
4. Tapetum

---

# 10. Tapetum

## Definition

The innermost layer of anther wall that provides nutrition to developing pollen grains is called tapetum.

---

Function:

- Provides nutrients.
- Helps pollen wall formation.

---

# 11. Pollen Grain

## Definition

A pollen grain represents the male gametophyte of flowering plants.

---

Structure:

Two layers:

## Exine

Outer layer made of:

\[
\boxed{\text{Sporopollenin}}
\]

---

## Intine

Inner layer made of:

- Cellulose
- Pectin

---

# 12. Development of Male Gametophyte

A mature pollen grain contains:

1. Vegetative cell
2. Generative cell

---

Generative cell divides to form:

\[
\boxed{\text{Two male gametes}}
\]

---

# PART C: DEVELOPMENT OF FEMALE GAMETOPHYTE

---

# 13. Ovule

## Definition

The structure inside ovary that develops into a seed after fertilization is called ovule.

---

Parts:

1. Funicle
2. Nucellus
3. Integuments
4. Micropyle

---

# 14. Megasporogenesis

## Definition

Formation of megaspores from megaspore mother cell is called megasporogenesis.

---

Occurs in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ovule}}
\]

---

# 15. Embryo Sac Development

The functional megaspore undergoes three mitotic divisions to form:

\[
\boxed{\text{7-celled, 8-nucleate embryo sac}}
\]

---

# Structure of Embryo Sac

Contains:

## Egg Apparatus

At micropylar end:

- One egg cell
- Two synergids

---

## Central Cell

Contains:

- Two polar nuclei

---

## Antipodal Cells

Three cells at chalazal end.

---

# PART D: POLLINATION

---

# 16. Pollination

## Definition

Transfer of pollen grains from anther to stigma is called pollination.

---

# Types of Pollination

---

# 17. Autogamy

## Definition

Transfer of pollen grains from anther to stigma of the same flower.

---

Example:

Pea

---

# 18. Geitonogamy

Transfer of pollen grains between different flowers of the same plant.

---

# 19. Xenogamy

Transfer of pollen grains between flowers of different plants of the same species.

---

Produces:

\[
\boxed{\text{Genetic variation}}
\]

---

# PART E: AGENCIES OF POLLINATION

---

Pollination occurs through:

1. Wind
2. Water
3. Animals

---

# 20. Wind Pollination (Anemophily)

Characteristics:

- Light pollen grains.
- Large amount of pollen production.
- Exposed stamens.

Example:

Maize

---

# 21. Water Pollination (Hydrophily)

Occurs through water.

Example:

Vallisneria

---

# 22. Animal Pollination (Zoophily)

Most common type.

Agents:

- Insects
- Birds
- Bats

Example:

Sunflower

---

# PART F: OUTBREEDING DEVICES

---

# 23. Outbreeding Devices

Mechanisms that prevent self-pollination are called outbreeding devices.

---

Types:

---

## 1. Dichogamy

Maturation of anther and stigma at different times.

---

## 2. Herkogamy

Physical separation of anther and stigma.

---

## 3. Self-incompatibility

Pollen grains are unable to fertilize the same flower.

---

## 4. Unisexuality

Male and female flowers are separate.

---

# PART G: POLLEN–PISTIL INTERACTION

---

# 24. Pollen–Pistil Interaction

The series of events from pollen landing on stigma until fertilization is called pollen–pistil interaction.

---

Steps:

1. Recognition of pollen.
2. Pollen germination.
3. Pollen tube formation.
4. Pollen tube reaches ovule.

---

# PART H: FERTILIZATION

---

# 25. Fertilization

## Definition

Fusion of male and female gametes to form a zygote is called fertilization.

---

# 26. Double Fertilization

## Definition

Two fertilization events occurring in angiosperms are called double fertilization.

---

Discovered by:

\[
\boxed{\text{S.G. Nawaschin}}
\]

---

Events:

## Syngamy

Male gamete + Egg

↓

\[
\boxed{\text{Zygote}}
\]

---

## Triple Fusion

Male gamete + Two polar nuclei

↓

\[
\boxed{\text{Endosperm}}
\]

---

# PART I: SEED DEVELOPMENT

---

# 27. Seed Formation

After fertilization:

- Ovule develops into seed.
- Zygote develops into embryo.
- Integuments form seed coat.

---

# 28. Endosperm Development

Endosperm provides nutrition to developing embryo.

---

Types:

1. Nuclear endosperm
2. Cellular endosperm
3. Helobial endosperm

---

# 29. Embryo Development

The zygote develops into embryo.

---

Embryo contains:

- Radicle
- Plumule
- Cotyledons

---

# PART J: FRUIT DEVELOPMENT

---

# 30. Fruit Formation

Fruit develops from:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ovary}}
\]

after fertilization.

---

Types:

## True Fruit

Develops only from ovary.

Example:

Mango

---

## False Fruit

Develops from ovary and other floral parts.

Example:

Apple

---

# PART K: SPECIAL PHENOMENA

---

# 31. Apomixis

## Definition

Seed formation without fertilization is called apomixis.

---

Importance:

- Maintains desirable characters.
- Useful in agriculture.

---

# 32. Parthenocarpy

## Definition

Development of fruit without fertilization is called parthenocarpy.

---

Produces:

\[
\boxed{\text{Seedless fruits}}
\]

---

Example:

Seedless grapes

---

# 33. Polyembryony

## Definition

Occurrence of more than one embryo in a single seed is called polyembryony.

---

Example:

Citrus

---

# Important Differences

## Autogamy vs Xenogamy

| Autogamy             | Xenogamy           |
| -------------------- | ------------------ |
| Same flower          | Different plant    |
| No genetic variation | Produces variation |

---

## Apomixis vs Parthenocarpy

| Apomixis                             | Parthenocarpy                         |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Seed formation without fertilization | Fruit formation without fertilization |
| Embryo develops                      | Seedless fruit develops               |

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Male gametophyte of flowering plants:

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Pollen grain}}
\]

---

## Example 2

Double fertilization occurs in:

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Angiosperms}}
\]

---

## Example 3

Outer wall of pollen grain is made of:

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Sporopollenin}}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing pollination and fertilization.
- Forgetting double fertilization is unique to angiosperms.
- Mixing apomixis and parthenocarpy.
- Forgetting pollen grain represents male gametophyte.
- Confusing ovule and ovary development.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Flowers are reproductive structures of angiosperms.
- Male gametophyte develops inside pollen grains.
- Female gametophyte develops inside ovule.
- Pollination transfers pollen to stigma.
- Double fertilization forms embryo and endosperm.
- Seeds develop from ovules and fruits develop from ovaries.
- Apomixis and parthenocarpy are special reproductive processes.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Sexual reproduction in flowering plants ensures seed formation and continuation of species.}
}
\]

---

# Introduction

Human reproduction is a complex process involving the formation of male and female gametes, fertilization, development of embryo and birth of a child.

This chapter covers:

- Male reproductive system
- Female reproductive system
- Spermatogenesis
- Oogenesis
- Menstrual cycle
- Fertilization
- Embryonic development
- Implantation
- Pregnancy
- Placenta
- Parturition
- Lactation

---

# PART A: MALE REPRODUCTIVE SYSTEM

---

# 1. Male Reproductive System

The male reproductive system produces male gametes (sperms) and male sex hormones.

---

Main parts:

1. Testes
2. Accessory ducts
3. Accessory glands
4. External genitalia

---

# 2. Testis

## Definition

Testes are the primary male reproductive organs responsible for sperm production and testosterone secretion.

---

Location:

\[
\boxed{\text{Scrotum}}
\]

---

Functions:

- Formation of sperm.
- Secretion of testosterone.

---

# 3. Structure of Testis

Each testis contains:

- Seminiferous tubules
- Interstitial cells (Leydig cells)

---

# 4. Seminiferous Tubules

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Spermatogenesis}}
\]

---

Contain:

- Germ cells
- Sertoli cells

---

# 5. Sertoli Cells

Functions:

- Provide nutrition to developing sperm.
- Support spermatogenesis.

---

# 6. Leydig Cells

Location:

Between seminiferous tubules.

---

Function:

Produce:

\[
\boxed{\text{Testosterone}}
\]

---

# 7. Male Accessory Ducts

Pathway of sperm:

Seminiferous tubules

↓

Rete testis

↓

Vasa efferentia

↓

Epididymis

↓

Vas deferens

↓

Urethra

---

# 8. Accessory Glands

Includes:

1. Seminal vesicles
2. Prostate gland
3. Bulbourethral glands

---

Functions:

- Add secretions to sperm.
- Form semen.

---

# PART B: FEMALE REPRODUCTIVE SYSTEM

---

# 9. Female Reproductive System

Produces female gametes and supports pregnancy.

---

Main parts:

1. Ovaries
2. Fallopian tubes
3. Uterus
4. Vagina

---

# 10. Ovary

## Definition

Ovaries are the primary female reproductive organs that produce ova and female hormones.

---

Functions:

- Oogenesis.
- Secretion of estrogen and progesterone.

---

# 11. Fallopian Tubes (Oviducts)

Parts:

1. Infundibulum
2. Ampulla
3. Isthmus

---

Function:

- Transport ovum.
- Site of fertilization.

---

# 12. Uterus

## Definition

A muscular organ where embryo develops is called uterus.

---

Functions:

- Implantation.
- Supports pregnancy.

---

# 13. Vagina

Functions:

- Birth canal.
- Receives sperm during intercourse.

---

# PART C: GAMETOGENESIS

---

# 14. Gametogenesis

## Definition

Formation of gametes is called gametogenesis.

---

Types:

1. Spermatogenesis
2. Oogenesis

---

# PART D: SPERMATOGENESIS

---

# 15. Spermatogenesis

## Definition

Formation of sperm from male germ cells is called spermatogenesis.

---

Occurs in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Seminiferous tubules}}
\]

---

# 16. Stages of Spermatogenesis

---

## Spermatogonia

Diploid cells that divide by mitosis.

↓

## Primary Spermatocyte

Undergoes meiosis I.

↓

## Secondary Spermatocytes

↓

## Spermatids

↓

## Spermatozoa

---

# 17. Spermiogenesis

## Definition

Transformation of spermatids into mature sperm is called spermiogenesis.

---

# 18. Structure of Sperm

A sperm consists of:

1. Head
2. Middle piece
3. Tail

---

## Head

Contains:

- Nucleus
- Acrosome

---

## Middle Piece

Contains:

- Mitochondria

Function:

Provides energy.

---

## Tail

Function:

Movement.

---

# PART E: OOGENESIS

---

# 19. Oogenesis

## Definition

Formation of female gametes (ova) is called oogenesis.

---

Occurs in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ovary}}
\]

---

# 20. Stages of Oogenesis

---

Oogonia

↓

Primary oocyte

↓

Secondary oocyte

↓

Ovum

---

# Differences Between Spermatogenesis and Oogenesis

| Spermatogenesis                | Oogenesis                  |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Occurs in testes               | Occurs in ovaries          |
| Produces sperm                 | Produces ovum              |
| Four functional gametes formed | One functional ovum formed |

---

# PART F: MENSTRUAL CYCLE

---

# 21. Menstrual Cycle

## Definition

The cyclic changes occurring in the female reproductive system during the reproductive period are called menstrual cycle.

---

Duration:

\[
\boxed{28\ days}
\]

---

# 22. Phases of Menstrual Cycle

---

## 1. Menstrual Phase

Days:

1–5

---

Events:

- Shedding of uterine lining.
- Menstrual bleeding occurs.

---

## 2. Follicular Phase

Days:

6–13

---

Events:

- Follicle development.
- Estrogen secretion increases.

---

## 3. Ovulation

Around:

\[
\boxed{14^{th}\ day}
\]

---

Cause:

LH surge

---

## 4. Luteal Phase

Days:

15–28

---

Events:

- Corpus luteum formation.
- Progesterone secretion.

---

# PART G: FERTILIZATION

---

# 23. Fertilization

## Definition

Fusion of sperm and ovum to form a zygote is called fertilization.

---

Site:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ampulla of fallopian tube}}
\]

---

# 24. Events of Fertilization

1. Sperm reaches ovum.
2. Acrosome releases enzymes.
3. Fusion of gametes occurs.
4. Zygote forms.

---

# PART H: EMBRYO DEVELOPMENT

---

# 25. Cleavage

## Definition

Rapid mitotic divisions of zygote are called cleavage.

---

Stages:

Zygote

↓

Morula

↓

Blastocyst

---

# 26. Blastocyst

Structure:

- Trophoblast
- Inner cell mass

---

Function:

- Trophoblast helps implantation.
- Inner cell mass forms embryo.

---

# 27. Implantation

## Definition

Attachment of blastocyst to uterine wall is called implantation.

---

Occurs:

\[
\boxed{\text{Endometrium of uterus}}
\]

---

# PART I: PREGNANCY

---

# 28. Pregnancy

## Definition

The period during which embryo develops inside uterus is called pregnancy.

---

Duration:

\[
\boxed{\text{About 9 months}}
\]

---

# 29. Placenta

## Definition

A temporary organ connecting mother and foetus is called placenta.

---

Functions:

- Exchange of gases.
- Transfer of nutrients.
- Removal of wastes.
- Hormone secretion.

---

Placental hormones:

- hCG
- Estrogen
- Progesterone
- hPL

---

# PART J: PARTURITION

---

# 30. Parturition

## Definition

The process of childbirth is called parturition.

---

Controlled by:

- Oxytocin hormone.

---

Steps:

1. Uterine contraction.
2. Expulsion of baby.
3. Placenta removal.

---

# PART K: LACTATION

---

# 31. Lactation

## Definition

Production of milk by mammary glands after childbirth is called lactation.

---

Hormones involved:

## Prolactin

Function:

Milk production.

---

## Oxytocin

Function:

Milk release.

---

## Male vs Female Gametogenesis

| Male                  | Female              |
| --------------------- | ------------------- |
| Spermatogenesis       | Oogenesis           |
| Produces many gametes | Produces one ovum   |
| Starts at puberty     | Begins before birth |

---

## Estrogen vs Progesterone

| Estrogen                   | Progesterone            |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Develops female characters | Maintains pregnancy     |
| Helps follicle growth      | Supports uterine lining |

---

Site of fertilization in humans:

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing fertilization and implantation.
- Forgetting sperm formation occurs in seminiferous tubules.
- Mixing estrogen and progesterone functions.
- Forgetting placenta acts as an endocrine organ.
- Confusing prolactin and oxytocin.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Male reproductive system produces sperm and testosterone.
- Female reproductive system produces ova and supports pregnancy.
- Spermatogenesis and oogenesis form gametes.
- Fertilization produces a zygote.
- Implantation allows embryo development.
- Placenta provides nutrition and hormonal support.
- Parturition results in childbirth and lactation provides nourishment to newborns.

# Chapter 3: Reproductive Health

> **Subject:** Biology

---

# Introduction

Reproductive health refers to the total well-being of individuals in all aspects related to reproduction, including physical, emotional and social health.

This chapter covers:

- Need for reproductive health
- Sexually transmitted diseases
- Birth control methods
- Family planning
- Medical termination of pregnancy
- Infertility
- Assisted reproductive technologies

---

# PART A: REPRODUCTIVE HEALTH

---

# 1. Reproductive Health

## Definition

Reproductive health is the ability to have a safe and healthy reproductive life with proper knowledge, care and freedom of choice.

---

# 2. Importance of Reproductive Health

It helps in:

- Preventing sexually transmitted diseases.
- Controlling population growth.
- Ensuring safe motherhood.
- Improving awareness about reproduction.
- Preventing unwanted pregnancies.

---

# 3. Reproductive Health Programmes

Government programmes focus on:

- Sex education.
- Maternal and child health.
- Family planning.
- Prevention of reproductive disorders.

---

# PART B: SEXUALLY TRANSMITTED DISEASES (STDs)

---

# 4. Sexually Transmitted Diseases

## Definition

Diseases transmitted mainly through sexual contact are called sexually transmitted diseases.

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Venereal diseases}}
\]

---

# 5. Common STDs

Examples:

| Disease        | Causative Organism |
| -------------- | ------------------ |
| Gonorrhoea     | Bacteria           |
| Syphilis       | Bacteria           |
| AIDS           | HIV virus          |
| Genital herpes | Herpes virus       |
| Genital warts  | HPV                |
| Hepatitis-B    | HBV                |

---

# 6. Symptoms of STDs

Common symptoms:

- Abnormal discharge.
- Pain during urination.
- Genital itching.
- Swelling or sores.
- Fever and weakness.

---

# 7. Prevention of STDs

Methods:

- Avoid unsafe sexual practices.
- Use condoms.
- Maintain personal hygiene.
- Avoid sharing infected needles.
- Early diagnosis and treatment.

---

# PART C: BIRTH CONTROL

---

# 8. Birth Control

## Definition

Methods used to prevent unwanted pregnancy are called contraceptive methods.

---

# 9. Need for Birth Control

Reasons:

- Population control.
- Spacing between children.
- Preventing unwanted pregnancies.
- Protecting maternal health.

---

# PART D: CONTRACEPTIVE METHODS

---

# 10. Natural Methods

Methods based on avoiding intercourse during fertile periods.

---

Examples:

## Periodic Abstinence

Avoiding intercourse during ovulation period.

---

## Withdrawal Method

Removal of penis before ejaculation.

---

## Lactational Amenorrhea

Temporary prevention of ovulation during breastfeeding.

---

# 11. Barrier Methods

Prevent sperm from reaching ovum.

---

Examples:

## Condoms

- Male condom.
- Female condom.

Advantages:

- Prevent pregnancy.
- Protect against STDs.

---

## Diaphragm

Placed over cervix to prevent sperm entry.

---

# 12. Intrauterine Devices (IUDs)

Devices placed inside uterus.

---

Examples:

## Copper-T

Releases copper ions.

Function:

- Prevents fertilization.

---

## Hormone-releasing IUDs

Release hormones to prevent pregnancy.

---

# 13. Oral Contraceptive Pills

Contain hormones:

- Estrogen
- Progesterone

---

Functions:

- Prevent ovulation.
- Prevent implantation.

---

# 14. Injectable and Implant Contraceptives

Release hormones slowly into the body.

---

# 15. Surgical Methods

Permanent methods of contraception.

---

## Vasectomy

Male sterilization.

Procedure:

- Vas deferens is cut and tied.

---

## Tubectomy

Female sterilization.

Procedure:

- Fallopian tubes are cut and tied.

---

# PART E: MEDICAL TERMINATION OF PREGNANCY (MTP)

---

# 16. MTP

## Definition

Intentional termination of pregnancy before full development of foetus is called medical termination of pregnancy.

---

Reasons:

- Unwanted pregnancy.
- Pregnancy due to contraceptive failure.
- Risk to mother's health.
- Genetic abnormalities.

---

# 17. Importance of MTP

- Prevents unsafe abortions.
- Protects maternal health.
- Helps in family planning.

---

# PART F: AMNIOCENTESIS

---

# 18. Amniocentesis

## Definition

A prenatal diagnostic technique used to detect genetic disorders in foetus is called amniocentesis.

---

Procedure:

- Amniotic fluid is collected.
- Foetal cells are analysed.

---

Detects:

- Chromosomal abnormalities.
- Genetic disorders.

---

# Note:

Misuse of amniocentesis for sex determination is illegal.

---

# PART G: INFERTILITY

---

# 19. Infertility

## Definition

The inability to conceive after regular unprotected intercourse is called infertility.

---

Causes:

## Male Factors

- Low sperm count.
- Poor sperm motility.
- Hormonal problems.

---

## Female Factors

- Ovulation problems.
- Blocked fallopian tubes.
- Uterine disorders.

---

# 20. Treatment of Infertility

Methods:

- Medicines.
- Surgery.
- Assisted reproductive technologies.

---

# PART H: ASSISTED REPRODUCTIVE TECHNOLOGIES (ART)

---

# 21. ART

## Definition

Techniques used to help infertile couples achieve pregnancy are called assisted reproductive technologies.

---

Major ART methods:

1. IVF
2. ZIFT
3. GIFT

---

# 22. In Vitro Fertilization (IVF)

## Definition

Fusion of sperm and ovum outside the body in laboratory conditions is called IVF.

---

Steps:

1. Collection of eggs.
2. Collection of sperm.
3. Fertilization in laboratory.
4. Embryo transfer to uterus.

---

Commonly called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Test tube baby technique}}
\]

---

# 23. Zygote Intrafallopian Transfer (ZIFT)

## Definition

Transfer of a zygote or early embryo into the fallopian tube is called ZIFT.

---

Process:

Zygote formed by IVF

↓

Transferred into fallopian tube

---

# 24. Gamete Intrafallopian Transfer (GIFT)

## Definition

Transfer of gametes directly into fallopian tube for fertilization is called GIFT.

---

Used when:

- Female can produce healthy eggs.
- Fertilization is possible inside body.

---

# Important Differences

## Vasectomy vs Tubectomy

| Vasectomy              | Tubectomy                  |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Male sterilization     | Female sterilization       |
| Vas deferens cut       | Fallopian tubes cut        |
| Prevents sperm release | Prevents egg-sperm meeting |

---

## IVF vs GIFT

| IVF                               | GIFT                             |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Fertilization occurs outside body | Fertilization occurs inside body |
| Embryo transferred                | Gametes transferred              |

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Disease caused by HIV:

Answer:

\[

\]

---

## Example 2

Male surgical contraceptive method:

\[

---

\[

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing STDs and genetic disorders.
- Mixing vasectomy and tubectomy.
- Forgetting condoms prevent STDs.
- Confusing IVF, ZIFT and GIFT.
- Forgetting amniocentesis is a diagnostic technique.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Reproductive health ensures safe and healthy reproductive life.
- STDs can be prevented through safe practices.
- Contraceptive methods help control pregnancy.
- MTP provides safe termination of unwanted pregnancies.
- Infertility can be treated using ART techniques.
- IVF, ZIFT and GIFT help infertile couples achieve pregnancy.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Reproductive health promotes responsible and healthy reproductive choices.}
}

# Introduction

Inheritance is the process by which genetic information is transferred from parents to offspring. Variation produces differences among individuals and helps in evolution.

This chapter covers:

- Mendelian genetics
- Laws of inheritance
- Monohybrid and dihybrid crosses
- Incomplete dominance
- Codominance
- Multiple alleles
- Chromosome theory
- Sex determination
- Linkage and crossing over
- Sex-linked inheritance
- Genetic disorders

---

# PART A: HEREDITY AND VARIATION

---

# 1. Heredity

## Definition

The transmission of genetic characters from parents to offspring is called heredity.

---

# 2. Variation

## Definition

Differences among individuals of the same species are called variations.

---

Importance:

- Helps adaptation.
- Provides raw material for evolution.

---

# PART B: MENDELIAN GENETICS

---

# 3. Gregor Johann Mendel

Known as:

\[
\boxed{\text{Father of Genetics}}
\]

---

Experiment:

- Conducted on pea plants (_Pisum sativum_).

---

Reasons for selecting pea plant:

- Short life cycle.
- Clear contrasting characters.
- Self-pollination possible.
- Easy hybridisation.

---

# 4. Mendel's Terms

---

## Gene

Unit of heredity responsible for a character.

---

## Allele

Alternative form of a gene.

Example:

Tallness:

T

Dwarfness:

t

---

## Dominant Character

Character expressed in heterozygous condition.

Example:

T = Tall

---

## Recessive Character

Character hidden in heterozygous condition.

Example:

t = Dwarf

---

# PART C: MENDEL'S LAWS

---

# 5. Law of Dominance

## Definition

When two contrasting alleles are present together, only one allele expresses itself.

---

Example:

Tt → Tall plant

---

# 6. Law of Segregation

## Definition

The two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation.

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Law of purity of gametes}}
\]

---

# 7. Law of Independent Assortment

## Definition

Genes controlling different characters assort independently during gamete formation.

---

# PART D: MONOHYBRID CROSS

---

# 8. Monohybrid Cross

## Definition

A cross involving one pair of contrasting characters is called monohybrid cross.

---

Example:

Tall plant × Dwarf plant

---

Parents:

TT × tt

---

F₁ Generation:

All:

\[
\boxed{Tt\ (Tall)}
\]

---

F₂ Generation:

Tt × Tt

---

Phenotypic ratio:

\[
\boxed{3:1}
\]

Tall : Dwarf

---

Genotypic ratio:

\[
\boxed{1:2:1}
\]

TT : Tt : tt

---

# PART E: DIHYBRID CROSS

---

# 9. Dihybrid Cross

## Definition

A cross involving two pairs of contrasting characters is called dihybrid cross.

---

Example:

Seed colour and seed shape in pea.

---

Phenotypic ratio:

\[
\boxed{9:3:3:1}
\]

---

Shows:

\[
\boxed{\text{Independent assortment}}
\]

---

# PART F: NON-MENDELIAN INHERITANCE

---

# 10. Incomplete Dominance

## Definition

A condition where neither allele is completely dominant and an intermediate phenotype appears.

---

Example:

Flower colour in Snapdragon.

---

Cross:

Red × White

↓

Pink flowers

---

Phenotypic ratio:

\[
1:2:1
\]

---

# 11. Codominance

## Definition

A condition where both alleles express equally in heterozygous condition.

---

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{AB blood group}}
\]

---

Genotype:

IAIB

---

Both A and B antigens are expressed.

---

# 12. Multiple Alleles

## Definition

Presence of more than two alleles controlling a character is called multiple allelism.

---

Example:

ABO blood group system.

---

Alleles:

- IA
- IB
- i

---

## Dominance vs Codominance

| Dominance                | Codominance             |

# 23. In Situ Conservation

## Definition

Protection of organisms in their natural habitats is called in situ conservation.

---

Examples:

- National parks.
- Wildlife sanctuaries.
- Biosphere reserves.

---

# 24. National Parks

Protected areas conserving wildlife and habitats.

---

Example:

- Yellowstone National Park.

---

# 25. Wildlife Sanctuaries

Areas where wild animals are protected from hunting and disturbance.

---

# 26. Biosphere Reserves

## Definition

Large protected areas conserving biodiversity along with sustainable use of resources.

---

Zones:

1. Core zone
2. Buffer zone
3. Transition zone

---

# PART I: EX SITU CONSERVATION

---

# 27. Ex Situ Conservation

## Definition

Conservation of organisms outside their natural habitats is called ex situ conservation.

---

Examples:

- Zoos.
- Botanical gardens.
- Seed banks.
- Gene banks.

---

A record containing information about endangered and threatened species is called the Red Data Book.

---

Maintained by:

\[

# PART K: SACRED GROVES

---

# 29. Sacred Groves

## Definition

Small forest patches protected by local communities due to religious and cultural beliefs are called sacred groves.

---

Importance:

- Preserve rare species.
- Protect biodiversity.

---

Examples in India:

- Khasi hills.
- Western Ghats.

# Important Differences

## In Situ vs Ex Situ Conservation

| In Situ                         | Ex Situ                              |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Conservation in natural habitat | Conservation outside natural habitat |
| Protects whole ecosystem        | Protects selected species            |
| Example: National parks         | Example: Zoos                        |

---

## Genetic Diversity vs Species Diversity

| Genetic Diversity        | Species Diversity  |
| ------------------------ | ------------------ |
| Variation within species | Variety of species |
| Gene level               | Species level      |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing biodiversity levels.
- Mixing in situ and ex situ conservation.
- Forgetting hotspots require high endemism and habitat loss.
- Confusing endangered and extinct species.
- Forgetting Red Data Book is maintained by IUCN.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Biodiversity includes genetic, species and ecosystem diversity.
- Biodiversity supports ecological balance and human welfare.
- Habitat loss, over-exploitation, invasive species and co-extinction cause biodiversity loss.
- Conservation can be in situ or ex situ.
- Hotspots protect highly diverse threatened regions.
- Biosphere reserves, sacred groves and protected areas help conserve biodiversity.
