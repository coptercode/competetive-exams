# Introduction

Gravitation is the force of attraction between any two masses in the universe.

It explains:

- Motion of planets
- Satellite movement
- Ocean tides
- Weight of objects
- Formation of stars and galaxies

This unit covers:

- Newton's law of gravitation
- Kepler's laws
- Acceleration due to gravity
- Variation of g
- Satellites
- Orbital velocity
- Escape velocity
- Weightlessness

---

# PART A: NEWTON'S LAW OF GRAVITATION

---

# 1. Gravitational Force

## Definition

The attractive force between any two bodies having mass is called gravitational force.

---

# 2. Universal Law of Gravitation

## Statement

Every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force that is:

1. Directly proportional to the product of their masses.
2. Inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

---

Mathematically:

\[
F\propto m_1m_2
\]

and

\[
F\propto\frac1{r^2}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
F=\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}
}
\]

---

where:

- F = gravitational force
- m₁, m₂ = masses
- r = distance between centres
- G = universal gravitational constant

---

# 3. Gravitational Constant (G)

Value:

\[
\boxed{
G=6.674\times10^{-11}Nm^2/kg^2
}
\]

---

Properties:

- Universal constant
- Same everywhere
- Independent of medium

---

# PART B: KEPLER'S LAWS OF PLANETARY MOTION

---

# 4. Kepler's First Law

## Law of Orbits

Every planet moves around the Sun in an elliptical orbit with the Sun at one focus.

---

# 5. Kepler's Second Law

## Law of Areas

The line joining the Sun and planet sweeps equal areas in equal intervals of time.

---

This means:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Areal velocity is constant}
}
\]

---

# 6. Kepler's Third Law

## Law of Periods

The square of the time period of revolution is directly proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
T^2\propto r^3
}
\]

or:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{T^2}{r^3}=constant
}
\]

---

# PART C: ACCELERATION DUE TO GRAVITY

---

# 7. Acceleration Due to Gravity (g)

## Definition

The acceleration produced in a body due to Earth's gravitational force is called acceleration due to gravity.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{g}
\]

---

Value:

\[
\boxed{
g=9.8m/s^2
}
\]

---

# 8. Relation Between G and g

Gravitational force on an object:

\[
F=\frac{GMm}{R^2}
\]

Weight:

\[
F=mg
\]

Equating:

\[
mg=\frac{GMm}{R^2}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
g=\frac{GM}{R^2}
}
\]

---

where:

- M = mass of Earth
- R = radius of Earth

---

# PART D: VARIATION OF g

---

# 9. Variation of g with Altitude

At height h above Earth's surface:

\[
\boxed{
g_h=
g
\left(
\frac{R}{R+h}
\right)^2
}
\]

---

As altitude increases:

\[
\boxed{
g\text{ decreases}
}
\]

---

# 10. Variation of g with Depth

At depth d below Earth's surface:

\[
\boxed{
g_d=g
\left(
1-\frac dR
\right)
}
\]

---

At Earth's centre:

\[
\boxed{
g=0
}
\]

---

# 11. Variation of g with Latitude

The value of g depends on Earth's rotation.

---

At poles:

\[
\boxed{
g\text{ maximum}
}
\]

At equator:

\[
\boxed{
g\text{ minimum}
}
\]

---

# PART E: GRAVITATIONAL POTENTIAL

---

# 12. Gravitational Potential Energy

Energy possessed by a body due to its position in gravitational field.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
U=-\frac{GMm}{r}
}
\]

---

Negative sign indicates gravitational force is attractive.

---

# 13. Gravitational Potential

Potential energy per unit mass.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
V=\frac UG
}
\]

or:

\[
\boxed{
V=-\frac{GM}{r}
}
\]

---

# PART A: ELASTICITY

---

# 1. Elasticity

## Definition

The property of a material by which it regains its original shape and size after removal of external force is called elasticity.

---

Examples:

- Steel spring
- Rubber band

---

# 2. Plasticity

## Definition

The property of a material by which it does not regain its original shape after removing force is called plasticity.

---

Examples:

- Clay
- Wax

---

# 3. Elastic Limit

The maximum value of external force up to which a body regains its original shape completely is called elastic limit.

---

# PART B: STRESS AND STRAIN

---

# 4. Stress

## Definition

The restoring force developed per unit area inside a deformed body is called stress.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Stress=\frac{Force}{Area}
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\sigma=\frac FA
}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{N/m^2}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{Pascal(Pa)}
\]

---

# Types of Stress

---

## 1. Longitudinal Stress

Stress produced due to change in length.

---

## 2. Bulk Stress

Stress produced due to change in volume.

---

## 3. Shearing Stress

Stress produced due to change in shape.

---

# 5. Strain

## Definition

The ratio of change in dimension to original dimension is called strain.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Strain=
\frac{Change}{Original\ value}
}
\]

---

Strain has no unit.

---

# Types of Strain

---

## 1. Longitudinal Strain

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Delta L}{L}
}
\]

---

## 2. Volume Strain

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Delta V}{V}
}
\]

---

## 3. Shear Strain

Change in angle of deformation.

---

# PART C: HOOKE'S LAW

---

# 6. Hooke's Law

## Statement

Within the elastic limit, stress is directly proportional to strain.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Stress\propto Strain
}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Stress}{Strain}=constant
}
\]

---

The constant is called elastic modulus.

---

# PART D: ELASTIC MODULI

---

# 7. Young's Modulus

## Definition

The ratio of longitudinal stress to longitudinal strain is called Young's modulus.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Y=
\frac{Longitudinal\ stress}
{Longitudinal\ strain}
}
\]

---

\[
\boxed{
Y=
\frac{F/A}{\Delta L/L}
}
\]

---

# 8. Bulk Modulus

## Definition

The ratio of volume stress to volume strain is called bulk modulus.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
K=
\frac{Pressure}
{Volume\ strain}
}
\]

---

\[
\boxed{
K=
-\frac{P}{\Delta V/V}
}
\]

---

# 9. Shear Modulus

Ratio of shearing stress to shearing strain.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
G=
\frac{Shear\ stress}
{Shear\ strain}
}
\]

---

# 10. Stress-Strain Curve

A graph showing relationship between stress and strain.

---

Important regions:

---

## Proportional Limit

---

## Elastic Limit

---

## Yield Point

---

## Plastic Region

Permanent deformation occurs.

---

## Breaking Point

Body breaks.

# PART E: FLUID MECHANICS

---

# 11. Pressure in Fluids

## Definition

Force acting normally per unit area is called pressure.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
P=\frac FA
}
\]

---

Pressure at depth h:

\[
\boxed{

}
\]

---

where:

- ρ = density
- h = depth
- g = acceleration due to gravity

---

# 12. Pascal's Law

## Statement

Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.

---

Applications:

- Hydraulic lift
- Hydraulic brakes
- Hydraulic press

---

# 13. Hydraulic Machines

Based on Pascal's law.

---

Hydraulic press:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{F_1}{A_1}
=

\frac{F_2}{A_2}
}
\]

---

---

# 14. Buoyancy

## Definition

The upward force exerted by a fluid on an immersed body is called buoyant force.

---

# 15. Archimedes' Principle

## Statement

A body immersed partially or completely in a fluid experiences an upward thrust equal to the weight of fluid displaced.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
F_b=V\rho g
}
\]

---

where:

- V = displaced volume
- ρ = fluid density

---

Applications:

- Ships
- Submarines
- Hydrometers

---

---

---

}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{Pa\,s}

---

# 18. Stokes' Law

## Statement

The viscous force acting on a small spherical body moving through a fluid is:

\boxed{

}
\]

---

where:

---

Formula:

\frac{2r^2(\rho-\sigma)g}
{9\eta}
}
\]

---

# 1. Kinetic Theory of Gases

## Definition

The kinetic theory of gases explains the properties of gases by assuming that gases consist of a large number of tiny molecules in random motion.

# 2. Assumptions of Kinetic Theory

1. A gas consists of a large number of molecules.

2. Molecules are in continuous random motion.

4. Molecules collide with each other and container walls elastically.

5. No force acts between molecules except during collisions.

\boxed{

}
\]

---

\[
V\propto T

Equal volumes of all gases at same temperature and pressure contain equal number of molecules.

Formula:

\boxed{

\[
\boxed{
v=\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}
}
\]

---

where:

- T = tension in string
- μ = mass per unit length

---

# 10. Velocity of Sound in Gas

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
v=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma P}{\rho}}
}
\]

---

where:

- γ = ratio of specific heats
- P = pressure
- ρ = density

---

# Factors Affecting Sound Velocity

Velocity increases with:

- Increase in temperature
- Increase in elasticity

Velocity decreases with:

- Increase in density

---

# PART E: SUPERPOSITION PRINCIPLE

---

# 11. Principle of Superposition

## Statement

When two or more waves overlap, the resultant displacement is equal to the algebraic sum of individual displacements.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
y=y_1+y_2
}
\]

---

Applications:

- Interference
- Beats
- Standing waves

---

# PART F: INTERFERENCE OF WAVES

---

# 12. Interference

## Definition

The phenomenon of redistribution of intensity due to superposition of two waves is called interference.

---

Types:

1. Constructive interference
2. Destructive interference

---

# 13. Constructive Interference

When two waves meet in same phase.

---

Condition:

\[
\boxed{
Path\ difference=n\lambda
}
\]

---

Result:

Maximum amplitude.

---

# 14. Destructive Interference

When two waves meet in opposite phase.

---

Condition:

\[
\boxed{
Path\ difference=
(2n+1)\frac{\lambda}{2}
}
\]

---

Result:

Minimum amplitude.

---

# PART G: STANDING WAVES

---

# 15. Standing Waves

## Definition

The waves formed by superposition of two waves having same frequency and amplitude travelling in opposite directions are called standing waves.

---

Features:

- Fixed nodes
- Fixed antinodes
- No net energy transfer

---

# 16. Nodes

Points where displacement is always zero.

---

# 17. Antinodes

Points where displacement is maximum.

---

# 18. Distance Between Nodes

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\lambda}{2}
}
\]

---

# 19. Distance Between Node and Antinode

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\lambda}{4}
}
\]

---

# PART H: WAVES IN STRINGS

---

# 20. Frequency of String

For a stretched string:

\[
\boxed{
f=
\frac1{2L}
\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}
}
\]

---

where:

- L = length of string
- T = tension
- μ = mass per unit length

---

# PART I: ORGAN PIPES

---

# 21. Open Organ Pipe

Both ends are open.

Fundamental frequency:

\[
\boxed{
f_1=
\frac{v}{2L}
}
\]

---

Harmonics:

\[
\boxed{
f_n=
\frac{nv}{2L}
}
\]

---

# 22. Closed Organ Pipe

One end closed.

Fundamental frequency:

\[
\boxed{
f_1=
\frac{v}{4L}
}
\]

---

Only odd harmonics occur:

\[
\boxed{
f_n=
\frac{(2n-1)v}{4L}
}
\]

---

# PART J: BEATS

---

# 23. Beats

## Definition

The periodic variation in intensity due to interference of two waves having slightly different frequencies is called beats.

---

Beat frequency:

\[
\boxed{
f_b=|f_1-f_2|
}
\]

---

Applications:

- Tuning musical instruments
- Determining unknown frequency

---

# PART K: DOPPLER EFFECT

---

# 24. Doppler Effect

## Definition

The apparent change in frequency of sound due to relative motion between source and observer is called Doppler effect.

---

Examples:

- Change in sound of moving vehicle
- Astronomical measurements

---

# 25. Doppler Effect Formula

General formula:

\[
\boxed{
f'=f
\frac{v\pm v_o}
{v\mp v_s}
}
\]

---

where:

- f' = apparent frequency
- f = actual frequency
- v = velocity of sound
- vo = velocity of observer
- vs = velocity of source

---

# Cases

---

## Observer Moving Towards Source

Frequency increases:

\[
\boxed{
f'=f\frac{v+v_o}{v}
}
\]

---

## Observer Moving Away

Frequency decreases:

\[
\boxed{
f'=f\frac{v-v_o}{v}
}
\]

---

## Source Moving Towards Observer

\[
\boxed{
f'=f\frac{v}{v-v_s}
}
\]

---

## Source Moving Away

\[
\boxed{
f'=f\frac{v}{v+v_s}
}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing wavelength and amplitude.
- Forgetting wave velocity formula.
- Mixing transverse and longitudinal waves.
- Assuming standing waves transfer energy.
- Forgetting closed pipes contain only odd harmonics.
- Using wrong signs in Doppler effect formula.
- Confusing beats frequency with actual frequency.
