# Introduction

Measurement is the process of comparing an unknown physical quantity with a known standard quantity.

This chapter covers:

- Physical quantities
- Fundamental and derived quantities
- SI system of units
- Measuring instruments
- Vernier caliper
- Screw gauge
- Errors in measurement

---

# PART A: PHYSICAL QUANTITIES

---

# 1. Physical Quantity

## Definition

A quantity that can be measured and expressed with a numerical value and unit is called a physical quantity.

---

Examples:

- Length
- Mass
- Time
- Temperature
- Speed

---

A physical quantity consists of:

\[
\boxed{\text{Numerical value + Unit}}
\]

Example:

\[
5m
\]

Here:

5 = numerical value

m = unit

---

# 2. Types of Physical Quantities

Physical quantities are classified into:

1. Fundamental quantities
2. Derived quantities

---

# PART B: FUNDAMENTAL QUANTITIES

---

# 3. Fundamental Quantity

## Definition

A physical quantity that is independent and cannot be expressed in terms of other quantities is called a fundamental quantity.

---

There are seven fundamental quantities in SI system.

---

# 4. SI Fundamental Quantities

| Quantity            | SI Unit  | Symbol |
| ------------------- | -------- | ------ |
| Length              | metre    | m      |
| Mass                | kilogram | kg     |
| Time                | second   | s      |
| Temperature         | kelvin   | K      |
| Electric current    | ampere   | A      |
| Amount of substance | mole     | mol    |
| Luminous intensity  | candela  | cd     |

---

# PART C: DERIVED QUANTITIES

---

# 5. Derived Quantity

## Definition

A physical quantity obtained by combining two or more fundamental quantities is called a derived quantity.

---

Examples:

### Area

\[
Area=Length\times Breadth
\]

Unit:

\[
m^2
\]

---

### Volume

\[
Volume=Length\times Breadth\times Height
\]

Unit:

\[
m^3
\]

---

### Speed

\[
Speed=\frac{Distance}{Time}
\]

Unit:

\[
m/s
\]

---

# PART D: SI SYSTEM OF UNITS

---

# 6. SI System

## Definition

The International System of Units used worldwide for measurement is called SI system.

---

SI means:

\[
\boxed{\text{International System of Units}}
\]

---

Advantages:

- Uniform system
- Easy comparison
- Used internationally

---

# 7. Common SI Prefixes

| Prefix | Symbol | Value |
| ------ | ------ | ----- |
| kilo   | k      | 10³   |
| centi  | c      | 10⁻²  |
| milli  | m      | 10⁻³  |
| micro  | μ      | 10⁻⁶  |
| nano   | n      | 10⁻⁹  |

---

---

# 8. Measuring Instruments

Different instruments are used depending on accuracy required.

Examples:

- Metre scale
- Vernier caliper
- Screw gauge

---

# 9. Metre Scale

Used to measure length.

---

\[
\boxed{1mm=0.1cm}
\]

---

# PART F: VERNIER CALIPER

---

# 10. Vernier Caliper

## Definition

A device used to measure small lengths accurately is called a vernier caliper.

---

Used for measuring:

- Diameter of objects
- Internal diameter
- External diameter
- Depth

---

# 11. Parts of Vernier Caliper

Main parts:

1. Main scale
2. Vernier scale
3. Jaws
4. Depth rod

---

# 12. Least Count of Vernier Caliper

## Definition

The smallest measurement that can be measured by an instrument is called least count.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Least\ Count=
1\ MSD-1\ VSD
}
\]

---

Usually:

\[
\boxed{0.01cm}
\]

---

# 13. Reading of Vernier Caliper

\[
\boxed{
Reading=
Main\ Scale\ Reading+
Vernier\ Scale\ Reading
}
\]

---

# PART G: SCREW GAUGE

---

# 14. Screw Gauge

## Definition

A precision instrument used to measure very small dimensions like thickness and diameter of wires is called screw gauge.

---

Used for measuring:

- Diameter of thin wires
- Thickness of sheets

---

# 15. Parts of Screw Gauge

Main parts:

1. Pitch scale
2. Circular scale
3. Ratchet
4. Spindle

---

# 16. Pitch of Screw Gauge

## Definition

The distance moved by the screw in one complete rotation is called pitch.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Pitch=
\frac{Distance\ moved}
{Number\ of\ rotations}
}
\]

---

# 17. Least Count of Screw Gauge

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Least\ Count=
\frac{Pitch}
{Number\ of\ divisions\ on\ circular\ scale}
}
\]

---

# 18. Screw Gauge Reading

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Reading=
Pitch\ Scale\ Reading+
Circular\ Scale\ Reading
}
\]

---

# PART H: ERRORS IN MEASUREMENT

---

# 19. Error

## Definition

The difference between measured value and actual value is called error.

---

# 20. Types of Errors

---

## 1. Systematic Error

Error that occurs due to instrument or method.

Examples:

- Zero error
- Calibration error

---

## 2. Random Error

Error that occurs due to unpredictable changes.

Example:

Small variations during measurement.

---

# 21. Accuracy and Precision

---

## Accuracy

## Definition

Closeness of measured value to the true value is called accuracy.

---

## Precision

## Definition

Closeness of repeated measurements to each other is called precision.

---

# Introduction

Motion is the change in position of an object with respect to time.

This chapter covers:

- Distance and displacement
- Speed and velocity
- Acceleration
- Types of motion
- Equations of motion
- Graphical representation of motion

---

# PART A: BASIC CONCEPTS OF MOTION

---

# 1. Motion

## Definition

An object is said to be in motion if its position changes with time relative to a reference point.

---

Examples:

- Moving car
- Flying bird
- Rotating fan

---

# 2. Rest

## Definition

An object is said to be at rest if its position does not change with time relative to a reference point.

---

Example:

A book kept on a table.

---

# 3. Reference Point

## Definition

A fixed point used to describe the position or motion of an object is called a reference point.

---

Example:

A tree can be used as a reference point to describe the motion of a car.

---

# PART B: DISTANCE AND DISPLACEMENT

---

# 4. Distance

## Definition

The total length of the path travelled by an object is called distance.

---

Properties:

- Scalar quantity
- Always positive
- Depends on actual path

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{metre\ (m)}
\]

---

# 5. Displacement

## Definition

The shortest distance between the initial and final positions of an object in a specified direction is called displacement.

---

Properties:

- Vector quantity
- Has magnitude and direction
- Can be zero

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{metre\ (m)}
\]

---

# 6. Difference Between Distance and Displacement

| Distance          | Displacement                                |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Total path length | Shortest distance                           |
| Scalar quantity   | Vector quantity                             |
| Always positive   | Can be positive, negative or zero           |
| Depends on path   | Depends only on initial and final positions |

---

# PART C: SPEED AND VELOCITY

---

# 7. Speed

## Definition

The distance travelled by an object per unit time is called speed.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Speed=\frac{Distance}{Time}
}
\]

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{m/s}
\]

---

# 8. Types of Speed

---

## Uniform Speed

An object travels equal distances in equal intervals of time.

---

Example:

A car moving at constant speed.

---

## Non-uniform Speed

An object travels unequal distances in equal intervals of time.

---

Example:

A bus moving in city traffic.

---

# 9. Average Speed

## Definition

Total distance travelled divided by total time taken is called average speed.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Average\ Speed=
\frac{Total\ Distance}
{Total\ Time}
}
\]

---

# 10. Velocity

## Definition

Displacement travelled per unit time is called velocity.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Velocity=
\frac{Displacement}{Time}
}
\]

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{m/s}
\]

---

# 11. Difference Between Speed and Velocity

| Speed           | Velocity          |
| --------------- | ----------------- |
| Distance/time   | Displacement/time |
| Scalar quantity | Vector quantity   |
| No direction    | Has direction     |

---

# PART D: ACCELERATION

---

# 12. Acceleration

## Definition

The rate of change of velocity with time is called acceleration.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
a=\frac{v-u}{t}
}
\]

---

Where:

a = acceleration

v = final velocity

u = initial velocity

t = time

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{m/s^2}
\]

---

# 13. Types of Acceleration

---

## Positive Acceleration

Velocity increases with time.

Example:

A speeding car.

---

## Negative Acceleration (Retardation)

Velocity decreases with time.

Example:

A braking vehicle.

---

## Zero Acceleration

Velocity remains constant.

Example:

Uniform motion.

---

# PART E: EQUATIONS OF MOTION

---

For uniformly accelerated motion:

---

# 14. First Equation of Motion

\[
\boxed{
v=u+at
}
\]

---

# 15. Second Equation of Motion

\[
\boxed{
s=ut+\frac12at^2
}
\]

---

# 16. Third Equation of Motion

\[
\boxed{
v^2=u^2+2as
}
\]

---

Where:

u = initial velocity

v = final velocity

a = acceleration

s = displacement

t = time

---

# PART F: GRAPHS OF MOTION

---

---

For uniform motion:

- Straight line graph
- Constant slope

---

# 18. Velocity-Time Graph

Shows relationship between velocity and time.

---

Slope represents acceleration:

\[
\boxed{
Acceleration = Slope\ of\ velocity-time\ graph
}
\]

---

Area under velocity-time graph gives displacement.

---

# PART G: TYPES OF MOTION

---

# 19. Uniform Motion

## Definition

An object travelling equal distances in equal intervals of time is said to be in uniform motion.

---

# 20. Non-uniform Motion

## Definition

An object travelling unequal distances in equal intervals of time is said to be in non-uniform motion.

---

# 21. Circular Motion

## Definition

Motion of an object along a circular path is called circular motion.

---

Example:

- Motion of clock hands
- Rotation of Earth

---

- Forgetting velocity has direction.
- Using wrong units.
- Mixing acceleration and velocity formulas.
- Applying equations of motion for non-uniform acceleration.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Motion describes change in position with time.
- Distance is total path travelled.
- Displacement is shortest distance in a direction.
- Speed and velocity measure rate of motion.
- Acceleration is change in velocity per unit time.
- Equations of motion solve uniformly accelerated motion problems.

\[

---

# Introduction

Force is an external agent that can change the state of rest or motion of an object.

This chapter covers:

- Force
- Effects of force
- Newton's laws of motion
- Inertia
- Momentum
- Balanced and unbalanced forces
- Friction

---

# PART A: FORCE

---

# 1. Force

## Definition

A push or pull acting on an object is called force.

---

Examples:

- Pushing a door
- Pulling a cart
- Kicking a ball

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{\text{Newton (N)}}
\]

---

# 2. Effects of Force

Force can:

1. Change the state of rest.
2. Change the state of motion.
3. Change speed.
4. Change direction.
5. Change shape of an object.

---

Example:

A football at rest starts moving when kicked.

---

# PART B: INERTIA

---

# 3. Inertia

## Definition

The tendency of an object to resist any change in its state of rest or motion is called inertia.

---

Newton's first law is also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Law of Inertia}}
\]

---

# 4. Types of Inertia

---

## 1. Inertia of Rest

An object at rest tends to remain at rest.

---

Example:

When a bus starts suddenly, passengers move backward.

---

## 2. Inertia of Motion

A moving object tends to continue moving.

---

Example:

Passengers move forward when a moving bus stops suddenly.

---

## 3. Inertia of Direction

An object tends to continue moving in the same direction.

---

Example:

A stone tied to a string moves in a circular path when rotated.

---

# PART C: MOMENTUM

---

# 5. Momentum

## Definition

The product of mass and velocity of an object is called momentum.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
p=mv
}
\]

---

Where:

p = momentum

m = mass

v = velocity

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{kg\,m/s}
\]

---

# Example

Find momentum of an object of mass 5 kg moving with velocity 4 m/s.

Given:

\[
m=5kg
\]

\[
v=4m/s
\]

\[
p=mv
\]

\[
=5\times4
\]

\[
\boxed{20kg\,m/s}
\]

---

# PART D: NEWTON'S LAWS OF MOTION

---

# 6. Newton's First Law of Motion

## Statement

An object remains at rest or continues in uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force.

---

Examples:

- A book remains on a table.
- A moving ball stops due to friction.

---

# 7. Newton's Second Law of Motion

## Statement

The rate of change of momentum is directly proportional to the applied force and occurs in the direction of force.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
F=ma
}
\]

---

Where:

F = force

m = mass

a = acceleration

---

# Derivation

Momentum:

\[
p=mv
\]

Change in momentum:

\[
m(v-u)
\]

Rate of change:

\[
\frac{m(v-u)}t
\]

Since:

\[
a=\frac{v-u}{t}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{F=ma}
\]

---

# 8. Newton's Third Law of Motion

## Statement

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

---

Examples:

- Recoil of gun
- Swimming
- Rocket propulsion

---

# PART E: CONSERVATION OF MOMENTUM

---

# 9. Law of Conservation of Momentum

## Statement

The total momentum of an isolated system remains constant if no external force acts on it.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
m_1u_1+m_2u_2
=

m_1v_1+m_2v_2
}
\]

---

Applications:

- Rocket motion
- Collision of objects
- Gun recoil

---

# PART F: BALANCED AND UNBALANCED FORCES

---

# 10. Balanced Forces

## Definition

Forces acting on an object that are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction are called balanced forces.

---

Effects:

- No change in motion
- Acceleration is zero

---

Example:

A book resting on a table.

---

# 11. Unbalanced Forces

## Definition

Forces that produce a change in motion are called unbalanced forces.

---

Effects:

- Change speed
- Change direction

---

Example:

Kicking a moving ball.

---

# PART G: FRICTION

---

# 12. Friction

## Definition

The force that opposes the relative motion between two surfaces in contact is called friction.

---

Direction:

Opposite to motion.

---

# 13. Types of Friction

---

## Static Friction

Friction acting on an object at rest.

---

## Sliding Friction

Friction when one surface slides over another.

---

## Rolling Friction

Friction when an object rolls over a surface.

---

Relationship:

\[
\boxed{
Rolling\ friction < Sliding\ friction < Static\ friction
}
\]

---

# 14. Advantages of Friction

- Helps us walk.
- Allows writing.
- Helps vehicles move.
- Helps braking.

---

# 15. Disadvantages of Friction

- Produces heat.
- Causes wear and tear.
- Reduces efficiency of machines.

---

# 16. Methods to Reduce Friction

- Lubrication
- Polishing surfaces
- Using ball bearings

---

# Introduction

Gravitation is the force of attraction between any two objects having mass.

This chapter covers:

- Universal law of gravitation
- Mass and weight
- Acceleration due to gravity
- Free fall
- Difference between mass and weight

---

# PART A: GRAVITATION

---

# 1. Gravitation

## Definition

The force of attraction between any two objects in the universe is called gravitation.

---

Examples:

- Falling of objects towards Earth
- Motion of planets around the Sun
- Motion of Moon around Earth

---

# 2. Gravity

## Definition

The gravitational force exerted by the Earth on objects near its surface is called gravity.

---

Example:

An apple falls towards Earth due to gravity.

---

# PART B: UNIVERSAL LAW OF GRAVITATION

---

# 3. Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation

## Statement

Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
F=\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}
}
\]

---

Where:

F = gravitational force

m₁, m₂ = masses of objects

r = distance between centres

G = universal gravitational constant

---

# 4. Gravitational Constant

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{G}
\]

Value:

\[
\boxed{
6.67\times10^{-11}Nm^2/kg^2
}
\]

---

# PART C: MASS AND WEIGHT

---

# 5. Mass

## Definition

The amount of matter contained in an object is called mass.

---

Properties:

- Constant everywhere
- Scalar quantity

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{kg}
\]

---

# 6. Weight

## Definition

The gravitational force acting on an object is called weight.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
W=mg
}
\]

---

Where:

W = weight

m = mass

g = acceleration due to gravity

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{Newton(N)}
\]

---

# 7. Difference Between Mass and Weight

| Mass                | Weight               |
| ------------------- | -------------------- |
| Amount of matter    | Gravitational force  |
| Constant everywhere | Changes with gravity |
| Scalar quantity     | Vector quantity      |
| Unit kg             | Unit N               |

---

# PART D: ACCELERATION DUE TO GRAVITY

---

# 8. Acceleration Due to Gravity

## Definition

The acceleration produced in a freely falling object due to Earth's gravitational force is called acceleration due to gravity.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{g}
\]

---

Value near Earth's surface:

\[
\boxed{
g=9.8m/s^2
}
\]

---

# 9. Factors Affecting g

The value of g changes with:

- Height above Earth's surface
- Depth below Earth's surface
- Location on Earth

---

## Definition

The motion of an object falling only under the influence of gravity is called free fall.

---

Examples:

Acceleration:

\[
\boxed{g}
\]

}

---

# 12. Thrust

## Definition

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{Newton(N)}
\]

---

# 13. Pressure

## Definition

The force acting per unit area is called pressure.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Pressure=\frac{Force}{Area}
}
\]

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{Pascal(Pa)}
\]

---

# 14. Factors Affecting Pressure

Pressure increases when:

- Force increases
- Area decreases

---

Examples:

- Sharp knife cuts easily.
- Nails have pointed ends.

---

# PART G: BUOYANCY

---

# 15. Buoyant Force

## Definition

The upward force exerted by a fluid on an immersed object is called buoyant force.

---

Example:

A ship floating on water.

---

# 16. Archimedes' Principle

## Statement

A body immersed in a fluid experiences an upward force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.

Substituting:

\[

\]

# PART A: NATURAL RESOURCES

---

# 1. Natural Resources

## Definition

Resources obtained from nature that are useful to living organisms are called natural resources.

---

Examples:

- Air
- Water
- Soil
- Minerals
- Forests

---

# 2. Types of Natural Resources

Natural resources are classified into:

1. Renewable resources
2. Non-renewable resources

---

# 3. Renewable Resources

## Definition

Resources that can be renewed or replaced naturally in a short period are called renewable resources.

---

Examples:

- Solar energy
- Wind energy
- Water
- Forests

---

# 4. Non-Renewable Resources

## Definition

Resources that are limited and take millions of years to form are called non-renewable resources.

---

Examples:

- Coal
- Petroleum
- Natural gas
- Minerals

---

# PART B: AIR AS A NATURAL RESOURCE

---

# 5. Atmosphere

## Definition

The layer of gases surrounding the Earth is called atmosphere.

---

Major gases:

| Gas                       | Percentage |
| ------------------------- | ---------- |
| Nitrogen                  | 78%        |
| Oxygen                    | 21%        |
| Carbon dioxide and others | 1%         |

---

# 6. Importance of Atmosphere

- Provides oxygen for respiration.
- Provides carbon dioxide for photosynthesis.
- Maintains Earth's temperature.
- Protects from harmful radiation.

---

# 7. Air Pollution

## Definition

The addition of harmful substances into air is called air pollution.

---

Causes:

- Vehicle emissions
- Industrial smoke
- Burning of fossil fuels

---

Effects:

- Respiratory diseases
- Global warming
- Acid rain

---

# PART C: WATER AS A NATURAL RESOURCE

---

# 8. Water Resource

Water is essential for:

- Drinking
- Agriculture
- Industry
- Survival of organisms

---

# 9. Water Cycle

## Definition

The continuous movement of water between atmosphere, land and oceans is called water cycle.

---

Steps:

1. Evaporation
2. Condensation
3. Precipitation
4. Collection

---

# 10. Water Pollution

## Definition

Contamination of water by harmful substances is called water pollution.

---

Causes:

- Industrial waste
- Sewage
- Fertilizers
- Pesticides

---

Effects:

- Death of aquatic organisms
- Spread of diseases
- Shortage of clean water

---

# PART D: SOIL AS A NATURAL RESOURCE

---

# 11. Soil

## Definition

The upper layer of Earth containing minerals, organic matter, water and air is called soil.

---

Importance:

- Supports plant growth.
- Provides habitat for organisms.
- Stores water and nutrients.

---

# 12. Soil Erosion

## Definition

The removal of top fertile soil by wind or water is called soil erosion.

---

Causes:

- Deforestation
- Overgrazing
- Heavy rainfall
- Improper farming

---

Effects:

- Loss of soil fertility
- Reduced agricultural production

---

# 13. Prevention of Soil Erosion

Methods:

- Afforestation
- Terrace farming
- Planting grass
- Contour farming

---

# PART E: BIOGEOCHEMICAL CYCLES

---

# 14. Biogeochemical Cycle

## Definition

The circulation of elements between living organisms and the environment is called a biogeochemical cycle.

---

Important cycles:

1. Carbon cycle
2. Nitrogen cycle
3. Water cycle
4. Oxygen cycle

---

# PART F: CARBON CYCLE

---

# 15. Carbon Cycle

## Definition

The movement of carbon between atmosphere, organisms and Earth is called carbon cycle.

---

Processes involved:

- Photosynthesis
- Respiration
- Decomposition
- Combustion

---

Importance:

- Maintains carbon balance.
- Supports life processes.

---

# PART G: NITROGEN CYCLE

---

# 16. Nitrogen Cycle

## Definition

The circulation of nitrogen between atmosphere, soil and living organisms is called nitrogen cycle.

---

Steps:

1. Nitrogen fixation
2. Nitrification
3. Assimilation
4. Decomposition
5. Denitrification

---

Importance:

- Helps plants make proteins.
- Maintains soil fertility.

---

# PART H: RESOURCE MANAGEMENT

---

# 17. Conservation of Natural Resources

## Definition

The careful use and protection of natural resources is called conservation.

---

Need for conservation:

- Prevent depletion of resources.
- Protect biodiversity.
- Maintain ecological balance.

---

# 18. Sustainable Development

## Definition

Development that meets present needs without harming future generations is called sustainable development.

---

Principles:

- Reduce waste
- Reuse materials
- Recycle resources
- Use renewable energy

---

# PART I: FOREST CONSERVATION

---

# 19. Importance of Forests

Forests provide:

- Oxygen
- Food
- Shelter for wildlife
- Climate regulation

---

# 20. Deforestation

## Definition

Large-scale removal of forests is called deforestation.

---

Effects:

- Loss of biodiversity
- Soil erosion
- Climate change

---

Prevention:

- Afforestation
- Forest protection
- Controlled cutting

---

# Important Points

- Natural resources support life on Earth.
- Renewable resources can be replaced naturally.
- Non-renewable resources are limited.
- Air, water and soil must be protected.
- Biogeochemical cycles maintain balance in nature.
- Conservation ensures resources for future generations.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing renewable and non-renewable resources.
- Forgetting soil is a natural resource.
- Mixing pollution and conservation concepts.
- Forgetting the steps of biogeochemical cycles.
- Ignoring the importance of forests.
