# Introduction

Ionic equilibrium deals with the equilibrium between ions present in aqueous solutions.

This unit covers:

- Acids and bases
- Acid-base theories
- Ionisation equilibrium
- pH and pOH calculations
- Buffer solutions
- Solubility product

Applications:

- Biological systems
- Water treatment
- Pharmaceutical chemistry
- Analytical chemistry

---

# PART A: ELECTROLYTES AND IONIC EQUILIBRIUM

---

# 1. Electrolytes

## Definition

Substances that produce ions when dissolved in water and conduct electricity are called electrolytes.

---

Examples:

\[
NaCl\rightarrow Na^+ + Cl^-
\]

\[
HCl\rightarrow H^+ + Cl^-
\]

---

# Types of Electrolytes

---

## Strong Electrolytes

Completely ionise in solution.

Examples:

- HCl
- NaOH
- NaCl

---

## Weak Electrolytes

Partially ionise in solution.

Examples:

- CH₃COOH
- NH₄OH

---

# 2. Ionic Equilibrium

## Definition

The equilibrium established between ions and unionised molecules in a weak electrolyte solution is called ionic equilibrium.

---

Example:

\[
CH_3COOH
\rightleftharpoons
H^+ + CH_3COO^-
\]

---

# PART B: THEORIES OF ACIDS AND BASES

---

# 3. Arrhenius Theory

## Acid

A substance that produces:

\[
H^+
\]

ions in water.

Example:

\[
HCl\rightarrow H^++Cl^-
\]

---

## Base

A substance that produces:

\[
OH^-
\]

ions in water.

Example:

\[
NaOH\rightarrow Na^++OH^-
\]

---

# Limitations

- Applicable only in aqueous solution.
- Cannot explain basic nature of NH₃.

---

# 4. Bronsted-Lowry Theory

## Acid

A proton donor.

Example:

\[
HCl\rightarrow H^++Cl^-
\]

---

## Base

A proton acceptor.

Example:

\[
NH_3+H^+
\rightarrow NH_4^+
\]

---

# Conjugate Acid-Base Pair

When an acid loses H⁺, it forms its conjugate base.

Example:

\[
HA\rightleftharpoons H^++A^-
\]

---

# 5. Lewis Theory

## Lewis Acid

Electron pair acceptor.

Examples:

\[
BF_3,\ AlCl_3
\]

---

## Lewis Base

Electron pair donor.

Examples:

\[
NH_3,\ OH^-
\]

---

# PART C: IONISATION CONSTANTS

---

# 6. Acid Dissociation Constant (Ka)

For weak acid:

\[
HA\rightleftharpoons H^++A^-
\]

---

\[
\boxed{
K_a=
\frac{[H^+][A^-]}{[HA]}
}
\]

---

Higher Ka:

\[
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{Stronger acid}}
\]

---

# 7. Base Dissociation Constant (Kb)

For weak base:

\[
BOH\rightleftharpoons B^++OH^-
\]

---

\[
\boxed{
K_b=
\frac{[B^+][OH^-]}{[BOH]}
}
\]

---

Higher Kb:

\[
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{Stronger base}}
\]

---

# 8. Ionic Product of Water

Water undergoes self-ionisation:

\[
H_2O
\rightleftharpoons
H^++OH^-
\]

---

\[
\boxed{
K_w=[H^+][OH^-]
}
\]

At 25°C:

\[
\boxed{
K_w=1.0\times10^{-14}
}
\]

---

# Relationship:

\[
\boxed{
K_aK_b=K_w
}
\]

---

# PART D: pH AND pOH

---

# 9. pH

## Definition

The negative logarithm of hydrogen ion concentration is called pH.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
pH=-\log[H^+]
}
\]

---

# 10. pOH

\[
\boxed{
pOH=-\log[OH^-]
}
\]

---

# Relation Between pH and pOH

At 25°C:

\[
\boxed{
pH+pOH=14
}
\]

---

# Nature of Solution

| pH  | Nature  |
| --- | ------- |
| <7  | Acidic  |
| =7  | Neutral |
| >7  | Basic   |

---

# 11. pH of Strong Acid

For strong acid:

\[
[H^+]=C
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
pH=-\log C
}
\]

---

# 12. pH of Strong Base

\[
[OH^-]=C
\]

\[
pOH=-\log C
\]

Then:

\[
\boxed{
pH=14-pOH
}
\]

---

# PART E: BUFFER SOLUTIONS

---

# 13. Buffer Solution

## Definition

A solution that resists change in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added is called a buffer solution.

---

# Types of Buffers

---

## 1. Acidic Buffer

Contains:

- Weak acid
- Salt of its conjugate base

Example:

\[
CH_3COOH+CH_3COONa
\]

---

## 2. Basic Buffer

Contains:

- Weak base
- Salt of its conjugate acid

Example:

\[
NH_4OH+NH_4Cl
\]

---

# 14. Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation

For acidic buffer:

\[
\boxed{
pH=pK_a+\log\frac{[Salt]}{[Acid]}
}
\]

---

For basic buffer:

\[
\boxed{
pOH=pK_b+\log\frac{[Salt]}{[Base]}
}
\]

---

# Importance of Buffers

- Blood pH regulation
- Pharmaceutical formulations
- Laboratory solutions

---

# PART F: SOLUBILITY PRODUCT

---

# 15. Solubility Equilibrium

For sparingly soluble salt:

\[
AB
\rightleftharpoons
A^++B^-
\]

---

Equilibrium constant:

\[
\boxed{
K_{sp}=[A^+][B^-]
}
\]

---

# 16. Solubility Product (Ksp)

## Definition

The product of ionic concentrations in a saturated solution is called solubility product.

---

Higher Ksp:

\[
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{Greater solubility}}
\]

---

# Examples

For:

\[
AgCl
\]

\[
AgCl
\rightleftharpoons
Ag^++Cl^-
\]

\[
\boxed{
K_{sp}=[Ag^+][Cl^-]
}
\]

---

For:

\[
CaF_2
\]

\[
CaF_2
\rightleftharpoons
Ca^{2+}+2F^-
\]

\[
\boxed{
K_{sp}=[Ca^{2+}][F^-]^2
}
\]

---

# PART G: COMMON ION EFFECT

---

# 17. Common Ion Effect

## Definition

The suppression of ionisation of a weak electrolyte by adding a strong electrolyte containing a common ion is called common ion effect.

---

Example:

Ionisation of acetic acid:

\[
CH_3COOH
\rightleftharpoons
H^++CH_3COO^-
\]

Addition of sodium acetate:

\[
CH_3COONa
\]

increases:

\[
CH_3COO^-
\]

and decreases ionisation.

---

# Important Formula Sheet

## Acid Constant

\[
K_a=
\frac{[H^+][A^-]}{[HA]}
\]

---

## Base Constant

\[
K_b=
\frac{[B^+][OH^-]}{[BOH]}
\]

---

## Water Constant

\[
K_w=[H^+][OH^-]
\]

---

## Relationship

\[
K_aK_b=K_w
\]

---

## pH

\[
pH=-\log[H^+]
\]

---

## pOH

\[
pOH=-\log[OH^-]
\]

---

## pH-pOH Relation

\[
pH+pOH=14
\]

---

## Buffer Equation

\[
pH=pK_a+\log\frac{Salt}{Acid}
\]

---

## Solubility Product

\[
K_{sp}=[ions]
\]

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

Find pH of:

\[
[H^+]=10^{-3}
\]

Solution:

\[
pH=-\log(10^{-3})
\]

\[
\boxed{pH=3}
\]

---

## Example 2

Find pOH if:

\[
pH=5
\]

Using:

\[
pH+pOH=14
\]

\[
5+pOH=14
\]

\[
\boxed{pOH=9}
\]

---

## Example 3

Write Ksp expression for AgCl.

Reaction:

\[
AgCl
\rightarrow
Ag^++Cl^-
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
K_{sp}=[Ag^+][Cl^-]
}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing Arrhenius and Bronsted theories.
- Forgetting pH uses hydrogen ion concentration.
- Using Celsius instead of correct temperature for Kw.
- Mixing Ka and Kb equations.
- Forgetting:

\[
pH+pOH=14
\]

- Writing incorrect Ksp expressions.
- Confusing buffer solution with normal salt solution.

---

# Unit Summary

- Ionic equilibrium explains behaviour of ions in solution.
- Acids donate H⁺ ions and bases accept H⁺ ions.
- pH measures acidity or basicity.
- Buffers maintain constant pH.
- Henderson-Hasselbalch equation calculates buffer pH.
- Ksp explains solubility of sparingly soluble salts.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Equilibrium controls the behaviour of ions in aqueous solutions.}
}
\]

# Introduction

Electrochemistry deals with the relationship between:

- Chemical reactions
- Electrical energy

It explains how chemical energy can be converted into electrical energy and vice versa.

This unit covers:

- Electrolytic conductance
- Conductivity
- Molar conductivity
- Kohlrausch's law
- Galvanic cells
- Electrode potential
- Nernst equation
- Electrochemical cells

Applications:

- Batteries
- Electroplating
- Corrosion prevention
- Fuel cells

---

# PART A: ELECTROLYTIC CONDUCTANCE

---

# 1. Electrolytes

## Definition

Substances that produce ions in aqueous solution and conduct electricity are called electrolytes.

Examples:

\[
NaCl\rightarrow Na^++Cl^-
\]

\[
HCl\rightarrow H^++Cl^-
\]

---

# 2. Conductance

## Definition

The ability of a solution to conduct electricity is called conductance.

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{G}
\]

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
G=\frac1R
}
\]

where:

- G = conductance
- R = resistance

Unit:

\[
\boxed{Siemens(S)}
\]

---

# 3. Resistance

According to Ohm's law:

\[
\boxed{
R=\frac{V}{I}
}
\]

where:

- V = potential difference
- I = current

---

# 4. Specific Conductance (Conductivity)

## Definition

Conductance of a solution kept between two electrodes of unit area separated by unit distance is called specific conductance.

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{\kappa}
\]

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\kappa=G\frac{l}{A}
}
\]

where:

- l = distance between electrodes
- A = area of electrodes

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{
S\,cm^{-1}
}
\]

---

# 5. Molar Conductivity

## Definition

The conductance of all ions produced by dissolving one mole of electrolyte in solution is called molar conductivity.

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{\Lambda_m}
\]

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_m=
\frac{\kappa\times1000}{C}
}
\]

where:

- C = concentration in mol L⁻¹

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{
S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}
}
\]

---

# 6. Variation of Molar Conductivity

---

## Strong Electrolytes

Example:

- HCl
- NaCl

Molar conductivity increases slightly on dilution.

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_m=\Lambda_m^0-A\sqrt C
}
\]

---

## Weak Electrolytes

Example:

- CH₃COOH

Molar conductivity increases greatly on dilution.

Reason:

Increase in ionisation.

---

# PART B: KOHLRAUSCH'S LAW

---

# 7. Kohlrausch's Law

## Statement

At infinite dilution, each ion contributes independently to the total molar conductivity of an electrolyte.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_m^0=
\lambda_+^0+\lambda_-^0
}
\]

---

where:

- \(\lambda_+^0\) = ionic conductivity of cation
- \(\lambda_-^0\) = ionic conductivity of anion

---

# Applications

---

## 1. Finding Limiting Conductivity

For weak electrolytes:

Example:

\[
CH_3COOH
\]

---

## 2. Degree of Dissociation

\[
\boxed{
\alpha=
\frac{\Lambda_m}{\Lambda_m^0}
}
\]

---

## 3. Dissociation Constant

\[
\boxed{
K_a=
\frac{C\alpha^2}{1-\alpha}
}
\]

---

# PART C: ELECTROCHEMICAL CELLS

---

# 8. Electrochemical Cell

## Definition

A device that converts chemical energy into electrical energy is called an electrochemical cell.

---

Types:

1. Galvanic cell
2. Electrolytic cell

---

# 9. Galvanic Cell

## Definition

A cell in which spontaneous chemical reaction produces electrical energy.

Example:

\[
\boxed{
Zn|Zn^{2+}||Cu^{2+}|Cu
}
\]

(Daniell cell)

---

# 10. Components of Galvanic Cell

---

## Anode

Oxidation occurs.

Example:

\[
Zn\rightarrow Zn^{2+}+2e^-
\]

---

## Cathode

Reduction occurs.

Example:

\[
Cu^{2+}+2e^-
\rightarrow Cu
\]

---

# Cell Reaction

\[
Zn+Cu^{2+}
\rightarrow
Zn^{2+}+Cu
\]

---

# 11. Salt Bridge

## Functions

1. Completes electrical circuit.
2. Maintains electrical neutrality.
3. Prevents mixing of solutions.

---

# PART D: ELECTRODE POTENTIAL

---

# 12. Electrode Potential

## Definition

The potential difference developed between electrode and electrolyte solution is called electrode potential.

---

# 13. Standard Electrode Potential

Measured under standard conditions:

- Concentration = 1M
- Pressure = 1 atm
- Temperature = 298K

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{E^0}
\]

---

# 14. Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)

## Definition

The standard hydrogen electrode is used as reference electrode for measuring electrode potentials.

---

Reaction:

\[
2H^++2e^-
\rightleftharpoons H_2
\]

---

Standard potential:

\[
\boxed{
E^0=0V
}
\]

---

# PART E: CELL POTENTIAL

---

# 15. EMF of Cell

The potential difference between cathode and anode is called cell potential.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
E_{cell}=E_{cathode}-E_{anode}
}
\]

---

If:

\[
E_{cell}>0
\]

Reaction is spontaneous.

---

# PART F: GIBBS ENERGY AND ELECTROCHEMISTRY

---

# 16. Relation Between Gibbs Energy and EMF

\[
\boxed{
\Delta G=-nFE_{cell}
}
\]

where:

- n = number of electrons
- F = Faraday constant

---

# 17. Equilibrium Constant Relation

\[
\boxed{
\Delta G=-RT\ln K
}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\log K=
\frac{nE^0}{0.0591}
}
\]

(at 298K)

---

# PART G: NERNST EQUATION

---

# 18. Nernst Equation

## Definition

The equation used to calculate electrode potential under non-standard conditions is called Nernst equation.

---

General equation:

\[
\boxed{
E=E^0-\frac{0.0591}{n}
\log Q
}
\]

(at 298K)

---

For cell:

\[
\boxed{
E_{cell}
=

E^0_{cell}
-\frac{0.0591}{n}
\log Q
}
\]

---

# Example

For:

\[
Zn+Cu^{2+}
\rightarrow
Zn^{2+}+Cu
\]

Reaction quotient:

\[
Q=
\frac{[Zn^{2+}]}{[Cu^{2+}]}
\]

---

# PART H: ELECTROLYSIS AND FARADAY'S LAWS

---

# 19. Faraday's First Law

## Statement

The mass of substance deposited is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity passed.

---

\[
\boxed{
m\propto Q
}
\]

---

# 20. Faraday's Second Law

## Statement

When the same quantity of electricity passes through different electrolytes, the masses deposited are proportional to their equivalent masses.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
m=\frac{EIt}{F}
}
\]

---

# Introduction

Surface chemistry deals with the phenomena occurring at the surface or interface between two phases.

This unit covers:

# PART A: ADSORPTION

---

# 1. Adsorption

## Definition

The accumulation of molecules or ions on the surface of a substance is called adsorption.

---

The substance on which adsorption occurs:

\[
\boxed{\text{Adsorbent}}
\]

The substance being adsorbed:

\[
\boxed{\text{Adsorbate}}
\]

---

Example:

Activated charcoal adsorbing gases.

---

# 2. Absorption

## Definition

The process in which a substance is uniformly distributed throughout the bulk of another substance is called absorption.

---

Example:

Water absorbing ammonia gas.

---

# Difference Between Adsorption and Absorption

| Adsorption                         | Absorption               |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Surface phenomenon                 | Bulk phenomenon          |
| Concentration increases on surface | Concentration is uniform |
| Depends on surface area            | Depends on volume        |

---

# 3. Types of Adsorption

Adsorption is mainly of two types:

1. Physical adsorption
2. Chemical adsorption

---

# PART B: PHYSISORPTION

---

# 4. Physical Adsorption (Physisorption)

## Definition

Adsorption due to weak van der Waals forces is called physical adsorption.

---

# Characteristics

- Weak forces
- Reversible
- Low heat of adsorption
- Multilayer formation possible

---

Examples:

Adsorption of gases on charcoal.

---

# Factors Affecting Physisorption

---

## 1. Surface Area

Greater surface area:

\[
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{More adsorption}}
\]

---

## 2. Temperature

Increase in temperature:

\[
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{Decreases adsorption}}
\]

---

## 3. Pressure

Increase in pressure:

\[
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{Increases adsorption}}
\]

---

# PART C: CHEMISORPTION

---

# 5. Chemical Adsorption (Chemisorption)

## Definition

Adsorption involving formation of chemical bonds between adsorbate and adsorbent is called chemisorption.

---

# Characteristics

- Strong chemical bonds
- Specific
- Usually irreversible
- Monolayer formation

---

Example:

Hydrogen adsorption on nickel.

---

# Difference Between Physisorption and Chemisorption

| Physisorption          | Chemisorption           |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Weak forces            | Chemical bonds          |
| Reversible             | Mostly irreversible     |
| Low heat of adsorption | High heat of adsorption |
| Multilayer             | Monolayer               |
| Non-specific           | Specific                |

---

# PART D: FREUNDLICH ADSORPTION ISOTHERM

---

# 6. Freundlich Adsorption Isotherm

It explains the relationship between amount of gas adsorbed and pressure at constant temperature.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{x}{m}=KP^{1/n}
}
\]

where:

- x = mass of gas adsorbed
- m = mass of adsorbent
- P = pressure
- K,n = constants

---

# Logarithmic Form

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{x}{m}
=

\log K+\frac1n\log P
}
\]

---

# Limitations

- Valid only at moderate pressure.
- Fails at high pressure.

---

# Characteristics

When catalyst and reactants are in different phases.

---

Reaction:

\[

Vanaspati

---

Examples:

Amylase:

\[
Starch\rightarrow Sugar
\]

---

# PART F: COLLOIDS

Particle size:

\boxed{

---

# PART G: PROPERTIES OF COLLOIDS

---

# 13. Tyndall Effect

## Definition

The scattering of light by colloidal particles is called Tyndall effect.

---

Cause:

Particles scatter incident light.

---

Applications:

- Identification of colloids
- Projector beams

---

# 14. Brownian Movement

## Definition

Random movement of colloidal particles is called Brownian movement.

---

Cause:

Collision with molecules of dispersion medium.

---

# 15. Electrophoresis

## Definition

Movement of colloidal particles towards oppositely charged electrodes in an electric field is called electrophoresis.

---

Applications:

- Purification of colloids
- Identification of particle charge

---

# 16. Coagulation

## Definition

The process of precipitation of colloidal particles is called coagulation.

---

Methods:

- Addition of electrolyte
- Heating
- Mixing opposite colloids

---

# PART H: EMULSIONS
