# Introduction

Chemistry is the branch of science that deals with the **composition, structure, properties, and transformations of matter**. Every chemical reaction follows definite quantitative relationships. Understanding these relationships forms the basis of chemical calculations known as **stoichiometry**.

This chapter introduces the fundamental concepts of atoms, molecules, moles, chemical laws, atomic masses, molecular masses, empirical formulas, molecular formulas, and concentration of solutions.

---

# Definition of Chemistry

Chemistry is the science that studies the **composition, structure, properties, and reactions of matter**.

# 1. Law of Conservation of Mass

## Statement

Matter can neither be created nor destroyed during a chemical reaction.

```
Mass of Reactants

=

Mass of Products
```

---

## Example

```
2H₂ + O₂

↓

2H₂O
```

Mass before reaction = Mass after reaction.

---

## Applications

- Balancing chemical equations.
- Stoichiometric calculations.

---

# 2. Law of Definite Proportions

## Proposed By

```
Joseph Proust
```

---

## Statement

A chemical compound always contains the same elements combined in the same proportion by mass.

## Example

```

```

by mass.

# 3. Law of Multiple Proportions

## Proposed By

```
John Dalton
```

---

## Statement

When two elements form more than one compound, the masses of one element combining with a fixed mass of the other bear a simple whole-number ratio.

---

## Example

| Compound | Carbon | Oxygen |
| -------- | ------ | ------ |
| CO       | 12 g   | 16 g   |
| CO₂      | 12 g   | 32 g   |

Ratio of oxygen

```
16 : 32

=

1 : 2
```

---

# 4. Gay-Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes

## Statement

When gases react, they do so in simple whole-number ratios by volume under the same conditions of temperature and pressure.

---

## Example

```
2 Vol H₂

+

1 Vol O₂

↓

2 Vol H₂O (Steam)
```

Volume Ratio

```
2 : 1 : 2
```

# Dalton's Atomic Theory

## Postulates

- Matter consists of tiny particles called atoms.
- Atoms cannot be created or destroyed in chemical reactions.
- Atoms of the same element are identical.
- Atoms of different elements differ in mass and properties.
- Compounds are formed by atoms combining in fixed ratios.
- Chemical reactions involve rearrangement of atoms.

---

# Limitations of Dalton's Theory

- Atoms are divisible into subatomic particles.
- Isotopes exist.
- Isobars exist.
- Nuclear reactions create and destroy atoms.

# Chapter Summary

- **Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry** introduces the quantitative foundation of chemistry through the study of matter, atoms, molecules, and moles.
- The **laws of chemical combination**—including the laws of conservation of mass, definite proportions, multiple proportions, and Gay-Lussac's law—establish the fixed relationships governing chemical reactions.
- **Dalton's Atomic Theory** explains the particulate nature of matter and the formation of compounds, although later discoveries revealed limitations such as isotopes and subatomic particles.
- The **mole concept**, based on **Avogadro's constant (6.022 × 10²³)**, connects the microscopic world of atoms and molecules with measurable laboratory quantities.

- Mastery of these concepts is essential for solving numerical problems and forms the basis for all subsequent topics in chemistry.

# Introduction

The atom is the basic unit of matter. Earlier, Dalton considered the atom to be indivisible, but later experiments by **J.J. Thomson, Ernest Rutherford, James Chadwick, and Niels Bohr** revealed that atoms consist of smaller particles called **electrons, protons, and neutrons**.

The study of atomic structure explains the arrangement of electrons around the nucleus, atomic spectra, quantum numbers, and electronic configuration.

# Chapter Summary

- The atom is composed of **electrons, protons, and neutrons**, discovered through landmark experiments by Thomson, Goldstein, Rutherford, Chadwick, and others.
- **Bohr's atomic model** successfully explained the stability of hydrogen atoms and their line spectrum by introducing quantized energy levels.
- The **dual nature of matter**, proposed by **de Broglie**, and **Heisenberg's uncertainty principle** laid the foundation for the **quantum mechanical model** of the atom.
- In the quantum model, electrons occupy **orbitals**, which are probability regions characterized by **quantum numbers**.
- The arrangement of electrons follows the **Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion principle, and Hund's rule**, giving rise to the electronic configuration of atoms.
- Understanding atomic structure is fundamental to explaining **chemical bonding, periodic properties, spectroscopy, and the behavior of elements**.

# Introduction

As the number of known elements increased, it became necessary to arrange them systematically. The **Modern Periodic Table**, based on the **Modern Periodic Law**, organizes elements according to their **atomic numbers**, revealing recurring (periodic) trends in their physical and chemical properties.

The periodic table helps predict the properties of known and newly discovered elements.

## Reasons

- To study a large number of elements systematically.
- To compare properties of elements.
- To predict properties of unknown elements.
- To understand periodic trends.
- To simplify the study of chemistry.

# Introduction

Atoms combine with one another to attain **stability** by achieving the electronic configuration of noble gases. The attractive force that holds atoms together is called a **chemical bond**. Understanding chemical bonding helps explain the structure, geometry, polarity, and properties of molecules.

The major theories explaining chemical bonding include:

- Octet Rule
- Ionic Bonding
- Covalent Bonding
- VSEPR Theory
- Valence Bond Theory (VBT)
- Hybridization
- Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT)

---

# 1. Chemical Bond

## Definition

A **chemical bond** is the attractive force that holds two or more atoms together to form a stable molecule or compound.

---

# Types of Chemical Bonds

```
             Chemical Bond
                    │
     ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
     ▼              ▼              ▼
 Ionic         Covalent      Coordinate
                    │
                    ▼
              Metallic Bond
```

---

# Octet Rule

## Statement

Atoms combine by **gaining, losing, or sharing electrons** to achieve **eight electrons** in their outermost shell.

---

## Exceptions

- Hydrogen (Duplet rule)
- Helium
- BeCl₂ (Incomplete octet)
- BF₃ (Incomplete octet)
- PCl₅ (Expanded octet)
- SF₆ (Expanded octet)

---

# Limitations of Octet Rule

- Cannot explain odd-electron molecules (NO, NO₂).
- Cannot explain expanded octets.
- Cannot explain molecular shapes.
- Cannot explain transition metal compounds.

---

# 2. Ionic Bond

## Definition

An ionic bond is formed by the **complete transfer of electrons** from one atom to another, resulting in oppositely charged ions held together by electrostatic attraction.

---

# Formation of NaCl

```
Na → Na⁺ + e⁻

Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻

Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl
```

---

# Characteristics of Ionic Compounds

- High melting point.
- High boiling point.
- Hard and brittle.
- Conduct electricity in molten or aqueous state.
- Soluble in polar solvents.

---

# Factors Favoring Ionic Bond Formation

- Low ionization enthalpy.
- High electron gain enthalpy.
- High lattice enthalpy.

---

# Lattice Enthalpy

## Definition

Energy released when one mole of an ionic solid is formed from its gaseous ions.

---

# Difference Between Ionic and Covalent Compounds

| Ionic Compound               | Covalent Compound   |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Electron transfer            | Electron sharing    |
| High melting point           | Lower melting point |
| Conduct electricity (molten) | Poor conductor      |
| Polar                        | Usually non-polar   |

# Introduction

**Thermodynamics** is the branch of chemistry that deals with the study of **energy changes** accompanying physical and chemical processes. It helps predict whether a reaction is feasible, how much heat is evolved or absorbed, and how energy is converted from one form to another.

The study of thermodynamics is based on the **First Law**, **Second Law**, **Entropy**, and **Gibbs Free Energy**.

---

# 1. Basic Terminology

## System

The part of the universe under study.

---

## Surroundings

Everything outside the system.

---


- Exchanges matter and energy.

Example

```

```

---

## Closed System

- Exchanges energy only.
- No exchange of matter.

Example

```
Gas inside a sealed cylinder
```

---

## Isolated System

- No exchange of matter or energy.

Example

```
Thermos flask (approximately)
```

---

# Introduction

Many chemical reactions do not proceed to completion but attain a state where the **forward and backward reactions occur at equal rates**. This state is called **chemical equilibrium**. Chemical equilibrium is dynamic in nature and is governed by the **Law of Mass Action**, **Equilibrium Constants**, **Le Chatelier's Principle**, and concepts of **ionic equilibrium** such as pH, buffer solutions, and solubility product.

---

# 1. Chemical Equilibrium

## Definition

Chemical equilibrium is the **dynamic state** in which the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the backward reaction.

---

# Characteristics

- Dynamic in nature.
- Forward rate = Backward rate.
- Concentrations remain constant.
- Occurs in a closed system.
- Can be attained from either direction.

---

# Dynamic Equilibrium

```
A + B

⇌

C + D

Forward Rate

=

Backward Rate
```

---

# Difference Between Physical and Chemical Equilibrium

| Physical Equilibrium | Chemical Equilibrium    |
| -------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Physical change      | Chemical change         |
| Example: Ice ⇌ Water | N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃         |
| No new substance     | New substances involved |

---

# 2. Law of Mass Action

## Statement

At constant temperature, the rate of a chemical reaction is proportional to the product of the active masses (concentrations) of the reactants.

---

For

```
aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD
```

---

# Equilibrium Constant (Kc)

```

=

──────────────

```

---

# Characteristics of Kc

- Constant at fixed temperature.
- Independent of catalyst.
- Depends only on temperature.

---

# Meaning of Kc

| Value of Kc | Interpretation                      |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Kc >> 1     | Products favored                    |
| Kc << 1     | Reactants favored                   |
| Kc ≈ 1      | Both present in appreciable amounts |

---

# 5. Le Chatelier's Principle

## Statement

When a system at equilibrium is disturbed by changing concentration, pressure, or temperature, it shifts in the direction that opposes the disturbance.

---

# Effect of Concentration

```
Increase Reactant

↓

Equilibrium shifts Right

↓

More Product
```

---

```
Increase Product

↓

Equilibrium shifts Left

↓

More Reactant
```

---

# Effect of Pressure

Applicable only for gaseous reactions.

Increase Pressure

↓

Shift toward fewer moles.

Decrease Pressure

↓

Shift toward greater moles.

---

# Effect of Temperature

### Exothermic Reaction

```
Heat + Reactants ⇌ Products
```

Increase temperature

↓

Shifts backward.

---

### Endothermic Reaction

Increase temperature

↓

Shifts forward.

---

# Effect of Catalyst

- No change in equilibrium position.
- Equilibrium reached faster.

---

# Effect of Inert Gas

### Constant Volume

No effect.

---

### Constant Pressure

May shift toward the side with more gaseous moles.

---

# Summary Table

| Change                             | Direction of Shift |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Increase reactant                  | Forward            |
| Increase product                   | Backward           |
| Increase pressure                  | Fewer gas moles    |
| Increase temperature (Exothermic)  | Backward           |
| Increase temperature (Endothermic) | Forward            |
| Catalyst                           | No shift           |

# Chapter Summary

- **Redox reactions** involve the simultaneous processes of **oxidation** (loss of electrons) and **reduction** (gain of electrons), with electrons transferred from the reducing agent to the oxidizing agent.
- The concept of **oxidation number** provides a systematic method for identifying oxidation and reduction in chemical reactions.
- Various types of redox reactions include **combination, decomposition, displacement, and disproportionation** reactions.
- Redox equations can be balanced using the **oxidation number method** or the **ion-electron (half-reaction) method**, ensuring conservation of both mass and charge.
- Redox reactions are fundamental to many natural and industrial processes such as **corrosion, combustion, metallurgy, batteries, electrolysis, respiration, and photosynthesis**.
