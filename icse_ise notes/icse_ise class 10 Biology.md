# Introduction

The **cell** is the structural and functional unit of life. Every living organism grows, repairs damaged tissues, and reproduces through **cell division**. During cell division, the genetic material (DNA) is accurately distributed into daughter cells with the help of **chromosomes**. Understanding chromosome structure and the different stages of cell division is essential for studying heredity, growth, and reproduction.

---

# 1. Cell

## Definition

A **Cell** is the basic structural, functional, and biological unit of all living organisms.

### Types of Cells

- Plant Cell
- Animal Cell

---

# 2. Chromosomes

## Definition

**Chromosomes** are thread-like structures present in the nucleus that carry hereditary information in the form of genes.

---

## Characteristics

- Present inside the nucleus.
- Made of DNA and proteins.
- Become visible during cell division.
- Carry genes responsible for inheritance.
- Human body cells contain **46 chromosomes (23 pairs)**.

---

# 3. Structure of a Chromosome

A chromosome consists of the following parts:

### Chromatid

One of the two identical halves of a replicated chromosome.

---

### Sister Chromatids

Two identical chromatids joined together at the centromere.

---

### Centromere

The constricted region joining the two sister chromatids.

**Functions**

- Holds sister chromatids together.
- Attaches spindle fibres during cell division.

---

### Telomere

The protective ends of chromosomes.

**Functions**

- Prevent chromosome damage.
- Protect DNA during replication.

---

### Gene

A segment of DNA that controls a particular inherited characteristic.

Examples

- Eye colour
- Blood group
- Hair colour

---

# 4. DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)

## Definition

DNA is the hereditary material present in chromosomes that carries genetic information.

---

## Structure

- Double Helix
- Two polynucleotide strands
- Sugar-Phosphate Backbone
- Nitrogen Bases

### Nitrogen Bases

- Adenine (A)
- Thymine (T)
- Guanine (G)
- Cytosine (C)

---

## Base Pairing Rule

```
A ↔ T
```

```
G ↔ C
```

---

# 5. Histones and Nucleosomes

## Histones

Proteins around which DNA is wrapped.

---

## Nucleosome

A DNA molecule wrapped around histone proteins.

### Function

- Packages DNA into chromosomes.
- Protects genetic material.
- Helps regulate gene expression.

---

# 6. Cell Cycle

## Definition

The **Cell Cycle** is the sequence of events through which a cell grows, duplicates its DNA, and divides into two daughter cells.

---

## Phases of the Cell Cycle

```
Interphase
        ↓
Mitosis / Meiosis
        ↓
Cytokinesis
```

---

# 7. Interphase

Interphase is the longest phase of the cell cycle.

It consists of three stages.

---

## G₁ Phase (Growth Phase)

- Cell grows.
- Organelles increase in number.
- Proteins are synthesized.

---

## S Phase (Synthesis Phase)

- DNA replication occurs.
- Each chromosome forms two sister chromatids.

---

## G₂ Phase

- Further cell growth.
- Preparation for cell division.
- Spindle proteins are synthesized.

---

# 8. Cell Division

## Definition

Cell division is the process by which one parent cell divides to produce new daughter cells.

There are two types:

- Mitosis
- Meiosis

---

# 9. Mitosis

## Definition

Mitosis is the division of a somatic (body) cell producing **two genetically identical diploid daughter cells**.

---

## Characteristics

- Occurs in body cells.
- One nuclear division.
- Chromosome number remains unchanged.
- Produces two daughter cells.

---

## Stages of Mitosis

### (i) Prophase

- Chromosomes condense.
- Nuclear membrane disappears.
- Spindle fibres appear.
- Centrioles move to opposite poles (animal cells).

---

### (ii) Metaphase

- Chromosomes arrange at the equator.
- Spindle fibres attach to centromeres.

---

### (iii) Anaphase

- Centromeres split.
- Sister chromatids separate.
- Chromatids move to opposite poles.

---

### (iv) Telophase

- Chromosomes uncoil.
- Nuclear membrane reforms.
- Two nuclei are formed.

---

# 10. Cytokinesis

## Definition

Cytokinesis is the division of the cytoplasm after nuclear division.

---

## Animal Cell

- Cleavage furrow develops.
- Cell divides inward.

---

## Plant Cell

- Cell plate forms.
- New cell wall develops.

---

# 11. Importance of Mitosis

- Growth
- Repair of tissues
- Replacement of worn-out cells
- Asexual reproduction
- Maintains chromosome number

---

# 12. Meiosis

## Definition

Meiosis is a special type of cell division that produces **four genetically different haploid cells** from one diploid cell.

---

## Characteristics

- Occurs in reproductive organs.
- Two successive divisions.
- Chromosome number is reduced to half.
- Produces gametes.

---

## Meiosis I

### Prophase I

- Homologous chromosomes pair.
- Crossing over occurs.
- Genetic recombination takes place.

---

### Metaphase I

- Homologous chromosomes arrange at the equator.

---

### Anaphase I

- Homologous chromosomes separate.

---

### Telophase I

- Two haploid cells are formed.

---

## Meiosis II

Similar to mitosis.

Results in four haploid daughter cells.

---

# 13. Differences Between Mitosis and Meiosis

| Mitosis                     | Meiosis                           |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| One division                | Two divisions                     |
| Two daughter cells          | Four daughter cells               |
| Diploid cells produced      | Haploid cells produced            |
| No crossing over            | Crossing over occurs              |
| Growth and repair           | Formation of gametes              |
| Chromosome number unchanged | Chromosome number reduced by half |

---

# 14. Significance of Meiosis

- Formation of gametes
- Maintains chromosome number in a species
- Introduces genetic variation
- Essential for sexual reproduction
- Promotes evolution

---

# 15. Chromosome Number in Humans

| ------------ | ----------------: |
| Somatic Cell |     46 (23 pairs) |
| Sperm Cell   |                23 |
| Ovum         |                23 |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **DNA** with **Chromosomes**.
- Assuming mitosis produces haploid cells.
- Forgetting that DNA replication occurs during the **S Phase**.
- Mixing up the stages of mitosis.
- Confusing sister chromatids with homologous chromosomes.
- Believing crossing over occurs in mitosis (it occurs only in **Prophase I of meiosis**).
- Forgetting that cytokinesis differs in plant and animal cells.
- Writing the chromosome number of gametes as **46** instead of **23**.

---

# Quick Revision

- Cell → Basic unit of life.
- Chromosomes contain genes.
- DNA is the hereditary material.
- Histones help package DNA.
- Interphase = G₁ + S + G₂.
- Mitosis → 2 identical diploid cells.
- Meiosis → 4 genetically different haploid cells.
- Crossing over occurs in **Prophase I**.
- Cytokinesis forms separate daughter cells.
- Human somatic cells = **46 chromosomes**.

---

# Chapter Summary

- The **cell** is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
- **Chromosomes** are made of DNA and proteins and contain genes that determine inherited characteristics.
- DNA is organized into **nucleosomes** by wrapping around **histone proteins**, allowing it to fit inside the nucleus.
- The **Cell Cycle** consists of **Interphase (G₁, S, G₂)** followed by **cell division** and **cytokinesis**.
- **Mitosis** produces two genetically identical diploid daughter cells for growth, repair, and asexual reproduction.
- **Meiosis** consists of two successive divisions that produce four genetically different haploid gametes, ensuring genetic variation and maintaining chromosome number across generations.
- **Crossing over** during **Prophase I of meiosis** increases genetic diversity.
- In humans, **somatic cells contain 46 chromosomes**, while **gametes contain 23 chromosomes**, and fertilization restores the diploid number.

# Introduction

**Genetics** is the branch of biology that deals with the study of **heredity** and **variation**. Heredity is the transmission of characteristics from parents to their offspring through genes. The fundamental principles of genetics were established by **Gregor Johann Mendel**, who is known as the **Father of Genetics**. His experiments on pea plants led to the formulation of the basic laws of inheritance.

---

# 1. Genetics

## Definition

**Genetics** is the branch of biology that studies heredity, variation, genes, and inheritance.

---

# 2. Heredity

## Definition

**Heredity** is the transmission of genetic characteristics from parents to offspring through genes.

### Examples

- Eye colour
- Blood group
- Hair texture
- Height

---

# 3. Variation

## Definition

**Variation** refers to the differences in characteristics among individuals of the same species.

---

## Importance of Variation

- Helps organisms adapt to changing environments.
- Increases chances of survival.
- Promotes evolution.
- Produces biodiversity.

---

# 4. Gregor Johann Mendel

## Contributions

- Conducted experiments on **Garden Pea (Pisum sativum)**.
- Studied inheritance of traits.
- Proposed the **Laws of Inheritance**.
- Introduced the concept of dominant and recessive traits.

---

# 5. Important Genetic Terms

## Gene

A **Gene** is the unit of heredity located on a chromosome.

---

## Alleles

Alternative forms of the same gene controlling the same characteristic.

Example

```
T → Tall

t → Dwarf
```

---

## Dominant Trait

A trait that expresses itself even in the presence of its alternative allele.

Example

```
Tall (T)
```

---

## Recessive Trait

A trait that expresses itself only when both alleles are recessive.

Example

```
Dwarf (t)
```

---

## Genotype

The genetic makeup of an organism.

Examples

```
TT

Tt

tt
```

---

## Phenotype

The observable physical appearance of an organism.

Examples

- Tall Plant
- Dwarf Plant
- Purple Flower

---

## Homozygous

Both alleles are identical.

Examples

```
TT

tt
```

---

## Heterozygous

The two alleles are different.

Example

```
Tt
```

---

# 6. Mendel's Laws of Inheritance

---

## Law of Dominance

### Statement

When two contrasting alleles are present together, only the dominant allele expresses itself, while the recessive allele remains hidden.

---

### Example

```
TT × tt

↓

All Tt

↓

Tall Plants
```

---

## Law of Segregation

### Statement

The two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation so that each gamete receives only one allele.

---

### Importance

- Explains purity of gametes.
- Prevents mixing of genes permanently.

---

## Law of Independent Assortment

### Statement

The inheritance of one pair of contrasting characters is independent of the inheritance of another pair.

---

### Importance

- Produces new combinations.
- Increases genetic variation.

---

# 7. Monohybrid Cross

## Definition

A **Monohybrid Cross** is a cross involving only one pair of contrasting characters.

---

## Example

Tall Plant

```
TT
```

×

Dwarf Plant

```
tt
```

---

### Parental Generation (P)

```
TT × tt
```

---

### Gametes

```
T

t
```

---

### F₁ Generation

| T   | T      |
| --- | ------ |
| t   | **Tt** | **Tt** |
| t   | **Tt** | **Tt** |

All offspring

```
Tall (Tt)
```

---

### Selfing of F₁

```
Tt × Tt
```

---

### F₂ Generation (Punnett Square)

|       | T   | t   |
| ----- | --- | --- |
| **T** | TT  | Tt  |
| **t** | Tt  | tt  |

---

### Phenotypic Ratio

```
3 Tall : 1 Dwarf
```

---

### Genotypic Ratio

```
1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt
```

---

# 8. Dihybrid Cross

## Definition

A **Dihybrid Cross** involves the inheritance of two pairs of contrasting characters.

---


# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Genotype** with **Phenotype**.
- Mixing up **Dominant** and **Recessive** traits.
- Writing the F₂ phenotypic ratio of a monohybrid cross as **1 : 2 : 1** instead of **3 : 1**.
- Forgetting that the **genotypic ratio** is **1 : 2 : 1**.
- Assuming the mother determines the sex of the child; it is determined by the father's X or Y chromosome.
- Confusing **Autosomes** with **Sex Chromosomes**.
- Assuming all genetic disorders are sex-linked.
- Mixing up **Homozygous** and **Heterozygous** genotypes.

- Genotypic Ratio → **1 : 2 : 1**.
- Dihybrid Cross → **9 : 3 : 3 : 1** phenotypic ratio.

# Chapter Summary

- **Genetics** is the study of heredity and variation, explaining how traits are transmitted from parents to offspring.
- **Gregor Johann Mendel**, through his experiments on pea plants, established the **Laws of Dominance, Segregation, and Independent Assortment**, forming the basis of modern genetics.
- **Genes**, located on chromosomes, exist in different forms called **alleles**, which may be **dominant** or **recessive**.
- A **Monohybrid Cross** results in a **3 : 1 phenotypic ratio** and a **1 : 2 : 1 genotypic ratio**, while a **Dihybrid Cross** produces the characteristic **9 : 3 : 3 : 1 phenotypic ratio**.
- In humans, females possess **XX** sex chromosomes and males possess **XY**. The **father determines the sex of the child** by contributing either an X or a Y chromosome.
- **Sex-linked inheritance** explains the transmission of disorders such as **Colour Blindness** and **Haemophilia**, which are usually inherited through the X chromosome.
- **Mutations** introduce genetic variation, contributing to evolution but may also lead to genetic disorders.
- Genetics has extensive applications in medicine, agriculture, biotechnology, forensic science, and genetic engineering.

# Introduction

Plants require **water** and **mineral salts** for growth, photosynthesis, respiration, and other metabolic activities. These substances are mainly absorbed by the **roots**, especially through the **root hairs**. Water enters the root cells by **osmosis**, while mineral ions are absorbed by both **diffusion** and **active transport**. The absorbed water and minerals are then transported to different parts of the plant through the **xylem**.

---

# 1. Plant Physiology

## Definition

**Plant Physiology** is the branch of biology that studies the functions and life processes of plants.

---

## Major Processes

- Absorption
- Transpiration
- Photosynthesis
- Respiration
- Transport of Water
- Growth
- Reproduction

---

# 2. Root System

## Definition

The **Root System** is the underground part of a plant responsible for anchorage and absorption of water and minerals.

---

## Functions

- Absorbs water.
- Absorbs mineral salts.
- Anchors the plant.
- Stores food (in some plants).
- Conducts water to the stem.

---

# 3. Root Hair

## Definition

A **Root Hair** is a long, thin, tubular extension of an epidermal cell of the root.

---

## Structure

- Thin cell wall
- Cell membrane
- Large vacuole
- Dense cytoplasm
- Nucleus

---

## Adaptations of Root Hairs

- Large surface area for absorption.
- Thin and permeable cell wall.
- Large vacuole containing concentrated cell sap.
- Close contact with soil particles.
- Numerous root hairs increase absorption efficiency.

---

# 4. Imbibition

## Definition

**Imbibition** is the absorption of water by solid colloidal substances without forming a solution.

---

## Examples

- Swelling of dry seeds.
- Absorption of water by dry wood.
- Germination of seeds.

---

## Importance

- Helps seed germination.
- Softens seed coat.
- Initiates metabolic activities.

---

# 5. Diffusion

## Definition

**Diffusion** is the movement of molecules from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.

---

## Characteristics

- Passive process.
- No energy required.
- Continues until equilibrium is reached.

---

## Examples

- Spread of perfume smell.
- Exchange of gases in leaves.
- Movement of mineral ions (in some cases).

---

# 6. Osmosis

## Definition

**Osmosis** is the movement of water molecules through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water concentration to a region of lower water concentration.

---

## Conditions Required

- Selectively permeable membrane.
- Difference in water concentration.
- Two solutions of different concentrations.

---

## Types of Solutions

### Hypotonic Solution

Contains more water and less solute.

Water enters the cell.

---

### Hypertonic Solution

Contains less water and more solute.

Water leaves the cell.

---

### Isotonic Solution

Equal concentration inside and outside the cell.

No net movement of water.

---

# 7. Turgidity

## Definition

A plant cell becomes **Turgid** when water enters by osmosis, causing the cell to swell.

---

## Characteristics

- Vacuole enlarges.
- Cell becomes firm.
- Turgor pressure develops.
- Plant remains erect.

---

## Importance

- Maintains rigidity.
- Supports leaves and stems.
- Helps in opening stomata.

---

# 8. Plasmolysis

## Definition

**Plasmolysis** is the shrinking of the cytoplasm away from the cell wall due to loss of water by osmosis.

---

## Causes

- Hypertonic solution.
- Loss of water from the cell.

---

## Effects

- Cell becomes flaccid.
- Plant wilts.
- Growth is reduced.

---

# 9. Active Transport

## Definition

**Active Transport** is the movement of substances from a region of lower concentration to a region of higher concentration using cellular energy (ATP).

---

## Characteristics

- Requires ATP.
- Moves against the concentration gradient.
- Carried out by carrier proteins.

---

## Importance

- Absorption of mineral ions.
- Essential nutrient uptake.
- Maintains ion balance.

---

- Root hairs have a large surface area and thin walls.

---

# Chapter Summary

- **Plant Physiology** deals with the study of the functions and life processes of plants.
- **Root hairs** are specialized epidermal cell extensions that efficiently absorb water and mineral salts due to their large surface area, thin walls, and concentrated cell sap.
- **Imbibition** is the absorption of water by dry colloidal substances and plays an important role in seed germination.
- **Diffusion** is the passive movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, while **Osmosis** specifically involves the movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane.
- A plant cell becomes **turgid** when water enters by osmosis, whereas **plasmolysis** occurs when water leaves the cell in a hypertonic solution.
- **Active Transport** uses ATP to move mineral ions against the concentration gradient into root cells.
- Water absorbed by the roots moves through the **xylem** to the stem and leaves, supplying the plant with essential water and dissolved minerals.
- Efficient absorption by roots is essential for plant growth, photosynthesis, respiration, and overall survival.

# Introduction

**Transpiration** is the process by which plants lose water in the form of water vapour from their aerial parts, mainly through the **stomata** present on leaves. Although it results in water loss, transpiration is essential for the **ascent of sap**, cooling of plants, transport of minerals, and maintenance of water balance. It is one of the most important physiological processes in plants.

---

# 1. Transpiration

## Definition

**Transpiration** is the loss of water as water vapour from the aerial parts of a plant, mainly through the stomata.

---

## Sites of Transpiration

- Stomata (Major route)
- Cuticle
- Lenticels

---

# 2. Types of Transpiration

## (i) Stomatal Transpiration

Occurs through the **stomata** of leaves.

### Characteristics

- About **80–90%** of total transpiration.
- Most common type.
- Controlled by guard cells.

---

## (ii) Cuticular Transpiration

Occurs through the waxy **cuticle** covering leaves.

### Characteristics

- Very little water loss.
- More common in young leaves.

---

## (iii) Lenticular Transpiration

Occurs through **lenticels** present on woody stems.

### Characteristics

- Small amount of water loss.
- Continuous process.
- Not controlled by guard cells.

---

# 3. Stomata

## Definition

**Stomata** are tiny pores present mainly on the lower surface of leaves that regulate gaseous exchange and transpiration.

---

## Structure of a Stoma

A stoma consists of:

- Stomatal pore
- Two guard cells
- Epidermal cells
- Chloroplasts (in guard cells)

---

# 4. Guard Cells

## Function

Guard cells regulate the opening and closing of stomata by changing their turgidity.

---

## Opening of Stomata

- Guard cells absorb water.
- Cells become turgid.
- Stomatal pore opens.

---

## Closing of Stomata

- Guard cells lose water.
- Cells become flaccid.
- Stomatal pore closes.

---

# 5. Role of Potassium Ions (K⁺)

The movement of **potassium ions (K⁺)** controls the turgidity of guard cells.

### Mechanism

- K⁺ ions enter guard cells.
- Water enters by osmosis.
- Guard cells become turgid.
- Stomata open.

When K⁺ ions leave:

- Water moves out.
- Guard cells become flaccid.
- Stomata close.

# Introduction

**Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants prepare their own food using **carbon dioxide**, **water**, and **sunlight** in the presence of **chlorophyll**. # 1. Photosynthesis

## Definition

**Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants synthesize glucose from carbon dioxide and water using sunlight in the presence of chlorophyll.

- The **circulatory system** consists of the **heart, blood, blood vessels, and lymphatic system**, ensuring transport of oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and wastes.
- **Blood** is made up of **plasma, red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets**, each performing specialized functions.
- The **heart** is a four-chambered muscular organ protected by the **pericardium**, with valves ensuring one-way blood flow.
- Humans have a **closed, double circulatory system**, comprising **pulmonary circulation** (heart–lungs–heart) and **systemic circulation** (heart–body–heart).
- **Arteries** generally carry oxygenated blood away from the heart, while **veins** generally return deoxygenated blood to the heart, with the pulmonary vessels being important exceptions.
- The **hepatic portal system** transports nutrient-rich blood from the digestive organs to the liver, while the **lymphatic system** maintains fluid balance and provides immune protection.
- Proper functioning of the circulatory system is essential for maintaining homeostasis, supporting metabolism, and ensuring the survival of all body tissues.

# Introduction

The **Excretory System** is responsible for removing **metabolic waste products** from the body and maintaining the internal environment (homeostasis). The major excretory organs are the **kidneys**, which filter blood to remove wastes such as **urea**, excess water, and salts. The kidneys also regulate the body's water balance, electrolyte balance, and blood pressure.

---

# 1. Excretion

## Definition

**Excretion** is the process of removing metabolic waste products and excess substances from the body.

---

## Excretory Products

- Urea
- Carbon dioxide
- Excess water
- Mineral salts
- Uric acid
- Creatinine

---

# 2. Human Excretory System

## Components

- Kidneys
- Ureters
- Urinary Bladder
- Urethra

---

### Kidneys

A pair of bean-shaped organs responsible for filtering blood and producing urine.

---

### Ureters

Muscular tubes that carry urine from the kidneys to the urinary bladder.

---

### Urinary Bladder

A muscular sac that temporarily stores urine.

---

### Urethra

A tube through which urine is expelled from the body.

---

# 3. Structure of the Kidney

Each kidney consists of:

- Renal Capsule
- Cortex
- Medulla
- Renal Pelvis
- Ureter

---

## Cortex

The outer region containing most of the nephrons.

---

## Medulla

The inner region containing renal pyramids and collecting ducts.

---

## Renal Pelvis

A funnel-shaped cavity that collects urine before it enters the ureter.

---

# 4. Nephron

## Definition

The **Nephron** is the structural and functional unit of the kidney.

Each human kidney contains about **one million nephrons**.

---

## Parts of a Nephron

- Bowman's Capsule
- Glomerulus
- Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)
- Loop of Henle
- Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)
- Collecting Duct

---

# 5. Bowman's Capsule

A cup-shaped structure that surrounds the glomerulus and collects the filtrate.

---

# 6. Glomerulus

A network of capillaries where **ultrafiltration** takes place.

---

# 7. Ultrafiltration

## Definition

**Ultrafiltration** is the filtration of blood under high pressure in the glomerulus, allowing water and small molecules to pass into Bowman's capsule.

---

## Substances Filtered

- Water
- Urea
- Glucose
- Amino acids
- Salts

---

## Substances Not Filtered

- Blood cells
- Plasma proteins

---

# 8. Selective Reabsorption

## Definition

**Selective Reabsorption** is the process by which useful substances are reabsorbed from the filtrate into the blood.

---

## Reabsorbed Substances

- Glucose
- Amino acids
- Most water
- Essential salts

---

## Site

Mainly in the **Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)**.

---

# 9. Tubular Secretion

## Definition

**Tubular Secretion** is the movement of additional waste substances from the blood into the nephron.

---

## Examples

- Hydrogen ions (H⁺)
- Potassium ions (K⁺)
- Ammonia
- Certain drugs

---

# 10. Urine Formation

Urine is formed in **three steps**:

```
Blood
   │
Ultrafiltration
   │
Selective Reabsorption
   │
Tubular Secretion
   │
Urine
```

---

# 11. Composition of Urine

Normal urine contains:

- Water (about 95%)
- Urea
- Uric acid
- Creatinine
- Mineral salts
- Pigments

---

## Normally Absent

- Glucose
- Blood cells
- Plasma proteins

---

# 12. Osmoregulation

## Definition

**Osmoregulation** is the maintenance of the body's water and salt balance.

---

## Importance

- Maintains water balance.
- Controls blood volume.
- Regulates electrolyte concentration.
- Prevents dehydration.

---

# 13. Role of ADH (Anti-Diuretic Hormone)

## Definition

**ADH** is a hormone secreted by the posterior pituitary gland that regulates water reabsorption in the kidneys.

---

## Functions

- Increases water reabsorption.
- Produces concentrated urine.
- Reduces water loss.
- Helps maintain blood pressure.

---

## During Dehydration

- More ADH is secreted.
- More water is reabsorbed.
- Less urine is produced.

---

## During Excess Water Intake

- Less ADH is secreted.
- Less water is reabsorbed.
- More dilute urine is produced.

---

# 14. Artificial Kidney (Dialysis)

## Definition

**Dialysis** is a medical procedure used to remove waste products and excess water from the blood when the kidneys fail to function properly.

---

## Uses

- Kidney failure
- Chronic kidney disease
- Temporary support until kidney transplant

---

# 15. Kidney Disorders

## Kidney Stones

Hard deposits of minerals formed inside the kidneys.

---

## Uraemia

Accumulation of urea in the blood due to kidney failure.

---

## Renal Failure

Loss of normal kidney function requiring dialysis or transplantation.

# Chapter Summary

- The **Excretory System** removes metabolic wastes and maintains the body's internal balance through the coordinated functions of the **kidneys, ureters, urinary bladder, and urethra**.
- The **kidney** is made up of millions of **nephrons**, each consisting of the **glomerulus, Bowman's capsule, proximal convoluted tubule, Loop of Henle, distal convoluted tubule, and collecting duct**.

- Disorders such as **kidney stones, uraemia, and renal failure** affect normal kidney function and may require **dialysis** or kidney transplantation.
- A healthy excretory system is essential for maintaining homeostasis, removing toxic wastes, and ensuring the proper functioning of all body systems.

# Introduction

The **Nervous System** is the control and coordination system of the human body. It receives information from the surroundings, processes it, and sends appropriate responses to different organs. It works together with the **endocrine system** to maintain body coordination. The basic structural and functional unit of the nervous system is the **neuron (nerve cell)**.

---

# 1. Nervous System

## Definition

The **Nervous System** is an organ system that controls, coordinates, and regulates all body activities by transmitting nerve impulses.

---

## Functions

- Controls voluntary actions.
- Controls involuntary actions.
- Receives sensory information.
- Coordinates body movements.
- Maintains balance and posture.
- Helps in thinking, learning, and memory.

---

# 2. Neuron (Nerve Cell)

## Definition

A **Neuron** is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system that transmits nerve impulses.

---

## Structure of a Neuron

A neuron consists of:

- Dendrites
- Cell Body (Cyton)
- Nucleus
- Axon
- Myelin Sheath
- Nodes of Ranvier
- Axon Terminals (Synaptic Knobs)

---

### Dendrites

Receive nerve impulses from receptors or other neurons.

---

### Cell Body (Cyton)

Contains the nucleus and controls the activities of the neuron.

---

### Axon

A long projection that carries nerve impulses away from the cell body.

---

### Myelin Sheath

A fatty insulating layer surrounding the axon.

#### Function

- Protects the axon.
- Increases the speed of impulse transmission.

---

### Nodes of Ranvier

Small gaps in the myelin sheath that allow rapid conduction of nerve impulses.

---

### Synapse

## Definition

A **Synapse** is the junction between two neurons or between a neuron and an effector organ.

---

## Function

- Transfers nerve impulses using neurotransmitters.

---

# 3. Types of Neurons

## Sensory Neuron

Carries impulses from receptors to the central nervous system.

---

## Motor Neuron

Carries impulses from the central nervous system to muscles or glands.

---

## Relay (Interneuron)

Connects sensory and motor neurons within the central nervous system.

---

# 4. Central Nervous System (CNS)

# Chapter Summary

- The **Nervous System** coordinates and controls all body activities by transmitting **nerve impulses** through specialized cells called **neurons**.
- A **neuron** consists of **dendrites, cell body, axon, myelin sheath, nodes of Ranvier, and synaptic terminals**, each playing a specific role in impulse transmission.
- The **Central Nervous System (CNS)** includes the **brain and spinal cord**, while the **Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)** consists of cranial and spinal nerves connecting the CNS to the rest of the body.
- The **cerebrum** controls intelligence, memory, thinking, and voluntary actions; the **cerebellum** coordinates balance and posture; and the **medulla oblongata** regulates involuntary activities such as breathing and heartbeat.
- A **reflex action** is a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus, carried through a **reflex arc** involving sensory, relay, and motor neurons.
- The **Autonomic Nervous System** is divided into the **sympathetic** ("fight or flight") and **parasympathetic** ("rest and digest") divisions, which regulate involuntary body functions.
- The nervous system works closely with the sense organs to receive stimuli, process information, and produce appropriate responses, making it essential for survival, coordination, and adaptation.

# Introduction

**Human Evolution** is the gradual process through which modern humans developed from ancient ape-like ancestors over millions of years. Evolution occurs through **variation, natural selection, adaptation, and inheritance**. Fossil evidence, comparative anatomy, and molecular biology provide strong evidence for the evolution of humans. The scientific study of human evolution helps us understand the origin and development of **Homo sapiens**.

---

# 1. Evolution

## Definition

**Evolution** is the gradual change in the characteristics of living organisms over successive generations, resulting in the formation of new species.

---

## Importance

- Explains the origin of species.
- Helps understand biodiversity.
- Shows the relationship among organisms.
- Explains adaptation to the environment.

---

# 2. Charles Darwin

## Introduction

**Charles Darwin** proposed the **Theory of Natural Selection** in his book _On the Origin of Species_ (1859).

---

## Theory of Natural Selection

### Main Points

- Organisms produce more offspring than can survive.
- Individuals show variations.
- There is competition for survival.
- Individuals with favourable variations survive.
- Useful variations are inherited.
- Over time, new species evolve.

---

# 3. Variation

## Definition

**Variation** refers to differences in characteristics among individuals of the same species.

---

## Importance

- Helps organisms adapt.
- Increases chances of survival.
- Provides the basis for evolution.

---

# 4. Adaptation

## Definition

**Adaptation** is the development of features that enable an organism to survive and reproduce in its environment.

---

## Examples

- Long neck of the giraffe.
- Camouflage in insects.
- Webbed feet in ducks.

---

# 5. Fossils

## Definition

**Fossils** are the preserved remains, impressions, or traces of ancient organisms found in rocks.

---

## Importance

- Provide evidence of evolution.
- Help determine the age of organisms.
- Show transitional forms.

---

# 6. Human Evolution

The major stages of human evolution are:

- Dryopithecus
- Ramapithecus
- Australopithecus
- Homo habilis
- Homo erectus
- Neanderthal Man
- Cro-Magnon Man
- Homo sapiens

---

## Dryopithecus

- Ape-like ancestor.
- Lived in forests.
- Walked on four limbs.

---

## Ramapithecus

- More human-like than Dryopithecus.
- Omnivorous.
- Better developed jaws.

---

## Australopithecus

- Walked upright.
- Used simple tools.
- Larger brain than apes.

---

## Homo habilis

- Known as the "Handy Man."
- Used stone tools.
- Brain size about 650–800 cc.

---

## Homo erectus

- Walked completely upright.
- Used fire.
- Made better tools.
- Brain size about 900–1200 cc.

---

## Neanderthal Man

- Lived in caves.
- Used advanced tools.
- Buried the dead.
- Brain size about 1400 cc.

---

## Cro-Magnon Man

- Skilled hunter.
- Made paintings.
- Developed language.
- Closely resembled modern humans.

---

## Homo sapiens

- Modern humans.
- Highly developed brain.
- Advanced language and culture.
- Scientific and technological development.

---

# 7. Evidences of Evolution

## Fossil Evidence

Shows gradual changes in organisms over time.

---

## Comparative Anatomy

Similar body structures indicate common ancestry.

### Example

Forelimbs of humans, whales, bats, and horses.

---

## Embryological Evidence

Embryos of vertebrates show similarities during early development.

---

## Molecular Evidence

Similarity in DNA and protein sequences supports common ancestry.
