# Introduction

Physics is the branch of science that studies **matter, energy, motion, force, and the laws governing the universe**. It forms the foundation for engineering, astronomy, medicine, and modern technology.

# 6. Significant Figures

## Definition

The meaningful digits in a measured quantity that include all certain digits and the first uncertain digit.

---

# Rules

1. All non-zero digits are significant.
2. Zeros between non-zero digits are significant.
3. Leading zeros are not significant.
4. Trailing zeros after a decimal are significant.
5. Exact numbers have infinite significant figures.

---

# Examples

| Number      | Significant Figures  |
| ----------- | -------------------- |
| 25.6        | 3                    |
| 0.0025      | 2                    |
| 1.230       | 4                    |
| 2000        | 1 (unless specified) |
| 2.000 × 10³ | 4                    |

---

# Rounding Off Rules

- Digit < 5 → Leave unchanged.
- Digit > 5 → Increase preceding digit by 1.
- Digit = 5 followed only by zeros → Round to even digit.

**500 cm**

### Question

## Example 5

### Question

A Vernier Callipers has 1 MSD = 1 mm and 10 Vernier divisions equal 9 mm. Find its least count.

### Solution

```

= 1 − 0.9

= 0.1 mm
```

# Introduction

**Kinematics** is the branch of mechanics that describes the **motion of objects without considering the forces causing the motion**. It deals with quantities such as **distance, displacement, speed, velocity, acceleration**, and the mathematical equations that describe motion.

Motion can occur in **one dimension (straight line)** or **two dimensions (plane)**.

---

# 1. Motion

## Definition

Motion is the change in the position of an object with time relative to a chosen **reference frame**.

---

# Types of Motion

- Translational Motion
- Rotational Motion
- Circular Motion
- Oscillatory Motion
- Projectile Motion

---

# Reference Frame

A reference frame is a coordinate system with respect to which the position and motion of an object are measured.

---

# Difference Between Rest and Motion

| Rest                        | Motion                      |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Position does not change    | Position changes with time  |
| Relative to reference frame | Relative to reference frame |

---

# 2. Distance and Displacement

## Distance

The total length of the actual path travelled by an object.

### Characteristics

- Scalar quantity.
- Always positive.
- Depends on path.

---

## Displacement

The shortest straight-line distance between the initial and final positions.

### Characteristics

- Vector quantity.
- Can be positive, negative or zero.
- Independent of path.

---

# Difference Between Distance and Displacement

| Distance           | Displacement                      |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| Scalar             | Vector                            |
| Actual path length | Shortest path                     |
| Never negative     | May be positive, negative or zero |
| Depends on path    | Independent of path               |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A car travels 120 m in 10 s. Find its speed.

### Solution

```
Speed = Distance / Time

= 120 / 10

= 12 m s⁻¹
```

### Answer

**12 m s⁻¹**

## Example 2

### Question

A body starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 4 m s⁻² for 5 s. Find its final velocity.

### Solution

```
v = u + at

= 0 + (4 × 5)

= 20 m s⁻¹
```

### Answer

**20 m s⁻¹**

---

## Example 3

### Question

A projectile is launched with a speed of 20 m s⁻¹ at 45°. Find its horizontal range (g = 9.8 m s⁻²).

### Solution

```
R = u² sin2θ / g

= (20² × sin90°) / 9.8

= 400 / 9.8

≈ 40.8 m
```

### Answer

**40.8 m**

---

## Example 4

### Question

Two perpendicular vectors have magnitudes 6 N and 8 N. Find the resultant.

### Solution

```
R = √(6² + 8²)

= √100

= 10 N
```

### Answer

**10 N**

---

## Example 5

### Question

A particle moves in a circle of radius 2 m with a speed of 10 m s⁻¹. Find its centripetal acceleration.

### Solution

```
ac = v²/r

= 10²/2

= 50 m s⁻²
```

### Answer

**50 m s⁻²**

- **Kinematics** describes the motion of objects without considering the forces responsible for that motion.
- Fundamental quantities such as **distance, displacement, speed, velocity, and acceleration** provide the basis for analyzing motion in one and two dimensions.
- The **equations of uniformly accelerated motion** are applicable only for constant acceleration and are widely used in solving straight-line motion problems.
- **Vector analysis** is essential for understanding motion in a plane, including **relative velocity** and **projectile motion**, where horizontal and vertical components are treated independently.
- In **uniform circular motion**, the speed remains constant but the direction of velocity changes continuously, resulting in **centripetal acceleration** directed towards the center of the circle.
- A strong understanding of kinematics forms the foundation for advanced topics such as **Newton's laws of motion, rotational dynamics, gravitation, and mechanics**.

# Introduction

# Chapter Summary

- **Elasticity** is the ability of a material to regain its original dimensions after the removal of a deforming force, whereas **plasticity** results in permanent deformation.
- **Stress** measures the internal restoring force per unit area, while **strain** represents the relative deformation produced in the material.
- **Hooke's Law** states that stress is directly proportional to strain within the elastic limit, providing the basis for defining elastic constants.
- The three elastic moduli—**Young's modulus, Bulk modulus, and Shear modulus**—describe resistance to changes in length, volume, and shape, respectively.
- **Poisson's ratio** relates lateral and longitudinal strains, and **elastic potential energy** represents the energy stored during elastic deformation.
- The principles of elasticity are extensively applied in **civil engineering, mechanical design, aerospace, robotics, biomedical engineering, and structural analysis**, ensuring the safe and efficient design of engineering systems.

# Introduction

**Thermodynamics** is the branch of physics that deals with the relationship between **heat, work, temperature, and energy**. It explains how heat energy is converted into mechanical work and vice versa.

Thermodynamics is widely used in **heat engines, refrigerators, air conditioners, power plants, automobiles, and industrial processes**.

- Exchanges neither matter nor energy.

# Introduction

**Oscillation** is the repeated back-and-forth motion of a body about its mean position. Oscillatory motion is common in nature and technology, such as **pendulums, springs, vibrations of molecules, musical instruments, and clocks**.

## Definition

A motion that repeats itself after equal intervals of time is called periodic motion.

# Examples

# 2. Oscillatory Motion

## Definition

Motion in which a body moves repeatedly to and fro about an equilibrium position.

# Examples

- Spring-mass system.
- Simple pendulum.
- Vibrating string.

# 2. Orbital Motion of Satellites

A satellite remains in orbit because the gravitational force provides the necessary centripetal force.

---

# Gravitational Force

```
F = GMm/r²
```

---

# Centripetal Force

```
F = mv²/r
```

---

Equating:

```
GMm/r² = mv²/r
```

Therefore,

```
v = √GM/r
```

---

# Orbital Velocity

## Definition

The velocity required for a satellite to remain in a stable circular orbit.

---

# Formula

```
Vo = √GM/r
```

Where:

- G = Gravitational constant
- M = Mass of Earth
- r = Orbital radius

---

# Near Earth's Surface

```
Vo = √gR
```

Value:

```
≈ 7.9 km/s
```

---

# 3. Time Period of Satellite

## Definition

The time taken by a satellite to complete one revolution around Earth.

---

# Formula

```
T = 2π√(r³/GM)
```

---

# Relation

```
T² ∝ r³
```

---

# Kepler's Third Law

The square of orbital period is directly proportional to the cube of orbital radius.

---

# 4. Escape Velocity

## Definition

The minimum velocity required by an object to escape from a planet's gravitational field.

---

# Formula

```
Ve = √2GM/R
```

or

```
Ve = √2gR
```

---

# Earth Escape Velocity

```
11.2 km/s
```

---

# Relation with Orbital Velocity

```
Ve = √2Vo
```

---

# Difference Between Orbital and Escape Velocity

| Orbital Velocity         | Escape Velocity            |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Keeps satellite in orbit | Allows object to escape    |
| 7.9 km/s                 | 11.2 km/s                  |
| Circular motion          | Leaves gravitational field |
