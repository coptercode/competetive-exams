Chapter 1: Number Systems

Comprehensive Study Notes & Summary

1. Introduction to the Number System

The number system is a way of representing numbers on the number line. Understanding how different sets of numbers nest within each other is fundamental to algebra.

  • Natural Numbers (\(\mathbb{N}\)): The counting numbers starting from 1.
    \(\mathbb{N} = \{1, 2, 3, 4, ...\}\)
  • Whole Numbers (\(\mathbb{W}\)): Natural numbers combined with zero.
    \(\mathbb{W} = \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, ...\}\)
  • Integers (\(\mathbb{Z}\)): Whole numbers and their negative counterparts.
    \(\mathbb{Z} = \{..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...\}\)
  • Rational Numbers (\(\mathbb{Q}\)): Any number that can be expressed in the form \(p/q\), where \(p\) and \(q\) are integers and \(q \neq 0\).
    • Examples: \(1/2\), \(-3/4\), \(5\) (since \(5 = 5/1\)), \(0\).
    • There are infinitely many rational numbers between any two given rational numbers.
Tip: Finding rational numbers between a and b:
If you need to find \(n\) rational numbers between \(a\) and \(b\), multiply the numerator and denominator by \((n+1)\).

2. Irrational Numbers and Real Numbers

Not all numbers can be written as fractions.

  • Irrational Numbers: Numbers that cannot be written in the form \(p/q\) (where \(p, q \in \mathbb{Z}\) and \(q \neq 0\)).
    • Examples: \(\sqrt{2}\), \(\sqrt{3}\), \(\pi\), \(0.101101110...\)
    • The decimal expansion of an irrational number is non-terminating and non-recurring.
  • Real Numbers (\(\mathbb{R}\)): The collection of all Rational and Irrational numbers combined. Every real number is represented by a unique point on the number line.

3. Decimal Representation of Rational Numbers

When you divide \(p\) by \(q\), the decimal expansion can only result in two scenarios:

  1. Terminating Decimal: The remainder becomes zero after some steps. Example: \(1/2 = 0.5\)
  2. Non-Terminating Recurring (Repeating) Decimal: The remainder never becomes zero, but a sequence of digits repeats endlessly. Example: \(1/3 = 0.\overline{3}\)
Conclusion:
  • If a decimal is terminating or non-terminating recurring → Rational Number.
  • If a decimal is non-terminating non-recurring → Irrational Number.

4. Operations on Real Numbers

When combining rational and irrational numbers through arithmetic operations, follow these rules:

  • Rational ± Irrational = Irrational
  • Rational × or ÷ Irrational (non-zero) = Irrational
  • Irrational ± or × or ÷ Irrational = Can be Rational OR Irrational

Useful Identities for Square Roots (where \(a, b > 0\)):

  • \(\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}\)
  • \(\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}\)
  • \((\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}) = a - b\)

5. Rationalization of Denominators

When the denominator of an expression contains a term with a square root, the process of converting it to an equivalent expression whose denominator is a rational number is called rationalizing the denominator.

  • Single term denominator: To rationalize \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}\), multiply numerator and denominator by \(\sqrt{a}\).
  • Binomial denominator: To rationalize \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{a} \pm \sqrt{b}}\), multiply numerator and denominator by its conjugate (\(\sqrt{a} \mp \sqrt{b}\)).

6. Laws of Exponents for Real Numbers

If \(a > 0\) is a real number and \(m\) and \(n\) are rational numbers, then:

  • \(a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}\)
  • \(\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}\)
  • \((a^m)^n = a^{mn}\)
  • \(a^{-m} = \frac{1}{a^m}\)
  • \(a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a^m}\)

Summary Checklist for Students

  • I can find rational numbers between two fractions.
  • I can convert repeating decimals into \(p/q\) fractions.
  • I can classify numbers as rational or irrational.
  • I can multiply, divide, add, and subtract expressions with roots.
  • I can easily rationalize binomial denominators using conjugates.
