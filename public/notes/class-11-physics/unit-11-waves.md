# Introduction

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another without permanent transfer of matter.

Waves are important in:

- Sound transmission
- Communication systems
- Musical instruments
- Light and electromagnetic phenomena
- Earthquake studies

This unit covers:

- Wave motion
- Transverse and longitudinal waves
- Wave equation
- Velocity of waves
- Superposition principle
- Standing waves
- Organ pipes
- Beats
- Doppler effect

---

# PART A: WAVE MOTION

---

# 1. Wave

## Definition

A wave is a disturbance that propagates through a medium or space and transfers energy.

---

Examples:

- Sound waves
- Water waves
- Light waves

---

# 2. Characteristics of Waves

---

## Wavelength (λ)

The distance between two successive points in the same phase is called wavelength.

Unit:

\[
\boxed{m}
\]

---

## Frequency (f)

Number of oscillations completed per second.

Unit:

\[
\boxed{Hz}
\]

---

## Time Period (T)

Time taken for one complete oscillation.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
T=\frac1f
}
\]

---

## Amplitude (A)

Maximum displacement of particles from mean position.

---

## Wave Velocity (v)

Distance travelled by wave per unit time.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
v=f\lambda
}
\]

---

# PART B: TYPES OF WAVES

---

# 3. Transverse Waves

## Definition

A wave in which particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation is called a transverse wave.

---

Examples:

- Waves on string
- Electromagnetic waves

---

Features:

- Has crests and troughs
- Can be polarized

---

# 4. Longitudinal Waves

## Definition

A wave in which particles vibrate parallel to the direction of wave propagation is called a longitudinal wave.

---

Examples:

- Sound waves
- Compression waves

---

Features:

- Contains compressions and rarefactions
- Cannot be polarized

---

# Difference Between Transverse and Longitudinal Waves

| Transverse Wave                                | Longitudinal Wave                         |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Particles vibrate perpendicular to propagation | Particles vibrate parallel to propagation |
| Has crests and troughs                         | Has compressions and rarefactions         |
| Can be polarized                               | Cannot be polarized                       |
| Example: light                                 | Example: sound                            |

---

# PART C: WAVE EQUATION

---

# 5. Progressive Wave

A wave travelling continuously through a medium is called a progressive wave.

---

General equation:

\[
\boxed{
y=A\sin(kx-\omega t)
}
\]

---

where:

- A = amplitude
- k = wave number
- ω = angular frequency

---

# 6. Wave Number

Number of wavelengths in distance \(2\pi\).

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}
}
\]

---

# 7. Angular Frequency

\[
\boxed{
\omega=2\pi f
}
\]

---

# 8. Wave Velocity

Relationship:

\[
\boxed{
v=f\lambda
}
\]

or:

\[
\boxed{
v=\frac{\omega}{k}
}
\]

---

# PART D: VELOCITY OF WAVES

---

# 9. Velocity of Transverse Waves on String

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
v=\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}
}
\]

---

where:

- T = tension in string
- μ = mass per unit length

---

# 10. Velocity of Sound in Gas

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
v=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma P}{\rho}}
}
\]

---

where:

- γ = ratio of specific heats
- P = pressure
- ρ = density

---

# Factors Affecting Sound Velocity

Velocity increases with:

- Increase in temperature
- Increase in elasticity

Velocity decreases with:

- Increase in density

---

# PART E: SUPERPOSITION PRINCIPLE

---

# 11. Principle of Superposition

## Statement

When two or more waves overlap, the resultant displacement is equal to the algebraic sum of individual displacements.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
y=y_1+y_2
}
\]

---

Applications:

- Interference
- Beats
- Standing waves

---

# PART F: INTERFERENCE OF WAVES

---

# 12. Interference

## Definition

The phenomenon of redistribution of intensity due to superposition of two waves is called interference.

---

Types:

1. Constructive interference
2. Destructive interference

---

# 13. Constructive Interference

When two waves meet in same phase.

---

Condition:

\[
\boxed{
Path\ difference=n\lambda
}
\]

---

Result:

Maximum amplitude.

---

# 14. Destructive Interference

When two waves meet in opposite phase.

---

Condition:

\[
\boxed{
Path\ difference=
(2n+1)\frac{\lambda}{2}
}
\]

---

Result:

Minimum amplitude.

---

# PART G: STANDING WAVES

---

# 15. Standing Waves

## Definition

The waves formed by superposition of two waves having same frequency and amplitude travelling in opposite directions are called standing waves.

---

Features:

- Fixed nodes
- Fixed antinodes
- No net energy transfer

---

# 16. Nodes

Points where displacement is always zero.

---

# 17. Antinodes

Points where displacement is maximum.

---

# 18. Distance Between Nodes

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\lambda}{2}
}
\]

---

# 19. Distance Between Node and Antinode

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\lambda}{4}
}
\]

---

# PART H: WAVES IN STRINGS

---

# 20. Frequency of String

For a stretched string:

\[
\boxed{
f=
\frac1{2L}
\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}
}
\]

---

where:

- L = length of string
- T = tension
- μ = mass per unit length

---

# PART I: ORGAN PIPES

---

# 21. Open Organ Pipe

Both ends are open.

Fundamental frequency:

\[
\boxed{
f_1=
\frac{v}{2L}
}
\]

---

Harmonics:

\[
\boxed{
f_n=
\frac{nv}{2L}
}
\]

---

# 22. Closed Organ Pipe

One end closed.

Fundamental frequency:

\[
\boxed{
f_1=
\frac{v}{4L}
}
\]

---

Only odd harmonics occur:

\[
\boxed{
f_n=
\frac{(2n-1)v}{4L}
}
\]

---

# PART J: BEATS

---

# 23. Beats

## Definition

The periodic variation in intensity due to interference of two waves having slightly different frequencies is called beats.

---

Beat frequency:

\[
\boxed{
f_b=|f_1-f_2|
}
\]

---

Applications:

- Tuning musical instruments
- Determining unknown frequency

---

# PART K: DOPPLER EFFECT

---

# 24. Doppler Effect

## Definition

The apparent change in frequency of sound due to relative motion between source and observer is called Doppler effect.

---

Examples:

- Change in sound of moving vehicle
- Astronomical measurements

---

# 25. Doppler Effect Formula

General formula:

\[
\boxed{
f'=f
\frac{v\pm v_o}
{v\mp v_s}
}
\]

---

where:

- f' = apparent frequency
- f = actual frequency
- v = velocity of sound
- vo = velocity of observer
- vs = velocity of source

---

# Cases

---

## Observer Moving Towards Source

Frequency increases:

\[
\boxed{
f'=f\frac{v+v_o}{v}
}
\]

---

## Observer Moving Away

Frequency decreases:

\[
\boxed{
f'=f\frac{v-v_o}{v}
}
\]

---

## Source Moving Towards Observer

\[
\boxed{
f'=f\frac{v}{v-v_s}
}
\]

---

## Source Moving Away

\[
\boxed{
f'=f\frac{v}{v+v_s}
}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing wavelength and amplitude.
- Forgetting wave velocity formula.
- Mixing transverse and longitudinal waves.
- Assuming standing waves transfer energy.
- Forgetting closed pipes contain only odd harmonics.
- Using wrong signs in Doppler effect formula.
- Confusing beats frequency with actual frequency.
