# Introduction

Kinetic theory explains the behaviour of gases by considering gases as a collection of continuously moving molecules.

It connects:

- Molecular motion
- Pressure
- Temperature
- Energy

This unit covers:

- Molecular nature of matter
- Ideal gas laws
- Pressure exerted by gas molecules
- Degrees of freedom
- Law of equipartition of energy
- Specific heat capacities
- Maxwell distribution
- Mean free path

---

# PART A: MOLECULAR NATURE OF MATTER

---

# 1. Kinetic Theory of Gases

## Definition

The kinetic theory of gases explains the properties of gases by assuming that gases consist of a large number of tiny molecules in random motion.

---

# 2. Assumptions of Kinetic Theory

1. A gas consists of a large number of molecules.

2. Molecules are in continuous random motion.

3. The size of molecules is very small compared to the volume of gas.

4. Molecules collide with each other and container walls elastically.

5. No force acts between molecules except during collisions.

6. The average kinetic energy of molecules depends only on temperature.

---

# PART B: IDEAL GAS LAWS

---

# 3. Boyle's Law

## Statement

At constant temperature, pressure of a gas is inversely proportional to its volume.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
PV=constant
}
\]

---

\[
P\propto\frac1V
\]

---

# 4. Charles' Law

## Statement

At constant pressure, volume of a gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\frac VT=constant
}
\]

---

\[
V\propto T
\]

---

# 5. Avogadro's Law

## Statement

Equal volumes of all gases at same temperature and pressure contain equal number of molecules.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
V\propto n
}
\]

---

# 6. Ideal Gas Equation

Combining gas laws:

\[
\boxed{
PV=nRT
}
\]

---

where:

- P = pressure
- V = volume
- n = number of moles
- R = universal gas constant
- T = temperature

---

# 7. Molecular Form of Gas Equation

Number of molecules:

\[
N=nN_A
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
PV=NkT
}
\]

---

where:

- N = number of molecules
- k = Boltzmann constant

---

Value:

\[
\boxed{
k=1.38\times10^{-23}JK^{-1}
}
\]

---

# PART C: PRESSURE OF AN IDEAL GAS

---

# 8. Pressure Due to Molecular Motion

Gas molecules continuously collide with container walls.

These collisions produce pressure.

---

Pressure equation:

\[
\boxed{
P=\frac13\rho \overline{v^2}
}
\]

---

where:

- ρ = density of gas
- \(\overline{v^2}\) = mean square velocity

---

# 9. Root Mean Square Velocity

The square root of average of squares of velocities is called RMS velocity.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
v_{rms}
=

\sqrt{\overline{v^2}}
}
\]

---

Also:

\[
\boxed{
v_{rms}
=

\sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}
}
\]

---

where:

M = molar mass

---

# PART D: TEMPERATURE AND MOLECULAR ENERGY

---

# 10. Kinetic Energy of Gas Molecule

Average kinetic energy:

\[
\boxed{
KE=\frac32kT
}
\]

---

For one mole:

\[
\boxed{
KE=\frac32RT
}
\]

---

Important result:

Average kinetic energy depends only on temperature.

---

# PART E: LAW OF EQUIPARTITION OF ENERGY

---

# 11. Degrees of Freedom

## Definition

The number of independent ways in which a molecule can possess energy is called degrees of freedom.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{f}
\]

---

# Types

---

## Monoatomic Gas

Examples:

- Helium
- Neon

Degrees of freedom:

\[
\boxed{f=3}
\]

---

## Diatomic Gas

Examples:

- Oxygen
- Nitrogen

At ordinary temperature:

\[
\boxed{f=5}
\]

---

# 12. Law of Equipartition of Energy

## Statement

Energy is equally distributed among all degrees of freedom.

---

Energy per degree:

\[
\boxed{
\frac12kT
}
\]

---

Total energy:

\[
\boxed{
E=\frac f2kT
}
\]

---

# PART F: SPECIFIC HEAT CAPACITY OF GASES

---

# 13. Specific Heat at Constant Volume

Heat supplied when volume remains constant.

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{C_v}
\]

---

# 14. Specific Heat at Constant Pressure

Heat supplied when pressure remains constant.

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{C_p}
\]

---

# 15. Relation Between Cp and Cv

\[
\boxed{
C_p-C_v=R
}
\]

---

# 16. Ratio of Specific Heats

\[
\boxed{
\gamma=\frac{C_p}{C_v}
}
\]

---

Values:

---

Monoatomic gas:

\[
\boxed{
\gamma=\frac53
}
\]

---

Diatomic gas:

\[
\boxed{
\gamma=\frac75
}
\]

---

# PART G: MAXWELL DISTRIBUTION

---

# 17. Maxwell Distribution of Molecular Speeds

## Definition

The distribution of gas molecules according to their speeds is called Maxwell distribution.

---

Important speeds:

1. Most probable speed
2. Average speed
3. RMS speed

---

# 18. Most Probable Speed

Speed possessed by maximum number of molecules.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
v_{mp}
=

\sqrt{\frac{2RT}{M}}
}
\]

---

# 19. Average Speed

\[
\boxed{
v_{avg}
=

\sqrt{\frac{8RT}{\pi M}}
}
\]

---

# 20. RMS Speed

\[
\boxed{
v_{rms}
=

\sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}
}
\]

---

Relationship:

\[
\boxed{
v_{mp}<v_{avg}<v_{rms}
}
\]

---

# PART H: MEAN FREE PATH

---

# 21. Mean Free Path

## Definition

The average distance travelled by a molecule between two successive collisions is called mean free path.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{\lambda}
\]

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\lambda=
\frac{1}
{\sqrt2\pi d^2 n}
}
\]

---

where:

- d = diameter of molecule
- n = number density

---

# 22. Factors Affecting Mean Free Path

Mean free path increases when:

- Temperature increases
- Pressure decreases

---

Mean free path decreases when:

- Molecular size increases
- Number of molecules increases

---

# Common Mistakes

- Using Celsius instead of Kelvin in gas equations.
- Confusing R and k.
- Forgetting RMS velocity is not average velocity.
- Mixing degrees of freedom values.
- Assuming all gases have same specific heat.
- Forgetting molecular collisions are elastic in ideal gas theory.
- Confusing pressure and force.

---

- Kinetic theory explains gas behaviour using molecular motion.
- Gas laws lead to the ideal gas equation.
- Gas pressure arises due to molecular collisions.
- Temperature represents average molecular kinetic energy.
- Equipartition theorem explains energy distribution.
- Specific heats depend on molecular structure.
- Maxwell distribution describes molecular speeds.
- Mean free path measures average collision distance.
