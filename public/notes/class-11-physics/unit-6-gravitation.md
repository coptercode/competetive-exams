# Introduction

Gravitation is the force of attraction between any two masses in the universe.

It explains:

- Motion of planets
- Satellite movement
- Ocean tides
- Weight of objects
- Formation of stars and galaxies

This unit covers:

- Newton's law of gravitation
- Kepler's laws
- Acceleration due to gravity
- Variation of g
- Satellites
- Orbital velocity
- Escape velocity
- Weightlessness

---

# PART A: NEWTON'S LAW OF GRAVITATION

---

# 1. Gravitational Force

## Definition

The attractive force between any two bodies having mass is called gravitational force.

---

# 2. Universal Law of Gravitation

## Statement

Every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force that is:

1. Directly proportional to the product of their masses.
2. Inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

---

Mathematically:

\[
F\propto m_1m_2
\]

and

\[
F\propto\frac1{r^2}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
F=\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}
}
\]

---

where:

- F = gravitational force
- m₁, m₂ = masses
- r = distance between centres
- G = universal gravitational constant

---

# 3. Gravitational Constant (G)

Value:

\[
\boxed{
G=6.674\times10^{-11}Nm^2/kg^2
}
\]

---

Properties:

- Universal constant
- Same everywhere
- Independent of medium

---

# PART B: KEPLER'S LAWS OF PLANETARY MOTION

---

# 4. Kepler's First Law

## Law of Orbits

Every planet moves around the Sun in an elliptical orbit with the Sun at one focus.

---

# 5. Kepler's Second Law

## Law of Areas

The line joining the Sun and planet sweeps equal areas in equal intervals of time.

---

This means:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Areal velocity is constant}
}
\]

---

# 6. Kepler's Third Law

## Law of Periods

The square of the time period of revolution is directly proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
T^2\propto r^3
}
\]

or:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{T^2}{r^3}=constant
}
\]

---

# PART C: ACCELERATION DUE TO GRAVITY

---

# 7. Acceleration Due to Gravity (g)

## Definition

The acceleration produced in a body due to Earth's gravitational force is called acceleration due to gravity.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{g}
\]

---

Value:

\[
\boxed{
g=9.8m/s^2
}
\]

---

# 8. Relation Between G and g

Gravitational force on an object:

\[
F=\frac{GMm}{R^2}
\]

Weight:

\[
F=mg
\]

Equating:

\[
mg=\frac{GMm}{R^2}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
g=\frac{GM}{R^2}
}
\]

---

where:

- M = mass of Earth
- R = radius of Earth

---

# PART D: VARIATION OF g

---

# 9. Variation of g with Altitude

At height h above Earth's surface:

\[
\boxed{
g_h=
g
\left(
\frac{R}{R+h}
\right)^2
}
\]

---

As altitude increases:

\[
\boxed{
g\text{ decreases}
}
\]

---

# 10. Variation of g with Depth

At depth d below Earth's surface:

\[
\boxed{
g_d=g
\left(
1-\frac dR
\right)
}
\]

---

At Earth's centre:

\[
\boxed{
g=0
}
\]

---

# 11. Variation of g with Latitude

The value of g depends on Earth's rotation.

---

At poles:

\[
\boxed{
g\text{ maximum}
}
\]

At equator:

\[
\boxed{
g\text{ minimum}
}
\]

---

# PART E: GRAVITATIONAL POTENTIAL

---

# 12. Gravitational Potential Energy

Energy possessed by a body due to its position in gravitational field.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
U=-\frac{GMm}{r}
}
\]

---

Negative sign indicates gravitational force is attractive.

---

# 13. Gravitational Potential

Potential energy per unit mass.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
V=\frac UG
}
\]

or:

\[
\boxed{
V=-\frac{GM}{r}
}
\]

---

# PART F: SATELLITES

---

# 14. Artificial Satellite

A body that revolves around a planet due to gravitational attraction is called an artificial satellite.

Examples:

- Communication satellites
- Weather satellites
- GPS satellites

---

# 15. Orbital Velocity

## Definition

The velocity required for a satellite to remain in a stable orbit is called orbital velocity.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
v_o=
\sqrt{\frac{GM}{R}}
}
\]

---

Near Earth's surface:

\[
\boxed{
v_o=\sqrt{gR}
}
\]

---

Value:

\[
\boxed{
7.9km/s
}
\]

---

# 16. Time Period of Satellite

Time taken by a satellite to complete one revolution.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
T=
2\pi\sqrt{\frac{R^3}{GM}}
}
\]

---

Using Kepler's law:

\[
\boxed{
T^2\propto R^3
}
\]

---

# 17. Escape Velocity

## Definition

The minimum velocity required for an object to escape Earth's gravitational field without returning is called escape velocity.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
v_e=\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}}
}
\]

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
v_e=\sqrt2v_o
}
\]

---

Value:

\[
\boxed{
11.2km/s
}
\]

---

# 18. Geostationary Satellite

A satellite that appears stationary relative to Earth is called a geostationary satellite.

---

Conditions:

- Moves from west to east
- Period = 24 hours
- Orbit lies in equatorial plane

---

Applications:

- Communication
- Television broadcasting
- Weather monitoring

---

# PART G: WEIGHTLESSNESS

---

# 19. Weight

Weight is the gravitational force acting on a body.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
W=mg
}
\]

---

# 20. Weightlessness

## Definition

The condition in which the apparent weight of a body becomes zero is called weightlessness.

---

Causes:

- Free fall motion
- Orbiting satellites

---

In satellite:

\[
\boxed{
g_{apparent}=0
}
\]

---

Examples:

- Astronauts floating in spacecraft
- Objects floating inside orbiting stations

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Find gravitational force between two masses:

\[
m_1=10kg
\]

\[
m_2=20kg
\]

Distance:

\[
r=2m
\]

Using:

\[
F=\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}
\]

\[
=

\frac{6.674\times10^{-11}\times10\times20}{4}
\]

\[
\boxed{
3.337\times10^{-9}N
}
\]

---

## Example 2

Find weight of a body of mass 5 kg.

Given:

\[
g=9.8m/s^2
\]

\[
W=mg
\]

\[
=5(9.8)
\]

\[
\boxed{49N}
\]

---

## Example 3

Find escape velocity if orbital velocity is:

\[
8km/s
\]

Using:

\[
v_e=\sqrt2v_o
\]

\[
=1.414(8)
\]

\[
\boxed{11.3km/s}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing mass and weight.
- Forgetting gravitational force is always attractive.
- Using diameter instead of radius in formulas.
- Mixing orbital velocity and escape velocity.
- Forgetting negative sign in gravitational potential energy.
- Assuming g is constant everywhere.
- Confusing geostationary and polar satellites.

---

# Unit Summary

- Gravitation is the universal attractive force between masses.
- Newton's law explains gravitational interaction.
- Kepler's laws describe planetary motion.
- Acceleration due to gravity changes with altitude, depth and latitude.
- Satellites remain in orbit due to gravitational force.
- Escape velocity allows objects to leave Earth's gravitational field.
- Weightlessness occurs during free fall.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Gravity controls the motion of planets, satellites and the universe.}
}
\]
