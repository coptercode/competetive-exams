# Introduction

Kinematics is the branch of mechanics that describes the motion of objects without considering the causes of motion.

This unit covers:

- Frame of reference
- Position and displacement
- Distance and speed
- Velocity and acceleration
- Equations of motion
- Free fall
- Vector motion
- Projectile motion
- Uniform circular motion

Applications:

- Vehicle motion analysis
- Satellite motion
- Sports science
- Engineering design

---

# PART A: FRAME OF REFERENCE

---

# 1. Frame of Reference

## Definition

A coordinate system with respect to which the position and motion of an object are measured is called a frame of reference.

---

Example:

A person sitting in a moving train:

- Relative to train → at rest
- Relative to ground → moving

---

# 2. Position

## Definition

The location of an object with respect to a reference point is called its position.

---

In one dimension:

\[
\boxed{x}
\]

represents position.

---

In two dimensions:

\[
\boxed{(x,y)}
\]

---

In three dimensions:

\[
\boxed{(x,y,z)}
\]

---

# 3. Distance

## Definition

The total path length travelled by an object is called distance.

---

Properties:

- Scalar quantity
- Always positive
- Depends on actual path

Unit:

\[
\boxed{metre(m)}
\]

---

# 4. Displacement

## Definition

The shortest distance between initial and final positions in a specified direction is called displacement.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta x=x_f-x_i
}
\]

---

Properties:

- Vector quantity
- Can be positive, negative or zero
- Independent of path

---

# Difference Between Distance and Displacement

| Distance                              | Displacement                   |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Scalar quantity                       | Vector quantity                |
| Actual path length                    | Shortest path                  |
| Always positive                       | Can be positive or negative    |
| Greater than or equal to displacement | Less than or equal to distance |

---

# PART B: SPEED AND VELOCITY

---

# 5. Speed

## Definition

Distance travelled per unit time is called speed.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Speed=\frac{Distance}{Time}
}
\]

Unit:

\[
m/s
\]

---

# 6. Average Speed

\[
\boxed{
Average\ Speed=
\frac{Total\ Distance}
{Total\ Time}
}
\]

---

# 7. Velocity

## Definition

Displacement per unit time is called velocity.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Velocity=
\frac{Displacement}{Time}
}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
m/s
\]

---

# 8. Average Velocity

\[
\boxed{
Average\ Velocity=
\frac{Total\ Displacement}
{Total\ Time}
}
\]

---

# 9. Instantaneous Velocity

Velocity at a particular instant of time is called instantaneous velocity.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
v=
\frac{dx}{dt}
}
\]

---

# PART C: ACCELERATION

---

# 10. Acceleration

## Definition

The rate of change of velocity with time is called acceleration.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
a=
\frac{v-u}{t}
}
\]

where:

- u = initial velocity
- v = final velocity
- t = time

---

Unit:

\[
m/s^2
\]

---

# 11. Types of Acceleration

---

## Uniform Acceleration

Velocity changes equally in equal intervals of time.

Example:

Free fall motion.

---

## Non-uniform Acceleration

Velocity changes unequally with time.

---

# PART D: EQUATIONS OF MOTION

---

# 12. Motion With Uniform Acceleration

For an object with:

Initial velocity:

\[
u
\]

Final velocity:

\[
v
\]

Acceleration:

\[
a
\]

Time:

\[
t
\]

Displacement:

\[
s
\]

---

# First Equation of Motion

\[
\boxed{
v=u+at
}
\]

}

# PART E: FREE FALL

---

# 13. Free Fall Motion

## Definition

The motion of an object falling only under the influence of gravity is called free fall.

---

Acceleration:

\[
\boxed{g}
\]

Value:

\[
\boxed{
g=9.8m/s^2
}
\]

---

# Equations of Free Fall

For downward motion:

\[
v=u+gt
\]

---

\[
s=ut+\frac12gt^2
\]

---

\[
v^2=u^2+2gs
\]

---

# Motion Upward

Acceleration:

\[
\boxed{-g}
\]

---

Equations:

\[
v=u-gt
\]

---

Maximum height:

\[
\boxed{
H=\frac{u^2}{2g}
}
\]

---

Time to reach maximum height:

\[
\boxed{
t=\frac ug
}
\]

---

# PART F: VECTOR KINEMATICS

---

# 14. Scalars and Vectors

---

## Scalar

Only magnitude.

Examples:

- Distance
- Speed
- Mass

---

## Vector

Magnitude and direction.

Examples:

- Displacement
- Velocity
- Acceleration

---

# 15. Addition of Vectors

Two vectors can be added using:

- Triangle law
- Parallelogram law

---

# 16. Resolution of Vector

A vector can be divided into components along coordinate axes.

For vector A:

Horizontal component:

\[
\boxed{
A_x=A\cos\theta
}
\]

Vertical component:

\[
\boxed{
A_y=A\sin\theta
}
\]

---

# PART G: PROJECTILE MOTION

---

# 17. Projectile

## Definition

An object projected into air and moving under gravity alone is called a projectile.

---

Examples:

- Thrown ball
- Bullet motion

---

# 18. Components of Projectile Velocity

Initial velocity:

\[
u
\]

Horizontal component:

\[
\boxed{
u_x=u\cos\theta
}
\]

Vertical component:

\[
\boxed{
u_y=u\sin\theta
}
\]

---

# 19. Time of Flight

The total time taken by projectile to return to the same level.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
T=
\frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}
}
\]

---

# 20. Maximum Height

\[
\boxed{
H=
\frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}
}
\]

---

# 21. Horizontal Range

The horizontal distance travelled by projectile.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
R=
\frac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}
}
\]

---

Maximum range occurs when:

\[
\boxed{
\theta=45^\circ
}
\]

---

# PART H: UNIFORM CIRCULAR MOTION

---

# 22. Circular Motion

Motion of an object along a circular path is called circular motion.

---

# 23. Angular Displacement

The angle swept by radius vector is called angular displacement.

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{\theta}
\]

---

# 24. Angular Velocity

Rate of change of angular displacement.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\omega=\frac{\theta}{t}
}
\]

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
v=r\omega
}
\]

---

# 25. Centripetal Acceleration

Acceleration directed towards centre of circle.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
a_c=\frac{v^2}{r}
}
\]

or:

\[
\boxed{
a_c=r\omega^2
}
\]

---

# 26. Centripetal Force

Force required to maintain circular motion.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
F_c=\frac{mv^2}{r}
}
\]

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

A car starts from rest and accelerates at:

\[
2m/s^2
\]

for:

\[
5s
\]

Find final velocity.

Given:

\[
u=0
\]

\[
a=2
\]

\[
t=5
\]

Using:

\[
v=u+at
\]

\[
v=0+2(5)
\]

\[
\boxed{10m/s}
\]

---

## Example 2

A ball is thrown upward with:

\[
u=20m/s
\]

Find maximum height.

Formula:

\[
H=\frac{u^2}{2g}
\]

\[
=\frac{400}{19.6}
\]

\[
\boxed{20.4m}
\]

---

## Example 3

A body moves in a circle with:

\[
v=10m/s
\]

and:

\[
r=5m
\]

Centripetal acceleration:

\[
a_c=\frac{v^2}{r}
\]

\[
=\frac{100}{5}
\]

\[
\boxed{20m/s^2}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing distance and displacement.
- Treating velocity as scalar.
- Forgetting acceleration direction.
- Using wrong sign for gravity.
- Mixing projectile formulas.
- Forgetting range is maximum at 45°.
- Confusing angular velocity and linear velocity.
- Forgetting centripetal force acts towards the centre.

---

# Unit Summary

- Kinematics describes motion without considering forces.
- Distance and speed are scalar quantities.
- Displacement and velocity are vector quantities.
- Equations of motion describe uniformly accelerated motion.
- Projectile motion combines horizontal and vertical motion.
- Circular motion requires centripetal acceleration and force.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Kinematics describes how objects move in space and time.}
}
\]
