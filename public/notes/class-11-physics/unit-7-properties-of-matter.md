# PART A: ELASTICITY

---

# 1. Elasticity

## Definition

The property of a material by which it regains its original shape and size after removal of external force is called elasticity.

---

Examples:

- Steel spring
- Rubber band

---

# 2. Plasticity

## Definition

The property of a material by which it does not regain its original shape after removing force is called plasticity.

---

Examples:

- Clay
- Wax

---

# 3. Elastic Limit

The maximum value of external force up to which a body regains its original shape completely is called elastic limit.

---

# PART B: STRESS AND STRAIN

---

# 4. Stress

## Definition

The restoring force developed per unit area inside a deformed body is called stress.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Stress=\frac{Force}{Area}
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\sigma=\frac FA
}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{N/m^2}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{Pascal(Pa)}
\]

---

# Types of Stress

---

## 1. Longitudinal Stress

Stress produced due to change in length.

---

## 2. Bulk Stress

Stress produced due to change in volume.

---

## 3. Shearing Stress

Stress produced due to change in shape.

---

# 5. Strain

## Definition

The ratio of change in dimension to original dimension is called strain.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Strain=
\frac{Change}{Original\ value}
}
\]

---

Strain has no unit.

---

# Types of Strain

---

## 1. Longitudinal Strain

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Delta L}{L}
}
\]

---

## 2. Volume Strain

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Delta V}{V}
}
\]

---

## 3. Shear Strain

Change in angle of deformation.

---

# PART C: HOOKE'S LAW

---

# 6. Hooke's Law

## Statement

Within the elastic limit, stress is directly proportional to strain.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Stress\propto Strain
}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Stress}{Strain}=constant
}
\]

---

The constant is called elastic modulus.

---

# PART D: ELASTIC MODULI

---

# 7. Young's Modulus

## Definition

The ratio of longitudinal stress to longitudinal strain is called Young's modulus.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Y=
\frac{Longitudinal\ stress}
{Longitudinal\ strain}
}
\]

---

\[
\boxed{
Y=
\frac{F/A}{\Delta L/L}
}
\]

---

# 8. Bulk Modulus

## Definition

The ratio of volume stress to volume strain is called bulk modulus.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
K=
\frac{Pressure}
{Volume\ strain}
}
\]

---

\[
\boxed{
K=
-\frac{P}{\Delta V/V}
}
\]

---

# 9. Shear Modulus

Ratio of shearing stress to shearing strain.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
G=
\frac{Shear\ stress}
{Shear\ strain}
}
\]

---

# 10. Stress-Strain Curve

A graph showing relationship between stress and strain.

---

Important regions:

---

## Proportional Limit

Stress is directly proportional to strain.

---

## Elastic Limit

Body returns to original shape.

---

## Yield Point

Permanent deformation begins.

---

## Plastic Region

Permanent deformation occurs.

---

## Breaking Point

Body breaks.

# PART E: FLUID MECHANICS

---

# 11. Pressure in Fluids

## Definition

Force acting normally per unit area is called pressure.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
P=\frac FA
}
\]

---

Pressure at depth h:

\[
\boxed{
P=h\rho g
}
\]

---

where:

- ρ = density
- h = depth
- g = acceleration due to gravity

---

# 12. Pascal's Law

## Statement

Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.

---

Applications:

- Hydraulic lift
- Hydraulic brakes
- Hydraulic press

---

# 13. Hydraulic Machines

Based on Pascal's law.

---

Hydraulic press:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{F_1}{A_1}
=

\frac{F_2}{A_2}
}
\]

---

# PART F: ARCHIMEDES' PRINCIPLE

---

# 14. Buoyancy

## Definition

The upward force exerted by a fluid on an immersed body is called buoyant force.

---

# 15. Archimedes' Principle

## Statement

A body immersed partially or completely in a fluid experiences an upward thrust equal to the weight of fluid displaced.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
F_b=V\rho g
}
\]

---

where:

- V = displaced volume
- ρ = fluid density

---

Applications:

- Ships
- Submarines
- Hydrometers

---

# PART G: VISCOSITY

---

# 16. Viscosity

## Definition

The internal friction between layers of a fluid is called viscosity.

---

Examples:

Honey has greater viscosity than water.

---

# 17. Coefficient of Viscosity

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\eta=
\frac{F}{A}
\frac{dx}{dv}
}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{Pa\,s}
\]

---

# 18. Stokes' Law

## Statement

The viscous force acting on a small spherical body moving through a fluid is:

\[
\boxed{
F=6\pi\eta rv
}
\]

---

where:

- η = viscosity
- r = radius
- v = velocity

---

# 19. Terminal Velocity

## Definition

The constant velocity acquired by a falling body when viscous force balances gravitational force is called terminal velocity.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
v_t=
\frac{2r^2(\rho-\sigma)g}
{9\eta}
}
\]

---

# PART H: SURFACE TENSION

---

# 20. Surface Tension

## Definition

The property of a liquid surface by which it behaves like a stretched elastic membrane is called surface tension.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
T=\frac FL
}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
N/m
\]

---

# 21. Molecular Forces

---

# Cohesive Force

Attractive force between molecules of same substance.

Example:

Water-water attraction.

---

# Adhesive Force

Attractive force between different substances.

Example:

Water-glass attraction.

---

# 22. Surface Energy

Energy required to increase surface area of liquid.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Surface\ Energy=T\times Area
}
\]

---

# 23. Capillary Rise

## Definition

The rise or fall of liquid in a narrow tube is called capillary action.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
h=
\frac{2T\cos\theta}
{r\rho g}
}
\]

---

where:

- T = surface tension
- r = tube radius
- ρ = liquid density

---

Examples:

- Rise of water in plants
- Ink flow in pen

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

A force of 100 N acts on an area of:

\[
2m^2
\]

Find stress.

\[
Stress=\frac FA
\]

\[
=\frac{100}{2}
\]

\[
\boxed{50Pa}
\]

---

## Example 2

Find pressure at depth 5 m in water.

Given:

\[
\rho=1000kg/m^3
\]

\[
g=9.8m/s^2
\]

\[
P=h\rho g
\]

\[
=5(1000)(9.8)
\]

\[
\boxed{49000Pa}
\]

---

## Example 3

Find buoyant force on an object displacing:

\[
0.02m^3
\]

of water.

\[
F_b=V\rho g
\]

\[
=0.02(1000)(9.8)
\]

\[
\boxed{196N}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing stress and pressure.
- Forgetting strain has no unit.
- Using wrong elastic modulus formula.
- Ignoring negative sign in bulk modulus.
- Confusing cohesive and adhesive forces.
- Forgetting buoyant force acts upward.
- Mixing viscosity and density.
- Using surface tension formula incorrectly.
