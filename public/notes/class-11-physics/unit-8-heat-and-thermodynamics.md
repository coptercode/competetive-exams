# Introduction

Thermodynamics is the branch of physics that deals with heat, temperature, energy transformation and the relationship between heat and work.

This unit covers:

- Thermal properties of matter
- Temperature scales
- Thermal expansion
- Specific heat capacity
- Thermodynamic systems
- Zeroth, first and second laws
- Heat engines
- Carnot engine
- Refrigerators

Applications:

- Engines
- Refrigeration systems
- Power plants
- Climate science
- Industrial processes

# PART A: TEMPERATURE AND HEAT

---

# 1. Temperature

## Definition

Temperature is the physical quantity that determines the degree of hotness or coldness of a body.

---

Temperature determines:

- Direction of heat flow
- Thermal equilibrium

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{Kelvin(K)}
\]

---

# 2. Heat

## Definition

Heat is the energy transferred from a hotter body to a colder body due to temperature difference.

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{Joule(J)}
\]

---

# 3. Thermal Equilibrium

Two bodies are in thermal equilibrium when they have the same temperature and no heat flows between them.

---

# PART C: THERMAL EXPANSION

---

# 7. Thermal Expansion

## Definition

The increase in dimensions of a substance when temperature increases is called thermal expansion.

---

Types:

1. Linear expansion
2. Area expansion
3. Volume expansion

---

# 8. Linear Expansion

Increase in length due to temperature change.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta L=L\alpha\Delta T
}
\]

---

where:

- L = original length
- α = coefficient of linear expansion
- ΔT = change in temperature

---

Final length:

\[
\boxed{
L'=L(1+\alpha\Delta T)
}
\]

---

# 9. Area Expansion

Increase in area:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta A=A\beta\Delta T
}
\]

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
\beta=2\alpha
}
\]

---

# 10. Volume Expansion

Increase in volume:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta V=V\gamma\Delta T
}
\]

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
\gamma=3\alpha
}
\]

---

# PART D: SPECIFIC HEAT CAPACITY

---

# 11. Specific Heat Capacity

## Definition

The amount of heat required to raise the temperature of unit mass of a substance by 1°C is called specific heat capacity.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Q=mc\Delta T
}
\]

---

where:

- Q = heat supplied
- m = mass
- c = specific heat capacity
- ΔT = temperature change

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{
J/kgK
}
\]

---

# 12. Heat Capacity

Heat required to raise temperature of a body by 1°C.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
C=mc
}
\]

---

# 13. Calorimetry

## Principle

Heat lost by hot body = Heat gained by cold body.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
m_1c_1(T_1-T)
=

m_2c_2(T-T_2)
}
\]

---

# PART E: THERMODYNAMIC SYSTEMS

---

# 14. Thermodynamic System

A collection of matter separated from surroundings for study is called a thermodynamic system.

---

Types:

---

## Open System

Exchanges both matter and energy.

Example:

Steam turbine.

---

## Closed System

Exchanges energy but not matter.

Example:

Closed bottle.

---

## Isolated System

Exchanges neither matter nor energy.

Example:

Ideal thermos flask.

---

# 15. State Variables

Properties that define the state of a system.

Examples:

- Pressure (P)
- Volume (V)
- Temperature (T)
- Internal energy (U)

---

# PART F: LAWS OF THERMODYNAMICS

---

# 16. Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics

## Statement

If two systems are separately in thermal equilibrium with a third system, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other.

---

Importance:

Defines temperature.

---

# 17. First Law of Thermodynamics

## Statement

Heat supplied to a system is used for increasing internal energy and doing external work.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Q=\Delta U+W
}
\]

---

where:

- Q = heat supplied
- ΔU = change in internal energy
- W = work done

---

Alternative:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta U=Q-W
}
\]

---

# 18. Work Done by Gas

For constant pressure:

\[
\boxed{
W=P\Delta V
}
\]

---

Expansion:

\[
W>0
\]

Compression:

\[
W<0
\]

---

# 19. Second Law of Thermodynamics

## Statement

Heat cannot spontaneously flow from a colder body to a hotter body without external work.

---

Another form:

No heat engine can convert all supplied heat into useful work.

---

# PART G: THERMODYNAMIC PROCESSES

---

# 20. Isothermal Process

Temperature remains constant.

\[
\boxed{
\Delta T=0
}
\]

For ideal gas:

\[
PV=constant
\]

---

# 21. Adiabatic Process

No heat exchange with surroundings.

\[
\boxed{
Q=0
}
\]

---

# 22. Isobaric Process

Pressure remains constant.

\[
\boxed{
P=constant
}
\]

---

# 23. Isochoric Process

Volume remains constant.

\[
\boxed{
V=constant
}
\]

# PART H: HEAT ENGINES

---

# 24. Heat Engine

## Definition

A device that converts heat energy into mechanical work is called a heat engine.

---

Components:

1. Source
2. Working substance
3. Sink

---

# 25. Efficiency of Heat Engine

Efficiency:

\[
\boxed{
\eta=
\frac{W}{Q_1}
}
\]

---

Also:

\[
\boxed{
\eta=
1-\frac{Q_2}{Q_1}
}
\]

---

where:

- Q₁ = heat absorbed
- Q₂ = heat rejected

---

# PART I: CARNOT ENGINE

---

# 26. Carnot Engine

An ideal heat engine operating between two temperatures is called a Carnot engine.

---

Efficiency:

\[
\boxed{
\eta=
1-\frac{T_2}{T_1}
}
\]

---

where:

- T₁ = source temperature
- T₂ = sink temperature

---

Maximum efficiency depends only on temperatures.

---

# PART J: REFRIGERATOR

---

# 27. Refrigerator

## Definition

A device that removes heat from a cold region and transfers it to a hotter region using external work.

---

# 28. Coefficient of Performance (COP)

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
COP=
\frac{Q_2}{W}
}
\]

---

where:

- Q₂ = heat removed from cold body
- W = work input

---

For refrigerator:

\[
\boxed{
COP=\frac{T_2}{T_1-T_2}
}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing heat and temperature.
- Forgetting Kelvin must be used in thermodynamic formulas.
- Mixing heat capacity and specific heat capacity.
- Using wrong sign convention in first law.
- Confusing heat engine efficiency and refrigerator COP.
- Forgetting no heat transfer occurs in adiabatic process.
- Using Celsius instead of Kelvin in Carnot efficiency.
