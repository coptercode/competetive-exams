# Introduction

Carboxylic acids are organic compounds containing the functional group:

\[
-COOH
\]

called the **carboxyl group**.

---

# General Formula

\[
R-COOH
\]

Where:

- R = Alkyl or aryl group
- COOH = Carboxyl group

---

# Examples

## Formic Acid

\[
HCOOH
\]

## Acetic Acid

\[
CH_3COOH
\]

---

The carboxyl group contains:

```

        +

Hydroxyl group (-OH)
```

Structure:

\[
R-C(=O)-OH
\]

Contains three -COOH groups.

Example:

# 4. Nomenclature of Carboxylic Acids

Rules:

1. Select longest carbon chain containing -COOH.
2. Replace "e" with "oic acid".
3. Carbon of COOH is always carbon number 1.

---

Examples:

\[
HCOOH
\]

Name:

```
Methanoic acid
```

---

\[
CH_3COOH
\]

Name:

```
Ethanoic acid
```

---

\[
CH_3CH_2COOH
\]

Name:

```
Propanoic acid
```

# 5. Preparation of Carboxylic Acids

---

# 1. Oxidation of Primary Alcohols

Primary alcohols oxidise to carboxylic acids.

Reaction:

\[
RCH_2OH
\rightarrow
RCOOH
\]

Reagents:

- KMnO₄
- K₂Cr₂O₇

---

# 2. Oxidation of Aldehydes

Aldehydes easily oxidise to acids.

\[
RCHO
\rightarrow
RCOOH
\]

---

# 3. Hydrolysis of Nitriles

Nitriles give carboxylic acids on hydrolysis.

\[
RCN+2H_2O
\rightarrow
RCOOH+NH_3
\]

---

# 4. Grignard Reagent Method

Reaction:

\[
RMgX+CO_2
\rightarrow
RCOOH
\]

---

# 5. Hydrolysis of Esters

\[
RCOOR'
+H_2O
\rightarrow
RCOOH+R'OH
\]

---

# 6. Physical Properties

---

# 1. Hydrogen Bonding

Carboxylic acids form strong hydrogen bonds.

They exist as dimers.

Structure:

```
Acid molecule
      ||
Hydrogen bonding
      ||
Acid molecule
```

---

Effects:

- High boiling points
- Higher melting points

---

# 2. Solubility

Lower acids are soluble in water.

Solubility decreases with increasing carbon chain length.

---

# 7. Acidity of Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids are acidic because they release H⁺ ions.

Reaction:

\[
RCOOH
\rightarrow
RCOO^-+H^+
\]

---

# Reason for Acidity

Carboxylate ion is resonance stabilised.

\[
RCOO^-
\]

---

# 8. Factors Affecting Acidity

---

# 1. Electron Withdrawing Groups

Increase acidity.

Examples:

- Cl
- NO₂
- F

---

Example:

\[
ClCH_2COOH
\]

is more acidic than:

\[
CH_3COOH
\]

---

# 2. Electron Donating Groups

Decrease acidity.

Examples:

- CH₃
- Alkyl groups

---

# Acidity Order

\[
FCH_2COOH

>

ClCH_2COOH

>

CH_3COOH
\]

# 12. Esterification Reaction

## Definition

Reaction between carboxylic acid and alcohol to form ester.

---

Reaction:

\[
RCOOH+R'OH
\rightarrow
RCOOR'+H_2O
\]

Catalyst:

\[
Conc.H_2SO_4
\]

---

Example:

Acetic acid + Ethanol

produces:

Ethyl acetate

---

# 13. Formation of Acid Chlorides

Carboxylic acids react with thionyl chloride.

Reaction:

\[
RCOOH+SOCl_2
\rightarrow
RCOCl+SO_2+HCl
\]

---

# 14. Reduction of Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids reduce to primary alcohols.

Reagent:

\[
LiAlH_4
\]

Reaction:

\[
RCOOH
\rightarrow
RCH_2OH
\]

---

# 15. Decarboxylation

## Definition

Removal of CO₂ from carboxylic acid salts.

---

Reaction:

\[
RCOONa+NaOH
\rightarrow
RH+Na_2CO_3
\]

Reagent:

Soda lime

---

Example:

\[
CH_3COONa
\rightarrow
CH_4
\]

---

# 16. Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) Reaction

## Definition

α-halogenation of carboxylic acids.

---

Reaction:

\[
RCH_2COOH
\rightarrow
RCHXCOOH
\]

Reagents:

- Br₂
- Red phosphorus

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. Why are carboxylic acids acidic?

**Answer:**

Carboxylic acids release H⁺ ions because the carboxylate ion formed is stabilised by resonance.

---

## 2. Explain esterification.

**Answer:**

Carboxylic acids react with alcohols in presence of concentrated H₂SO₄ to form esters.

---

## 3. What is decarboxylation?

**Answer:**

Removal of CO₂ from sodium salts of carboxylic acids using soda lime is called decarboxylation.

---

## 4. Why do carboxylic acids have high boiling points?

**Answer:**

Due to strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding and dimer formation.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing aldehydes and carboxylic acids.
- Forgetting resonance stabilisation of carboxylate ion.
- Mixing esterification and hydrolysis.
- Forgetting CO₂ removal in decarboxylation.
- Writing wrong oxidation products.

# Chapter Summary

- **Carboxylic Acids** are organic compounds containing the -COOH functional group.
- They are acidic due to resonance stabilisation of the carboxylate ion.
- They can be prepared by oxidation, hydrolysis, and Grignard reactions.
- Important reactions include esterification, reduction, decarboxylation, and HVZ reaction.
- Carboxylic acids and their derivatives have major industrial and biological applications.
