# Introduction

**Elasticity** is the property of materials by which they regain their original shape and size after the deforming force is removed. It plays an important role in engineering, construction, machine design, bridges, buildings, springs, and structural materials.

This chapter deals with **stress, strain, Hooke's law, elastic constants, Young's modulus, Bulk modulus, Shear modulus, Poisson's ratio, and elastic energy**.

---

# 1. Elasticity

## Definition

Elasticity is the property of a material by which it regains its original shape and size after the removal of the deforming force.

---

# Examples

- Spring
- Rubber band (within elastic limit)
- Steel wire
- Bow and arrow
- Elastic cord

---

# Plasticity

## Definition

Plasticity is the property of a material by which it does not regain its original shape after the removal of the deforming force.

---

# Examples

- Clay
- Putty
- Wax
- Lead

---

# Difference Between Elasticity and Plasticity

| Elasticity             | Plasticity            |
| ---------------------- | --------------------- |
| Regains original shape | Permanent deformation |
| Temporary deformation  | Permanent deformation |
| Example: Steel         | Example: Clay         |

---

# 2. Deforming Force

## Definition

A force that changes the shape or dimensions of a body is called a deforming force.

---

# Types of Deformation

- Change in length
- Change in volume
- Change in shape

---

# 3. Stress

## Definition

Stress is the restoring force developed per unit area inside a body due to deformation.

---

# Formula

```
Stress

=

Force

────────

Area
```

or

```
σ = F/A
```

---

# SI Unit

```
Pascal (Pa)

=

N m⁻²
```

---

# Dimensional Formula

```
[ML⁻¹T⁻²]
```

---

# Types of Stress

## Longitudinal Stress

Acts perpendicular to the cross-sectional area.

### Tensile Stress

Produces elongation.

### Compressive Stress

Produces compression.

---

## Shearing Stress

Acts parallel to the surface.

---

## Bulk Stress

Acts uniformly in all directions.

---

# Difference Between Tensile and Compressive Stress

| Tensile Stress   | Compressive Stress |
| ---------------- | ------------------ |
| Increases length | Decreases length   |
| Pulling force    | Pushing force      |

---

# 4. Strain

## Definition

Strain is the ratio of change in dimension to the original dimension.

---

# Longitudinal Strain

```

=

─────

L
```

---

# Shear Strain

=

```

---

# Volumetric Strain

```
Volumetric Strain

=

```

---

# Characteristics

- Dimensionless quantity.
- No SI unit.

---

# Difference Between Stress and Strain

| Stress              | Strain               |
| ------------------- | -------------------- |
| Force per unit area | Ratio of deformation |
| Unit: Pascal        | No unit              |

---

# 5. Hooke's Law

## Statement

Within the elastic limit, stress is directly proportional to strain.

---

# Mathematical Form

```
Stress

∝

Strain
```

or

```
Stress

=

Elastic Constant × Strain
```

---

# Stress-Strain Graph

```
Stress
│
│          B
│         •
│       /
│     /
│   /
│ •A
└────────────────── Strain
```

---

# Important Points

- OA → Hooke's law obeyed.
- A → Limit of proportionality.
- B → Elastic limit.
- Beyond elastic limit → Permanent deformation.

---

# Elastic Limit

The maximum stress up to which a body regains its original dimensions after removal of force.

---

# Yield Point

Beyond this point, permanent deformation begins.

---

# Breaking Point

The material fractures.

---

# Difference Between Proportional Limit and Elastic Limit

| Proportional Limit         | Elastic Limit                   |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Stress ∝ Strain            | Material regains original shape |
| Comes before elastic limit | Slightly higher value           |

---

# 6. Elastic Moduli

# 10. Poisson's Ratio

## Definition

The ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain.

---

# Formula

```
ν

=

Lateral Strain

────────────────

---

# Characteristics

- No unit.
- Usually lies between 0 and 0.5.

---

# Applications

- Civil engineering.
- Structural analysis.

# 11. Elastic Potential Energy

## Definition

---

# Formula

---

```

½ × Stress × Strain
```

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A force of 200 N acts on an area of 0.02 m². Find the stress.

### Solution

```
Stress = F/A

= 200/0.02

= 10000 Pa
```

### Answer

**10000 Pa**

---

## Example 2

### Question

A wire of length 2 m increases by 2 mm. Find the longitudinal strain.

### Solution

```
ΔL = 2 mm = 0.002 m

Strain

= 0.002/2

= 0.001
```

### Answer

**0.001**

---

## Example 3

### Question

A spring of spring constant 500 N m⁻¹ is stretched by 0.1 m. Find the elastic potential energy.

### Solution

```
U = ½kx²

= ½ × 500 × (0.1)²

= 2.5 J
```

### Answer

**2.5 J**

---

## Example 4

### Question

A material has a bulk modulus of 2 × 10⁹ Pa. Find its compressibility.

### Solution

```
Compressibility

= 1/K

= 1/(2×10⁹)

= 5×10⁻¹⁰ Pa⁻¹
```

### Answer

**5 × 10⁻¹⁰ Pa⁻¹**

---

## Example 5

### Question

A rod experiences a stress of 2 × 10⁸ Pa and a strain of 0.001. Find Young's modulus.

### Solution

```
Y

= Stress/Strain

= (2×10⁸)/0.001

= 2×10¹¹ Pa
```

### Answer

**2 × 10¹¹ Pa**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **stress** with **pressure**.
- Writing units for **strain** (strain has no unit).
- Applying Hooke's law beyond the elastic limit.
- Using incorrect formulas for Young's, Bulk, and Shear moduli.
- Ignoring unit conversion (mm to m, cm² to m²).
- Confusing elastic limit with breaking point.

# Chapter Summary

- **Elasticity** is the ability of a material to regain its original dimensions after the removal of a deforming force, whereas **plasticity** results in permanent deformation.
- **Stress** measures the internal restoring force per unit area, while **strain** represents the relative deformation produced in the material.
- **Hooke's Law** states that stress is directly proportional to strain within the elastic limit, providing the basis for defining elastic constants.
- The three elastic moduli—**Young's modulus, Bulk modulus, and Shear modulus**—describe resistance to changes in length, volume, and shape, respectively.
- **Poisson's ratio** relates lateral and longitudinal strains, and **elastic potential energy** represents the energy stored during elastic deformation.
- The principles of elasticity are extensively applied in **civil engineering, mechanical design, aerospace, robotics, biomedical engineering, and structural analysis**, ensuring the safe and efficient design of engineering systems.
