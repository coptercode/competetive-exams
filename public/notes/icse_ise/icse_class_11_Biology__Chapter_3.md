# Introduction

The **Plant Kingdom (Kingdom Plantae)** comprises a vast group of multicellular, eukaryotic, autotrophic organisms capable of performing **photosynthesis** due to the presence of chlorophyll. Plants play a vital role in maintaining ecological balance by producing oxygen, fixing carbon dioxide, and serving as the primary producers in food chains.

Based on the **degree of differentiation, vascular tissue, seed formation, and reproductive structures**, the plant kingdom is classified into:

- Algae
- Bryophytes
- Pteridophytes
- Gymnosperms
- Angiosperms

---

# 1. Characteristics of Kingdom Plantae

- Multicellular and eukaryotic.
- Cell wall made of cellulose.
- Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll.
- Autotrophic mode of nutrition.
- Reserve food mainly as starch.
- Reproduce sexually and asexually.
- Life cycle shows **alternation of generations**.

# 2. Algae

## Definition

Algae are **simple, chlorophyll-bearing, autotrophic, thalloid plants** found mainly in aquatic habitats.

---

## General Characteristics

- Mostly aquatic.
- Body is a thallus.
- No true roots, stems, or leaves.
- Photosynthetic.
- Reproduction by vegetative, asexual, and sexual methods.

---

# Classification of Algae

## A. Chlorophyceae (Green Algae)

### Characteristics

- Green due to chlorophyll **a** and **b**.
- Cell wall made of cellulose.
- Food stored as starch.
- Mostly freshwater.

---

### Examples

- _Chlamydomonas_
- _Volvox_
- _Spirogyra_
- _Ulothrix_
- _Chara_

---

## B. Phaeophyceae (Brown Algae)

### Characteristics

- Brown due to **fucoxanthin** pigment.
- Marine habitat.
- Cell wall contains cellulose and algin.
- Food stored as laminarin and mannitol.

---

### Examples

- _Laminaria_
- _Sargassum_
- _Fucus_
- _Ectocarpus_

---

## C. Rhodophyceae (Red Algae)

### Characteristics

- Red due to **phycoerythrin**.
- Mostly marine.
- Cell wall contains cellulose and pectin.
- Food stored as Floridean starch.

---

### Examples

- _Polysiphonia_
- _Gelidium_
- _Gracilaria_
- _Porphyra_

---

# Comparison of Algal Classes

| Character   | Chlorophyceae    | Phaeophyceae                  | Rhodophyceae                    |
| ----------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Pigments    | Chlorophyll a, b | Chlorophyll a, c, Fucoxanthin | Chlorophyll a, d, Phycoerythrin |
| Colour      | Green            | Brown                         | Red                             |
| Habitat     | Freshwater       | Marine                        | Marine                          |
| Stored Food | Starch           | Laminarin, Mannitol           | Floridean starch                |
| Cell Wall   | Cellulose        | Cellulose + Algin             | Cellulose + Pectin              |

# 3. Bryophytes

## Definition

Bryophytes are **small, non-vascular plants** that usually grow in moist places and require water for fertilization.

They are called the

```
"Amphibians of the Plant Kingdom"
```

because they live on land but depend on water for reproduction.

---

## Characteristics

- No vascular tissue.
- Dominant gametophyte.
- Sporophyte dependent on gametophyte.
- Rhizoids instead of roots.
- Reproduce by spores.

---

## Life Cycle

```
Spore
   │
   ▼
Gametophyte
   │
Gametes
   │
Fertilization
   ▼
Zygote
   ▼
Sporophyte
   ▼
Spores
```

---

## Examples

- _Riccia_
- _Marchantia_
- _Funaria_
- _Sphagnum_

---

# Importance of Bryophytes

- Soil formation.
- Prevent soil erosion.
- Water retention.
- Formation of peat (_Sphagnum_).

---

# 4. Pteridophytes

## Definition

Pteridophytes are the **first vascular land plants** that reproduce through spores.

They are known as

```
Vascular Cryptogams
```

---

## Characteristics

- True roots, stems, and leaves.
- Well-developed xylem and phloem.
- Dominant sporophyte.
- Reproduce through spores.
- No flowers or seeds.

---

## Homospory and Heterospory

### Homospory

Produces one type of spore.

Example

- _Lycopodium_

---

### Heterospory

Produces two types of spores.

- Microspores
- Megaspores

Examples

- _Selaginella_
- _Salvinia_

---

## Importance

Heterospory is considered the first evolutionary step toward **seed formation**.

---

## Examples

- _Pteris_
- _Selaginella_
- _Lycopodium_
- _Equisetum_

---

# 5. Gymnosperms

## Definition

Gymnosperms are seed-producing plants in which the seeds are **naked**, i.e., not enclosed within fruits.

---

## Characteristics

- Woody perennial plants.
- Well-developed vascular tissues.
- Naked seeds.
- Cones present.
- Wind pollination.
- Dominant sporophyte.

---

## Root Modifications

### Coralloid Roots

Found in

```
Cycas
```

Contain nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria.

---

## Important Plants

### Cycas

Characteristics

- Palm-like appearance.
- Coralloid roots.
- Dioecious.

---

### Pinus

Characteristics

- Needle-like leaves.
- Cone-bearing.
- Monoecious.
- Resin-producing.

---

# Life Cycle of Gymnosperms

```
Sporophyte
     │
 Cones
     │
Spores
     │
Gametophytes
     │
Fertilization
     ▼
 Naked Seeds
     ▼
 New Plant
```

# 6. Angiosperms

## Definition

Angiosperms are flowering plants whose seeds are enclosed inside fruits.

They are the largest group of plants.

---

## Characteristics

- Flowers present.
- Fruits present.
- Double fertilization.
- Well-developed vascular tissues.
- Seeds enclosed within fruits.

---

# Classification

## Monocotyledons

Characteristics

- One cotyledon.
- Parallel venation.
- Fibrous root.
- Floral parts in multiples of 3.

Examples

- Rice
- Wheat
- Maize

---

## Dicotyledons

Characteristics

- Two cotyledons.
- Reticulate venation.
- Tap root.
- Floral parts in multiples of 4 or 5.

Examples

- Pea
- Mango
- Sunflower

---

# Double Fertilization

Discovered by

**S. G. Nawaschin (1898)**

---

## Process

One male gamete

↓

Fuses with egg

↓

Forms

```
Diploid Zygote (2n)
```

---

Second male gamete

↓

Fuses with

```
Two Polar Nuclei
```

↓

Forms

```
Primary Endosperm Nucleus (3n)
```

---

This process is called

```
Double Fertilization
```

---

# Difference Between Gymnosperms and Angiosperms

| Gymnosperms                 | Angiosperms                  |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Naked seeds                 | Seeds enclosed in fruits     |
| Flowers absent              | Flowers present              |
| Fruits absent               | Fruits present               |
| Double fertilization absent | Double fertilization present |
| Cones present               | Flowers present              |

---

# Difference Between Bryophytes and Pteridophytes

| Bryophytes             | Pteridophytes                |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| No vascular tissue     | Vascular tissue present      |
| Gametophyte dominant   | Sporophyte dominant          |
| Rhizoids present       | True roots present           |
| No true stem or leaves | True stem and leaves present |

# Important Scientific Terms

| Term                       | Meaning                                          |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Thallus                    | Undifferentiated plant body                      |
| Rhizoid                    | Root-like structure in bryophytes                |
| Sporophyte                 | Diploid spore-producing generation               |
| Gametophyte                | Haploid gamete-producing generation              |
| Homospory                  | One type of spore                                |
| Heterospory                | Two types of spores                              |
| Coralloid Root             | Nitrogen-fixing root of _Cycas_                  |
| Double Fertilization       | Fusion of two male gametes with different nuclei |
| Endosperm                  | Nutritive tissue for embryo                      |
| Alternation of Generations | Alternation between gametophyte and sporophyte   |

## Example 1

### Question

Why are bryophytes called the amphibians of the plant kingdom?

### Solution

Bryophytes live on land but require water for fertilization and reproduction.

### Answer

They are called amphibians of the plant kingdom because they depend on water for sexual reproduction.

Production of microspores and megaspores.

Which plant possesses coralloid roots?

### Solution

## Example 5

### Question

What are the products of double fertilization?

### Solution

One male gamete fuses with the egg to form the **diploid zygote (2n)**, while the other fuses with the two polar nuclei to form the **triploid primary endosperm nucleus (3n)**.

### Answer

- Diploid zygote (2n)
- Triploid primary endosperm nucleus (3n)

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **gymnosperms** with **angiosperms**.
- Forgetting that bryophytes lack vascular tissues.
- Mixing up **homospory** and **heterospory**.
- Assuming algae possess true roots, stems, and leaves.
- Confusing the pigments and storage products of different algal classes.
- Forgetting that **double fertilization** occurs only in angiosperms.

# Chapter Summary

- The **Plant Kingdom** consists of multicellular, eukaryotic, autotrophic organisms that carry out photosynthesis and exhibit **alternation of generations**.
- Plants are classified into **Algae, Bryophytes, Pteridophytes, Gymnosperms, and Angiosperms** based on body organization, vascular tissues, seed formation, and reproductive structures.
- **Algae** are simple thalloid plants classified into **green, brown, and red algae** based on pigments, storage products, and cell wall composition.
- **Bryophytes** are non-vascular plants with a dominant gametophyte, whereas **Pteridophytes** are the first vascular plants with a dominant sporophyte and spore-based reproduction.
- **Gymnosperms** produce naked seeds on cones, while **Angiosperms** are flowering plants that produce enclosed seeds and exhibit the unique process of **double fertilization**.
- The Plant Kingdom forms the foundation of terrestrial ecosystems by providing food, oxygen, medicines, raw materials, and ecological stability, making it one of the most important groups of living organisms.
