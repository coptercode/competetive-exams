# Introduction

**Plant Growth and Development** is the process by which plants increase in size, undergo differentiation, and attain maturity. Growth is a permanent increase in size and dry mass, while development includes all the changes from seed germination to senescence.

Plant growth is regulated by **internal factors (plant hormones)** and **external factors (light, temperature, water, oxygen, and nutrients).**

---

# 1. Plant Growth

## Definition

Plant growth is a **permanent and irreversible increase in the size, volume, dry weight, and number of cells** of a plant.

---

# Characteristics of Plant Growth

- Permanent and irreversible.
- Increases cell number and size.
- Requires energy.
- Occurs throughout the life of the plant.
- Controlled by genes and hormones.

---

# Phases of Growth

```
              Plant Growth
                    │
      ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
      ▼             ▼             ▼
 Meristematic   Elongation   Maturation
     Phase         Phase         Phase
```

---

## A. Meristematic Phase

### Characteristics

- Active cell division.
- Cells are small and thin-walled.
- Dense cytoplasm.
- Large nucleus.
- No vacuoles.

---

## B. Elongation Phase

### Characteristics

- Cells enlarge rapidly.
- Vacuoles increase in size.
- Cell wall loosens.
- Maximum increase in length occurs.

---

## C. Maturation Phase

### Characteristics

- Cells attain final shape.
- Differentiation occurs.
- Specialized functions develop.

---

# Growth Rate

## Definition

Growth rate is the increase in size or mass per unit time.

---

# Types of Growth

### Arithmetic Growth

Growth occurs at a constant rate.

Formula

```
Lt = L₀ + rt
```

Where

- Lt = Final length
- L₀ = Initial length
- r = Growth rate
- t = Time

---

### Geometric Growth

Growth is proportional to existing size.

Formula

```
Wt = W₀eʳᵗ
```

# Grand Period of Growth

The complete period from the beginning of growth until maturity.

---

# 2. Development

## Definition

Development is the sum total of **growth, differentiation, maturation, and senescence**.

---

# Differentiation

Cells become specialized for specific functions.

Example

- Xylem
- Phloem

---

# Dedifferentiation

Mature cells regain the ability to divide.

Example

- Cork cambium
- Interfascicular cambium

---

# Redifferentiation

Dedifferentiated cells become specialized again.

---

# Difference Between Growth and Development

| Growth           | Development                             |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Increase in size | Overall life changes                    |
| Quantitative     | Quantitative and qualitative            |
| Measurable       | Includes differentiation and maturation |

---

# 3. Plant Growth Regulators (PGRs)

## Definition

Plant Growth Regulators are organic substances produced in very small amounts that regulate plant growth and development.

---

# Classification

```
       Plant Growth Regulators
                │
      ┌─────────┴─────────┐
      ▼                   ▼
 Growth Promoters   Growth Inhibitors
```

---

# Growth Promoters

- Auxins
- Gibberellins
- Cytokinins

---

# Growth Inhibitors

- Abscisic Acid (ABA)
- Ethylene

---

# 4. Auxins

## Discovery

Charles Darwin first observed phototropic response.

Auxin was later isolated by **Frits Went**.

---

## Natural Auxin

```
IAA
(Indole-3-Acetic Acid)
```

---

## Functions

- Cell elongation.
- Apical dominance.
- Root initiation.
- Phototropism.
- Geotropism.
- Fruit development.
- Prevents premature fruit drop.

---

# Applications

- Rooting hormones.
- Weed control (2,4-D).
- Parthenocarpy.
- Tissue culture.

---

# 5. Gibberellins

## Discovery

Discovered from

```
Gibberella fujikuroi
```

---

## Functions

- Stem elongation.
- Seed germination.
- Breaks dormancy.
- Flowering.
- Fruit enlargement.
- Bolting in rosette plants.

---

# Applications

- Increase sugarcane yield.
- Malting of barley.
- Larger fruits.
- Breaking seed dormancy.

---

# 6. Cytokinins

## Discovery

Discovered by

```
F. Skoog
```

---

## Functions

- Cell division.
- Delays leaf senescence.
- Promotes lateral bud growth.
- Chloroplast development.

---

# Applications

- Tissue culture.
- Delays aging.
- Increases shelf life.

---

# Difference Between Auxins, Gibberellins and Cytokinins

| Auxins           | Gibberellins     | Cytokinins         |
| ---------------- | ---------------- | ------------------ |
| Cell elongation  | Stem elongation  | Cell division      |
| Root initiation  | Seed germination | Delays senescence  |
| Apical dominance | Bolting          | Lateral bud growth |

---

# 7. Abscisic Acid (ABA)

## Definition

ABA is a growth-inhibiting hormone.

---

## Functions

- Induces seed dormancy.
- Causes stomatal closure.
- Stress tolerance.
- Leaf abscission.
- Inhibits growth.

---

# Applications

- Drought resistance.
- Prevents water loss.
- Seed storage.

---

# 8. Ethylene

## Definition

Ethylene is a gaseous plant hormone.

---

## Functions

- Fruit ripening.
- Leaf abscission.
- Flower senescence.
- Breaks dormancy.
- Promotes flowering in pineapple.

---

# Applications

- Artificial fruit ripening.
- Synchronizing flowering.
- Fruit storage management.

---

# Difference Between ABA and Ethylene

| ABA              | Ethylene            |
| ---------------- | ------------------- |
| Growth inhibitor | Gaseous hormone     |
| Seed dormancy    | Fruit ripening      |
| Closes stomata   | Promotes abscission |
| Stress hormone   | Ripening hormone    |

---

# 9. Photoperiodism

## Definition

Photoperiodism is the response of plants to the relative duration of day and night.

---

# Types

```
Photoperiodism
      │
 ┌────┼───────────┐
 ▼    ▼           ▼
Short-Day
Long-Day
Day-Neutral
```

---

## Short-Day Plants

Flower when day length is shorter than the critical photoperiod.

Examples

- Chrysanthemum
- Tobacco
- Soybean

---

## Long-Day Plants

Flower when day length is longer than the critical photoperiod.

Examples

- Wheat
- Spinach
- Radish

---

## Day-Neutral Plants

Flower irrespective of day length.

Examples

- Tomato
- Cotton
- Sunflower

---

# Phytochrome

Light-sensitive pigment involved in photoperiodism.

---

# Difference Between Short-Day and Long-Day Plants

| Short-Day              | Long-Day             |
| ---------------------- | -------------------- |
| Long night required    | Short night required |
| Example: Chrysanthemum | Example: Wheat       |

---

# 10. Vernalisation

## Definition

Vernalisation is the induction of flowering by exposing plants to **low temperature**.

---

## Importance

- Early flowering.
- Increased yield.
- Uniform flowering.
- Shortens vegetative phase.

---

## Examples

- Wheat
- Barley
- Cabbage
- Sugar beet

---

# Difference Between Vernalisation and Photoperiodism

| Vernalisation         | Photoperiodism          |
| --------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Temperature dependent | Light dependent         |
| Low temperature       | Day length              |
| Promotes flowering    | Controls flowering time |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Which hormone promotes cell elongation?

### Solution

Auxin stimulates elongation of stem and coleoptile cells.

### Answer

Auxin (IAA).

---

## Example 2

### Question

Which plant hormone is responsible for fruit ripening?

### Solution

Ethylene accelerates fruit ripening in climacteric fruits.

### Answer

Ethylene.

---

## Example 3

### Question

Name the hormone that induces seed dormancy.

### Solution

Abscisic Acid inhibits germination and induces dormancy.

### Answer

Abscisic Acid (ABA).

---

## Example 4

### Question

What is vernalisation?

### Solution

It is the induction of flowering by exposing plants to prolonged low temperatures.

### Answer

Flowering induced by low temperature.

---

## Example 5

### Question

Which hormone delays leaf senescence?

### Solution

Cytokinins slow down aging by maintaining chlorophyll and protein synthesis.

### Answer

Cytokinins.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **growth** with **development**.
- Mixing up the functions of auxins and gibberellins.
- Assuming ABA promotes growth instead of inhibiting it.
- Confusing photoperiodism with vernalisation.
- Forgetting that ethylene is the only gaseous plant hormone.
- Mixing up arithmetic and geometric growth.

# Chapter Summary

- **Plant Growth** is a permanent and irreversible increase in size, while **development** includes growth, differentiation, maturation, and senescence.
- Growth occurs in three phases: **meristematic**, **elongation**, and **maturation**, and follows characteristic growth patterns such as arithmetic and geometric growth.
- **Plant Growth Regulators (PGRs)** coordinate growth and development. **Auxins, gibberellins, and cytokinins** promote growth, whereas **abscisic acid (ABA)** and **ethylene** mainly regulate inhibition, dormancy, stress responses, and fruit ripening.
- **Photoperiodism** controls flowering based on day length, while **vernalisation** promotes flowering through prolonged exposure to low temperatures.
- External factors such as **light, water, temperature, oxygen, and minerals**, together with internal genetic and hormonal factors, regulate plant growth.
- Understanding plant growth and development is essential for **modern agriculture, horticulture, plant breeding, tissue culture, crop improvement, and sustainable food production**.
