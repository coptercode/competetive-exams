# Introduction

**Biodiversity** refers to the variety and variability of living organisms present on Earth.

It includes diversity among:

- Genes
- Species
- Ecosystems

---

# 1. Levels of Biodiversity

```
              Biodiversity

                    │

     ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐

     ▼              ▼              ▼

Genetic        Species        Ecosystem
Diversity     Diversity       Diversity
```

---

# 2. Genetic Diversity

## Definition

Variation in genes among individuals of the same species.

---

Examples:

- Different varieties of rice
- Different breeds of dogs
- Varieties of mango

---

# Importance

- Helps adaptation.
- Provides evolutionary potential.

---

# 3. Species Diversity

## Definition

Variety and abundance of different species in an area.

---

Examples:

- Number of plant and animal species in a forest.

---

# 4. Ecosystem Diversity

## Definition

Variation among different ecosystems.

---

Examples:

- Forests
- Deserts
- Wetlands
- Oceans

---

# 5. Biodiversity Distribution

Biodiversity is not equally distributed worldwide.

---

# Factors Affecting Biodiversity

- Climate
- Temperature
- Rainfall
- Evolutionary history

---

# 6. Latitudinal Gradient

## Definition

Biodiversity generally increases from poles towards the equator.

---

Reason:

Tropical regions have:

- Stable climate
- More sunlight
- Longer evolutionary time

---

# 7. Species-Area Relationship

Proposed by:

## Alexander von Humboldt

---

## Concept

Species richness increases with area.

---

Equation:

\[
S = CA^Z
\]

Where:

- S = Species richness
- A = Area
- C and Z = Constants

---

# 8. Importance of Biodiversity

Biodiversity provides ecological and economic benefits.

# 9. Consumptive Use Value

Direct use of biological resources.

Examples:

- Food
- Fuel
- Medicines

---

# 10. Productive Use Value

Commercially valuable products.

Examples:

- Timber
- Silk
- Pharmaceuticals

---

# 11. Ecological Value

Biodiversity maintains:

- Ecosystem stability
- Pollination
- Nutrient cycling
- Climate regulation

---

# 12. Ethical Value

Every species has a right to exist.

Humans have responsibility to conserve biodiversity.

---

# 13. Biodiversity Loss

## Definition

Reduction in the variety and number of species.

---

# Causes of Biodiversity Loss

The major causes are called:

## The Evil Quartet

```
             Evil Quartet

                  │

 ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐

 ▼                ▼                ▼

Habitat        Overexploitation   Alien Species
Loss                              Invasion

                  │

                  ▼

              Co-extinction
```

---

# 14. Habitat Loss and Fragmentation

## Definition

Destruction and division of natural habitats.

---

Examples:

- Deforestation
- Urbanization
- Agriculture expansion

---

Effects:

- Species extinction
- Reduced population size

---

# 15. Overexploitation

## Definition

Excessive use of biological resources.

---

Examples:

- Overfishing
- Hunting
- Excessive logging

---

# 16. Alien Species Invasion

## Definition

Introduction of non-native species into an ecosystem.

---

Effects:

- Competition with native species
- Extinction of native organisms

---

Examples:

## Water Hyacinth

Scientific name:

```
Eichhornia crassipes
```

---

## Lantana camara

A harmful invasive plant.

---

# 17. Co-extinction

## Definition

Extinction of one species results in extinction of another dependent species.

---

Example:

Host-specific parasites disappearing with their host.

---

# 18. Extinction

## Definition

Complete disappearance of a species from Earth.

---

# Causes

- Habitat destruction
- Climate change
- Pollution
- Human activities

---

# Examples of Extinct Species

- Dodo
- Passenger pigeon

---

# 19. Red Data Book

Maintained by:

## IUCN

(International Union for Conservation of Nature)

---

Contains information about:

- Threatened species
- Endangered species

---

# 20. Conservation of Biodiversity

## Definition

Protection and sustainable management of biodiversity.

---

# Types of Conservation

```
              Conservation

                    │

        ┌───────────┴───────────┐

        ▼                       ▼

```

---

# 21. In-situ Conservation

## Definition

Protection of species in their natural habitats.

---

Examples:

- National parks
- Wildlife sanctuaries
- Biosphere reserves

---

# 22. National Parks

Protected areas for conservation of wildlife and ecosystems.

---

Examples:

- Jim Corbett National Park
- Kaziranga National Park

---

# 23. Wildlife Sanctuaries

Areas where wild animals are protected.

---

# 24. Biosphere Reserves

Large protected areas conserving:

- Biodiversity
- Local culture
- Ecosystem processes

---

# 25. Ex-situ Conservation

## Definition

Conservation of species outside their natural habitats.

---

Examples:

- Zoos
- Botanical gardens
- Seed banks
- Gene banks

---

# 26. Cryopreservation

## Definition

Preservation of biological materials at extremely low temperatures.

---

Used for:

- Seeds
- Gametes
- Embryos

---

# 27. Biodiversity Hotspots

## Definition

Regions with:

1. High species richness
2. High endemism
3. High threat level

---

# Criteria

A hotspot must have:

- At least 1500 endemic plant species
- 70% habitat loss

---

# Biodiversity Hotspots in India

India has parts of:

1. Himalaya
2. Indo-Burma region
3. Western Ghats-Sri Lanka
4. Sundaland (Nicobar region)

---

# 28. Sacred Groves

## Definition

Forest patches protected by local communities due to religious beliefs.

---

Importance:

- Preserve rare species.
- Maintain biodiversity.

---

# 29. Ecosystem Services

Nature provides benefits called ecosystem services.

---

Examples:

- Oxygen production
- Pollination
- Climate regulation
- Soil formation

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. Define biodiversity.

**Answer:**

Biodiversity is the variety and variability of living organisms present on Earth.

---

## 2. Explain the three levels of biodiversity.

**Answer:**

The three levels are:

1. Genetic diversity
2. Species diversity
3. Ecosystem diversity

---

## 3. What are biodiversity hotspots?

**Answer:**

Regions with high species richness, high endemism, and severe habitat loss are called biodiversity hotspots.

---

## 4. Differentiate in-situ and ex-situ conservation.

| In-situ                      | Ex-situ                    |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Natural habitat conservation | Outside natural habitat    |
| National parks               | Zoos and botanical gardens |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing species diversity and ecosystem diversity.
- Forgetting the Evil Quartet causes.
- Mixing in-situ and ex-situ methods.
- Writing endangered species as extinct species.
- Forgetting IUCN role.

# Chapter Summary

- **Biodiversity and Conservation** explains the variety of life forms and methods to protect them.
- Biodiversity exists at genetic, species, and ecosystem levels.
- Human activities such as habitat destruction, overexploitation, and invasive species cause biodiversity loss.
- Conservation strategies include in-situ methods like national parks and ex-situ methods like zoos and gene banks.
- Biodiversity hotspots and ecosystem services highlight the importance of protecting Earth's biological resources.
