# Introduction

The **dual nature of matter and radiation** states that:

- Light behaves like a wave as well as a particle.
- Matter particles also show wave properties.

This concept forms the foundation of quantum physics.

---

# 1. Wave Nature of Light

Light shows wave properties:

- Interference
- Diffraction
- Polarisation

These phenomena prove the wave nature of light.

---

# 2. Particle Nature of Light

Light also behaves as particles called:

```
Photons
```

Evidence:

```
Photoelectric Effect
```

---

# 3. Photon

## Definition

A photon is a packet of electromagnetic energy.

---

Energy of photon:

\[
E=h\nu
\]

Where:

- h = Planck's constant
- ν = Frequency

---

Using:

\[
\nu=\frac c\lambda
\]

Energy becomes:

\[
E=\frac{hc}{\lambda}
\]

---

# Properties of Photon

1. Zero rest mass.
2. Travels with speed of light.
3. Has energy and momentum.
4. Cannot exist at rest.

---

# 4. Photoelectric Effect

## Definition

The phenomenon of emission of electrons from a metal surface when electromagnetic radiation of suitable frequency falls on it is called photoelectric effect.

---

Emitted electrons:

```
Photoelectrons
```

---

# 6. Important Terms in Photoelectric Effect

---

# 1. Photoelectron

Electrons emitted from metal surface due to light.

---

# 2. Photoelectric Current

Current produced due to flow of photoelectrons.

---

# 3. Threshold Frequency

## Definition

Minimum frequency of incident radiation required to emit electrons from a metal surface.

Symbol:

\[
\nu_0
\]

---

# 4. Work Function

## Definition

Minimum energy required to remove an electron from a metal surface.

---

Formula:

\[
\phi=h\nu_0
\]

---

Unit:

\[
eV
\]

---

# 5. Stopping Potential

## Definition

Minimum negative potential applied to stop the most energetic photoelectrons.

Symbol:

\[
V_0
\]

---

# 7. Laws of Photoelectric Effect

---

## Law 1

For a given metal, photoelectric emission occurs only if frequency is greater than threshold frequency.

\[
\nu>\nu_0
\]

---

## Law 2

Photoelectric current is directly proportional to intensity of incident light.

---

## Law 3

Maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons depends on frequency, not intensity.

---

## Law 4

Photoelectric emission is instantaneous.

---

# 8. Einstein's Photoelectric Equation

Einstein explained photoelectric effect using photon theory.

---

Energy conservation:

\[
Photon\ Energy=
Work\ Function+
Maximum\ Kinetic\ Energy
\]

---

Formula:

\[
h\nu=\phi+K_{max}
\]

---

Since:

\[
K_{max}=\frac12mv^2
\]

Therefore:

\[
h\nu=\phi+\frac12mv^2
\]

---

# In Terms of Stopping Potential

\[
K_{max}=eV_0
\]

Therefore:

\[
h\nu=\phi+eV_0
\]

---

# 10. Failure of Wave Theory of Light

Classical wave theory failed to explain:

1. Threshold frequency
2. Instantaneous emission
3. Dependence of kinetic energy on frequency

---

# 11. Matter Waves

## de Broglie Hypothesis

According to de Broglie:

Every moving particle has an associated wave.

---

Matter has:

```
Particle nature + Wave nature
```

---

# 12. de Broglie Wavelength

Formula:

\[
\lambda=\frac hp
\]

---

Since:

\[
p=mv
\]

Therefore:

\[
\lambda=\frac h{mv}
\]

---

Where:

- λ = Wavelength
- h = Planck constant
- m = Mass
- v = Velocity

---

# 13. de Broglie Wavelength for Electron

For electron accelerated through potential V:

\[
\lambda=
\frac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}}
\]

---

Approximate formula:

\[
\lambda=
\frac{12.27}{\sqrt V}
\mathring{A}
\]

---

# 14. Davisson-Germer Experiment

## Purpose

To prove wave nature of electrons.

---

Experiment:

Electrons are scattered by nickel crystal.

---

Observation:

Diffraction pattern was obtained.

---

Conclusion:

```
Electrons show wave nature
```

---

# 15. Matter Wave Properties

1. Associated with moving particles.
2. Independent of charge.
3. Wavelength depends on momentum.
4. Significant for microscopic particles.

---

# 16. Wave-Particle Duality

## Definition

The concept that particles and waves exhibit both wave-like and particle-like properties.

---

Examples:

| Radiation | Wave Nature  | Particle Nature |
| --------- | ------------ | --------------- |
| Light     | Interference | Photon          |
| Electron  | Diffraction  | Particle        |

- Forgetting threshold frequency concept.
- Mixing photon energy and kinetic energy.
