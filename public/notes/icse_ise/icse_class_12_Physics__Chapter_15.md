Examples:

- Diodes

# 1. Semiconductor

## Definition

A semiconductor is a material whose electrical conductivity lies between conductors and insulators.

---

Examples:

- Silicon (Si)
- Germanium (Ge)

---

# Comparison of Materials

| Conductors        | Semiconductors      | Insulators            |
| ----------------- | ------------------- | --------------------- |
| High conductivity | Medium conductivity | Very low conductivity |
| Copper            | Silicon             | Rubber                |

# 4. Intrinsic Semiconductor

## Definition

A pure semiconductor without impurities is called an intrinsic semiconductor.

---

Examples:

- Pure Silicon
- Pure Germanium

---

Characteristics:

- Number of electrons = Number of holes

\[
n_e=n_h
\]

---

# 5. Extrinsic Semiconductor

## Definition

A semiconductor whose conductivity is increased by adding impurities is called an extrinsic semiconductor.

---

Process:

```
Doping
```

---

# 6. Doping

## Definition

The process of adding a small amount of impurity to a pure semiconductor is called doping.

---

Types:

```
Extrinsic Semiconductor

          │

 ┌────────┴────────┐

 ▼                 ▼
```

# 7. n-Type Semiconductor

## Definition

A semiconductor obtained by adding pentavalent impurity is called n-type semiconductor.

---

Examples of impurities:

- Phosphorus
- Arsenic
- Antimony

---

Pentavalent atoms have:

```
5 valence electrons
```

---

Extra electron becomes:

```
Free electron
```

---

Majority carriers:

```
Electrons
```

---

Minority carriers:

```
Holes
```

---

# 8. p-Type Semiconductor

## Definition

A semiconductor obtained by adding trivalent impurity is called p-type semiconductor.

---

Examples:

- Boron
- Aluminium
- Gallium

---

Trivalent atoms have:

```
3 valence electrons
```

---

Creates:

```
Holes
```

---

Majority carriers:

```
Holes
```

---

Minority carriers:

```
Electrons
```

---

# 9. p-n Junction

## Definition

The boundary formed when p-type and n-type semiconductors are joined is called p-n junction.

---

# Formation of Depletion Region

When p and n regions join:

- Electrons diffuse from n-side to p-side.
- Holes diffuse from p-side to n-side.

A region without free charge carriers is formed.

Called:

```
Depletion layer
```

---

# 10. Barrier Potential

## Definition

The potential difference developed across the depletion region is called barrier potential.

---

Values:

Silicon:

\[
0.7V
\]

Germanium:

\[
0.3V
\]

---

```
 ▼   ▼
```

---

# 12. Forward Bias

Connection:

p-side → Positive terminal

n-side → Negative terminal

---

Effect:

- Depletion width decreases.
- Current increases.

---

# 13. Reverse Bias

Connection:

p-side → Negative terminal

n-side → Positive terminal

---

Effect:

- Depletion width increases.
- Very small current flows.

---

# 14. p-n Junction Diode

---

# 15. V-I Characteristics of Diode

## Forward Bias

Initially no current.

After threshold voltage:

Current increases rapidly.

---

## Reverse Bias

Small reverse current flows.

At breakdown voltage:

Current increases suddenly.

# 23. Structure of Transistor

A transistor has three regions:

```

Base

Collector
```

---

# Functions

## Emitter

Supplies charge carriers.

---

## Base

Controls current.

---

## Collector

Collects charge carriers.

---

# 24. Transistor Action

For amplification:

- Emitter-base junction is forward biased.
- Collector-base junction is reverse biased.

---

# 25. Current Relations in Transistor

\[
I_E=I_C+I_B
\]

---

Current Gain:

\[
\beta=\frac{I_C}{I_B}
\]

---

Where:

- IE = Emitter current
- IC = Collector current
- IB = Base current

---

# 26. Transistor as Amplifier

---

# Board Important Questions

## 1. What is doping?

**Answer:**

The process of adding a small amount of impurity to a pure semiconductor to increase conductivity is called doping.

---

## 2. Differentiate p-type and n-type semiconductors.

| p-type                      | n-type                          |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Trivalent impurity          | Pentavalent impurity            |
| Holes are majority carriers | Electrons are majority carriers |

---

## 3. Explain p-n junction diode.

**Answer:**

A p-n junction diode is a semiconductor device that allows current flow mainly in one direction.

---

## 4. Explain Zener diode.

**Answer:**

A Zener diode is a diode designed to operate in reverse breakdown region and maintain constant voltage.

---

## 5. Explain transistor action.

**Answer:**

A transistor amplifies signals by controlling collector current using a small base current.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing p-type and n-type carriers.
- Forgetting diode bias conditions.
- Mixing LED and photodiode functions.
- Forgetting transistor current relation.
- Confusing NAND and NOR gates.
