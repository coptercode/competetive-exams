# Introduction

Plants require a continuous supply of **water, minerals, and organic food** for growth, metabolism, and survival. The transport system in plants consists of specialized tissues—**xylem** and **phloem**—which transport water, minerals, and food to different parts of the plant.

Transport in plants mainly involves:

- Diffusion
- Facilitated Diffusion
- Active Transport
- Osmosis
- Water Absorption
- Ascent of Sap
- Transpiration
- Translocation of Food

---

# 1. Means of Transport

Plants transport substances through both passive and active mechanisms.

```
           Transport
               │
    ┌──────────┼──────────┐
    ▼          ▼          ▼

```

---

# 2. Diffusion

## Definition

**Diffusion** is the movement of molecules from a region of **higher concentration to lower concentration** until equilibrium is reached.

---

## Characteristics

- Passive process
- No ATP required
- Occurs in gases, liquids, and solids
- Continues until equilibrium

---

## Examples

- Exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide through stomata
- Diffusion of gases during respiration

---

# Factors Affecting Diffusion

- Concentration gradient
- Temperature
- Surface area
- Size of molecules
- Distance

---

# 3. Facilitated Diffusion

## Definition

Movement of molecules across membranes with the help of **carrier proteins** or **channel proteins**.

---

## Characteristics

- Passive process
- No ATP required
- Specific transport proteins involved

---

## Examples

- Glucose transport
- Ion channels

---

# Difference Between Diffusion and Facilitated Diffusion

| Diffusion             | Facilitated Diffusion    |
| --------------------- | ------------------------ |
| No transport proteins | Requires proteins        |
| Simple movement       | Carrier/channel mediated |
| Less specific         | Highly specific          |

---

# 4. Active Transport

## Definition

Active transport is the movement of substances **against the concentration gradient** using metabolic energy (ATP).

---

## Characteristics

- Requires ATP
- Carrier proteins involved
- Highly selective

---

## Examples

- Mineral absorption by root hairs
- Ion transport

---

# Difference Between Passive and Active Transport

| Passive Transport        | Active Transport         |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| No ATP required          | ATP required             |
| High → Low concentration | Low → High concentration |
| Simple movement          | Carrier-mediated         |

---

# 5. Osmosis

## Definition

Osmosis is the movement of water molecules through a **selectively permeable membrane** from a region of **higher water potential** to a region of **lower water potential**.

---

## Conditions Necessary

- Selectively permeable membrane
- Difference in water potential

---

# Types of Solutions

## Hypotonic Solution

- Water enters the cell.
- Cell becomes turgid.

---

## Hypertonic Solution

- Water leaves the cell.
- Cell shrinks.

---

## Isotonic Solution

- No net movement of water.

---

# Plasmolysis

## Definition

Shrinkage of the protoplast due to loss of water in a hypertonic solution.

---

# Deplasmolysis

## Definition

Recovery of a plasmolysed cell when placed in a hypotonic solution.

---

# Difference Between Plasmolysis and Deplasmolysis

| Plasmolysis         | Deplasmolysis      |
| ------------------- | ------------------ |
| Water leaves cell   | Water enters cell  |
| Cell shrinks        | Cell regains size  |
| Hypertonic solution | Hypotonic solution |

---

# 6. Water Potential

## Definition

Water potential (Ψ) is the potential energy of water relative to pure water.

Pure water has

```
Ψ = 0
```

---

# Components of Water Potential

```
Water Potential (Ψ)

↓

Solute Potential (Ψs)

+

Pressure Potential (Ψp)
```

Formula

```
Ψ = Ψs + Ψp
```

---

## Apoplast Pathway

---

## Symplast Pathway

# 8. Ascent of Sap

## Definition

Upward movement of water and dissolved minerals from roots to aerial parts through xylem.

---

# Theories

## Root Pressure Theory

Pushes water upward for short distances.

---

## Cohesion-Tension Theory

Proposed by

```
Dixon and Joly
```

### Explanation

- Cohesion between water molecules.
- Adhesion to xylem walls.
- Transpiration creates tension.
- Water column pulled upward.

This is the **most accepted theory**.

---

# 9. Transpiration

## Definition

Loss of water in the form of water vapour from the aerial parts of plants.

---

# Types of Transpiration

```
Transpiration
      │
 ┌────┼─────────┐
 ▼    ▼         ▼
Stomatal
Cuticular
Lenticular
```

---

## A. Stomatal Transpiration

Occurs through stomata.

Accounts for about **90–95%** of total transpiration.

---

## B. Cuticular Transpiration

Occurs through cuticle.

---

## C. Lenticular Transpiration

Occurs through lenticels.

---

# Functions of Transpiration

- Produces transpiration pull
- Cooling effect
- Mineral transport
- Maintains water balance
- Prevents overheating

---

# Factors Affecting Transpiration

## External Factors

- Temperature
- Wind
- Humidity
- Light

---

## Internal Factors

- Number of stomata
- Leaf area
- Cuticle thickness

---

# Stomatal Mechanism

```
Guard Cells

↓

Water enters

↓

Guard cells become turgid

↓

Stomata open
```

When guard cells lose water, stomata close.

---

# Difference Between Open and Closed Stomata

| Open Stomata         | Closed Stomata        |
| -------------------- | --------------------- |
| Guard cells turgid   | Guard cells flaccid   |
| Gas exchange occurs  | Gas exchange reduced  |
| Transpiration active | Transpiration reduced |

---

# 10. Translocation of Food

## Definition

Movement of prepared food from leaves to storage and growing regions through **phloem**.

---

# Pressure Flow Hypothesis

Proposed by

```
Münch
```

---

## Mechanism

```
Leaves (Source)

↓

Sucrose enters Phloem

↓

Pressure develops

↓

Food moves

↓

Storage Organ (Sink)
```

---

# Source

Produces food.

Examples

- Mature leaves

---

# Sink

Uses or stores food.

Examples

- Roots
- Fruits
- Seeds

---

# Difference Between Xylem and Phloem Transport

| Xylem                        | Phloem                  |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Water transport              | Food transport          |
| Mostly upward                | Bidirectional           |
| Dead tissue                  | Living tissue           |
| Driven by transpiration pull | Driven by pressure flow |

---

# Difference Between Apoplast and Symplast Pathway

| Apoplast                   | Symplast                     |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Cell wall pathway          | Cytoplasmic pathway          |
| Faster                     | Slower                       |
| No plasma membrane crossed | Plasma membrane crossed once |
| Less regulated             | Highly regulated             |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **diffusion** with **osmosis**.
- Assuming active transport occurs without ATP.
- Mixing up xylem and phloem functions.
- Confusing apoplast and symplast pathways.
- Forgetting that transpiration mainly occurs through stomata.
- Mixing up plasmolysis and deplasmolysis.

# Chapter Summary

- **Transport in Plants** involves the movement of water, minerals, and food through specialized tissues and transport mechanisms.
- Water and minerals are absorbed by **root hairs** and move through **apoplast** and **symplast** pathways before entering the xylem.
- The **Cohesion-Tension Theory** best explains the ascent of sap, where transpiration creates a pulling force that lifts water through xylem vessels.
- **Transpiration** plays a vital role in cooling the plant, maintaining water balance, and facilitating mineral transport, with stomata accounting for most water loss.
- Food prepared during photosynthesis is transported through **phloem** from **sources** (leaves) to **sinks** (growing and storage organs) according to the **Pressure Flow Hypothesis**.
- Understanding plant transport mechanisms is fundamental to **plant physiology, agriculture, irrigation practices, crop improvement, and environmental conservation**.
