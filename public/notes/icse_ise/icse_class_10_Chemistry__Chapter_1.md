# Introduction

The **Periodic Table** is a systematic arrangement of all known elements based on their **atomic numbers**. Elements are arranged in such a way that those with similar chemical properties are placed in the same group. The modern periodic table helps predict the physical and chemical properties of elements based on periodic trends such as **atomic radius, ionisation potential, electron affinity, and electronegativity**.

---

# 1. Modern Periodic Table

## Definition

The **Modern Periodic Table** is the arrangement of elements in increasing order of their **atomic numbers**.

---

## Features

- Contains **118 elements**.
- Arranged into **7 periods** (horizontal rows).
- Arranged into **18 groups** (vertical columns).
- Elements in the same group have similar chemical properties.
- Properties repeat periodically due to similar electronic configurations.

---

# 2. Periodic Properties

## Definition

**Periodic Properties** are the properties of elements that change in a regular pattern across periods and down groups.

---

# 3. Atomic Radius

## Definition

**Atomic Radius** is the distance from the centre of the nucleus to the outermost electron shell.

---

## Trend Across a Period

- **Decreases from left to right.**

### Reason

The nuclear charge increases while the number of electron shells remains the same, pulling electrons closer to the nucleus.

---

## Trend Down a Group

- **Increases from top to bottom.**

### Reason

Each successive element has an additional electron shell, increasing the size of the atom.

---

# 4. Ionisation Potential (Ionisation Energy)

## Definition

**Ionisation Potential (IP)** is the minimum energy required to remove the most loosely bound electron from an isolated gaseous atom.

---

## Trend Across a Period

- **Increases from left to right.**

### Reason

The atomic size decreases and nuclear attraction increases, making it harder to remove an electron.

---

## Trend Down a Group

- **Decreases from top to bottom.**

### Reason

The outermost electron is farther from the nucleus and is less strongly attracted.

---

# 5. Electron Affinity

## Definition

**Electron Affinity (EA)** is the amount of energy released when a neutral gaseous atom gains an electron.

---

## Trend Across a Period

- Generally **increases** from left to right.

---

## Trend Down a Group

- Generally **decreases** from top to bottom.

---

## Note

Halogens have **high electron affinity** because they readily gain one electron to complete their octet.

---

# 6. Electronegativity

## Definition

**Electronegativity** is the tendency of an atom to attract the shared pair of electrons towards itself in a covalent bond.

---

## Trend Across a Period

- **Increases from left to right.**

---

## Trend Down a Group

- **Decreases from top to bottom.**

---

## Most Electronegative Element

```
Fluorine (F)
```

---

# 7. Metallic and Non-Metallic Character

## Metallic Character

The tendency of an element to lose electrons and form positive ions.

### Trend

- Decreases across a period.
- Increases down a group.

---

## Non-Metallic Character

The tendency of an element to gain electrons.

### Trend

- Increases across a period.
- Decreases down a group.

---

# 8. Valency

## Definition

**Valency** is the combining capacity of an atom.

---

## Across a Period

```
1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 3 → 2 → 1 → 0
```

---

## Down a Group

Valency generally remains **constant**.

---

# 9. Periodic Trends Summary

| Property               | Across a Period     | Down a Group        |
| ---------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------- |
| Atomic Radius          | Decreases           | Increases           |
| Ionisation Potential   | Increases           | Decreases           |
| Electron Affinity      | Generally Increases | Generally Decreases |
| Electronegativity      | Increases           | Decreases           |
| Metallic Character     | Decreases           | Increases           |
| Non-metallic Character | Increases           | Decreases           |

---

# Difference Between Atomic Radius and Ionic Radius

| Atomic Radius            | Ionic Radius                         |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Size of a neutral atom   | Size of an ion                       |
| Depends on atomic shells | Depends on gain or loss of electrons |
| Used for neutral atoms   | Used for cations and anions          |

# Applications

Knowledge of periodic properties is useful in:

- Designing new materials.
- Studying chemical reactions.
- Explaining properties of compounds.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Why does atomic radius decrease across a period?

### Solution

The number of protons increases while electrons are added to the same shell, increasing nuclear attraction.

### Answer

Atomic radius decreases because the increasing nuclear charge pulls electrons closer to the nucleus.

---

## Example 2

### Question

Which element has the highest electronegativity?

### Solution

Among all elements, one has the strongest attraction for shared electrons.

### Answer

```
Fluorine (F)
```

---

## Example 3

### Question

Why does ionisation potential decrease down a group?

### Solution

Additional electron shells increase the distance between the nucleus and the outermost electron.

### Answer

The outermost electron is farther from the nucleus and can be removed more easily.

---

## Example 4

### Question

State the trend of metallic character across a period.

### Solution

Elements become less likely to lose electrons as we move from left to right.

### Answer

Metallic character **decreases** across a period.

---

## Example 5

### Question

What is electron affinity?

### Solution

It measures the tendency of an atom to accept an electron.

### Answer

The energy released when a neutral gaseous atom gains an electron.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **atomic number** with **atomic mass**.
- Mixing up **electron affinity** and **electronegativity**.
- Assuming atomic radius increases across a period (it decreases).
- Forgetting that **fluorine** has the highest electronegativity.
- Confusing ionisation potential with electron affinity.
- Assuming metallic character increases across a period.
- Forgetting that valency generally remains constant within a group.

# Chapter Summary

- The **Modern Periodic Table** arranges elements according to increasing **atomic number**, with **7 periods** and **18 groups**.
- **Periodic properties** show regular trends due to changes in atomic structure and electronic configuration.
- **Atomic radius** decreases across a period because of increasing nuclear charge and increases down a group due to the addition of electron shells.
- **Ionisation potential**, **electron affinity**, and **electronegativity** generally increase across a period and decrease down a group.
- **Metallic character** decreases across a period but increases down a group, while **non-metallic character** shows the opposite trend.
- Understanding these periodic trends helps predict the **reactivity, bonding behaviour, and chemical properties** of elements, making the periodic table a powerful tool in chemistry.
