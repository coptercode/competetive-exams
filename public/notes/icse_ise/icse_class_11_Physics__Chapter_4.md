# Introduction

**Work, Energy, and Power** are fundamental concepts in mechanics that explain how forces transfer energy and produce motion. Work is done when a force causes displacement, energy is the capacity to do work, and power is the rate at which work is performed.

The **Work-Energy Theorem** and the **Law of Conservation of Energy** are among the most important principles in physics and engineering.

---

# 1. Work

## Definition

Work is said to be done when a force acting on a body produces displacement in the direction of the force.

---

# Formula

```
W = F s cosθ
```

Where

- W = Work
- F = Force
- s = Displacement
- θ = Angle between force and displacement

---

# SI Unit

```
Joule (J)
```

---

# Dimensional Formula

```
[ML²T⁻²]
```

---

# Types of Work

## Positive Work

Force and displacement are in the same direction.

```
θ = 0°

W = +Fs
```

---

## Negative Work

Force and displacement are in opposite directions.

```
θ = 180°

W = -Fs
```

---

## Zero Work

Force is perpendicular to displacement.

```
θ = 90°

W = 0
```

---

# Difference Between Positive and Negative Work

| Positive Work                         | Negative Work       |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Force and displacement same direction | Opposite directions |
| Energy supplied                       | Energy removed      |
| Example: Pulling a cart               | Friction            |

---

# Variable Force

When force is not constant,

```
W = ∫F·dx
```

Area under the Force–Displacement graph gives the work done.

---

# Force-Displacement Graph

```
Force
 │
 │      /|
 │     / |
 │    /  |
 │___/___|____ Displacement

Area = Work Done
```

# 2. Energy

## Definition

Energy is the capacity of a body to do work.

---

# SI Unit

```
Joule (J)
```

---

# Types of Energy

- Kinetic Energy
- Potential Energy
- Thermal Energy
- Chemical Energy
- Electrical Energy
- Nuclear Energy
- Light Energy

---

# Difference Between Kinetic and Potential Energy

| Kinetic Energy      | Potential Energy                   |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Due to motion       | Due to position                    |
| Depends on velocity | Depends on height or configuration |

---

# 3. Kinetic Energy

## Definition

Energy possessed by a body due to its motion.

---

# Formula

```
KE = ½mv²
```

---

# Derivation

From

```
W = Fs
```

Using

```
F = ma
```

and

```
v² = u² + 2as
```

For

```
u = 0
```

```
W = ½mv²
```

Therefore,

```
KE = ½mv²
```

---

# Dimensional Formula

```
[ML²T⁻²]
```

---

# Dependence

- Directly proportional to mass.
- Proportional to square of velocity.

---

# 4. Potential Energy

## Definition

Energy possessed by a body due to its position or configuration.

---

# Gravitational Potential Energy

```
PE = mgh
```

Where

- m = Mass
- g = Acceleration due to gravity
- h = Height

---

# Elastic Potential Energy

Energy stored in a stretched or compressed spring.

---

## Hooke's Law

Within elastic limit,

```
F = kx
```

Where

- k = Spring constant
- x = Extension or compression

---

## Elastic Potential Energy

```
U = ½kx²
```

---

# Difference Between Gravitational and Elastic Potential Energy

| Gravitational PE  | Elastic PE             |
| ----------------- | ---------------------- |
| Depends on height | Depends on deformation |
| Formula: mgh      | Formula: ½kx²          |

---

# 5. Work-Energy Theorem

## Statement

The net work done on a body is equal to the change in its kinetic energy.

---

# Mathematical Form

```
W = ΔKE
```

or

```
W = KE₂ − KE₁
```

---

# Importance

- Relates force with energy.
- Simplifies motion analysis.
- Used in engineering and mechanics.

---

# 6. Conservative Forces

## Definition

A force is conservative if the work done depends only on the initial and final positions, not on the path followed.

---

# Examples

- Gravitational force
- Electrostatic force
- Spring force

---

# Properties

- Work over a closed path is zero.
- Potential energy can be defined.

---

# Non-Conservative Forces

Examples

- Friction
- Air resistance
- Viscous force

---

# Difference Between Conservative and Non-Conservative Forces

| Conservative         | Non-Conservative     |
| -------------------- | -------------------- |
| Path independent     | Path dependent       |
| Closed path work = 0 | Closed path work ≠ 0 |
| PE can be defined    | PE cannot be defined |

---

# 7. Law of Conservation of Energy

## Statement

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed but can only be transformed from one form to another.

---

# Mathematical Form

```
Total Energy

=

Constant
```

---

# Example

A freely falling body.

```
Top

PE Maximum

KE Zero

↓

Middle

PE ↓

KE ↑

↓

Ground

PE Zero

KE Maximum
```

---

# Mechanical Energy

```
Mechanical Energy

=

KE + PE
```

---

## Definition

---

# Formula

```

```

---

# SI Unit

```
Watt (W)
```

---

# Average Power

```
Average Power

=

Work

──────

Time
```

---

# Instantaneous Power

```

```

---

# Horsepower


# Difference Between Work and Power

| Work               | Power                   |

---

# Efficiency

## Definition

Efficiency is the ratio of useful output work to input work.

---

# Formula

```
η

=

Useful Output

────────────

Input

×100%
```

---

# 9. Collisions

## Definition

A collision is an event in which two bodies exert forces on each other for a short duration.

---

# Types

### Elastic Collision

- Momentum conserved.
- Kinetic energy conserved.

---

### Inelastic Collision

- Momentum conserved.
- Kinetic energy not conserved.

---

### Perfectly Inelastic Collision

Bodies stick together after collision.

---

# Coefficient of Restitution

```
e

=

Relative Speed of Separation

────────────────────────

Relative Speed of Approach
```

---

# Difference Between Elastic and Inelastic Collision

| Elastic            | Inelastic          |
| ------------------ | ------------------ |
| Momentum conserved | Momentum conserved |
| KE conserved       | KE decreases       |
| e = 1              | 0 < e < 1          |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A force of 20 N moves a body through 5 m in the same direction. Find the work done.

### Solution

```
W = Fs cos0°

= 20 × 5

= 100 J
```

### Answer

**100 J**

---

## Example 2

### Question

Find the kinetic energy of a 4 kg body moving with a speed of 6 m s⁻¹.

### Solution

```
KE = ½mv²

= ½ × 4 × 6²

= 72 J
```

### Answer

**72 J**

---

## Example 3

### Question

A 2 kg body is raised to a height of 10 m. Find its potential energy (g = 9.8 m s⁻²).

### Solution

```
PE = mgh

= 2 × 9.8 × 10

= 196 J
```

### Answer

**196 J**

---

## Example 4

### Question

A machine performs 500 J of work in 10 s. Find its power.

### Solution

```
P = W/t

= 500/10

= 50 W
```

### Answer

**50 W**

---

## Example 5

### Question

A spring of spring constant 200 N m⁻¹ is compressed by 0.1 m. Find the energy stored.

### Solution

```
U = ½kx²

= ½ × 200 × (0.1)²

= 1 J
```

### Answer

**1 J**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **force** with **work**.
- Ignoring the angle θ in the work formula.
- Using **mgh** instead of **½mv²** or vice versa.
- Forgetting the negative sign in **F = -kx**.
- Assuming energy is lost instead of transformed.
- Confusing power with energy.

# Chapter Summary

- **Work** is done when a force produces displacement, while **energy** is the ability to perform work and **power** measures the rate of doing work.
- The **work-energy theorem** states that the net work done on a body equals the change in its kinetic energy, linking force and motion.
- **Potential energy** may be gravitational or elastic, with elastic systems obeying **Hooke's Law** within their elastic limits.
- **Conservative forces** conserve mechanical energy, whereas **non-conservative forces** such as friction convert mechanical energy into other forms.
- The **law of conservation of energy** is a universal principle stating that energy can only be transformed, not created or destroyed.
- These concepts are widely applied in engineering, transportation, power generation, sports, and modern technology, forming the basis for advanced mechanics and thermodynamics.
