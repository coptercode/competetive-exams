# Introduction

**Evolution** is the gradual change in organisms over generations that results in the formation of new species.

It explains:

- Origin of life
- Diversity of organisms
- Adaptation
- Species formation

---

# 1. Origin of Life

## Definition

The origin of life refers to the appearance of the first living organisms on Earth.

---

# Theories of Origin of Life

```
              Origin of Life

                    │

      ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐

      ▼             ▼             ▼

Special Creation  Spontaneous   Chemical Evolution

                  Generation
```

---

# 2. Special Creation Theory

## Concept

Life was created by a supernatural power.

---

# Limitations

- No scientific evidence.
- Does not explain evolution.

---

# 3. Spontaneous Generation Theory

## Concept

Life originated suddenly from non-living matter.

Example:

Old belief:

```
Maggots arise from decaying meat
```

---

# Disproved By:

## Louis Pasteur

His experiments proved that life comes only from pre-existing life.

---

# 4. Chemical Evolution Theory

Proposed by:

## Oparin and Haldane

---

# Concept

Life originated through gradual chemical changes on primitive Earth.

---

# Primitive Earth Conditions

Atmosphere contained:

- Methane (CH₄)
- Ammonia (NH₃)
- Hydrogen (H₂)
- Water vapour

No free oxygen.

---

# Miller-Urey Experiment

## Scientists:

Stanley Miller and Harold Urey

---

# Experiment

They created primitive Earth conditions in laboratory.

Used:

- Methane
- Ammonia
- Hydrogen
- Water vapour
- Electric sparks

---

# Result

Formation of:

- Amino acids
- Organic molecules

---

# Conclusion

Organic molecules could form naturally under early Earth conditions.

---

# 6. Fossil Evidence

## Definition

Fossils are preserved remains or impressions of ancient organisms.

---

# Importance

They show:

- Ancient life forms
- Evolutionary changes
- Extinct organisms

---

# Fossil Examples

## Archaeopteryx

Shows connection between:

```
Reptiles + Birds
```

---

# Fossil Record

Shows gradual changes in organisms over time.

---

# 7. Anatomical Evidence

Based on comparison of body structures.

---

# Types

## Homologous Organs

Structures having:

- Same origin
- Different functions

---

Examples:

Forelimbs of:

- Human
- Whale
- Bat

---

# Significance

Shows common ancestry.

---

# Analogous Organs

Structures having:

- Different origin
- Same function

---

Examples:

Wings of:

- Birds
- Butterflies

---

# Significance

Shows adaptation.

---

# Vestigial Organs

Reduced structures with little or no function.

Examples:

- Appendix
- Wisdom teeth
- Coccyx

---

# 8. Embryological Evidence

Embryos of different vertebrates show similarities during early development.

---

Example:

Fish and human embryos show similarities in early stages.

---

# 9. Molecular Evidence

Based on comparison of:

- DNA
- Proteins

---

Greater similarity indicates closer evolutionary relationship.

---

# 10. Theories of Evolution

Major theories:

1. Lamarckism
2. Darwinism
3. Mutation Theory
4. Modern Synthetic Theory

---

# 11. Lamarckism

Proposed by:

## Jean Baptiste Lamarck

---

# Theory of Inheritance of Acquired Characters

Organisms acquire traits during life and pass them to offspring.

---

# Example

Giraffe neck evolution.

---

# Explanation

Ancestors stretched neck to reach leaves.

Longer neck acquired and inherited.

---

# Limitations

Acquired characters are not inherited genetically.

---

# 12. Darwinism

Proposed by:

## Charles Darwin

Book:

```
On the Origin of Species
```

(1859)

---

# Theory of Natural Selection

Nature selects organisms with favourable variations.

---

# Main Points

## Overproduction

Organisms produce more offspring than can survive.

---

## Struggle for Existence

Competition occurs for:

- Food
- Space
- Resources

---

## Variation

Individuals show differences.

---

## Survival of the Fittest

Individuals with favourable traits survive and reproduce.

---

## Natural Selection

Useful variations accumulate over generations.

---

# 13. Natural Selection Example

## Industrial Melanism

Example:

Peppered moth

---

Before industrialization:

Light moths survived.

---

After pollution:

Dark moths survived due to camouflage.

---

# 14. Mutation Theory

Proposed by:

## Hugo de Vries

---

# Concept

Evolution occurs due to sudden genetic changes called mutations.

---

# Example

Mutation in plants causing new varieties.

---

# 15. Modern Synthetic Theory

Combines:

- Darwin's natural selection
- Mendelian genetics

---

# Factors Responsible for Evolution

## 1. Mutation

Creates new variations.

---

## 2. Recombination

Produces new gene combinations.

---

## 3. Natural Selection

Selects favourable variations.

---

## 4. Genetic Drift

Random changes in gene frequency.

---

## 5. Gene Flow

Movement of genes between populations.

---

# 16. Hardy-Weinberg Principle

## Definition

A population remains genetically stable if evolutionary forces are absent.

---

# Equation

\[
p^2+2pq+q^2=1
\]

where:

- p = frequency of one allele
- q = frequency of another allele

---

# Conditions for Genetic Equilibrium

- No mutation
- No migration
- Random mating
- Large population
- No natural selection

---

# Factors Affecting Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium

```
Evolutionary Forces

        │

 ┌──────┼──────┐

 ▼      ▼      ▼

Mutation Selection Migration

        │

        ▼

 Genetic Drift
```

---

# 17. Adaptive Radiation

## Definition

Evolution of different species from a common ancestor in a geographical area.

---

# Example

Darwin's finches of Galapagos Islands.

---

# Australian Marsupials

Many marsupials evolved from a common ancestor.

---

# 18. Convergent Evolution

## Definition

Different organisms develop similar characteristics due to similar environmental conditions.

---

Example:

- Shark
- Dolphin

Both have streamlined bodies.

---

# 19. Divergent Evolution

## Definition

Organisms from a common ancestor develop different characteristics.

---

Example:

Mammalian forelimbs.

---

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. Explain Darwin's theory of natural selection.

**Answer:**

Darwin proposed that organisms produce variations and individuals with favourable variations survive and reproduce, causing evolutionary change.

---

## 2. Differentiate homologous and analogous organs.

| Homologous                | Analogous                  |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Same origin               | Different origin           |
| Different functions       | Same function              |
| Shows divergent evolution | Shows convergent evolution |

---

## 3. State Hardy-Weinberg equation.

**Answer:**

\[
p^2+2pq+q^2=1
\]

---

## 4. What is adaptive radiation?

**Answer:**

Evolution of different species from a common ancestor in a particular geographical region.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing Lamarckism with Darwinism.
- Mixing homologous and analogous organs.
- Forgetting Hardy-Weinberg conditions.
- Writing mutation as always harmful.
- Confusing convergent and divergent evolution.

---

# Quick Revision

- Evolution = gradual change over generations.
- Miller-Urey proved chemical evolution possibility.
- Darwin proposed natural selection.
- Lamarck proposed acquired characters.
- Homologous organs → divergent evolution.
- Analogous organs → convergent evolution.
- Hardy-Weinberg equation:

\[
p^2+2pq+q^2=1
\]

- Human evolution ends with:

```
Homo sapiens
```

# Chapter Summary

- **Evolution** explains the origin and diversity of life through gradual genetic changes.
- Evidence from fossils, anatomy, embryology, and molecular biology supports evolutionary theory.
- Darwin's natural selection explains how favourable variations are preserved.
- Modern evolutionary theory combines genetics with natural selection.
- Adaptive radiation, genetic changes, and natural selection lead to biodiversity.
- Human evolution represents millions of years of gradual biological and cultural development.
