# Introduction

**Electrolysis** is the chemical decomposition of an electrolyte by passing an electric current through its molten or aqueous solution. It is based on the movement of **ions** toward oppositely charged electrodes. Electrolysis is widely used in **electroplating, electro-refining, extraction of metals, and industrial chemical production**.

---

# 1. Electrolysis

## Definition

**Electrolysis** is the process of chemical decomposition of an electrolyte by the passage of electric current.

---

## Conditions Required

- An electrolyte.
- Two electrodes.
- A source of direct current (DC).

---

# 2. Electrolyte

## Definition

An **Electrolyte** is a substance that conducts electricity in its molten or aqueous state due to the presence of free ions.

---

## Examples

- Sodium chloride solution
- Copper sulphate solution
- Dilute sulfuric acid
- Molten lead bromide

---

# 3. Non-Electrolyte

## Definition

A **Non-Electrolyte** is a substance that does not conduct electricity because it does not produce ions.

---

## Examples

- Sugar solution
- Alcohol
- Distilled water
- Benzene

---

# 4. Electrolytic Cell

## Definition

An **Electrolytic Cell** is a device in which electrical energy is converted into chemical energy through electrolysis.

---

## Components

- Battery (DC source)
- Electrolyte
- Cathode
- Anode
- Connecting wires

---

# 5. Electrodes

---

## Cathode

### Definition

The **Cathode** is the **negative electrode** connected to the negative terminal of the battery.

### At the Cathode

- Cations move towards it.
- Reduction takes place.
- Electrons are gained.

---

## Anode

### Definition

The **Anode** is the **positive electrode** connected to the positive terminal of the battery.

### At the Anode

- Anions move towards it.
- Oxidation takes place.
- Electrons are lost.

---

# 6. Ions

---

## Cations

Positively charged ions.

### Examples

- H⁺
- Cu²⁺
- Na⁺
- Ag⁺

Move towards the **Cathode**.

---

## Anions

Negatively charged ions.

### Examples

- Cl⁻
- OH⁻
- SO₄²⁻
- NO₃⁻

Move towards the **Anode**.

---

# 7. Characteristics of Electrolytes

---

## Strong Electrolytes

### Definition

Completely ionize in solution.

### Examples

- HCl
- NaOH
- H₂SO₄
- NaCl

---

## Weak Electrolytes

### Definition

Partially ionize in solution.

### Examples

- CH₃COOH
- NH₄OH

---

## Non-Electrolytes

Do not ionize.

### Examples

- Sugar
- Alcohol
- Urea

---

# 8. Preferential Discharge of Ions

## Definition

When more than one ion is present, the ion discharged first at an electrode is determined by its **position in the electrochemical series** and **concentration**.

---

## Factors Affecting Preferential Discharge

- Electrochemical series.
- Concentration of ions.
- Nature of electrodes.

---

## General Rule

- Less reactive cations are discharged first at the cathode.
- Less reactive anions are discharged first at the anode.

---

# 9. Electrolysis of Acidified Water

## Electrolyte

Dilute sulfuric acid.

---

## At Cathode

```
2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂↑
```

Hydrogen gas is liberated.

---

## At Anode

```
2H₂O → O₂↑ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻
```

Oxygen gas is liberated.

---

## Overall Reaction

```
2H₂O

↓

2H₂ + O₂
```

---

# 10. Electrolysis of Copper Sulphate Solution

## Using Copper Electrodes

---

### Cathode

```
Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu
```

Copper is deposited.

---

### Anode

```
Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻
```

Copper dissolves.

---

## Observation

- Cathode gains mass.
- Anode loses mass.
- Blue colour remains unchanged.

---

# 11. Electroplating

## Definition

**Electroplating** is the process of coating one metal with a thin layer of another metal by electrolysis.

---

## Purpose

- Prevent corrosion.
- Improve appearance.
- Increase durability.
- Reduce wear and tear.

---

## Examples

- Silver plating.
- Gold plating.
- Chromium plating.
- Nickel plating.

---

## Electroplating of Copper

### Cathode

Object to be plated.

### Anode

Pure copper.

### Electrolyte

Copper sulphate solution.

---

# 12. Electro-Refining of Copper

## Definition

**Electro-Refining** is the purification of an impure metal by electrolysis.

---

## Setup

### Anode

Impure copper.

### Cathode

Pure copper sheet.

### Electrolyte

Copper sulphate solution acidified with dilute sulfuric acid.

---

## Observation

- Pure copper deposits on the cathode.
- Impurities settle as **anode mud**.

---

## Uses

- Purification of copper.
- Manufacture of electrical wires.
- Electronic industries.

---

# Difference Between Electrolysis and Electroplating

| Electrolysis                    | Electroplating                     |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Chemical decomposition          | Coating of a metal                 |
| Produces chemical change        | Produces protective layer          |
| Used in extraction and refining | Used for decoration and protection |
| May produce gases               | Deposits metal coating             |

---

# Difference Between Strong and Weak Electrolytes

| Strong Electrolyte | Weak Electrolyte  |
| ------------------ | ----------------- |
| Completely ionizes | Partially ionizes |
| High conductivity  | Low conductivity  |
| Example: HCl       | Example: CH₃COOH  |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing the **cathode** and **anode** in an electrolytic cell.
- Forgetting that **reduction** occurs at the cathode and **oxidation** at the anode.
- Mixing up **electrolysis** with **electroplating**.
- Assuming distilled water is a good electrolyte.
- Forgetting the concept of **preferential discharge**.
- Confusing **electrolytes** with **non-electrolytes**.
- Assuming electroplating changes the entire object instead of only coating its surface.

# Quick Revision

- Electrolysis → Chemical decomposition using electricity.
- Electrolyte → Conducts electricity through ions.
- Non-electrolyte → Does not conduct electricity.
- Cathode → Negative electrode, reduction occurs.
- Anode → Positive electrode, oxidation occurs.
- Cations → Move to cathode.
- Anions → Move to anode.
- Strong electrolyte → Complete ionization.
- Electroplating → Metal coating.
- Electro-refining → Metal purification.

# Chapter Summary

- **Electrolysis** is the decomposition of an electrolyte by passing direct electric current through its molten or aqueous solution.
- **Electrolytes** conduct electricity because they contain free ions, whereas **non-electrolytes** do not produce ions and therefore cannot conduct electricity.
- In an electrolytic cell, **cations move to the cathode**, where **reduction** occurs, while **anions move to the anode**, where **oxidation** takes place.
- The **preferential discharge of ions** depends on factors such as the electrochemical series, ion concentration, and the nature of the electrodes.
- **Electroplating** is used to coat objects with a thin layer of another metal to improve appearance and prevent corrosion.
- **Electro-refining** is an important industrial process for purifying metals such as copper, where pure metal is deposited on the cathode and impurities collect as **anode mud**.
- Electrolysis has numerous applications in **metal extraction, purification, electroplating, chemical manufacturing, and industrial processes**.
