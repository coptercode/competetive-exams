# Introduction

Electricity and magnetism are two closely related branches of physics that play a vital role in modern life. Electricity is associated with the flow of electric charges, while magnetism deals with the forces produced by magnets and electric currents. This chapter introduces static electricity, electric current, electric circuits, magnetic fields, magnetic induction, and electromagnets.

---

# 1. Static Electricity

## Definition

**Static electricity** is the accumulation of electric charges on the surface of an object without continuous movement of charges.

---

## Cause of Static Electricity

Static electricity is produced due to the **transfer of electrons** from one object to another when they are rubbed together.

Examples:

- Rubbing a plastic comb with dry hair.
- Rubbing a glass rod with silk.
- Rubbing an ebonite rod with fur.

---

## Types of Electric Charges

### Positive Charge (+)

Formed when an object loses electrons.

---

### Negative Charge (−)

Formed when an object gains electrons.

---

## Law of Electric Charges

- **Like charges repel each other.**
- **Unlike charges attract each other.**

---

## Electroscope

An **electroscope** is an instrument used to detect the presence of electric charge.

### Construction

- Metal knob
- Conducting rod
- Thin gold leaves or aluminium leaves
- Glass case

### Working

When a charged object touches or comes near the knob:

- Charges are transferred.
- Both leaves acquire the same type of charge.
- The leaves diverge due to repulsion.

### Applications

- Detecting electric charges.
- Identifying the type of charge.
- Demonstrating electrostatic principles.

---

# 2. Current Electricity

## Electric Current

Electric current is the **rate of flow of electric charge** through a conductor.

### Formula

```
I = Q / t
```

where:

- **I** = Current
- **Q** = Charge
- **t** = Time

---

## SI Unit

```
ampere (A)
```

---

### Definition of One Ampere

One ampere is the current when **1 coulomb** of charge flows through a conductor in **1 second**.

```
1 A = 1 C/s
```

---

## Electric Charge

### SI Unit

```
coulomb (C)
```

---

## Potential Difference (Voltage)

Potential difference is the work done in moving a unit positive charge between two points.

### SI Unit

```
volt (V)
```

---

## Sources of Electricity

- Electric cell
- Battery
- Generator
- Solar cell

---

## Conductors

Materials that allow electric current to pass through them.

Examples:

- Copper
- Aluminium
- Silver

---

## Insulators

Materials that do not allow electric current to pass through them.

Examples:

- Plastic
- Rubber
- Glass
- Wood

---

## Electric Circuit

An electric circuit is a closed conducting path through which electric current flows.

---

## Basic Circuit Components

| Component | Symbol | Function                    |
| --------- | ------ | --------------------------- |
| Cell      | ─      |                             | Source of electrical energy |
| Battery   | ─      |                             | ─                           |     | Combination of cells |
| Switch    | —o/ o— | Opens or closes the circuit |
| Bulb      | ⊗      | Produces light              |
| Resistor  | /\/\/\ | Limits current              |
| Ammeter   | (A)    | Measures current            |
| Voltmeter | (V)    | Measures voltage            |

---

## Open Circuit

The switch is open.

Result:

- No current flows.
- Bulb does not glow.

---

## Closed Circuit

The switch is closed.

Result:

- Current flows.
- Bulb glows.

---

# 3. Magnetic Fields

## Magnet

A magnet is a material that attracts magnetic substances such as iron, cobalt, and nickel.

---

## Magnetic Poles

Every magnet has two poles:

- North Pole (N)
- South Pole (S)

---

## Law of Magnetic Poles

- Like poles repel.
- Unlike poles attract.

---

## Magnetic Field

A magnetic field is the region around a magnet where its magnetic force can be experienced.

---

## Magnetic Field Lines

Characteristics:

- Originate from the North Pole.
- Enter the South Pole.
- Never intersect.
- Are closer where the magnetic field is stronger.

---

## Neutral Point

A neutral point is a location where the magnetic field of the Earth and the magnet cancel each other.

At this point:

```
Net Magnetic Field = 0
```

---

# 4. Magnetic Induction

## Definition

Magnetic induction is the process by which an unmagnetized magnetic material becomes temporarily magnetized when placed near a magnet.

---

## Induced Poles

When a magnetic material is brought near a magnet:

- The nearer end develops the opposite pole.
- The farther end develops the same pole.

---

## Magnetic Materials

### Soft Iron

Characteristics:

- Easily magnetized.
- Easily demagnetized.
- Used for electromagnets.

---

### Steel

Characteristics:

- Difficult to magnetize.
- Retains magnetism for a long time.
- Used for permanent magnets.

---

# 5. Electromagnets

## Definition

An electromagnet is a temporary magnet produced when electric current flows through a coil wound around a soft iron core.

---

## Construction

Components:

- Soft iron core
- Insulated copper wire
- Battery
- Switch

---

## Working

When current flows through the coil:

- A magnetic field is produced.
- The soft iron core becomes magnetized.
- When current stops, magnetism disappears.

---

## Factors Affecting the Strength of an Electromagnet

- Number of turns of the coil.
- Magnitude of current.
- Nature of the core.
- Length of the coil.

---

## Applications

- Electric bells
- Relays
- Loudspeakers
- Electric motors
- Magnetic cranes
- MRI machines

---

# Difference Between Permanent Magnet and Electromagnet

| Permanent Magnet       | Electromagnet                    |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Made of steel          | Made of soft iron                |
| Always magnetic        | Magnetic only when current flows |
| Fixed strength         | Strength can be varied           |
| Cannot be switched off | Can be switched on and off       |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A charge of **20 C** flows through a conductor in **5 s**.

Find the current.

### Solution

Using

```
I = Q/t
```

```
I = 20/5

= 4 A
```

### Answer

```
4 A
```

---

## Example 2

### Question

State whether **soft iron** or **steel** is used to make an electromagnet.

### Solution

Soft iron is easily magnetized and demagnetized.

### Answer

```
Soft Iron
```

---

## Example 3

### Question

A battery supplies **10 C** of charge in **2 seconds**.

Find the current.

### Solution

```
I = Q/t

= 10/2

= 5 A
```

### Answer

```
5 A
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing static electricity with current electricity.
- Assuming current flows in an open circuit.
- Mixing up conductors and insulators.
- Forgetting that magnetic field lines never intersect.
- Confusing soft iron with steel.
- Assuming electromagnets work without electric current.
- Using incorrect SI units for current and charge.
- Confusing voltage with current.

# Chapter Summary

- **Static electricity** is produced by the accumulation of electric charges due to friction.
- **Electric current** is the rate of flow of electric charge and is measured in **amperes (A)**.
- Electric circuits consist of components such as cells, switches, bulbs, resistors, ammeters, and voltmeters.
- **Conductors** allow current to pass, whereas **insulators** resist the flow of current.
- Magnets have **North** and **South** poles, and magnetic field lines indicate the direction and strength of the magnetic field.
- **Magnetic induction** is the temporary magnetization of a material placed near a magnet.
- **Soft iron** is suitable for electromagnets, while **steel** is used for permanent magnets.
- **Electromagnets** produce magnetic fields only when electric current flows through a coil.
- Electricity and magnetism are closely related and form the basis of many electrical and electronic devices used in everyday life.
