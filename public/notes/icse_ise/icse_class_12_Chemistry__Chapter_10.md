# Introduction

Amines are organic compounds derived from ammonia (**NH₃**) by replacing one or more hydrogen atoms with alkyl or aryl groups.

General formula:

\[
NH_3 \rightarrow RNH_2,\ R_2NH,\ R_3N
\]

---

# 1. Classification of Amines

Amines are classified based on the number of carbon groups attached to nitrogen.

```
                 Amines

                    │

        ┌───────────┼───────────┐

        ▼           ▼           ▼

     Primary     Secondary    Tertiary

      RNH₂        R₂NH         R₃N
```

---

# 2. Primary Amines (1°)

One alkyl/aryl group attached to nitrogen.

General formula:

\[
R-NH_2
\]

Examples:

## Methylamine

\[
CH_3NH_2
\]

## Aniline

\[
C_6H_5NH_2
\]

---

# 3. Secondary Amines (2°)

Two carbon groups attached to nitrogen.

Formula:

\[
R_2NH
\]

Example:

\[
(CH_3)_2NH
\]

Dimethylamine

---

# 4. Tertiary Amines (3°)

Three carbon groups attached to nitrogen.

Formula:

\[
R_3N
\]

Example:

\[
(CH_3)_3N
\]

Trimethylamine

---

# 5. Classification Based on Carbon Group

## Aliphatic Amines

Nitrogen attached to alkyl groups.

Example:

\[
CH_3NH_2
\]

---

## Aromatic Amines

Nitrogen attached to aromatic ring.

Example:

Aniline

\[
C_6H_5NH_2
\]

---

# 6. Nomenclature of Amines

Rules:

1. Select longest carbon chain.
2. Replace "e" with "amine".
3. Give position number to -NH₂ group.

---

Examples:

\[
CH_3NH_2
\]

Name:

```
Methanamine
```

Common name:

Methylamine

---

\[
CH_3CH_2NH_2
\]

Name:

```
Ethanamine
```

---

```
sp³ hybridised
```

```

```

\[
107^\circ

# 8. Preparation of Amines

---

# 1. Reduction of Nitro Compounds

Nitro compounds reduce to primary amines.

Reaction:

\[
RNO_2+6[H]
\rightarrow
RNH_2+2H_2O
\]

Reducing agents:

- Sn/HCl
- Fe/HCl
- H₂/Ni

---

Example:

\[
C_6H_5NO_2
\rightarrow
C_6H_5NH_2
\]

Nitrobenzene → Aniline

---

# 2. Ammonolysis of Alkyl Halides

Alkyl halides react with ammonia.

Reaction:

\[
RX+NH_3
\rightarrow
RNH_2+HX
\]

---

# 3. Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis

Used for preparation of primary amines.

Reaction:

```
Potassium phthalimide

        ↓

Alkyl halide

        ↓

Primary amine
```

---

# 4. Hoffmann Bromamide Reaction

## Definition

Conversion of amide into primary amine with one less carbon atom.

Reaction:

\[
RCONH_2+Br_2+4NaOH
\rightarrow
RNH_2+Na_2CO_3+NaBr+H_2O
\]

---

# 9. Physical Properties of Amines

---

# 1. Hydrogen Bonding

Primary and secondary amines form hydrogen bonds.

Effects:

- Higher boiling points
- Greater solubility

---

# 2. Solubility

Lower amines are soluble in water.

Reason:

Hydrogen bonding with water.

---

# 10. Basic Nature of Amines

Amines are basic because nitrogen has a lone pair of electrons.

Reaction:

\[
RNH_2+H_2O
\rightarrow
RNH_3^+ +OH^-
\]

---

# Basicity Order

For aliphatic amines:

\[
(CH_3)_2NH

>

CH_3NH_2

>

NH_3

>

(CH_3)_3N
\]

---

# Factors Affecting Basicity

## 1. Electron Donating Groups

Increase basicity.

Example:

Alkyl groups.

---

## 2. Resonance

Aromatic amines are less basic.

Example:

Aniline.

# 12. Reaction with Acids

Amines form salts with acids.

Reaction:

\[
RNH_2+HCl
\rightarrow
RNH_3^+Cl^-
\]

---

# 13. Alkylation

Amines react with alkyl halides.

Example:

\[
RNH_2+RX
\rightarrow
R_2NH
\]

Further reaction gives tertiary amines and quaternary salts.

---

# 14. Acylation

Primary and secondary amines react with acid chlorides.

Reaction:

\[
RNH_2+RCOCl
\rightarrow
RCONHR
\]

Product:

Amide

---

# 15. Carbylamine Reaction

## Definition

Primary amines react with chloroform and alcoholic KOH to form isocyanides.

Reaction:

\[
RNH_2+CHCl_3+KOH
\rightarrow
RNC
\]

---

Observation:

Bad smell of isocyanide.

---

Only:

```
Primary amines give this test
```

---

# 16. Reaction with Nitrous Acid

Nitrous acid:

\[
HNO_2
\]

---

# Primary Aliphatic Amines

Produce alcohol and nitrogen gas.

\[
RNH_2+HNO_2
\rightarrow
ROH+N_2+H_2O
\]

---

# Primary Aromatic Amines

Form diazonium salts.

\[
ArNH_2+HNO_2+HCl
\rightarrow
ArN_2^+Cl^-+2H_2O
\]

---

# 17. Diazotisation Reaction

## Definition

Conversion of primary aromatic amine into diazonium salt.

---

Conditions:

Temperature:

\[
273-278K
\]

---

Example:

Aniline → Benzene diazonium chloride

---

# 18. Reactions of Diazonium Salts

---

# 1. Sandmeyer Reaction

Replacement of diazonium group by halogen.

Reaction:

\[
ArN_2^+Cl^-
\rightarrow
ArCl+N_2
\]

Reagents:

- CuCl
- CuBr

---

# 2. Gattermann Reaction

Uses copper powder and HX.

---

# 3. Coupling Reaction

Diazonium salts react with phenols or amines to form azo compounds.

---

Example:

\[
Ar-N=N-Ar
\]

Azo dye

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. Why are amines basic?

**Answer:**

Amines are basic because nitrogen contains a lone pair of electrons that can accept a proton.

---

## 2. Explain Hoffmann bromamide reaction.

**Answer:**

Amides react with bromine and alkali to form primary amines with one carbon atom less.

---

## 3. What is diazotisation?

**Answer:**

Conversion of primary aromatic amines into diazonium salts using nitrous acid at 273–278 K is called diazotisation.

---

## 4. What is carbylamine test?

**Answer:**

Primary amines react with chloroform and alcoholic KOH to form foul-smelling isocyanides.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing primary, secondary, and tertiary amines.
- Forgetting aniline is less basic than aliphatic amines.
- Mixing diazotisation and carbylamine reactions.
- Forgetting temperature requirement of diazotisation.
- Writing wrong products of Hoffmann reaction.

# Chapter Summary

- **Amines** are nitrogen-containing organic compounds derived from ammonia.
- They are classified as primary, secondary, and tertiary based on nitrogen substitution.
- Amines are basic due to the lone pair on nitrogen.
- Important preparation methods include nitro reduction, Gabriel synthesis, and Hoffmann bromamide reaction.
- Aromatic amines undergo diazotisation to form diazonium salts.
- Diazonium salts are useful intermediates in the preparation of dyes and aromatic compounds.
