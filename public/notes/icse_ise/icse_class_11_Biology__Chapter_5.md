# Introduction

**Morphology** is the branch of botany that deals with the **external structure and form of plants**. Flowering plants (Angiosperms) exhibit well-developed organs such as **roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits, and seeds**, each performing specialized functions.

The study of morphology helps in:

- Identification of plants
- Classification
- Understanding adaptations
- Agricultural and horticultural practices

# 1. Root System

## Definition

The **root** is the descending, underground part of the plant that develops from the **radicle** of the embryo.

---

## Functions

- Anchorage
- Absorption of water and minerals
- Storage of food
- Conduction
- Vegetative propagation

---

# Types of Root Systems

## A. Tap Root System

### Characteristics

- Develops from radicle.
- Primary root persists.
- Secondary and tertiary roots arise.

### Examples

- Mustard
- Pea
- Mango

---

## B. Fibrous Root System

### Characteristics

- Primary root is short-lived.
- Numerous roots arise from the base of the stem.

### Examples

- Wheat
- Rice
- Maize

---

## C. Adventitious Root System

### Characteristics

Roots arise from any plant part other than the radicle.

### Examples

- Banyan
- Sugarcane
- Grass

---

# 4. Inflorescence

## Definition

Arrangement of flowers on the floral axis is called **inflorescence**.

---

# Types

## Racemose Inflorescence

Main axis continues to grow.

Flowers arranged in **acropetal succession**.

Examples

- Mustard
- Sunflower

---

## Cymose Inflorescence

Main axis ends in a flower.

Flowers arranged in **basipetal succession**.

Examples

- Jasmine
- Ixora

---

# Difference Between Racemose and Cymose

| Racemose                    | Cymose                   |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Main axis continues to grow | Main axis ends in flower |
| Acropetal arrangement       | Basipetal arrangement    |
| Indeterminate               | Determinate              |

# Floral Whorls

## Calyx

Made up of sepals.

Function

---

## Corolla

Made up of petals.

Function

---

## Androecium

Male reproductive part.

Made up of stamens.

Each stamen consists of

- Anther
- Filament

---

## Gynoecium

Female reproductive part.

Consists of

- Stigma
- Style
- Ovary

---

Pneumatophores

Differentiate between reticulate and parallel venation.

### Solution

Reticulate venation forms a network of veins (e.g., mango), whereas parallel venation has veins running parallel (e.g., wheat).

Reticulate – network of veins; Parallel – veins run parallel.

Arrangement of ovules inside the ovary.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing tap roots with adventitious roots.
- Mixing up underground stem modifications such as tubers, bulbs, rhizomes, and corms.
- Interchanging venation and phyllotaxy.
- Confusing racemose and cymose inflorescences.
- Forgetting the order of floral whorls.
- Mixing up superior and inferior ovary positions.

# Chapter Summary

- **Morphology of Flowering Plants** deals with the external structure and organization of angiosperms, including the **root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit, and seed**.
- The **root system** performs absorption, anchorage, storage, and support through various modifications such as **prop roots, stilt roots, and pneumatophores**.
- The **stem** supports aerial organs and exhibits underground, sub-aerial, and aerial modifications for storage, protection, photosynthesis, and vegetative propagation.
- **Leaves** vary in venation, phyllotaxy, and modifications, while **inflorescences** are classified into **racemose** and **cymose** types based on the growth of the main axis.
- The **flower** is the reproductive organ composed of four floral whorls, with variations in symmetry, ovary position, and placentation.
- **Fruits and seeds** ensure the protection, nourishment, and dispersal of the next generation, making morphology fundamental to plant identification, taxonomy, agriculture, horticulture, and evolutionary studies.
