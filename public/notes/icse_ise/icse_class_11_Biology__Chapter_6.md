# Introduction

**Plant Anatomy** is the branch of botany that deals with the **internal structure and organization of plant tissues and organs**. It explains how different tissues are arranged within roots, stems, and leaves and how these tissues function together.

The study of anatomy is essential for understanding:

- Plant growth
- Water and food transport
- Mechanical support
- Secondary growth
- Adaptations

---

# 1. Plant Tissues

## Definition

A **tissue** is a group of cells having a common origin, similar structure, and performing a specific function.

---

# 2. Meristematic Tissue

## Definition

Meristematic tissues consist of **actively dividing, undifferentiated cells** responsible for plant growth.

---

## Characteristics

- Thin cell wall
- Dense cytoplasm
- Large nucleus
- Vacuoles absent or very small
- Cells divide continuously

---

# Types of Meristem

## A. Apical Meristem

### Location

- Root tip
- Shoot tip

### Function

- Increases plant length.
- Causes **primary growth**.

---

## B. Intercalary Meristem

### Location

- Base of leaves
- Internodes (especially grasses)

### Function

- Elongation of internodes.
- Regrowth after grazing.

---

## C. Lateral Meristem

### Examples

- Vascular cambium
- Cork cambium

### Function

- Increases girth.
- Causes **secondary growth**.

---

# Comparison of Meristems

| Meristem    | Location                  | Function           |
| ----------- | ------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Apical      | Root & shoot tips         | Increase in length |
| Intercalary | Base of leaves/internodes | Elongation         |
| Lateral     | Sides of stem/root        | Increase in girth  |

---

# 3. Permanent Tissues

## Definition

Permanent tissues are formed from meristematic tissues after differentiation.

---

# Classification

```
Permanent Tissue
       │
 ┌─────┴────────────┐
 ▼                  ▼
Simple          Complex
```

---

# A. Simple Permanent Tissues

---

## Parenchyma

### Characteristics

- Living cells
- Thin cellulose walls
- Large vacuoles
- Intercellular spaces present

---

### Functions

- Storage
- Photosynthesis (Chlorenchyma)
- Buoyancy (Aerenchyma)
- Healing

---

## Sclerenchyma

### Characteristics

- Dead cells
- Thick lignified walls
- Mechanical support

---

### Types

- Fibres
- Sclereids

---

## Collenchyma

### Characteristics

- Living cells
- Unevenly thickened corners
- Flexible support

---

### Function

Provides mechanical strength while allowing flexibility.

---

# Difference Between Simple Permanent Tissues

| Character | Parenchyma | Collenchyma        | Sclerenchyma        |
| --------- | ---------- | ------------------ | ------------------- |
| Cell Type | Living     | Living             | Dead                |
| Cell Wall | Thin       | Unevenly thickened | Thick lignified     |
| Function  | Storage    | Flexible support   | Mechanical strength |

---

# 4. Complex Permanent Tissues

Complex tissues consist of more than one type of cell working together.

---

# A. Xylem

## Function

Conducts

- Water
- Minerals

Provides support.

---

## Components

### Tracheids

- Dead
- Long cells
- Conduct water

---

### Vessels

- Dead
- Tube-like
- Efficient water transport

---

### Xylem Fibres

Provide mechanical strength.

---

### Xylem Parenchyma

Only living element of xylem.

Stores food.

---

# B. Phloem

## Function

Transports prepared food.

---

## Components

### Sieve Tube Elements

Conduct food.

---

### Companion Cells

Help sieve tubes.

---

### Phloem Fibres

Provide support.

---

### Phloem Parenchyma

Stores food.

---

# Difference Between Xylem and Phloem

| Xylem                   | Phloem                 |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Conducts water          | Conducts food          |
| Mostly dead cells       | Mostly living cells    |
| Movement mainly upward  | Bidirectional movement |
| Provides strong support | Limited support        |

---

# 5. Tissue Systems

Plants possess three tissue systems.

---

## Epidermal Tissue System

### Components

- Epidermis
- Stomata
- Trichomes
- Root hairs

---

### Functions

- Protection
- Reduces water loss
- Gas exchange

---

## Ground Tissue System

Includes

- Cortex
- Endodermis
- Pericycle
- Pith
- Medullary rays

---

### Functions

- Storage
- Support
- Photosynthesis

---

## Vascular Tissue System

Consists of

- Xylem
- Phloem

Responsible for transport.

---

# 6. Anatomy of Dicot Root

## Characteristics

- Epiblema with root hairs
- Cortex present
- Endodermis with Casparian strips
- Pericycle single layered
- Radial vascular bundles
- Small pith

---

## Arrangement

```
Root Hair
    │
Epidermis
    │
Cortex
    │
Endodermis
    │
Pericycle
    │
Xylem & Phloem
    │
Pith
```

---

# 7. Anatomy of Monocot Root

## Characteristics

- Similar to dicot root.
- Polyarch xylem.
- Large pith.
- No secondary growth.

---

# Difference Between Dicot and Monocot Root

| Dicot Root               | Monocot Root            |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Small pith               | Large pith              |
| Xylem usually 2–6 arms   | Polyarch xylem          |
| Secondary growth present | Secondary growth absent |

---

# 8. Anatomy of Dicot Stem

## Characteristics

- Epidermis with cuticle
- Cortex
- Endodermis
- Pericycle
- Vascular bundles arranged in a ring
- Open vascular bundles
- Large pith

---

# 9. Anatomy of Monocot Stem

## Characteristics

- Scattered vascular bundles
- Closed vascular bundles
- Ground tissue undifferentiated
- Secondary growth absent

---

# Difference Between Dicot and Monocot Stem

| Dicot Stem                 | Monocot Stem               |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Vascular bundles in a ring | Scattered vascular bundles |
| Open bundles               | Closed bundles             |
| Cambium present            | Cambium absent             |
| Secondary growth present   | Secondary growth absent    |

---

# 10. Secondary Growth

## Definition

Secondary growth is the increase in the **girth (thickness)** of stems and roots due to the activity of lateral meristems.

Occurs mainly in

- Dicot stems
- Gymnosperms

---

# Types of Cambium

## A. Vascular Cambium

Produces

- Secondary xylem (inside)
- Secondary phloem (outside)

---

## B. Cork Cambium (Phellogen)

Produces

- Cork (Phellem)
- Secondary cortex (Phelloderm)

---

# Bark

Bark consists of tissues lying **outside the vascular cambium**, including cork, cork cambium, and secondary cortex.

---

# Annual Rings

Formed due to seasonal activity of vascular cambium.

Each ring represents

```
One Year
```

Used to determine the age of a tree.

---

# Heartwood and Sapwood

## Heartwood

- Inner dark wood
- Dead
- Strong
- Provides support

---

## Sapwood

- Outer light wood
- Living
- Conducts water

---

# Difference Between Heartwood and Sapwood

| Heartwood      | Sapwood          |
| -------------- | ---------------- |
| Dead           | Living           |
| Dark coloured  | Light coloured   |
| No conduction  | Conducts water   |
| Strong support | Active transport |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Which tissue is responsible for primary growth?

### Solution

Primary growth occurs due to the activity of the **apical meristem**.

### Answer

Apical meristem.

---

## Example 2

### Question

Name the only living component of xylem.

### Solution

Among the xylem elements, **xylem parenchyma** remains living.

### Answer

Xylem parenchyma.

---

## Example 3

### Question

Differentiate between open and closed vascular bundles.

### Solution

Open vascular bundles possess cambium and can undergo secondary growth, whereas closed vascular bundles lack cambium and cannot undergo secondary growth.

### Answer

Open bundles contain cambium; closed bundles do not.

---

## Example 4

### Question

Which tissue provides flexibility to young stems?

### Solution

**Collenchyma** provides flexible mechanical support.

### Answer

Collenchyma.

---

## Example 5

### Question

Why does secondary growth occur mainly in dicot stems?

### Solution

Dicot stems possess **vascular cambium**, which produces secondary xylem and phloem, increasing the stem's girth.

### Answer

Because dicot stems contain vascular cambium.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **parenchyma**, **collenchyma**, and **sclerenchyma**.
- Mixing up xylem and phloem functions.
- Assuming secondary growth occurs in monocots.
- Forgetting that xylem parenchyma is the only living xylem element.
- Confusing sapwood with heartwood.
- Mixing up open and closed vascular bundles.

# Chapter Summary

- **Anatomy of Flowering Plants** deals with the internal organization of tissues and organs, explaining how plants grow, transport materials, and provide structural support.
- Plant tissues are classified into **meristematic** and **permanent tissues**, with permanent tissues further divided into **simple** and **complex** tissues.
- **Xylem** conducts water and minerals, while **phloem** transports food throughout the plant body.
- The anatomical differences between **dicot and monocot roots and stems** are important for identification and understanding plant evolution.
- **Secondary growth**, brought about by **vascular cambium** and **cork cambium**, increases the girth of dicot stems and roots, producing wood and bark.
- Knowledge of plant anatomy is fundamental to **botany, agriculture, forestry, horticulture, plant breeding, and wood technology**, forming the basis for understanding plant structure and function.
