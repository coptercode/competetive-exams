# Introduction

**Chemical Bonding** is the force of attraction that holds atoms together to form molecules or compounds. Atoms combine to achieve a **stable electronic configuration**, usually by completing their outermost shell (octet or duplet). The three main types of chemical bonds studied at the ICSE Class 10 level are **Electrovalent (Ionic) Bonds**, **Covalent Bonds**, and **Coordinate (Dative Covalent) Bonds**.

---

# 1. Chemical Bond

## Definition

A **Chemical Bond** is the attractive force that holds atoms or ions together in a molecule or compound.

---

## Why Do Atoms Form Bonds?

Atoms combine to achieve:

- Stable electronic configuration.
- Noble gas configuration.
- Lower energy state.

---

# 2. Octet Rule

## Definition

The **Octet Rule** states that atoms tend to gain, lose, or share electrons to acquire **eight electrons** in their outermost shell.

### Exception

Hydrogen and Helium follow the **Duplet Rule**, requiring only **two electrons**.

---

# 3. Electrovalent (Ionic) Bond

## Definition

An **Electrovalent (Ionic) Bond** is formed by the **complete transfer of electrons** from one atom to another.

---

## Formation of Sodium Chloride (NaCl)

### Electronic Configuration

- Sodium (Na): **2,8,1**
- Chlorine (Cl): **2,8,7**

### Electron Transfer

```
Na  →  Na⁺ + e⁻

Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻
```

### Bond Formation

```
Na⁺  +  Cl⁻  →  NaCl
```

---

## Electron Dot Structure

```
Na•      :Cl:

↓

Na⁺ [:Cl:]⁻
```

---

## Formation of Magnesium Chloride (MgCl₂)

### Electronic Configuration

- Magnesium: **2,8,2**
- Chlorine: **2,8,7**

```
Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻

2Cl + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻

Mg²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ → MgCl₂
```

---

## Properties of Ionic Compounds

- High melting point.
- High boiling point.
- Hard and brittle.
- Conduct electricity in molten or aqueous state.
- Soluble in water.
- Insoluble in organic solvents.

---

# 4. Covalent Bond

## Definition

A **Covalent Bond** is formed by the **sharing of one or more pairs of electrons** between atoms.

---

## Types of Covalent Bonds

### Single Bond

One pair of electrons shared.

Example:

```
H₂
```

---

### Double Bond

Two pairs of electrons shared.

Example:

```
O₂
```

---

### Triple Bond

Three pairs of electrons shared.

Example:

```
N₂
```

# 6. Coordinate (Dative Covalent) Bond

## Definition

A **Coordinate Bond** is a covalent bond in which **both shared electrons are donated by the same atom**.

---

## Formation

One atom:

- Has a lone pair of electrons.
- Donates both electrons to another atom.

---

## Representation

```
A → B
```

Arrow indicates the donor atom.

---

## Examples

- Ammonium ion (NH₄⁺)
- Hydronium ion (H₃O⁺)

---

## Formation of Ammonium Ion

```
NH₃ + H⁺

↓

NH₄⁺
```

The nitrogen atom donates its lone pair to the hydrogen ion.

---

# 7. Comparison of Ionic and Covalent Compounds

| Ionic Compounds                                | Covalent Compounds         |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Formed by electron transfer                    | Formed by electron sharing |
| Usually between metals and non-metals          | Usually between non-metals |
| High melting point                             | Low melting point          |
| Conduct electricity when molten or in solution | Do not conduct electricity |
| Soluble in water                               | Usually insoluble in water |
| Hard and brittle                               | Usually soft               |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

What is an ionic bond?

### Solution

It is formed by complete transfer of electrons between atoms.

### Answer

An ionic bond is formed by the complete transfer of electrons from one atom to another.

---

## Example 2

### Question

What type of bond is present in methane (CH₄)?

### Solution

Carbon shares electrons with hydrogen atoms.

### Answer

```
Covalent Bond
```

---

## Example 3

### Question

What is a coordinate bond?

### Solution

Both electrons of the shared pair are donated by one atom.

### Answer

A covalent bond in which both shared electrons are donated by the same atom.

---

## Example 4

### Question

Why do ionic compounds conduct electricity only in molten or aqueous state?

### Solution

In these states, ions are free to move and carry electric current.

### Answer

Because the ions become mobile and can conduct electricity.

---

## Example 5

### Question

### Solution

Certain ions are formed when one atom donates a lone pair of electrons.

### Answer

```

```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **electron transfer** with **electron sharing**.
- Assuming all compounds conduct electricity.
- Mixing up ionic and covalent compounds.
- Forgetting that a **coordinate bond** originates from one donor atom.
- Drawing incorrect Lewis dot structures.
- Assuming ionic compounds conduct electricity in the solid state.
- Confusing **polar** and **non-polar** covalent bonds.

# Chapter Summary

- **Chemical bonding** enables atoms to achieve a stable electronic configuration by following the **octet or duplet rule**.
- **Electrovalent (ionic) bonds** are formed through the complete transfer of electrons, producing oppositely charged ions held together by electrostatic attraction.
- **Covalent bonds** are formed by the sharing of electron pairs between non-metal atoms and may be single, double, or triple bonds.
- **Coordinate (dative covalent) bonds** are a special type of covalent bond in which both shared electrons are donated by a single atom.
- **Lewis electron dot structures** help represent the arrangement of valence electrons and the formation of chemical bonds.
- Ionic compounds generally have **high melting and boiling points**, conduct electricity in the molten or aqueous state, and are soluble in water, whereas covalent compounds generally have **lower melting points**, do not conduct electricity, and are often insoluble in water.
- Understanding chemical bonding is essential for explaining the **structure, properties, and behaviour** of compounds in chemistry.
