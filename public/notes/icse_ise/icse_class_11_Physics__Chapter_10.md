# Introduction

**Thermodynamics** is the branch of physics that deals with the relationship between **heat, work, temperature, and energy**. It explains how heat energy is converted into mechanical work and vice versa.

Thermodynamics is widely used in **heat engines, refrigerators, air conditioners, power plants, automobiles, and industrial processes**.

---

# 1. Thermodynamic System

## Definition

A **thermodynamic system** is a specified quantity of matter or a region in space chosen for study.

---

# Surroundings

Everything outside the system is called the **surroundings**.

---

# Boundary

The real or imaginary surface separating the system from its surroundings.

---

# Types of Systems

## Open System

- Exchanges both matter and energy with surroundings.

**Example:** Boiling water in an open vessel.

---

## Closed System

- Exchanges only energy.
- No exchange of matter.

**Example:** Gas in a sealed cylinder.

---

## Isolated System

- Exchanges neither matter nor energy.

**Example:** Thermos flask (approximately).

---

# Difference Between Open, Closed and Isolated Systems

| Open                        | Closed                | Isolated                            |
| --------------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Exchanges matter and energy | Exchanges only energy | Exchanges neither matter nor energy |

---

# 2. Thermodynamic Variables

## State Variables

Properties that define the state of a system.

- Pressure (P)
- Volume (V)
- Temperature (T)
- Internal Energy (U)

---

# Equation of State

For an ideal gas,

```
PV = nRT
```

Where

- P = Pressure
- V = Volume
- n = Number of moles
- R = Universal gas constant
- T = Absolute temperature

---

# Universal Gas Constant

```
R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹
```

---

# 3. Internal Energy

## Definition

Internal energy is the total energy possessed by the molecules of a system due to their random motion and intermolecular forces.

---

# Symbol

```
U
```

---

# Characteristics

- State function.
- Depends only on temperature (for an ideal gas).

---

# Change in Internal Energy

```
ΔU = Uf − Ui
```

---

# 4. Heat

## Definition

Heat is the energy transferred between two bodies due to a temperature difference.

---

# Symbol

```
Q
```

---

# Sign Convention

- Heat supplied to the system → Positive.
- Heat released by the system → Negative.

---

# 5. Work Done

## Definition

Work is done when a gas expands or contracts against an external pressure.

---

# Formula

```
W = PΔV
```

(For constant pressure)

---

# General Formula

```
W = ∫PdV
```

---

# Sign Convention

- Expansion → Positive work.
- Compression → Negative work.

---

# Difference Between Heat and Work

| Heat                          | Work                          |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Due to temperature difference | Due to mechanical interaction |
| Path function                 | Path function                 |

---

# 6. First Law of Thermodynamics

## Statement

The heat supplied to a system is used to increase its internal energy and to perform external work.

---

# Mathematical Form

```
Q = ΔU + W
```

or

```
ΔU = Q − W
```

---

# Significance

- Law of conservation of energy.
- Heat and work are forms of energy.

---

# Applications

- Heat engines.
- Refrigerators.
- Power plants.

---

# 7. Thermodynamic Processes

---

# Isothermal Process

## Definition

Temperature remains constant.

```
ΔT = 0
```

---

# Characteristics

- Internal energy remains constant.
- Heat supplied equals work done.

---

# Formula

```
PV = Constant
```

(Boyle's Law)

---

# Work Done

```
      V₂
W = nRT ln(──)
      V₁
```

---

# Isobaric Process

## Definition

Pressure remains constant.

```
ΔP = 0
```

---

# Work Done

```
W = P(V₂ − V₁)
```

---

# Isochoric Process

## Definition

Volume remains constant.

```
ΔV = 0
```

---

# Characteristics

- No work done.

```
W = 0
```

---

# First Law

```
Q = ΔU
```

---

# Adiabatic Process

## Definition

No heat exchange occurs.

```
Q = 0
```

---

# First Law

```
ΔU = -W
```

---

# Adiabatic Equation

```
PVᵞ = Constant
```

Where

```
γ = Cp/Cv
```

---

# Difference Between Thermodynamic Processes

| Isothermal    | Isobaric   | Isochoric  | Adiabatic      |
| ------------- | ---------- | ---------- | -------------- |
| T constant    | P constant | V constant | Q = 0          |
| PV = Constant | W = PΔV    | W = 0      | PVᵞ = Constant |

---

# 8. Heat Capacities

## Heat Capacity

Amount of heat required to raise the temperature of a body by 1 K.

---

# Formula

```
C = Q/ΔT
```

---

# Specific Heat Capacity

```
c = Q/mΔT
```

---

# Molar Heat Capacity

Heat required to raise the temperature of one mole by 1 K.

---

# Molar Heat Capacity at Constant Volume

```
Cv
```

---

# Molar Heat Capacity at Constant Pressure

```
Cp
```

---

# Relation

```
Cp > Cv
```

---

# Mayer's Formula

```
Cp − Cv = R
```

---

# Ratio of Heat Capacities

```
γ = Cp/Cv
```

---

# Difference Between Cp and Cv

| Cp                | Cv              |
| ----------------- | --------------- |
| Constant pressure | Constant volume |
| Larger            | Smaller         |

---

# 9. Second Law of Thermodynamics

## Kelvin-Planck Statement

No heat engine can convert all heat into work.

---

## Clausius Statement

Heat cannot flow from a colder body to a hotter body without external work.

---

# Importance

- Explains irreversibility.
- Defines efficiency.
- Basis of entropy.

---

# 10. Heat Engine

## Definition

A device that converts heat energy into mechanical work.

---

# Components

- Source
- Working substance
- Sink

---

# Efficiency

```
η

=

W

──────

Q₁
```

or

```
η

=

Q₁−Q₂

────────

Q₁
```

---

# Maximum Efficiency

```
η = 1 − T₂/T₁
```

(Carnot Engine)

---

# 11. Carnot Engine

## Characteristics

- Reversible engine.
- Maximum possible efficiency.
- Operates between two temperatures.

---

# Carnot Cycle

1. Isothermal Expansion
2. Adiabatic Expansion
3. Isothermal Compression
4. Adiabatic Compression

---

# Carnot Efficiency

```
η = 1 − T₂/T₁
```

Where

- T₁ = Source temperature
- T₂ = Sink temperature

---

# Difference Between Real Engine and Carnot Engine

| Real Engine      | Carnot Engine      |
| ---------------- | ------------------ |
| Irreversible     | Reversible         |
| Lower efficiency | Maximum efficiency |

---

# 12. Refrigerator

## Definition

A refrigerator transfers heat from a cold body to a hot body using external work.

---

# Coefficient of Performance (COP)

```
COP

=

Q₂

──────

W
```

---

# Applications

- Refrigerators.
- Air conditioners.
- Cold storage.

---

# Difference Between Heat Engine and Refrigerator

| Heat Engine           | Refrigerator                |
| --------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Produces work         | Consumes work               |
| Heat flows hot → cold | Heat transferred cold → hot |

---

# 13. Entropy

Entropy is the measure of the randomness or disorder of a system.

---

# Symbol

```
S
```

---

# Formula

For a reversible process,

```
ΔS = Q/T
```

---

# Characteristics

- State function.
- Increases in irreversible processes.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A gas receives 500 J of heat and performs 200 J of work. Find the change in internal energy.

### Solution

```
ΔU = Q − W

= 500 − 200

= 300 J
```

### Answer

**300 J**

---

## Example 2

### Question

A gas expands at constant pressure of 1000 Pa from 2 m³ to 5 m³. Find the work done.

### Solution

```
W = PΔV

= 1000 × (5 − 2)

= 3000 J
```

### Answer

**3000 J**

---

## Example 3

### Question

Find the efficiency of a heat engine that absorbs 1000 J and rejects 300 J.

### Solution

```
η

= (1000 − 300)/1000

= 0.7

= 70%
```

### Answer

**70%**

---

## Example 4

### Question

Find the Carnot efficiency for T₁ = 600 K and T₂ = 300 K.

### Solution

```
η = 1 − T₂/T₁

= 1 − 300/600

= 0.5

= 50%
```

### Answer

**50%**

---

## Example 5

### Question

Calculate the coefficient of performance of a refrigerator that removes 600 J of heat by consuming 200 J of work.

### Solution

```
COP

= 600/200

= 3
```

### Answer

**3**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **heat** with **internal energy**.
- Using Celsius instead of Kelvin in thermodynamic equations.
- Applying the wrong sign convention for heat and work.
- Forgetting that **W = 0** in an isochoric process.
- Assuming **Cp = Cv**.
- Confusing efficiency with coefficient of performance (COP).

# Chapter Summary

- **Thermodynamics** studies the relationship between heat, work, and internal energy in physical systems.
- The **First Law of Thermodynamics** expresses the conservation of energy by relating heat supplied, work done, and change in internal energy.
- Different **thermodynamic processes**—isothermal, isobaric, isochoric, and adiabatic—describe how gases behave under varying conditions.
- The **Second Law of Thermodynamics** introduces the concept of irreversibility and limits the efficiency of heat engines.
- The **Carnot engine** represents the most efficient reversible heat engine, while the **coefficient of performance (COP)** measures the effectiveness of refrigerators and heat pumps.
- Thermodynamics has extensive applications in **power generation, refrigeration, aerospace engineering, automotive systems, industrial manufacturing, and renewable energy technologies**.
