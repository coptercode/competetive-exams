# Introduction

When electric charges move, they produce a magnetic field.

**Magnetic effects of current** deals with:

- Magnetic field produced by current
- Force on moving charges
- Motion of charged particles in magnetic fields
- Electrical measuring instruments

---

# 1. Magnetic Field

## Definition

The region around a magnet or current carrying conductor where magnetic force can be experienced is called a magnetic field.

---

# Magnetic Field Vector

Symbol:

\[
\vec{B}
\]

Unit:

\[
Tesla(T)
\]

---

# 2. Magnetic Field Lines

## Properties

1. Magnetic field lines form closed loops.
2. Outside a magnet they move from North to South.
3. Inside a magnet they move from South to North.
4. Two magnetic field lines never intersect.
5. Density of lines represents field strength.

---

# 3. Oersted Experiment

## Observation

A current carrying wire deflects a nearby compass needle.

---

## Conclusion

Electric current produces a magnetic field.

---

# 4. Biot-Savart Law

## Statement

The magnetic field due to a small current element is directly proportional to:

- Current
- Length element
- Sine of angle

and inversely proportional to square of distance.

---

# Formula

\[
dB=
\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}
\frac{Idl\sin\theta}{r^2}
\]

---

Where:

- dB = Small magnetic field
- I = Current
- dl = Current element
- r = Distance
- μ₀ = Permeability of free space

---

# Direction

Given by:

```
Right Hand Thumb Rule
```

---

# 5. Magnetic Field Due to Straight Current Carrying Conductor

For a long straight wire:

\[
B=
\frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}
\]

---

Where:

- I = Current
- r = Distance from wire

---

# Variation

\[
B\propto\frac1r
\]

Magnetic field decreases with distance.

---

# 6. Magnetic Field Due to Circular Current Loop

At centre:

\[
B=
\frac{\mu_0 NI}{2R}
\]

---

Where:

- N = Number of turns
- R = Radius of coil

---

# Direction

Using:

```
Right Hand Rule
```

---

# Magnetic Field on Axis of Circular Loop

\[
B=
\frac{\mu_0NIR^2}
{2(R^2+x^2)^{3/2}}
\]

---

# 7. Ampere's Circuital Law

## Statement

The line integral of magnetic field around a closed path is equal to μ₀ times the current enclosed.

---

Formula:

\[
\oint B.dl=\mu_0I
\]

---

# Applications

Used to find magnetic fields in:

- Solenoid
- Toroid
- Infinite wire

---

# 8. Magnetic Field Due to Solenoid

## Definition

A long coil consisting of many turns of wire is called a solenoid.

---

Magnetic field inside solenoid:

\[
B=\mu_0 nI
\]

---

Where:

- n = Number of turns per unit length
- I = Current

---

# Properties

Inside solenoid:

```
Uniform magnetic field
```

Outside:

```
Almost zero
```

---

# 9. Toroid

## Definition

A solenoid bent into circular form is called a toroid.

---

Magnetic field:

\[
B=
\frac{\mu_0NI}{2\pi r}
\]

---

# 10. Force on a Moving Charge in Magnetic Field

A moving charge experiences magnetic force.

---

# Lorentz Force

\[
F=q(\vec{v}\times\vec{B})
\]

---

Magnitude:

\[
F=qvB\sin\theta
\]

---

Cases:

## θ = 0°

\[
F=0
\]

Charge moves parallel to field.

---

## θ = 90°

Maximum force.

\[
F=qvB
\]

---

# 11. Motion of Charged Particle in Magnetic Field

---

# Case 1: Velocity Perpendicular to Magnetic Field

Particle moves in circular path.

---

Magnetic force provides centripetal force:

\[
qvB=\frac{mv^2}{r}
\]

---

Radius:

\[
r=\frac{mv}{qB}
\]

---

# Time Period

\[
T=
\frac{2\pi m}{qB}
\]

---

# Frequency

\[
f=
\frac{qB}{2\pi m}
\]

---

# Case 2: Velocity at an Angle

Particle follows:

```
Helical path
```

---

# 12. Cyclotron

## Definition

A device used to accelerate charged particles using electric and magnetic fields.

---

# Principle

A charged particle moving perpendicular to magnetic field experiences circular motion.

---

# Cyclotron Frequency

\[
f=
\frac{qB}{2\pi m}
\]

---

# Components

1. Two hollow D-shaped electrodes (Dees)
2. Magnetic field
3. Alternating electric field

---

# Limitations

- Cannot accelerate neutral particles.
- Not suitable for very high-speed relativistic particles.

---

# 13. Force on Current Carrying Conductor

A current carrying conductor placed in magnetic field experiences force.

---

Formula:

\[
F=BIL\sin\theta
\]

---

Where:

- B = Magnetic field
- I = Current
- L = Length of conductor

---

# Direction

Using:

```
Fleming's Left Hand Rule
```

---

# 14. Force Between Two Parallel Conductors

Two parallel current carrying wires exert forces on each other.

---

Force per unit length:

\[
\frac FL=
\frac{\mu_0I_1I_2}{2\pi d}
\]

---

# Nature of Force

Same direction currents:

```
Attraction
```

Opposite direction currents:

```
Repulsion
```

---

# 15. Definition of Ampere

One ampere is the current which produces a force of:

\[
2\times10^{-7}Nm^{-1}
\]

between two parallel conductors separated by one metre.

---

# 16. Magnetic Dipole

A current carrying loop behaves like a magnetic dipole.

---

# Magnetic Dipole Moment

Formula:

\[
M=NIA
\]

---

Where:

- N = Number of turns
- I = Current
- A = Area

---

Unit:

\[
Am^2
\]

---

# 17. Torque on Current Loop

A current carrying coil in magnetic field experiences torque.

---

Formula:

\[
\tau=NIAB\sin\theta
\]

---

Maximum torque:

\[
\tau=NIAB
\]

---

# 18. Moving Coil Galvanometer

## Definition

A device used to detect and measure small electric currents.

---

# Principle

A current carrying coil placed in magnetic field experiences torque.

---

# Torque:

\[
\tau=NIAB
\]

---

At equilibrium:

\[
NIAB=k\theta
\]

---

Current:

\[
I=
\frac{k\theta}{NAB}
\]

---

# 19. Conversion of Galvanometer

---

# Galvanometer to Ammeter

A low resistance shunt is connected parallel.

---

Shunt resistance:

\[
S=
\frac{I_gG}{I-I_g}
\]

---

Where:

- G = Galvanometer resistance
- Ig = Galvanometer current

---

# Galvanometer to Voltmeter

A high resistance is connected in series.

---

Resistance:

\[
R=
\frac{V}{I_g}-G
\]

---

# Board Important Questions

## 1. State Biot-Savart law.

**Answer:**

The magnetic field due to a current element is directly proportional to current, length element and sine of angle, and inversely proportional to square of distance.

---

## 2. Explain cyclotron principle.

**Answer:**

A charged particle moving perpendicular to a magnetic field experiences circular motion and gains energy through alternating electric fields.

---

## 3. Derive force between parallel conductors.

Important derivation:

\[
\frac FL=
\frac{\mu_0I_1I_2}{2\pi d}
\]

---

## 4. Explain moving coil galvanometer.

**Answer:**

A galvanometer works on the principle that a current carrying coil placed in a magnetic field experiences torque.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing magnetic field and magnetic force.
- Forgetting direction rules.
- Mixing Fleming's left and right hand rules.
- Using wrong radius formula in circular motion.
- Forgetting number of turns in coil equations.

- **Magnetic Effects of Current** explains how electric currents produce magnetic fields.
- Biot-Savart law calculates magnetic fields produced by current elements.
- Ampere's law helps determine magnetic fields in symmetric systems.

- Cyclotrons accelerate charged particles using electric and magnetic fields.
