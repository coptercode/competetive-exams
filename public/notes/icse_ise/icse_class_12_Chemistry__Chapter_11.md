# Introduction

Cyanides and isocyanides are organic compounds containing the functional group:

\[
-CN
\]

They differ in the way the cyanide group is attached to carbon.

---

# 1. Cyanides

## Definition

Compounds in which the carbon atom of the cyanide group is attached to the carbon chain are called cyanides.

General formula:

\[
R-C\equiv N
\]

---

Examples:

## Methyl cyanide

\[
CH_3-CN
\]

IUPAC name:

```
Ethanenitrile
```

---

## Ethyl cyanide

\[
C_2H_5-CN
\]

IUPAC name:

```
Propanenitrile
```

---

# 2. Isocyanides

## Definition

Compounds in which the nitrogen atom of the cyanide group is attached to the carbon chain are called isocyanides.

General formula:

\[
R-N\equiv C
\]

---

Examples:

## Methyl isocyanide

\[
CH_3-NC
\]

---

# Difference Between Cyanides and Isocyanides

| Cyanides                       | Isocyanides                      |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| R-C≡N                          | R-N≡C                            |
| Carbon attached to alkyl group | Nitrogen attached to alkyl group |
| More stable                    | Less stable                      |
| Normal nitriles                | Carbylamines                     |

---

# 5. Preparation of Cyanides

---

# 1. From Alkyl Halides

Alkyl halides react with alcoholic KCN.

Reaction:

\[
RX+KCN
\rightarrow
RCN+KX
\]

---

Example:

\[
C_2H_5Br+KCN
\rightarrow
C_2H_5CN+KBr
\]

---

# Important Point

KCN gives:

```
Cyanides
```

because CN⁻ attacks through carbon.

---

# 2. From Amides

Amides undergo dehydration.

Reaction:

\[
RCONH_2
\rightarrow
RCN+H_2O
\]

Dehydrating agents:

- P₂O₅
- SOCl₂

---

# 3. From Diazonium Salts

Aromatic cyanides are prepared by Sandmeyer reaction.

Reaction:

\[
ArN_2^+Cl^-+KCN
\rightarrow
ArCN+N_2
\]

---

# 6. Preparation of Isocyanides

## Carbylamine Reaction

Primary amines react with chloroform and alcoholic KOH.

Reaction:

\[
RNH_2+CHCl_3+3KOH
\rightarrow
RNC+3KCl+3H_2O
\]

---

Only:

```
Primary amines
```

give this reaction.

---

# 7. Physical Properties

---

# Cyanides

- Usually liquids or solids.
- Polar compounds.
- Have unpleasant odour.

---

# Isocyanides

- Extremely unpleasant smell.
- Less stable than cyanides.

---

# 8. Chemical Reactions of Cyanides

---

# 1. Hydrolysis

Cyanides undergo hydrolysis to form amides and acids.

---

## Partial Hydrolysis

\[
RCN+H_2O
\rightarrow
RCONH_2
\]

Product:

Amide

---

## Complete Hydrolysis

\[
RCN+2H_2O
\rightarrow
RCOOH+NH_3
\]

Product:

Carboxylic acid

---

# 2. Reduction

Cyanides reduce to primary amines.

Reaction:

\[
RCN+4[H]
\rightarrow
RCH_2NH_2
\]

Reagents:

- LiAlH₄
- H₂/Ni

---

# 3. Addition of Grignard Reagent

Cyanides react with Grignard reagent to form ketones.

Reaction:

\[
RCN+RMgX
\rightarrow
RCO-R
\]

---

# 9. Chemical Reactions of Isocyanides

Isocyanides are less stable and undergo hydrolysis.

Reaction:

\[
RNC+2H_2O
\rightarrow
RNH_2+HCOOH
\]

---

# 10. Important Reactions

---

# Sandmeyer Reaction

Conversion of diazonium salts into cyanides.

Reaction:

\[
ArN_2^+Cl^- +
CuCN
\rightarrow
ArCN+N_2
\]

---

# Carbylamine Test

Used to identify primary amines.

Reaction:

\[
RNH_2+CHCl_3+KOH
\rightarrow
RNC
\]

Observation:

```
Foul smelling isocyanide
```

---

# 11. Cyanides in Organic Synthesis

Cyanides are important because they increase carbon chain length.

Example:

\[
RX+KCN
\rightarrow
RCN
\]

One extra carbon atom is introduced.

---

# 12. Uses of Cyanides

## 2. Industrial Applications

# 13. Toxicity of Cyanides

Cyanides are highly poisonous.

Reason:

They inhibit cellular respiration.

---

Mechanism:

Cyanide blocks:

```
Cytochrome oxidase enzyme
```

---

Effect:

Cells cannot use oxygen.

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. Differentiate cyanides and isocyanides.

**Answer:**

Cyanides have the structure R-CN where carbon is attached to the alkyl group, whereas isocyanides have the structure R-NC where nitrogen is attached to the alkyl group.

---

## 2. Explain preparation of cyanides.

**Answer:**

Cyanides are prepared by heating alkyl halides with alcoholic KCN.

\[
RX+KCN\rightarrow RCN+KX
\]

---

## 3. What is carbylamine reaction?

**Answer:**

Primary amines react with chloroform and alcoholic KOH to produce foul-smelling isocyanides.

---

## 4. Why are cyanides poisonous?

**Answer:**

Cyanides inhibit cytochrome oxidase enzyme, preventing cells from using oxygen.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing cyanides and isocyanides.
- Writing KCN and AgCN products as same.
- Forgetting primary amine requirement for carbylamine test.
- Mixing nitriles with amines.
- Forgetting cyanide adds one carbon atom in synthesis.

# Chapter Summary

- **Cyanides and Isocyanides** are nitrogen-containing organic compounds with different bonding arrangements.
- Cyanides contain the group R-CN, while isocyanides contain R-NC.
- Cyanides are prepared mainly using KCN and alkyl halides.
- Isocyanides are prepared through the carbylamine reaction.
- Cyanides undergo hydrolysis, reduction, and addition reactions.
- These compounds are useful in organic synthesis but many cyanides are highly toxic.
