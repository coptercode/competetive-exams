# Introduction

**Wave Optics** deals with the study of the wave nature of light.

It explains:

- Interference
- Diffraction
- Polarisation

These phenomena cannot be explained by ray optics.

---

# 1. Wave Nature of Light

Light shows wave properties such as:

- Interference
- Diffraction
- Polarisation

---

# 2. Huygens' Principle

## Statement

Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets that spread in all directions with the speed of light.

---

# Wavefront

## Definition

A wavefront is a surface joining all points having the same phase of vibration.

---

# Types of Wavefronts

```
Wavefronts

     │

 ┌───┴───┐

 ▼       ▼

Spherical Plane
```

---

# 1. Spherical Wavefront

Produced by:

```
Point source
```

Example:

Bulb

---

# 2. Plane Wavefront

Produced when source is far away.

---

# 3. Huygens Principle Explanation of Reflection

According to Huygens:

- Every point on incident wavefront produces secondary wavelets.
- The new wavefront gives reflected wave.

---

# Laws of Reflection

\[
i=r
\]

---

# 4. Huygens Principle Explanation of Refraction

When light enters another medium:

Speed changes.

---

Refractive index:

\[
\mu=\frac{c}{v}
\]

---

# 5. Interference of Light

## Definition

The phenomenon of redistribution of light intensity due to superposition of two light waves is called interference.

---

# Principle of Superposition

When two waves overlap:

\[
Y=Y_1+Y_2
\]

---

# Conditions for Interference

For sustained interference:

1. Sources must be coherent.
2. Same frequency.
3. Constant phase difference.
4. Nearly equal intensity.

---

# Coherent Sources

## Definition

Sources having:

- Same frequency
- Constant phase difference

are called coherent sources.

---

# 6. Types of Interference

```
Interference

      │

 ┌────┴────┐

 ▼         ▼

Constructive Destructive
```

---

# Constructive Interference

Waves meet in same phase.

Result:

```
Maximum intensity
```

---

Condition:

\[
Path\ difference=n\lambda
\]

---

# Destructive Interference

Waves meet in opposite phase.

Result:

```
Minimum intensity
```

---

Condition:

\[
Path\ difference=(2n+1)\frac{\lambda}{2}
\]

---

# 7. Young's Double Slit Experiment (YDSE)

## Principle

Interference of light waves from two coherent sources.

---

Setup:

```
S₁      Screen

 |        |

S₂  →     Pattern
```

---

# Fringe Width

## Definition

Distance between two consecutive bright or dark fringes.

---

Formula:

\[
\beta=\frac{\lambda D}{d}
\]

---

Where:

- β = Fringe width
- λ = Wavelength
- D = Distance between screen and slit
- d = Distance between slits

---

# 8. Position of Bright Fringes

Condition:

\[
x_n=n\beta
\]

---

# 9. Position of Dark Fringes

Condition:

\[
x_n=
(2n+1)\frac{\beta}{2}
\]

---

# 10. Intensity in Interference

Resultant intensity:

\[
I=I_1+I_2+2\sqrt{I_1I_2}\cos\phi
\]

---

# Maximum Intensity

\[
I_{max}
=

(\sqrt{I_1}+\sqrt{I_2})^2
\]

---

# Minimum Intensity

\[
I_{min}
=

(\sqrt{I_1}-\sqrt{I_2})^2
\]

---

# 11. Diffraction of Light

## Definition

The bending of light around the edges of obstacles is called diffraction.

---

Occurs when:

Obstacle size ≈ wavelength of light

---

# Single Slit Diffraction

Condition for minima:

\[
a\sin\theta=n\lambda
\]

---

Where:

- a = Width of slit
- n = Order of minimum

---

# 12. Diffraction Pattern

Characteristics:

- Central maximum is brightest.
- Central maximum is widest.
- Secondary maxima have lower intensity.

---

# 13. Difference Between Interference and Diffraction

| Interference                  | Diffraction                    |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Due to two coherent sources   | Due to bending around obstacle |
| Bright fringes equally spaced | Unequal intensity pattern      |
| Fringes have similar width    | Central maximum is wider       |

---

# 14. Polarisation of Light

## Definition

The phenomenon of restricting vibrations of light waves to one direction is called polarisation.

---

It proves:

```
Light is a transverse wave
```

---

# 15. Plane Polarised Light

Light whose vibrations are restricted to one plane is called plane polarised light.

---

# 16. Polaroids

## Definition

Materials that produce plane polarised light are called polaroids.

---

Uses:

- Sunglasses
- Photography
- LCD screens

---

# 17. Brewster's Law

## Statement

The refractive index of a transparent material is equal to the tangent of polarising angle.

---

Formula:

\[
\mu=\tan i_p
\]

---

Where:

\[
i_p
\]

= Polarising angle

---

# 18. Malus Law

## Statement

Intensity of transmitted light through an analyser depends on angle between polariser and analyser.

---

Formula:

\[
I=I_0\cos^2\theta
\]

---

# 19. Resolving Power

## Definition

Ability of an optical instrument to distinguish two close objects.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing interference and diffraction.
- Forgetting coherent source conditions.
- Mixing bright and dark fringe equations.
- Forgetting polarisation proves transverse nature.
- Using wrong fringe width formula.
