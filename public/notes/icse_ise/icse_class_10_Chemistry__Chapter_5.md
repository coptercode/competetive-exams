# Introduction

The **Mole Concept** is one of the fundamental concepts in chemistry. It provides a link between the microscopic world of atoms and molecules and the measurable quantities used in laboratories. **Stoichiometry** deals with the quantitative relationship between reactants and products in a chemical reaction. Using balanced chemical equations, chemists can calculate masses, moles, volumes of gases, and the number of particles involved in chemical reactions.

---

# 1. Mole

## Definition

A **Mole** is the amount of substance containing **6.022 × 10²³** particles (atoms, molecules, or ions).

---

## Avogadro Constant

```
1 Mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles
```

This number is known as the **Avogadro Constant**.

---

# 2. Molar Mass

## Definition

**Molar Mass** is the mass of one mole of a substance.

---

## Unit

```
g/mol
```

---

## Example

- H₂O = 18 g/mol
- CO₂ = 44 g/mol
- NaCl = 58.5 g/mol

---

# 3. Relative Atomic Mass (Ar)

## Definition

The **Relative Atomic Mass (Ar)** is the average mass of an atom compared to **1/12th the mass of a carbon-12 atom**.

---

## Example

- H = 1
- O = 16
- C = 12
- Na = 23
- Cl = 35.5

---

# 4. Relative Molecular Mass (Mr)

## Definition

The **Relative Molecular Mass (Mr)** is the sum of the relative atomic masses of all atoms present in a molecule.

---

## Formula

```
Mr = Sum of Atomic Masses
```

---

## Example

### Water

```
H₂O

Mr = (2 × 1) + 16

= 18
```

---

### Carbon Dioxide

```
CO₂

Mr = 12 + (2 × 16)

= 44
```

---

# 5. Mole Formula

## Formula

```
Number of Moles

      Mass
= ----------------
   Molar Mass
```

---

## Rearranged Formula

```
Mass = Moles × Molar Mass
```

---

# 6. Avogadro's Law

## Statement

**Equal volumes of all gases under the same temperature and pressure contain an equal number of molecules.**

---

## Importance

- Explains gas volume relationships.
- Used in stoichiometric calculations.
- Basis of Gram Molecular Volume.

---

# 7. Gay-Lussac's Law of Combining Volumes

## Statement

When gases react, they combine in **simple whole-number ratios by volume**, provided all measurements are made at the same temperature and pressure.

---

## Example

```
2H₂ + O₂

↓

2H₂O
```

Volume Ratio

```
2 : 1 : 2
```

---

# 8. Gram Molecular Volume (GMV)

## Definition

The volume occupied by **one mole of any gas at STP**.

---

## Value

```
22.4 dm³

or

22.4 litres
```

---

## Conditions

- Temperature = 273 K
- Pressure = 1 atm

---

# 9. Vapour Density (VD)

## Definition

**Vapour Density** is the ratio of the mass of a given volume of a gas to the mass of an equal volume of hydrogen under the same conditions.

---

## Formula

```
Mr = 2 × Vapour Density
```

---

## Example

If Vapour Density = 22

```
Mr = 2 × 22

= 44
```

---

# 10. Empirical Formula

## Definition

The **Empirical Formula** gives the **simplest whole-number ratio** of atoms in a compound.

---

## Example

```
Glucose

Molecular Formula = C₆H₁₂O₆

Empirical Formula = CH₂O
```

---

# 11. Molecular Formula

## Definition

The **Molecular Formula** gives the **actual number of atoms** present in one molecule.

---

## Formula

```
Molecular Formula

=

(Empirical Formula)n
```

---

## Relation

```
n

=

Molecular Mass
------------------------
Empirical Formula Mass
```

---

# 12. Stoichiometry

## Definition

**Stoichiometry** is the calculation of the quantities of reactants and products involved in a chemical reaction.

---

## Steps in Stoichiometric Calculations

1. Write the balanced equation.
2. Calculate molar masses.
3. Convert mass into moles.
4. Apply mole ratio.
5. Convert back to required unit.

---

# Difference Between Empirical and Molecular Formula

| Empirical Formula       | Molecular Formula      |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Simplest ratio of atoms | Actual number of atoms |
| CH₂O                    | C₆H₁₂O₆                |
| Lowest ratio            | Exact composition      |

---

# Difference Between Mole and Molecule

| Mole                   | Molecule                      |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Quantity of substance  | Smallest independent particle |
| 6.022 × 10²³ particles | One particle                  |
| Used in calculations   | Used to represent compounds   |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **molar mass** with **molecular mass**.
- Forgetting that **1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles**.
- Using incorrect atomic masses in calculations.
- Mixing up **empirical** and **molecular** formulas.
- Forgetting that **GMV = 22.4 dm³ only at STP**.
- Ignoring the need to **balance chemical equations** before calculations.
- Confusing **Avogadro's Law** with **Gay-Lussac's Law**.

---

# Quick Revision

- Mole = **6.022 × 10²³ particles**.
- Moles = Mass ÷ Molar Mass.
- Mr = Sum of atomic masses.
- Mr = **2 × Vapour Density**.
- GMV at STP = **22.4 dm³**.
- Empirical Formula → Simplest ratio.
- Molecular Formula → Actual formula.
- Avogadro's Law → Equal gas volumes contain equal molecules.
- Gay-Lussac's Law → Gases combine in simple volume ratios.
- Stoichiometry → Quantitative study of chemical reactions.

# Chapter Summary

- The **mole** is the SI unit for the amount of substance and contains **6.022 × 10²³ particles**, known as **Avogadro's number**.
- **Relative atomic mass (Ar)** and **relative molecular mass (Mr)** help determine the masses of atoms and molecules, while **molar mass** is the mass of one mole of a substance.
- **Avogadro's Law** states that equal volumes of gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules, and **one mole of any gas occupies 22.4 dm³ at STP**, known as the **Gram Molecular Volume (GMV)**.
- **Gay-Lussac's Law of Combining Volumes** explains that reacting gases combine in simple whole-number volume ratios.
- **Empirical formulas** show the simplest ratio of atoms, whereas **molecular formulas** show the actual number of atoms in a molecule.
- **Stoichiometry** uses balanced chemical equations to calculate the quantities of reactants and products, making it essential in laboratory work, industrial chemistry, and scientific research.
