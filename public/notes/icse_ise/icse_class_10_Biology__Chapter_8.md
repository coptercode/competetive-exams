# Introduction

The **Excretory System** is responsible for removing **metabolic waste products** from the body and maintaining the internal environment (homeostasis). The major excretory organs are the **kidneys**, which filter blood to remove wastes such as **urea**, excess water, and salts. The kidneys also regulate the body's water balance, electrolyte balance, and blood pressure.

---

# 1. Excretion

## Definition

**Excretion** is the process of removing metabolic waste products and excess substances from the body.

---

## Excretory Products

- Urea
- Carbon dioxide
- Excess water
- Mineral salts
- Uric acid
- Creatinine

---

# 2. Human Excretory System

## Components

- Kidneys
- Ureters
- Urinary Bladder
- Urethra

---

### Kidneys

A pair of bean-shaped organs responsible for filtering blood and producing urine.

---

### Ureters

Muscular tubes that carry urine from the kidneys to the urinary bladder.

---

### Urinary Bladder

A muscular sac that temporarily stores urine.

---

### Urethra

A tube through which urine is expelled from the body.

---

# 3. Structure of the Kidney

Each kidney consists of:

- Renal Capsule
- Cortex
- Medulla
- Renal Pelvis
- Ureter

---

## Cortex

The outer region containing most of the nephrons.

---

## Medulla

The inner region containing renal pyramids and collecting ducts.

---

## Renal Pelvis

A funnel-shaped cavity that collects urine before it enters the ureter.

---

# 4. Nephron

## Definition

The **Nephron** is the structural and functional unit of the kidney.

Each human kidney contains about **one million nephrons**.

---

## Parts of a Nephron

- Bowman's Capsule
- Glomerulus
- Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)
- Loop of Henle
- Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)
- Collecting Duct

---

# 5. Bowman's Capsule

A cup-shaped structure that surrounds the glomerulus and collects the filtrate.

---

# 6. Glomerulus

A network of capillaries where **ultrafiltration** takes place.

---

# 7. Ultrafiltration

## Definition

**Ultrafiltration** is the filtration of blood under high pressure in the glomerulus, allowing water and small molecules to pass into Bowman's capsule.

---

## Substances Filtered

- Water
- Urea
- Glucose
- Amino acids
- Salts

---

## Substances Not Filtered

- Blood cells
- Plasma proteins

---

# 8. Selective Reabsorption

## Definition

**Selective Reabsorption** is the process by which useful substances are reabsorbed from the filtrate into the blood.

---

## Reabsorbed Substances

- Glucose
- Amino acids
- Most water
- Essential salts

---

## Site

Mainly in the **Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)**.

---

# 9. Tubular Secretion

## Definition

**Tubular Secretion** is the movement of additional waste substances from the blood into the nephron.

---

## Examples

- Hydrogen ions (H⁺)
- Potassium ions (K⁺)
- Ammonia
- Certain drugs

---

# 10. Urine Formation

Urine is formed in **three steps**:

```
Blood
   │
Ultrafiltration
   │
Selective Reabsorption
   │
Tubular Secretion
   │
Urine
```

---

# 11. Composition of Urine

Normal urine contains:

- Water (about 95%)
- Urea
- Uric acid
- Creatinine
- Mineral salts
- Pigments

---

## Normally Absent

- Glucose
- Blood cells
- Plasma proteins

---

# 12. Osmoregulation

## Definition

**Osmoregulation** is the maintenance of the body's water and salt balance.

---

## Importance

- Maintains water balance.
- Controls blood volume.
- Regulates electrolyte concentration.
- Prevents dehydration.

---

# 13. Role of ADH (Anti-Diuretic Hormone)

## Definition

**ADH** is a hormone secreted by the posterior pituitary gland that regulates water reabsorption in the kidneys.

---

## Functions

- Increases water reabsorption.
- Produces concentrated urine.
- Reduces water loss.
- Helps maintain blood pressure.

---

## During Dehydration

- More ADH is secreted.
- More water is reabsorbed.
- Less urine is produced.

---

## During Excess Water Intake

- Less ADH is secreted.
- Less water is reabsorbed.
- More dilute urine is produced.

---

# 14. Artificial Kidney (Dialysis)

## Definition

**Dialysis** is a medical procedure used to remove waste products and excess water from the blood when the kidneys fail to function properly.

---

## Uses

- Kidney failure
- Chronic kidney disease
- Temporary support until kidney transplant

---

# 15. Kidney Disorders

## Kidney Stones

Hard deposits of minerals formed inside the kidneys.

---

## Uraemia

Accumulation of urea in the blood due to kidney failure.

---

## Renal Failure

Loss of normal kidney function requiring dialysis or transplantation.

---

# Difference Between Excretion and Egestion

| Excretion                         | Egestion                      |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Removal of metabolic wastes       | Removal of undigested food    |
| Performed by kidneys, lungs, skin | Performed by digestive system |
| Includes urea and salts           | Includes faeces               |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Bowman's Capsule** with the **Glomerulus**.
- Assuming glucose is normally present in urine.
- Mixing up **Ultrafiltration** and **Selective Reabsorption**.
- Forgetting the role of **ADH**.
- Confusing **Excretion** with **Egestion**.
- Assuming all filtered substances are excreted.
- Forgetting that proteins and blood cells are not filtered under normal conditions.
- Mixing up the ureter and urethra.

# Chapter Summary

- The **Excretory System** removes metabolic wastes and maintains the body's internal balance through the coordinated functions of the **kidneys, ureters, urinary bladder, and urethra**.
- The **kidney** is made up of millions of **nephrons**, each consisting of the **glomerulus, Bowman's capsule, proximal convoluted tubule, Loop of Henle, distal convoluted tubule, and collecting duct**.
- Urine formation involves **three major processes**: **Ultrafiltration**, **Selective Reabsorption**, and **Tubular Secretion**.
- **Ultrafiltration** removes water and small solutes from blood, while **Selective Reabsorption** returns useful substances such as glucose, amino acids, water, and salts back into the bloodstream.
- **ADH (Anti-Diuretic Hormone)** regulates water reabsorption and plays a key role in **osmoregulation**, helping maintain water balance and blood pressure.
- Disorders such as **kidney stones, uraemia, and renal failure** affect normal kidney function and may require **dialysis** or kidney transplantation.
- A healthy excretory system is essential for maintaining homeostasis, removing toxic wastes, and ensuring the proper functioning of all body systems.
