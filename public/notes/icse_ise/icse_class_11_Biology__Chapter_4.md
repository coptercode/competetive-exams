# Introduction

The **Kingdom Animalia** consists of multicellular, eukaryotic, heterotrophic organisms that lack cell walls. Animals exhibit a high degree of organization, specialized tissues, locomotion, and complex organ systems. They occupy diverse habitats ranging from deep oceans to deserts and play essential roles in ecosystems.

Animals are classified based on:

- Level of organization
- Body symmetry
- Germ layers
- Body cavity (Coelom)
- Segmentation
- Presence or absence of notochord

The Animal Kingdom is broadly divided into:

- **Non-Chordates**
- **Chordates**

# 2. Levels of Structural Organisation

## A. Cellular Level

Cells are loosely arranged without forming tissues.

Example

```
Porifera
```

---

## B. Tissue Level

Cells form tissues.

Example

```
Cnidaria
```

---

## C. Organ Level

Different tissues combine to form organs.

Example

```
Platyhelminthes
```

---

## D. Organ System Level

Organs work together as organ systems.

Examples

- Arthropoda
- Chordata

---

# 3. Body Symmetry

## Asymmetrical

No plane divides the body into equal halves.

Example

```
Sponges
```

---

## Radial Symmetry

Any plane passing through the center divides the body equally.

Examples

- Hydra
- Jellyfish
- Starfish (Adult)

---

## Bilateral Symmetry

Only one plane divides the body into equal halves.

Examples

- Human
- Earthworm
- Cockroach

---

# 4. Germ Layers

## Diploblastic Animals

Body develops from

- Ectoderm
- Endoderm

Mesoglea lies between them.

Example

```
Cnidaria
```

---

## Triploblastic Animals

Body develops from

- Ectoderm
- Mesoderm
- Endoderm

Examples

- Earthworm
- Frog
- Human

---

# 5. Body Cavity (Coelom)

## Acoelomate

No body cavity.

Example

```
Platyhelminthes
```

---

## Pseudocoelomate

False body cavity.

Example

```
Aschelminthes (Nematoda)
```

---

## True Coelomate

Body cavity completely lined by mesoderm.

Examples

- Annelida
- Mollusca
- Arthropoda
- Echinodermata
- Chordata

---

# 6. Segmentation

## Metamerism

The body is divided into repeated segments.

Examples

- Earthworm
- Cockroach

---

# 7. Notochord

## Definition

A flexible rod-like supporting structure present during embryonic development.

# 8. Non-Chordates

---

# Phylum Porifera

## Characteristics

- Simplest multicellular animals.
- Cellular level organization.
- Asymmetrical.
- Numerous pores (Ostia).
- Water canal system.
- Skeleton of spicules or spongin fibres.

---

## Canal System

```
Water

↓

Ostia

↓

Spongocoel

↓

Osculum
```

---

## Examples

- Sycon
- Spongilla
- Euspongia

---

# Phylum Cnidaria (Coelenterata)

## Characteristics

- Radial symmetry.
- Diploblastic.
- Tissue level organization.
- Gastrovascular cavity.
- Cnidoblasts (stinging cells).

---

## Examples

- Hydra
- Aurelia
- Physalia
- Adamsia
- Coral

---

# Phylum Ctenophora

## Characteristics

- Marine animals.
- Eight rows of ciliary comb plates.
- Bioluminescent.
- Radial symmetry.

---

## Examples

- Pleurobrachia
- Ctenoplana

---

# Phylum Platyhelminthes

## Characteristics

- Flatworms.
- Bilateral symmetry.
- Triploblastic.
- Acoelomate.
- Mostly parasitic.

---

## Examples

- Taenia
- Fasciola
- Planaria

---

# Phylum Aschelminthes (Nematoda)

## Characteristics

- Cylindrical body.
- Pseudocoelom.
- Complete digestive tract.
- Separate sexes.

---

## Examples

- Ascaris
- Wuchereria
- Ancylostoma

---

# Phylum Annelida

## Characteristics

- True coelom.
- Metameric segmentation.
- Closed circulatory system.
- Nephridia for excretion.

---

## Examples

- Earthworm
- Leech
- Nereis

---

# Phylum Arthropoda

## Characteristics

- Largest animal phylum.
- Chitinous exoskeleton.
- Jointed appendages.
- Open circulatory system.
- Compound eyes.

---

## Examples

- Cockroach
- Honeybee
- Butterfly
- Crab
- Spider
- Scorpion

---

# Phylum Mollusca

## Characteristics

- Soft body.
- Muscular foot.
- Mantle present.
- Shell generally present.
- Radula for feeding.

---

## Examples

- Pila
- Octopus
- Unio
- Sepia

---

# Phylum Echinodermata

## Characteristics

- Exclusively marine.
- Endoskeleton of calcareous plates.
- Water vascular system.
- Tube feet.
- Adults show radial symmetry.

---

## Examples

- Starfish
- Sea Urchin
- Sea Cucumber

---

# 9. Chordates

## Diagnostic Features

- Notochord.
- Dorsal hollow nerve cord.
- Pharyngeal gill slits.
- Post-anal tail.
- Closed circulatory system.

# 10. Vertebrate Classes

---

## Pisces

### Characteristics

- Aquatic.
- Gills for respiration.
- Fins for movement.
- Two-chambered heart.
- Cold-blooded.

---

### Examples

- Rohu
- Shark

---

## Amphibia

### Characteristics

- Live on land and water.
- Moist skin.
- Three-chambered heart.
- Respiration by lungs and skin.

---

### Examples

- Frog
- Salamander

---

## Reptilia

### Characteristics

- Dry scales.
- Internal fertilization.
- Mostly three-chambered heart.
- Cold-blooded.

---

### Examples

- Snake
- Lizard
- Crocodile

---

## Aves

### Characteristics

- Feathers.
- Wings.
- Four-chambered heart.
- Warm-blooded.
- Oviparous.

---

### Examples

- Pigeon
- Peacock
- Crow

---

## Mammalia

### Characteristics

- Mammary glands.
- Hair present.
- Four-chambered heart.
- Warm-blooded.
- Mostly viviparous.

---

### Examples

- Human
- Tiger
- Whale
- Bat

---

# Comparison of Vertebrate Classes

| Class    | Heart       | Respiration  | Body Covering | Temperature  |
| -------- | ----------- | ------------ | ------------- | ------------ |
| Pisces   | 2 Chambers  | Gills        | Scales        | Cold-blooded |
| Amphibia | 3 Chambers  | Skin & Lungs | Moist Skin    | Cold-blooded |
| Reptilia | 3 Chambers* | Lungs        | Dry Scales    | Cold-blooded |
| Aves     | 4 Chambers  | Lungs        | Feathers      | Warm-blooded |
| Mammalia | 4 Chambers  | Lungs        | Hair          | Warm-blooded |

*Exception: Crocodile has a four-chambered heart.

# Difference Between Non-Chordates and Chordates

| Non-Chordates             | Chordates                                     |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Notochord absent          | Notochord present                             |
| Ventral nerve cord        | Dorsal hollow nerve cord                      |
| Gill slits absent         | Gill slits present (at least embryonic stage) |
| Heart dorsal (if present) | Heart ventral                                 |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Which phylum possesses a water vascular system?

### Solution

The water vascular system is a unique characteristic of **Echinodermata**.

### Answer

Echinodermata

---

## Example 2

### Question

Why is Arthropoda considered the largest phylum?

### Solution

It contains the maximum number of described animal species, including insects, arachnids, and crustaceans.

### Answer

Because it includes the largest number of animal species.

---

## Example 3

### Question

State one characteristic feature of chordates.

### Solution

Chordates possess a **notochord** at least during embryonic development.

### Answer

Presence of a notochord.

---

## Example 4

### Question

Which vertebrate class has feathers?

### Solution

Feathers are the distinguishing feature of **Class Aves**.

### Answer

Aves

## Example 5

### Question

Name one reptile having a four-chambered heart.

### Solution

Although most reptiles have a three-chambered heart, the **crocodile** possesses a four-chambered heart.

### Answer

Crocodile

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing radial symmetry with bilateral symmetry.
- Mixing up acoelomates and pseudocoelomates.
- Forgetting that **Arthropoda** has an open circulatory system.
- Assuming all reptiles have a three-chambered heart (crocodile is an exception).
- Confusing **Cnidaria** with **Ctenophora**.
- Forgetting that the **notochord** is the defining feature of chordates.

# Chapter Summary

- The **Animal Kingdom** includes multicellular, heterotrophic organisms that are classified based on structural organization, symmetry, germ layers, coelom, segmentation, and the presence of a notochord.
- **Non-chordates** include phyla such as **Porifera, Cnidaria, Platyhelminthes, Nematoda, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, and Echinodermata**, each possessing unique structural and functional characteristics.
- **Chordates** are distinguished by the presence of a **notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal gill slits, and post-anal tail**, and include vertebrates ranging from **Pisces to Mammalia**.
- The vertebrate classes exhibit progressive evolutionary adaptations in **circulatory systems, respiration, thermoregulation, and reproduction**.
- Understanding animal classification provides the basis for studying **comparative anatomy, physiology, evolution, ecology, zoology, veterinary science, and biodiversity conservation**.
