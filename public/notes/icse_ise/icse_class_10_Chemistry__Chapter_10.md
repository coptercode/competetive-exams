# Introduction

**Nitric Acid (HNO₃)** is a **strong mineral acid** that is colourless when pure but turns yellow upon exposure to light due to the formation of **nitrogen dioxide (NO₂)**. It is one of the most important industrial chemicals and is extensively used in the manufacture of **fertilizers, explosives, dyes, medicines, plastics, and laboratory reagents**. Industrially, nitric acid is manufactured by the **Ostwald Process**.

---

# 1. Nitric Acid

## Definition

**Nitric Acid** is a strong, highly corrosive mineral acid composed of hydrogen, nitrogen, and oxygen.

---

## Molecular Formula

```
HNO₃
```

---

## Nature

- Strong acid.
- Strong oxidizing agent.
- Colourless when pure.
- Turns yellow on storage.
- Highly corrosive.
- Completely ionizes in water.

---

# 2. Laboratory Preparation of Nitric Acid

## Principle

Nitric acid is prepared by heating **a nitrate salt** with **concentrated sulfuric acid**.

---

## Chemical Equation

Using Potassium Nitrate:

```
KNO₃ + H₂SO₄

↓

KHSO₄ + HNO₃
```

---

## Apparatus Required

- Round-bottom flask.
- Retort.
- Receiver cooled with water.

---

## Collection

Nitric acid vapours are condensed to obtain liquid nitric acid.

---

# 3. Industrial Preparation – Ostwald Process

## Principle

Ammonia is oxidized to nitric oxide, which is further oxidized and absorbed in water to produce nitric acid.

---

## Step 1: Oxidation of Ammonia

```
4NH₃ + 5O₂

↓

4NO + 6H₂O
```

Catalyst: Platinum-Rhodium Gauze

Temperature: ~800°C

---

## Step 2: Oxidation of Nitric Oxide

```
2NO + O₂

↓

2NO₂
```

---

## Step 3: Formation of Nitric Acid

```
4NO₂ + O₂ + 2H₂O

↓

4HNO₃
```

---

# 4. Physical Properties

- Colourless liquid.
- Sharp, irritating smell.
- Highly corrosive.
- Completely miscible with water.
- Turns yellow on exposure to light.
- Strong oxidizing agent.

---

# 5. Chemical Properties

---

## (i) Ionization in Water

```
HNO₃ + H₂O

↓

H₃O⁺ + NO₃⁻
```

---

## (ii) Reaction with Metals

Example:

```
Cu + 4HNO₃ (Conc.)

↓

Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO₂ + 2H₂O
```

### Observation

Brown fumes of **nitrogen dioxide (NO₂)** are evolved.

> **Note:** Nitric acid usually **does not liberate hydrogen gas** with metals because it is a strong oxidizing agent.

---

## (iii) Reaction with Metal Oxides

```
CuO + 2HNO₃

↓

Cu(NO₃)₂ + H₂O
```

---

## (iv) Reaction with Bases

```
NaOH + HNO₃

↓

NaNO₃ + H₂O
```

---

## (v) Reaction with Carbonates

```
CaCO₃ + 2HNO₃

↓

Ca(NO₃)₂ + CO₂ + H₂O
```

---

# 6. Oxidizing Nature of Nitric Acid

## Definition

Nitric acid acts as a **strong oxidizing agent** because it supplies oxygen during chemical reactions.

---

## Example

```
C + 4HNO₃

↓

CO₂ + 4NO₂ + 2H₂O
```

---

## Importance

- Oxidizes metals.
- Oxidizes non-metals.
- Used in chemical industries.

---

# 7. Uses of Nitric Acid

- Manufacture of fertilizers.
- Manufacture of explosives (TNT, Nitroglycerin).
- Manufacture of dyes.
- Manufacture of medicines.
- Metal etching.
- Gold purification.
- Laboratory reagent.
- Plastic manufacturing.

---

# 8. Aqua Regia

## Definition

A mixture of:

```
3 parts HCl

+

1 part HNO₃
```

---

## Uses

- Dissolves gold.
- Dissolves platinum.
- Cleaning laboratory apparatus.

---

# Difference Between Hydrochloric Acid and Nitric Acid

| Hydrochloric Acid             | Nitric Acid                  |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Formula: HCl                  | Formula: HNO₃                |
| Not an oxidizing agent        | Strong oxidizing agent       |
| Liberates H₂ with many metals | Usually does not liberate H₂ |
| Contains chloride ions        | Contains nitrate ions        |

---

# Difference Between Laboratory and Industrial Preparation

| Laboratory Method       | Ostwald Process                    |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Nitrate + Conc. H₂SO₄   | Ammonia oxidation                  |
| Small-scale preparation | Large-scale industrial production  |
| Simple distillation     | Catalytic oxidation and absorption |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **nitric acid** with **hydrochloric acid**.
- Forgetting that nitric acid is a **strong oxidizing agent**.
- Assuming nitric acid liberates **hydrogen gas** with metals.
- Mixing up the **Haber Process** (ammonia) and **Ostwald Process** (nitric acid).
- Forgetting the catalyst used in the Ostwald Process (**Platinum-Rhodium gauze**).
- Confusing **NO** (colourless) with **NO₂** (brown gas).
- Forgetting the composition of **aqua regia** (3:1 ratio of HCl to HNO₃).

---

# Quick Revision

- Formula → **HNO₃**
- Strong mineral acid.
- Strong oxidizing agent.
- Laboratory preparation → **Nitrate + Conc. H₂SO₄**.
- Industrial preparation → **Ostwald Process**.
- Catalyst → **Platinum-Rhodium gauze**.
- Brown gas → **NO₂**.
- Does not usually liberate **H₂** with metals.
- Aqua regia → **3HCl : 1HNO₃**.
- Uses → Fertilizers, explosives, dyes, medicines.

# Chapter Summary

- **Nitric acid (HNO₃)** is a strong mineral acid and powerful oxidizing agent widely used in industries and laboratories.
- It is prepared in the laboratory by heating **potassium nitrate or sodium nitrate with concentrated sulfuric acid**, while industrially it is manufactured by the **Ostwald Process**, involving the catalytic oxidation of ammonia.
- Nitric acid reacts with **metals, metal oxides, bases, and carbonates**, forming nitrates, but unlike most acids, it generally **does not evolve hydrogen gas** because of its oxidizing nature.
- During reactions with metals like copper, **brown nitrogen dioxide (NO₂)** gas is produced.
- Nitric acid is an essential raw material for the manufacture of **fertilizers, explosives, dyes, plastics, pharmaceuticals, and laboratory chemicals**.
- Together with hydrochloric acid, it forms **aqua regia**, which is capable of dissolving noble metals such as **gold and platinum**.
