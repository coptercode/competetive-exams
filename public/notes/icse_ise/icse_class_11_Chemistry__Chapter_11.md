# Introduction

**Organic Chemistry** is the branch of chemistry that deals with the study of **carbon compounds**, their structure, properties, preparation, reactions, and applications. Carbon has the unique ability to form stable covalent bonds with itself and other elements, giving rise to millions of compounds.

This chapter introduces the basic principles of organic chemistry, including **classification of organic compounds, nomenclature, isomerism, electronic effects, reaction intermediates, purification methods, qualitative and quantitative analysis**.

---

# 1. Organic Compounds

## Definition

Organic compounds are compounds containing **carbon** and usually **hydrogen**, along with elements such as oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur, phosphorus, and halogens.

---

# Characteristics of Organic Compounds

- Mostly covalent.
- Low melting and boiling points.
- Poor conductors of electricity.
- Combustible.
- Show isomerism.
- Exhibit catenation.

---

# Difference Between Organic and Inorganic Compounds

| Organic Compounds       | Inorganic Compounds              |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Mostly carbon compounds | Generally do not contain carbon  |
| Covalent                | Mostly ionic                     |
| Low melting point       | High melting point               |
| Poor conductors         | Good conductors (molten/aqueous) |

# 4. Homologous Series

## Definition

A series of organic compounds having the same functional group and similar chemical properties, with successive members differing by **–CH₂–**.

---

## Characteristics

- Same functional group.
- Similar chemical properties.
- Gradual change in physical properties.
- Common general formula.

---

# Example

| Compound | Formula |
| -------- | ------- |
| Methane  | CH₄     |
| Ethane   | C₂H₆    |
| Propane  | C₃H₈    |
| Butane   | C₄H₁₀   |

# 7. Electronic Effects

---

# Inductive Effect (I)

Permanent displacement of electrons through sigma bonds.

---

## Types

```
+I Effect

Electron Releasing
```

```
−I Effect

Electron Withdrawing
```

---

# Resonance Effect (R)

Delocalization of π-electrons within a molecule.

---

# Hyperconjugation

Delocalization involving σ-electrons adjacent to a π-system or carbocation.

---

# Difference Between Inductive and Resonance Effects

| Inductive Effect | Resonance Effect         |
| ---------------- | ------------------------ |
| Through σ bonds  | Through π electrons      |
| Permanent        | Permanent delocalization |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **common names** with **IUPAC names**.
- Forgetting the priority order of functional groups.
- Mixing up **chain** and **position** isomerism.
- Confusing **carbocations** with **carbanions**.
- Applying the wrong purification method.
- Forgetting the general formulas of alkanes, alkenes, and alkynes.

# Chapter Summary

- **Organic chemistry** deals with the study of **carbon compounds**, their structures, reactions, and applications.
- Organic compounds are classified based on their **carbon skeleton** and **functional groups**, and are systematically named using **IUPAC nomenclature**.
- **Isomerism** explains how compounds with the same molecular formula can differ in structure or spatial arrangement.
- **Electronic effects** such as the **inductive effect, resonance, and hyperconjugation** influence the stability and reactivity of organic molecules.
- Organic reactions proceed through intermediates such as **carbocations, carbanions, and free radicals**, leading to substitution, addition, elimination, and rearrangement reactions.
- Techniques like **distillation, crystallization, chromatography**, and **Lassaigne's test** are widely used for the purification and analysis of organic compounds, forming the basis for advanced studies in organic chemistry.
