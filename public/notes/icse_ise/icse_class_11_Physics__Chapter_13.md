# Introduction

**Waves** are disturbances that transfer **energy from one place to another without transferring matter**.

Waves are present everywhere in nature, including **sound waves, water waves, light waves, seismic waves, and electromagnetic waves**.

This chapter explains **wave motion, types of waves, wave equation, superposition principle, interference, standing waves, beats, and Doppler effect**.

---

# 1. Wave Motion

## Definition

A wave is a disturbance that propagates through a medium or space and transfers energy.

---

# Characteristics of Waves

- Transfer energy.
- Do not transfer matter permanently.
- Produced by oscillations.
- Have wavelength, frequency, and velocity.

---

# Examples

- Sound waves.
- Water waves.
- Light waves.
- Earthquake waves.

---

# 2. Types of Waves

---

# Mechanical Waves

Require a material medium for propagation.

Examples:

- Sound waves.
- Water waves.
- Seismic waves.

---

# Electromagnetic Waves

Do not require a medium.

Examples:

- Light.
- Radio waves.
- X-rays.

---

# Difference Between Mechanical and Electromagnetic Waves

| Mechanical Waves      | Electromagnetic Waves                    |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Require medium        | Can travel in vacuum                     |
| Produced by particles | Produced by electric and magnetic fields |
| Example: Sound        | Example: Light                           |

---

# 3. Transverse Waves

## Definition

Particles of the medium vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation.

---

# Examples

- Light waves.
- Waves on strings.
- Water surface waves.

---

# Characteristics

- Have crests and troughs.
- Can be polarized.

---

# 4. Longitudinal Waves

## Definition

Particles vibrate parallel to the direction of wave propagation.

---

# Examples

- Sound waves.
- Compression waves.

---

# Characteristics

- Have compressions and rarefactions.
- Cannot be polarized.

---

# Difference Between Transverse and Longitudinal Waves

| Transverse               | Longitudinal                    |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| Vibrations perpendicular | Vibrations parallel             |
| Has crests and troughs   | Has compression and rarefaction |
| Can be polarized         | Cannot be polarized             |

---

# 5. Important Terms in Waves

---

# Amplitude (A)

Maximum displacement of particles from their mean position.

---

# Wavelength (λ)

Distance between two successive points having the same phase.

Unit:

```
metre (m)
```

---

# Frequency (f)

Number of vibrations per second.

Unit:

```
Hertz (Hz)
```

---

# Time Period (T)

Time taken for one complete vibration.

Formula:

```
T = 1/f
```

---

# Wave Velocity

Distance travelled by wave per unit time.

Formula:

```
v = fλ
```

---

# Angular Frequency

```
ω = 2πf
```

---

# Wave Number

```
k = 2π/λ
```

---

# 6. Progressive Waves

## Definition

A wave that travels continuously through a medium transferring energy.

---

# Wave Equation

```
y = A sin(ωt − kx)
```

Where:

- y = Displacement
- A = Amplitude
- ω = Angular frequency
- k = Wave number

---

# Characteristics

- Energy transfer occurs.
- Amplitude remains constant.
- Particles vibrate with same frequency.

---

# 7. Wave Equation Derivation

General form:

```
y = A sin(ωt − kx)
```

---

Using:

```
ω = 2πf
```

and

```
k = 2π/λ
```

we get:

```
y = A sin 2π(ft − x/λ)
```

---

# 8. Principle of Superposition

## Statement

When two or more waves overlap, the resultant displacement is the algebraic sum of individual displacements.

---

# Formula

```
y = y₁ + y₂
```

---

# Applications

- Interference.
- Beats.
- Standing waves.

---

# 9. Interference of Waves

## Definition

The phenomenon of redistribution of energy when two waves overlap.

---

# Types

## Constructive Interference

Waves combine to increase amplitude.

Condition:

```
Path Difference = nλ
```

---

## Destructive Interference

Waves cancel each other.

Condition:

```
Path Difference = (2n+1)λ/2
```

---

# Difference Between Constructive and Destructive Interference

| Constructive        | Destructive         |
| ------------------- | ------------------- |
| Amplitude increases | Amplitude decreases |
| Same phase          | Opposite phase      |

---

# 10. Standing Waves

## Definition

A stationary wave formed due to interference of two waves having same frequency and amplitude travelling in opposite directions.

---

# Equation

```
y = 2A sin kx cosωt
```

---

# Characteristics

- No energy transfer.
- Fixed nodes and antinodes.
- Amplitude varies from point to point.

---

# Nodes

Points where displacement is always zero.

---

# Antinodes

Points where displacement is maximum.

---

# Distance Between

## Two successive nodes:

```
λ/2
```

---

## Node and antinode:

```
λ/4
```

---

# 11. Waves on a String

Velocity of transverse wave:

```
v = √(T/μ)
```

Where:

- T = Tension
- μ = Mass per unit length

---

# Factors Affecting Velocity

Velocity increases with:

- Increase in tension.

Velocity decreases with:

- Increase in mass density.

---

# 12. Harmonics and Overtones

---

# Fundamental Frequency

Lowest frequency of vibration.

---

# String Fixed at Both Ends

Frequency:

```
fn = n(v/2L)
```

Where:

- n = Harmonic number
- L = Length of string

---

# First Harmonic

```
f₁ = v/2L
```

---

# Second Harmonic

```
f₂ = 2v/2L
```

---

# Third Harmonic

```
f₃ = 3v/2L
```

---

# Applications

- Musical instruments.
- Guitar strings.
- Violin.
- Flute.

---

# 13. Beats

## Definition

Periodic variation in intensity produced when two waves of slightly different frequencies interfere.

---

# Beat Frequency

```
fb = |f₁ − f₂|
```

---

# Applications

- Tuning musical instruments.
- Measuring unknown frequency.

---

# 14. Doppler Effect

## Definition

The apparent change in frequency due to relative motion between source and observer.

---

# Examples

- Change in pitch of ambulance siren.
- Radar speed measurement.

---

# Formula

```
f' = f (v ± vo)/(v ∓ vs)
```

Where:

- f' = Apparent frequency
- f = Actual frequency
- vo = Observer velocity
- vs = Source velocity
- v = Velocity of sound

---

# Cases

## Source Approaching Observer

Frequency increases.

---

## Source Moving Away

Frequency decreases.

---

# Applications

- Astronomy.
- Medical ultrasound.
- Radar.
- Traffic monitoring.

---

# 15. Sound Waves

## Definition

Sound is a mechanical longitudinal wave produced by vibrating bodies.

---

# Characteristics

- Requires medium.
- Cannot travel in vacuum.
- Velocity depends on medium.

---

# Speed of Sound

In air:

```
≈ 343 m/s
```

---

# Factors Affecting Sound Velocity

- Temperature.
- Density.
- Pressure.

---

# Intensity of Sound

Power transmitted per unit area.

Formula:

```
I = P/A
```

---

# Decibel Scale

Sound intensity level:

```
β = 10 log(I/I₀)
```

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A wave has frequency 50 Hz and wavelength 4 m. Find velocity.

### Solution

```
v = fλ

= 50 × 4

= 200 m/s
```

### Answer

**200 m/s**

---

## Example 2

### Question

Find the frequency of a wave having time period 0.02 s.

### Solution

```
f = 1/T

= 1/0.02

= 50 Hz
```

### Answer

**50 Hz**

---

## Example 3

### Question

Two sound waves have frequencies 500 Hz and 504 Hz. Find beat frequency.

### Solution

```
fb = |504−500|

= 4 Hz
```

### Answer

**4 Hz**

---

## Example 4

### Question

A string has tension 100 N and mass per unit length 0.01 kg/m. Find wave velocity.

### Solution

```
v = √(T/μ)

= √(100/0.01)

= 100 m/s
```

### Answer

**100 m/s**

---

## Example 5

### Question

A sound source moves towards an observer. What happens to frequency?

### Solution

When source approaches:

```
Apparent frequency increases.
```

### Answer

**Frequency increases**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing frequency with wavelength.
- Forgetting that sound cannot travel in vacuum.
- Mixing up transverse and longitudinal waves.
- Using incorrect signs in Doppler effect.
- Forgetting that standing waves do not transfer energy.
- Confusing nodes and antinodes.

---

# Chapter Summary

- **Waves** are disturbances that transfer energy without permanent transfer of matter.
- Waves may be **mechanical or electromagnetic**, and mechanical waves may be **transverse or longitudinal** depending on particle vibration direction.
- The **wave equation**, **superposition principle**, and **interference** explain the behavior of multiple wave systems.
- **Standing waves** result from interference of opposite travelling waves and contain fixed nodes and antinodes.
- **Beats** occur due to interference of waves with slightly different frequencies, while the **Doppler effect** explains apparent frequency changes due to relative motion.
- Wave concepts are essential in **communication, music, medical imaging, astronomy, radar, sonar, and modern engineering technologies**.
