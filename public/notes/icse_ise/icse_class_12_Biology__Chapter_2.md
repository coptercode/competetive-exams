# Introduction

Sexual reproduction in flowering plants involves the formation of male and female gametes and their fusion to form a zygote.

The reproductive organ of angiosperms is the **flower**.

---

# 1. Flower as a Reproductive Structure

A typical flower consists of four whorls:

```
                 FLOWER
                    │
 ┌──────────────────┼──────────────────┐
 ▼                  ▼                  ▼
Calyx           Corolla          Androecium
(Sepals)        (Petals)          (Male)
                                      │
                                      ▼
                               Gynoecium
                                (Female)
```

---

# Floral Parts

## 1. Calyx

- Outer whorl.
- Made of sepals.
- Protects flower bud.

---

## 2. Corolla

- Made of petals.
- Attracts pollinators.

---

## 3. Androecium

Male reproductive part.

Consists of:

- Stamens

Each stamen has:

```
Stamen
  │
 ┌┴────┐
 │     │
Anther Filament
```

---

## 4. Gynoecium

Female reproductive part.

Consists of:

```
Carpel/Pistil

   Stigma
      │
     Style
      │
     Ovary
```

---

# 2. Structure of Anther

The anther contains pollen sacs.

A typical anther is:

- Bilobed
- Dithecous
- Four microsporangia

---

# Microsporangium

## Definition

A pollen sac where pollen grains are produced.

---

# Layers of Microsporangium Wall

From outside to inside:

```
Epidermis
    ↓
Endothecium
    ↓
Middle Layers
    ↓
Tapetum
```

---

# Tapetum

## Function

- Provides nutrition to developing pollen grains.
- Helps pollen wall formation.

---

# 3. Microsporogenesis

## Definition

Formation of male gametes through development of pollen grains.

---

# Process

```
Microspore Mother Cell
          │
          ▼
     Meiosis
          │
          ▼
   Microspore Tetrad
          │
          ▼
    Pollen Grain
```

---

# Pollen Grain Structure

A mature pollen grain contains:

## Outer Wall

### Exine

- Made of sporopollenin.
- Highly resistant.

---

## Inner Wall

### Intine

- Made of cellulose and pectin.

---

# Pollen Components

A mature pollen grain has:

1. Vegetative cell
2. Generative cell

---

Generative cell divides to form:

```
Two Male Gametes
```

## 7-celled and 8-nucleate

---

# Structure

```
              Embryo Sac

        Egg Apparatus
              │
     ┌────────┼────────┐
     ▼        ▼        ▼
 Synergid   Egg   Synergid
```

---

# Egg Apparatus

Located at micropylar end.

Contains:

- One egg cell

---

Contains:

Three cells at chalazal end.

# 7. Outbreeding Devices

Plants prevent self-pollination through:

---

## 1. Dichogamy

Different maturation time of anther and stigma.

Types:

- Protandry
- Protogyny

---

## 2. Herkogamy

Physical separation of anther and stigma.

---

## 3. Self-Incompatibility

Genetic mechanism preventing self fertilization.

---

## 4. Unisexuality

Male and female flowers separate.

# 9. Fertilization in Flowering Plants

## Double Fertilization

A unique feature of angiosperms.

It involves:

1. Syngamy
2. Triple fusion

---

# Syngamy

Fusion of:

```
Male gamete + Egg
```

Forms:

```
Diploid Zygote (2n)
```

---

# Triple Fusion

Fusion of:

```
Male gamete
+
Two Polar Nuclei
```

Forms:

```
Primary Endosperm Nucleus (3n)
```

---

# Double Fertilization Process

```
              Male Gametes
                    │
        ┌───────────┴───────────┐
        ▼                       ▼
      Egg                  Polar Nuclei
        │                       │
        ▼                       ▼
    Zygote              Endosperm
      (2n)                 (3n)
```

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. What is double fertilization?

**Answer:**

Double fertilization is a process in angiosperms where one male gamete fuses with egg to form zygote and another fuses with polar nuclei to form endosperm.

---

## 2. Why is tapetum important?

**Answer:**

Tapetum provides nutrition and helps in pollen wall formation.

---

## 3. Describe embryo sac structure.

**Answer:**

A mature embryo sac is 7-celled and 8-nucleate containing egg apparatus, polar nuclei, and antipodal cells.

---

## 4. Differentiate autogamy and xenogamy.

| Autogamy         | Xenogamy          |
| ---------------- | ----------------- |
| Same flower      | Different plant   |
| Self pollination | Cross pollination |

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing pollination with fertilization.
- Forgetting double fertilization products.
- Writing embryo sac as 8-celled.
- Confusing ovule and ovary development.
- Mixing microsporogenesis and megasporogenesis.

# Chapter Summary

- Sexual reproduction in flowering plants involves the formation of male and female gametes followed by fertilization.
- Microsporogenesis produces pollen grains, while megasporogenesis produces the embryo sac.
- Pollination transfers pollen to stigma and initiates pollen tube growth.
- Angiosperms show **double fertilization**, producing both zygote and endosperm.
- Post-fertilization events lead to formation of seeds and fruits.
- This process ensures genetic variation and continuation of flowering plant species.
