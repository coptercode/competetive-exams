# Introduction

**Gravitation** is the universal force of attraction between any two bodies having mass. It governs the motion of **planets, satellites, stars, galaxies**, and even objects falling on Earth.

Sir **Isaac Newton** formulated the **Universal Law of Gravitation**, while **Johannes Kepler** explained planetary motion through his three laws. This chapter also covers **acceleration due to gravity, gravitational potential, escape velocity, orbital motion, and satellites**.

---

# 1. Gravitation

## Definition

Gravitation is the mutual force of attraction between any two masses in the universe.

---

# Universal Gravitation

Every particle attracts every other particle with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

---

# Universal Law of Gravitation

## Statement

Every two bodies in the universe attract each other with a force directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

---

## Formula

```
          Gm₁m₂
F = ---------------
            r²
```

Where

- F = Gravitational force
- G = Universal gravitational constant
- m₁, m₂ = Masses
- r = Distance between their centres

---

## SI Unit

```
Newton (N)
```

---

## Dimensional Formula

```
[MLT⁻²]
```

---

# Universal Gravitational Constant (G)

## Value

```

```

---

## SI Unit

```

```

---

## Dimensional Formula


# Characteristics of Gravitational Force

- Always attractive.
- Acts along the line joining the centres.
- Long-range force.
- Independent of the medium.
- Obeys the inverse square law.

---

# Difference Between Gravitation and Gravity

| Gravitation                       | Gravity                                |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Attraction between any two masses | Attraction between Earth and an object |
| Universal phenomenon              | Special case of gravitation            |

# 2. Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion

---

# First Law (Law of Orbits)

Every planet moves around the Sun in an **elliptical orbit** with the Sun at one focus.

---

# Second Law (Law of Areas)

The line joining a planet and the Sun sweeps out **equal areas in equal intervals of time**.

---

# Third Law (Law of Periods)

The square of the time period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis.

```
T² ∝ r³
```

or

```
T²/r³ = Constant
```

---

# Applications of Kepler's Laws

- Planetary motion.
- Satellite orbits.
- Astronomy.
- Space exploration.

# 3. Acceleration Due to Gravity (g)

## Definition

Acceleration produced in a freely falling body due to Earth's gravitational pull.

---

# Formula

```
      GM
g = --------
      R²
```

Where

- M = Mass of Earth
- R = Radius of Earth

---

## SI Unit

```
m s⁻²
```

---

## Standard Value

```
g = 9.8 m s⁻²
```

---

# Relationship Between G and g

```
      GM
g = --------
      R²
```

---

# Variation of g with Altitude

At height

```
h
```

above Earth's surface,

```
          GM
gₕ = --------------
       (R+h)²
```

For

```
h << R
```

```
gₕ = g(1 − 2h/R)
```

---

# Variation of g with Depth

At depth

```
d
```

```
g_d = g(1 − d/R)
```

---

# Variation of g Due to Earth's Rotation

- Maximum at poles.
- Minimum at equator.

Reason:

Centrifugal force reduces effective gravity at the equator.

# 4. Gravitational Potential Energy

## Definition

The energy possessed by a body due to its position in a gravitational field.

---

# Near Earth's Surface

```
PE = mgh
```

---

# General Formula

```
         GMm
U = - --------
           r
```

---

# Characteristics

- Always negative.
- Zero at infinity.
- Increases towards zero as distance increases.

---

# Difference Between PE Near Earth and Universal PE

| Near Earth              | Universal        |
| ----------------------- | ---------------- |
| mgh                     | -GMm/r           |
| Valid for small heights | Valid everywhere |

# 6. Escape Velocity

## Definition

The minimum velocity required for a body to escape Earth's gravitational field without further propulsion.

---

# Formula

```
       2GM
Ve = √------
        R
```

or

```
Ve = √2gR
```

---

# Value for Earth

```
11.2 km s⁻¹
```

---

# Characteristics

- Independent of mass.
- Depends on the planet.

---

# Applications

- Spacecraft launch.
- Rocket science.
- Space missions.

# 7. Orbital Velocity

## Definition

The velocity required for a satellite to remain in a stable circular orbit.

---

# Formula

```
       GM
Vo = √------
        R+h
```

For orbit near Earth,

```

```

---

# Value Near Earth's Surface

```

```

---

| Escape Velocity            | Orbital Velocity |

# 8. Artificial Satellites

## Definition

An artificial satellite is a man-made object revolving around Earth.

---

# Types

### Geostationary Satellite

- Above equator.
- Time period = 24 h.
- Appears stationary.

Examples

- Communication satellites.

---

### Polar Satellite

- Passes over poles.
- Used for weather forecasting.
- Remote sensing.
- Mapping.

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Calculate the weight of a 60 kg person on Earth.

### Solution

```
W = mg

= 60 × 9.8

= 588 N
```

### Answer

**588 N**

---

## Example 2

### Question

Find the escape velocity of Earth if g = 9.8 m s⁻² and R = 6.4 × 10⁶ m.

### Solution

```
Ve = √2gR

= √(2 × 9.8 × 6.4×10⁶)

≈ 1.12 × 10⁴ m s⁻¹
```

### Answer

**11.2 km s⁻¹**

---

## Example 3

### Question

A satellite moves near Earth's surface. Find its orbital velocity.

### Solution

```
Vo = √gR

≈ 7.9 km s⁻¹
```

### Answer

**7.9 km s⁻¹**

---

## Example 4

### Question

Calculate the gravitational force between two masses of 5 kg each placed 2 m apart.

### Solution

```
F = Gm₁m₂/r²

= (6.67×10⁻¹¹ × 5 × 5)/4

≈ 4.17×10⁻¹⁰ N
```

### Answer

**4.17 × 10⁻¹⁰ N**

---

## Example 5

### Question

What is the weight of a body whose mass is 10 kg?

### Solution

```
W = mg

= 10 × 9.8

= 98 N
```

### Answer

**98 N**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **mass** with **weight**.
- Forgetting the negative sign in gravitational potential energy.
- Using **mgh** for large distances from Earth.
- Confusing **escape velocity** with **orbital velocity**.
- Assuming **g** is constant everywhere.
- Forgetting that **G** is universal while **g** varies with location.

# Chapter Summary

- **Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation** explains the attractive force between any two masses and forms the basis of celestial mechanics.
- **Kepler's laws** describe the motion of planets and are consistent with Newton's law of gravitation.
- The **acceleration due to gravity (g)** varies with altitude, depth, and Earth's rotation, while **mass remains constant** and **weight changes with gravity**.
- **Gravitational potential** and **potential energy** describe the energy associated with a body's position in a gravitational field.
- **Escape velocity** allows a body to leave Earth's gravitational field, whereas **orbital velocity** enables a satellite to remain in a stable orbit.
- Artificial satellites, including **geostationary** and **polar satellites**, play an essential role in communication, navigation, weather forecasting, remote sensing, and space exploration.
