# Introduction

The **p-block elements** are those in which the last electron enters the **p-orbital** of the valence shell. They occupy **Groups 13 to 18** of the Modern Periodic Table. In this chapter, the focus is on **Group 13 (Boron Family)** and **Group 14 (Carbon Family)**, their electronic configurations, properties, important compounds, anomalous behavior, and industrial applications.

---

# 1. Position of p-Block Elements

## General Electronic Configuration

```
ns² np¹–⁶
```

---

# Groups Included

```
13 → Boron Family

14 → Carbon Family

15 → Nitrogen Family

16 → Oxygen Family

17 → Halogens

18 → Noble Gases
```

---

# Difference Between s-Block and p-Block Elements

| s-Block                        | p-Block                         |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| Last electron enters s-orbital | Last electron enters p-orbital  |
| Groups 1 & 2                   | Groups 13–18                    |
| Mostly metals                  | Metals, metalloids & non-metals |

---

# 2. Group 13 Elements (Boron Family)

## Members

- Boron (B)
- Aluminium (Al)
- Gallium (Ga)
- Indium (In)
- Thallium (Tl)

---

# Electronic Configuration

```
ns² np¹
```

---

# General Properties

- Valency = 3.
- Oxidation state = +3.
- Metallic character increases down the group.
- Atomic radius increases down the group.
- Ionization enthalpy generally decreases.

---

# Anomalous Behaviour of Boron

Due to

- Small atomic size.
- High ionization enthalpy.
- Absence of d-orbitals.
- High electronegativity.

---

# Differences Between Boron and Other Group 13 Elements

| Boron                    | Other Elements         |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------- |
| Non-metal                | Metals                 |
| Forms covalent compounds | Mostly ionic compounds |
| Does not form B³⁺ ion    | Form M³⁺ ions          |
| High melting point       | Lower melting point    |

---

# Diagonal Relationship

Boron resembles silicon because of

- Similar atomic size.
- Similar electronegativity.
- Similar charge density.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Why does boron show anomalous behavior?

### Solution

Boron has a very small atomic size, high ionization enthalpy, and no vacant d-orbitals.

### Answer

**Due to its small size, high ionization enthalpy, and absence of d-orbitals.**

---

## Example 2

### Question

Name the hardest allotrope of carbon.

### Solution

Diamond has a three-dimensional tetrahedral network of strong covalent bonds.

### Answer

**Diamond**

---

## Example 3

### Question

Why is graphite a good conductor of electricity?

### Solution

Each carbon atom in graphite has one delocalized electron that can move freely between layers.

### Answer

**Because of the presence of free (delocalized) electrons.**

---

## Example 4

### Question

Write the chemical formula of borax.

### Solution

Borax is hydrated sodium tetraborate.

### Answer

**Na₂B₄O₇·10H₂O**

---

## Example 5

### Question

State one use of zeolites.

### Solution

Zeolites act as ion exchangers.

### Answer

**They are used for softening hard water.**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **borax** with **boric acid**.
- Mixing up **carbon monoxide (CO)** and **carbon dioxide (CO₂)**.
- Forgetting the amphoteric nature of **Al₂O₃**.
- Confusing **diamond** and **graphite** properties.
- Writing the wrong formula for **silica (SiO₂)** and **silicates**.
- Forgetting that **carbon shows maximum catenation**.

# Chapter Summary

- The **p-block elements** possess valence electrons in **p-orbitals** and include elements with a wide range of physical and chemical properties.
- **Group 13 (Boron family)** consists of boron and aluminium, with boron showing anomalous behavior due to its small size and aluminium exhibiting amphoteric nature.
- Important boron compounds such as **borax** and **boric acid** have significant industrial and medicinal applications.
- **Group 14 (Carbon family)** includes carbon and silicon, with carbon exhibiting exceptional **catenation** and existing in various **allotropic forms** such as diamond and graphite.
- Compounds like **carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, silica, silicates, and zeolites** play vital roles in industry, construction, water treatment, electronics, and environmental science.
- Understanding the chemistry of Groups **13 and 14** provides a foundation for advanced studies in **inorganic chemistry, materials science, and industrial applications**.
