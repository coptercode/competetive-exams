# Introduction

When an object is immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward force called **upthrust** or **buoyant force**. This phenomenon explains why some objects float while others sink. **Archimedes' Principle** and the **Law of Floatation** are fundamental concepts used in designing ships, submarines, hydrometers, and many other engineering applications.

---

# 1. Upthrust (Buoyant Force)

## Definition

**Upthrust** is the upward force exerted by a fluid on an object that is partially or completely immersed in it.

It acts opposite to the force of gravity.

---

## SI Unit

```
newton (N)
```

---

## Causes of Upthrust

Fluid pressure increases with depth.

Since the bottom surface of an immersed object is at a greater depth than its top surface:

- Pressure on the bottom is greater.
- Pressure on the top is smaller.

The difference in pressure produces an upward force called **upthrust**.

---

## Factors Affecting Upthrust

Upthrust depends on:

- Volume of fluid displaced
- Density of the fluid
- Acceleration due to gravity

### Formula

```
Upthrust = Volume of Fluid Displaced × Density × g
```

or

```
Fᵦ = Vρg
```

where:

- **Fᵦ** = Buoyant force
- **V** = Volume of displaced fluid
- **ρ** = Density of the fluid
- **g** = Acceleration due to gravity

---

## Apparent Loss of Weight

When an object is immersed in a fluid, its apparent weight decreases.

### Formula

```
Apparent Weight

= Actual Weight − Upthrust
```

---

# 2. Archimedes' Principle

## Statement

> **When a body is wholly or partially immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upthrust equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.**

---

## Mathematical Expression

```
Upthrust

= Weight of Fluid Displaced
```

---

## Verification of Archimedes' Principle

### Apparatus

- Eureka can
- Measuring cylinder
- Overflow vessel
- Water
- Solid object

### Procedure

1. Fill the Eureka can with water.
2. Allow excess water to overflow.
3. Place a measuring cylinder under the spout.
4. Immerse the object completely.
5. Collect the displaced water.
6. Measure its volume and weight.
7. Compare it with the loss of weight of the object.

### Observation

The weight of displaced water equals the apparent loss in weight of the object.

---

## Applications

- Ship design
- Submarines
- Hydrometers
- Lactometers
- Determining volume of irregular solids

---

# 3. Relative Density (Specific Gravity)

## Definition

Relative Density (RD) is the ratio of the density of a substance to the density of water at 4°C.

### Formula

```
Relative Density

= Density of Substance / Density of Water
```

---

## SI Unit

Relative Density has **no unit**, since it is a ratio.

---

## Relative Density Using Archimedes' Principle

```
RD

= Weight in Air

÷

Loss of Weight in Water
```

where

```
Loss of Weight

= Weight in Air − Weight in Water
```

---

## Interpretation

- RD > 1 → Substance sinks in water.
- RD < 1 → Substance floats on water.
- RD = 1 → Substance remains suspended.

---

# 4. Law of Floatation

## Statement

> **A floating body displaces a weight of fluid equal to its own weight.**

---

## Conditions for Floatation

An object floats when:

```
Weight of Object

=

Buoyant Force
```

or

```
Weight of Object

=

Weight of Fluid Displaced
```

---

## Floating and Sinking

### Object Floats

- Density of object < Density of liquid

### Object Sinks

- Density of object > Density of liquid

### Object Remains Suspended

- Densities are equal

---

## Centre of Gravity and Stability

### Stable Equilibrium

Centre of gravity remains below the metacentre.

Example:

- Ships

---

### Unstable Equilibrium

Centre of gravity lies above the metacentre.

Example:

- Overloaded boats

---

# 5. Applications of Floatation

## Ships

Ships are made of steel, which is denser than water.

They float because:

- Their hollow structure increases volume.
- Average density becomes less than that of water.
- They displace a large volume of water.

---

## Plimsoll Line

The **Plimsoll Line** is marked on ships to indicate the maximum safe loading level.

Functions:

- Prevents overloading.
- Ensures sufficient buoyancy.
- Improves safety.

---

## Submarines

Submarines use **ballast tanks**.

### To Dive

- Tanks are filled with water.
- Density increases.
- Submarine sinks.

### To Rise

- Water is pumped out.
- Air is filled into tanks.
- Density decreases.
- Submarine floats.

---

## Hydrometer

A hydrometer measures the relative density of liquids.

### Principle

It floats at different depths depending on the density of the liquid.

Applications:

- Battery testing
- Milk testing
- Alcohol industry

---

## Lactometer

A lactometer is a special type of hydrometer used to determine the purity of milk.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

An object weighs **60 N** in air and **45 N** when immersed in water.

Find the upthrust.

### Solution

```
Upthrust

= Weight in Air − Weight in Water

= 60 − 45

= 15 N
```

### Answer

```
15 N
```

---

## Example 2

### Question

A body weighs **80 N** in air and **60 N** in water.

Find its Relative Density.

### Solution

```
Loss of Weight

= 80 − 60

= 20 N

RD

= 80 / 20

= 4
```

### Answer

```
Relative Density = 4
```

---

## Example 3

### Question

A body displaces **0.02 m³** of water.

Given:

```
Density of water

= 1000 kg/m³

g = 9.8 m/s²
```

Find the buoyant force.

### Solution

```
Fᵦ

= Vρg

= 0.02 × 1000 × 9.8

= 196 N
```

### Answer

```
196 N
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **weight** with **apparent weight**.
- Assuming all heavy objects sink.
- Forgetting that floating depends on **average density**, not just mass.
- Using incorrect units while calculating Relative Density.
- Ignoring the volume of displaced fluid.
- Confusing **Archimedes' Principle** with the **Law of Floatation**.
- Forgetting that Relative Density has **no unit**.
- Incorrectly calculating loss of weight.

# Chapter Summary

- **Upthrust** is the upward force exerted by a fluid on an immersed object.
- Upthrust depends on the **volume of displaced fluid**, **density of the fluid**, and **gravitational acceleration**.
- **Archimedes' Principle** states that the upthrust on an immersed body equals the weight of the displaced fluid.
- The **apparent weight** of an object in a fluid is less than its actual weight.
- **Relative Density** is the ratio of the density of a substance to the density of water and has **no unit**.
- According to the **Law of Floatation**, a floating body displaces a weight of fluid equal to its own weight.
- Ships float because of their large volume and low average density.
- **Submarines** control buoyancy using ballast tanks.
- **Hydrometers** and **lactometers** measure the relative density of liquids.
- These principles have important applications in marine engineering, fluid mechanics, and industrial measurements.
