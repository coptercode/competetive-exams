# Introduction

Unlike animals, plants do not possess a nervous system. However, they coordinate their growth, development, and responses to environmental stimuli through **plant hormones (phytohormones)**. These chemical messengers regulate processes such as growth, flowering, fruit ripening, seed germination, dormancy, and responses to light, gravity, water, and touch.

---

# 1. Chemical Coordination

## Definition

**Chemical Coordination** is the regulation and control of various plant activities through chemical substances called **plant hormones (phytohormones).**

---

## Importance

- Regulates plant growth.
- Controls flowering and fruiting.
- Coordinates responses to environmental stimuli.
- Helps plants survive under stress.
- Controls seed germination and dormancy.

---

# 2. Plant Hormones (Phytohormones)

## Definition

**Plant Hormones** are naturally occurring chemical substances produced in small quantities that regulate plant growth and development.

---

## Major Plant Hormones

- Auxins
- Gibberellins
- Cytokinins
- Abscisic Acid (ABA)
- Ethylene

---

# 3. Auxins

## Definition

**Auxins** are growth-promoting hormones mainly produced in the shoot tips and young leaves.

---

## Functions

- Promote cell elongation.
- Stimulate root formation.
- Maintain apical dominance.
- Promote fruit development.
- Control phototropism.
- Control geotropism.

---

## Applications

- Rooting of stem cuttings.
- Weed control (synthetic auxins).
- Prevention of premature fruit drop.
- Development of seedless fruits.

---

# 4. Gibberellins

## Definition

**Gibberellins** are plant hormones that promote stem elongation and seed germination.

---

## Functions

- Stimulate stem growth.
- Break seed dormancy.
- Promote flowering.
- Increase fruit size.
- Stimulate enzyme production during germination.

---

## Applications

- Malting in brewing industry.
- Improving fruit size.
- Increasing crop yield.

---

# 5. Cytokinins

## Definition

**Cytokinins** are plant hormones that stimulate cell division.

---

## Functions

- Promote cell division.
- Delay ageing (senescence) of leaves.
- Stimulate lateral bud growth.
- Help in tissue culture.
- Promote nutrient movement.

---

## Applications

- Plant tissue culture.
- Increasing shelf life of vegetables and flowers.
- Crop improvement.

---

# 6. Abscisic Acid (ABA)

## Definition

**Abscisic Acid (ABA)** is a growth-inhibiting hormone known as the **stress hormone** of plants.

---

## Functions

- Closes stomata during water shortage.
- Induces seed dormancy.
- Inhibits growth.
- Promotes leaf and fruit fall.
- Helps plants survive drought.

---

## Importance

ABA helps plants conserve water during unfavorable conditions.

---

# 7. Ethylene

## Definition

**Ethylene** is a gaseous plant hormone involved in fruit ripening.

---

## Functions

- Accelerates fruit ripening.
- Promotes leaf fall.
- Promotes flower wilting.
- Breaks dormancy in some seeds.
- Enhances fruit maturation.

---

## Applications

- Artificial ripening of fruits.
- Synchronizing flowering.
- Agricultural crop management.

---

# 8. Tropic Movements

## Definition

**Tropic Movements** are directional growth movements of plants in response to external stimuli.

---

## Characteristics

- Growth movements.
- Permanent.
- Direction depends on the stimulus.
- Controlled mainly by auxins.

---

# 9. Types of Tropic Movements

---

## (i) Phototropism

### Definition

Growth response of a plant towards or away from light.

### Example

- Shoot bends towards sunlight.

---

### Types

**Positive Phototropism**

Growth towards light.

**Negative Phototropism**

Growth away from light.

---

## (ii) Geotropism (Gravitropism)

### Definition

Growth response of plants to gravity.

---

### Examples

Roots

→ Positive geotropism

Shoots

→ Negative geotropism

---

## (iii) Hydrotropism

### Definition

Growth response towards water.

---

### Example

Roots grow towards moist soil.

---

## (iv) Thigmotropism

### Definition

Growth response to touch.

---

### Example

Tendrils of climbers coil around support.

---

## (v) Chemotropism

### Definition

Growth response to chemicals.

---

### Example

Pollen tube grows towards the ovule due to chemical attraction.

---

# 10. Difference Between Plant Hormones

| Hormone     | Major Function                         |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Auxin       | Cell elongation                        |
| Gibberellin | Stem growth and seed germination       |
| Cytokinin   | Cell division                          |
| ABA         | Growth inhibition and stomatal closure |
| Ethylene    | Fruit ripening                         |

---

# 11. Difference Between Tropic and Nastic Movements

| Tropic Movement               | Nastic Movement                   |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Directional                   | Non-directional                   |
| Growth movement               | Usually turgor movement           |
| Permanent                     | Mostly temporary                  |
| Depends on stimulus direction | Independent of stimulus direction |
| Example: Phototropism         | Example: Folding of Mimosa leaves |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Auxins** with **Gibberellins**.
- Assuming **ABA** promotes growth (it inhibits growth).
- Forgetting that **Ethylene** is a gaseous hormone.
- Mixing up **Phototropism** and **Geotropism**.
- Confusing **Hydrotropism** with **Chemotropism**.
- Assuming all plant movements are tropic movements.
- Forgetting that tropic movements are **growth movements**.
- Mixing up **Tropic** and **Nastic** movements.

# Chapter Summary

- **Chemical coordination in plants** is carried out by **plant hormones (phytohormones)**, which regulate growth, development, and responses to environmental changes.
- **Auxins** promote cell elongation, root formation, and tropic movements such as phototropism and geotropism.
- **Gibberellins** stimulate stem elongation, seed germination, flowering, and fruit growth.
- **Cytokinins** promote cell division, delay ageing (senescence), and play a vital role in tissue culture.
- **Abscisic Acid (ABA)** acts as the **stress hormone**, inducing seed dormancy and closing stomata during water deficiency.
- **Ethylene**, the only gaseous plant hormone, promotes fruit ripening, leaf fall, and flower wilting.
- Plants exhibit **tropic movements** in response to external stimuli such as light (**phototropism**), gravity (**geotropism**), water (**hydrotropism**), touch (**thigmotropism**), and chemicals (**chemotropism**).
- Plant hormones are widely used in agriculture, horticulture, crop improvement, tissue culture, and fruit production, making them essential for modern plant science.
