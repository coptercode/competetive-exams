# Introduction

**Acids, Bases, and Salts** are important classes of chemical compounds found in everyday life. They play a vital role in industries, agriculture, medicine, food preservation, and laboratory reactions. According to the **Arrhenius Theory**, acids produce **hydrogen ions (H⁺)** or **hydronium ions (H₃O⁺)** in aqueous solution, while bases produce **hydroxide ions (OH⁻)**. Their properties can be studied using indicators, pH values, and chemical reactions.

---

# 1. Acids

## Definition (Arrhenius Theory)

An **Acid** is a substance that produces **H⁺ ions** (or **H₃O⁺ ions**) when dissolved in water.

---

## Examples

- Hydrochloric Acid (HCl)
- Sulfuric Acid (H₂SO₄)
- Nitric Acid (HNO₃)
- Acetic Acid (CH₃COOH)

---

## Properties of Acids

- Sour in taste.
- Turn **blue litmus red**.
- Conduct electricity in aqueous solution.
- React with metals to produce hydrogen gas.
- React with bases to form salts and water.
- Have **pH less than 7**.

---

# 2. Bases

## Definition (Arrhenius Theory)

A **Base** is a substance that produces **OH⁻ ions** in aqueous solution.

---

## Examples

- Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH)
- Potassium Hydroxide (KOH)
- Calcium Hydroxide [Ca(OH)₂]
- Ammonium Hydroxide (NH₄OH)

---

## Properties of Bases

- Bitter in taste.
- Slippery to touch.
- Turn **red litmus blue**.
- Conduct electricity in solution.
- Neutralize acids.
- Have **pH greater than 7**.

---

# 3. Alkalis

## Definition

An **Alkali** is a **water-soluble base**.

---

## Examples

- NaOH
- KOH
- NH₄OH

---

# 4. Indicators

## Definition

**Indicators** are substances that change colour in acidic or basic solutions.

---

## Common Indicators

| Indicator           | Acid       | Base        |
| ------------------- | ---------- | ----------- |
| Blue Litmus         | Red        | Blue        |
| Red Litmus          | Red        | Blue        |
| Methyl Orange       | Red        | Yellow      |
| Phenolphthalein     | Colourless | Pink        |
| Universal Indicator | Red–Orange | Blue–Violet |

---

# 5. pH Scale

## Definition

The **pH Scale** measures the acidity or alkalinity of a solution.

---

## Range

```
0 ---------------------- 7 ----------------------14
Acidic              Neutral                 Basic
```

---

## Interpretation

- pH < 7 → Acidic
- pH = 7 → Neutral
- pH > 7 → Basic

---

# 6. Neutralization Reaction

## Definition

A **Neutralization Reaction** is the reaction between an acid and a base to produce **salt and water**.

---

## General Equation

```
Acid + Base → Salt + Water
```

---

## Example

```
HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
```

---

# 7. Salts

## Definition

A **Salt** is an ionic compound formed when the hydrogen ion of an acid is replaced by a metal or ammonium ion.

---

# 8. Classification of Salts

---

## (i) Normal Salts

All replaceable hydrogen atoms are replaced.

### Examples

- NaCl
- Na₂SO₄

---

## (ii) Acid Salts

Contain replaceable hydrogen atoms.

### Examples

- NaHSO₄
- NaHCO₃

---

## (iii) Basic Salts

Contain hydroxyl groups.

### Example

- Basic Lead Carbonate

---

## (iv) Double Salts

Contain two simple salts crystallized together.

### Examples

- Potash Alum
- Mohr's Salt

---

# Difference Between Acids and Bases

| Acids             | Bases             |
| ----------------- | ----------------- |
| Produce H⁺ ions   | Produce OH⁻ ions  |
| Blue litmus → Red | Red litmus → Blue |
| pH < 7            | pH > 7            |
| Sour taste        | Bitter taste      |
| React with metals | Neutralize acids  |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

What is an acid according to Arrhenius?

### Solution

It releases hydrogen ions in aqueous solution.

### Answer

A substance that produces **H⁺ (or H₃O⁺) ions** in water.

---

## Example 2

### Question

What is the pH of a neutral solution?

### Solution

A neutral solution has equal concentrations of H⁺ and OH⁻ ions.

### Answer

```
7
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **bases** with **alkalis** (all alkalis are bases, but not all bases are alkalis).
- Forgetting that acids produce **H⁺ ions** and bases produce **OH⁻ ions**.
- Mixing up the colour changes of indicators.
- Assuming all salts are neutral.
- Confusing **acid salts** with **acidic salts**.
- Forgetting that pH **7** is neutral.
- Writing incorrect products for neutralization reactions.

# Quick Revision

- Acid → Produces **H⁺ ions**.
- Base → Produces **OH⁻ ions**.
- Alkali → Water-soluble base.
- pH < 7 → Acidic.
- pH = 7 → Neutral.
- pH > 7 → Basic.
- Neutralization → Acid + Base → Salt + Water.
- Normal Salt → No replaceable hydrogen.
- Acid Salt → Contains replaceable hydrogen.
- Double Salt → Two salts crystallized together.

# Chapter Summary

- **Acids** release **H⁺ (or H₃O⁺) ions**, while **bases** release **OH⁻ ions** in aqueous solutions according to the Arrhenius theory.
- **Indicators** such as litmus, methyl orange, phenolphthalein, and universal indicator help identify acidic and basic solutions.
- The **pH scale** ranges from **0 to 14**, where values below **7** are acidic, **7** is neutral, and above **7** are basic.
- **Neutralization reactions** produce **salt and water** when acids react with bases.
- Salts are classified into **normal salts, acid salts, basic salts, and double salts**, and can be prepared by several methods such as neutralization, direct combination, precipitation, and reactions of acids with metals or carbonates.
- Heating certain salts causes decomposition, producing gases and new compounds depending on the type of salt.
- Acids, bases, and salts have numerous applications in **industry, agriculture, medicine, laboratories, and daily life**.
