# Introduction

Light is a form of energy that enables us to see objects. It travels in straight lines and undergoes phenomena such as reflection, refraction, and dispersion. This chapter focuses on the reflection of light from plane and spherical mirrors, image formation, ray diagrams, mirror formula, sign conventions, and magnification.

---

# 1. Plane Reflection Rules

## Reflection of Light

Reflection is the bouncing back of light into the same medium after striking a surface.

---

## Laws of Reflection

### First Law

The incident ray, reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.

### Second Law

The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.

```
∠i = ∠r
```

---

## Terms Related to Reflection

### Incident Ray

The ray of light falling on a reflecting surface.

### Reflected Ray

The ray that bounces back after reflection.

### Normal

An imaginary line drawn perpendicular to the reflecting surface at the point of incidence.

### Angle of Incidence (i)

The angle between the incident ray and the normal.

### Angle of Reflection (r)

The angle between the reflected ray and the normal.

---

## Image Formed by a Plane Mirror

### Characteristics

- Virtual and erect
- Same size as the object
- Laterally inverted
- Formed behind the mirror
- Image distance equals object distance

---

## Lateral Inversion

Lateral inversion is the apparent reversal of left and right in the image formed by a plane mirror.

---

## Multiple Reflections

When two plane mirrors are inclined at an angle **θ**, the number of images formed is:

```
Number of Images (N)

= (360° / θ) − 1
```

If **360° / θ** is not an integer, then:

```
N = Integer part of (360° / θ)
```

### Special Case

For two parallel mirrors:

```
Number of Images = Infinite
```

---

# 2. Spherical Mirrors

A spherical mirror is a mirror whose reflecting surface is part of a hollow sphere.

---

## Types of Spherical Mirrors

### Concave Mirror

- Reflecting surface is curved inward.
- Also called a converging mirror.

### Convex Mirror

- Reflecting surface is curved outward.
- Also called a diverging mirror.

---

## Important Terms

### Pole (P)

The centre of the reflecting surface.

### Centre of Curvature (C)

The centre of the sphere of which the mirror is a part.

### Radius of Curvature (R)

Distance between the pole and the centre of curvature.

```
R = 2f
```

### Principal Axis

The straight line passing through the pole and the centre of curvature.

### Principal Focus (F)

The point where rays parallel to the principal axis meet (concave mirror) or appear to diverge from (convex mirror).

### Focal Length (f)

Distance between the pole and the principal focus.

---

# 3. Ray Diagram Construction

## Rules for Drawing Ray Diagrams

### Ray 1

A ray parallel to the principal axis passes through the principal focus after reflection.

---

### Ray 2

A ray passing through the principal focus becomes parallel to the principal axis after reflection.

---

### Ray 3

A ray passing through the centre of curvature retraces its path after reflection.

---

### Ray 4

A ray striking the pole reflects obeying the laws of reflection.

---

## Image Formation by Concave Mirror

| Object Position | Image Position  | Nature         | Size              |
| --------------- | --------------- | -------------- | ----------------- |
| At Infinity     | At Focus        | Real, Inverted | Highly Diminished |
| Beyond C        | Between C and F | Real, Inverted | Diminished        |
| At C            | At C            | Real, Inverted | Same Size         |
| Between C and F | Beyond C        | Real, Inverted | Enlarged          |
| At F            | At Infinity     | Real, Inverted | Highly Enlarged   |
| Between F and P | Behind Mirror   | Virtual, Erect | Enlarged          |

---

## Image Formation by Convex Mirror

| Object Position | Image Position         | Nature         | Size       |
| --------------- | ---------------------- | -------------- | ---------- |
| Anywhere        | Between Pole and Focus | Virtual, Erect | Diminished |

---

# 4. Mirror Formula

The mirror formula relates the object distance, image distance, and focal length.

### Formula

```
1/f = 1/v + 1/u
```

where:

- **f** = Focal Length
- **u** = Object Distance
- **v** = Image Distance

---

## Cartesian Sign Convention

### Rules

- All distances are measured from the pole.
- Distances measured in the direction of incident light are positive.
- Distances measured opposite to the direction of incident light are negative.
- Heights above the principal axis are positive.
- Heights below the principal axis are negative.

---

## Sign Convention Table

| Quantity                   | Concave Mirror | Convex Mirror |
| -------------------------- | -------------- | ------------- |
| Focal Length (f)           | Negative       | Positive      |
| Radius of Curvature (R)    | Negative       | Positive      |
| Object Distance (u)        | Negative       | Negative      |
| Real Image Distance (v)    | Negative       | —             |
| Virtual Image Distance (v) | Positive       | Positive      |

---

# 5. Linear Magnification

Magnification indicates how much larger or smaller the image is compared to the object.

### Formula

```
m = h₂ / h₁
```

where:

- **m** = Magnification
- **h₁** = Height of Object
- **h₂** = Height of Image

---

Another useful relation:

```
m = -v/u
```

---

## Interpretation

### Positive Magnification

- Image is virtual.
- Image is erect.

### Negative Magnification

- Image is real.
- Image is inverted.

### Magnitude of Magnification

- **m > 1** → Enlarged Image
- **m = 1** → Same Size
- **m < 1** → Diminished Image

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A concave mirror has a focal length of **15 cm**. An object is placed **30 cm** in front of the mirror.

Find the image distance.

### Solution

Using:

```
1/f = 1/v + 1/u
```

According to sign convention:

```
f = -15 cm

u = -30 cm
```

```
1/(-15) = 1/v + 1/(-30)

1/v = -1/30

v = -30 cm
```

### Answer

```
Image Distance = -30 cm
```

---

## Example 2

### Question

An object is **8 cm** high and its image is **16 cm** high.

Find the magnification.

### Solution

```
m = h₂/h₁

= 16/8

= 2
```

### Answer

```
Magnification = 2
```

---

## Example 3

### Question

Two plane mirrors are inclined at **90°**.

Find the number of images formed.

### Solution

```
N = (360° / 90°) − 1

= 4 − 1

= 3
```

### Answer

```
3 Images
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing concave and convex mirrors.
- Forgetting the Cartesian sign convention.
- Using incorrect signs in the mirror formula.
- Confusing real and virtual images.
- Forgetting that convex mirrors always form virtual and diminished images.
- Incorrectly calculating magnification.
- Drawing inaccurate ray diagrams.
- Mixing radius of curvature and focal length.

# Chapter Summary

- **Reflection** is the bouncing back of light from a surface.
- The **laws of reflection** state that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, and the incident ray, reflected ray, and normal lie in the same plane.
- **Plane mirrors** form virtual, erect, and laterally inverted images.
- **Concave mirrors** can produce both real and virtual images depending on the object's position.
- **Convex mirrors** always produce virtual, erect, and diminished images.
- The **mirror formula** relates focal length, object distance, and image distance.
- The **Cartesian sign convention** is essential for solving numerical problems correctly.
- **Magnification** determines the size and orientation of the image.
- Spherical mirrors have numerous applications in transportation, medicine, astronomy, and daily life.
