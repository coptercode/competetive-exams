# Introduction

**Radioactivity** is the spontaneous disintegration of unstable atomic nuclei accompanied by the emission of invisible radiations. It was discovered by **Henri Becquerel** in **1896**, and later extensively studied by **Marie Curie** and **Pierre Curie**. Radioactivity has numerous applications in **medicine, agriculture, industry, archaeology, and nuclear power generation**, but excessive exposure can be harmful to living organisms.

---

# 1. Radioactivity

## Definition

**Radioactivity** is the spontaneous emission of radiations from the nucleus of an unstable atom without any external influence.

---

## Characteristics

- Spontaneous process.
- Random in nature.
- Independent of temperature, pressure, and chemical reactions.
- Accompanied by the emission of α, β, and γ radiations.
- Causes the nucleus to become more stable.

---

# 2. Discovery of Radioactivity

## Henri Becquerel (1896)

- Discovered natural radioactivity while working with uranium salts.
- Observed that uranium emitted invisible radiations capable of blackening photographic plates.

---

## Marie and Pierre Curie

- Discovered radioactive elements:
  - Radium (Ra)
  - Polonium (Po)
- Coined the term **Radioactivity**.

---

# 3. Radioactive Elements

## Examples

- Uranium (U)
- Radium (Ra)
- Thorium (Th)
- Polonium (Po)

---

# 4. Types of Radioactive Radiations

There are three types:

1. Alpha (α) Rays
2. Beta (β) Rays
3. Gamma (γ) Rays

---

# (i) Alpha Rays (α)

## Nature

Helium nuclei

```
²⁴He
```

---

## Charge

```
+2
```

---

## Mass

```
4 u
```

---

## Properties

- Positively charged.
- Heavy particles.
- Low penetrating power.
- High ionising power.
- Deflected by electric and magnetic fields.

---

## Penetration

Stopped by:

```
Paper Sheet
```

---

# (ii) Beta Rays (β)

## Nature

Fast-moving electrons.

---

## Charge

```
−1
```

---

## Mass

Very small

```
1/1837 u
```

---

## Properties

- Negatively charged.
- Moderate penetrating power.
- Moderate ionising power.
- Deflected strongly by electric and magnetic fields.

---

## Penetration

Stopped by:

```
Aluminium Sheet
```

---

# (iii) Gamma Rays (γ)

## Nature

Electromagnetic waves.

---

## Charge

```
No Charge
```

---

## Mass

```
Zero
```

---

## Properties

- Highly penetrating.
- Weak ionising power.
- Travel with the speed of light.
- Not deflected by electric or magnetic fields.

---

## Penetration

Stopped by:

```
Thick Lead
or
Concrete
```

---

# Comparison of α, β and γ Rays

| Property                    | Alpha (α)      | Beta (β) | Gamma (γ)            |
| --------------------------- | -------------- | -------- | -------------------- |
| Nature                      | Helium nucleus | Electron | Electromagnetic wave |
| Charge                      | +2             | −1       | 0                    |
| Mass                        | 4 u            | 1/1837 u | 0                    |
| Speed                       | Low            | High     | Speed of light       |
| Penetrating Power           | Low            | Medium   | Very High            |
| Ionising Power              | High           | Medium   | Low                  |
| Deflected by Electric Field | Yes            | Yes      | No                   |
| Deflected by Magnetic Field | Yes            | Yes      | No                   |

# 5. Radioactive Decay

## Definition

The spontaneous disintegration of an unstable nucleus into a more stable nucleus by emitting radiations.

---

## Example

```
Uranium

↓

Thorium

↓

Radium

↓

Lead (Stable)
```

---

# 6. Half-Life

## Definition

**Half-life** is the time required for half of the radioactive atoms in a sample to decay.

---

## Symbol

```
T½
```

---

## Characteristics

- Constant for each radioactive element.
- Independent of external conditions.

---

# 7. Background Radiation

## Definition

The natural radiation present in the environment.

---

## Sources

- Cosmic rays.

- Soil.
- Air.
- Radioactive materials.
- Medical equipment.

# 9. Hazards of Radioactivity

Excessive exposure may cause:

- Skin burns.
- Cancer.
- Genetic mutations.
- Sterility.
- Cell damage.
- Radiation sickness.

---

# Safety Precautions

- Lead shielding.
- Protective clothing.
- Minimize exposure time.
- Maintain safe distance.
- Use remote handling devices.

# Difference Between Alpha, Beta and Gamma Rays

| Alpha            | Beta                 | Gamma                |
| ---------------- | -------------------- | -------------------- |
| Heavy particle   | Electron             | Electromagnetic wave |
| Low penetration  | Moderate penetration | Highest penetration  |
| High ionisation  | Moderate ionisation  | Low ionisation       |
| Stopped by paper | Stopped by aluminium | Stopped by lead      |

---

# Difference Between Radioactivity and Nuclear Fission

| Radioactivity           | Nuclear Fission               |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Natural process         | Artificial or induced process |
| Spontaneous decay       | Splitting of heavy nucleus    |
| Releases α, β or γ rays | Produces enormous energy      |

## Example 2

Which radioactive radiation has the greatest penetrating power?

### Solution

Among α, β, and γ rays, gamma rays penetrate the most.

### Answer

**Gamma (γ) Rays.**

### Question

Define half-life.

### Solution

Half-life refers to the decay of radioactive nuclei.

### Answer

The time required for half of the radioactive atoms in a sample to decay.

---

### Question

Name one medical application of radioactivity.

### Solution

Radioactive isotopes are widely used in hospitals.

### Answer

**Cancer treatment (Radiotherapy).**

---

## Example 5

### Question

Why are alpha particles less penetrating than gamma rays?

### Solution

Alpha particles are heavy and interact strongly with matter, whereas gamma rays are electromagnetic waves with no mass or charge.

### Answer

Because alpha particles have greater mass and charge, they lose energy quickly and are easily absorbed.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **radioactivity** with **nuclear fission**.
- Mixing up the properties of **α, β, and γ rays**.
- Assuming **gamma rays have mass or charge** (they do not).
- Forgetting that **half-life is constant** for a given radioactive element.
- Confusing **penetrating power** with **ionising power**.
- Ignoring radiation safety measures.

# Chapter Summary

- **Radioactivity** is the spontaneous disintegration of unstable atomic nuclei, discovered by **Henri Becquerel**, with important contributions from **Marie and Pierre Curie**.
- Radioactive substances emit **alpha (α)**, **beta (β)**, and **gamma (γ)** radiations, each having distinct properties in terms of **charge, mass, ionising power, and penetrating power**.
- The **half-life** of a radioactive substance is the fixed time required for half of its radioactive nuclei to decay, and it is unaffected by physical or chemical conditions.
- Radioactivity has numerous beneficial applications in **medicine, industry, agriculture, archaeology, scientific research, and nuclear power generation**.
- However, excessive exposure to radioactive radiation can cause **serious health hazards**, making proper **radiation safety measures** such as lead shielding and controlled exposure essential.
- The study of radioactivity has transformed modern science and technology, contributing significantly to healthcare, energy production, and our understanding of atomic structure.
