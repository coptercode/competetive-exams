# Introduction

Electricity is an essential part of our daily lives. **Household circuits** distribute electrical energy safely from the power station to our homes. This chapter explains the **domestic electric supply, live, neutral and earth wires, switches, sockets, fuses, miniature circuit breakers (MCBs), earthing, electrical hazards, and safety precautions**.

---

# 1. Domestic Electric Supply

## Definition

A **domestic electric supply** is the electrical power supplied to homes from the distribution network.

---

## Supply in India

```
Voltage = 230 V

Frequency = 50 Hz

Type = Alternating Current (AC)
```

---

## Source

```
Power Station
      │
Transmission Lines
      │
Distribution Transformer
      │
Household Supply
```

---

# 2. Alternating Current (AC)

## Definition

**Alternating Current (AC)** is the electric current whose magnitude and direction change periodically.

---

## Characteristics

- Changes direction 50 times per second (50 Hz).
- Used for domestic power supply.
- Can be transmitted over long distances efficiently.

---

# 4. Wires Used in Household Circuits

---

## (i) Live Wire

### Colour

```
Brown
```

(Older systems: Red)

---

### Function

- Carries current from the supply to the appliance.
- Has high potential.

---

## (ii) Neutral Wire

### Colour

```
Blue
```

(Older systems: Black)

---

### Function

Returns current to the source.

---

## (iii) Earth Wire

### Colour

```
Green or Green-Yellow
```

---

### Function

Provides protection against electric shock by carrying leakage current safely to the ground.

---

# Difference Between Live, Neutral and Earth Wire

| Live Wire                    | Neutral Wire    | Earth Wire              |
| ---------------------------- | --------------- | ----------------------- |
| Brown                        | Blue            | Green/Green-Yellow      |
| High potential               | Zero potential  | Safety wire             |
| Carries current to appliance | Returns current | Carries leakage current |

---

# 5. Switch

## Definition

A **switch** is a device used to make or break an electric circuit.

---

## Position

Always connected in the **live wire**.

---

## Reason

Switching off disconnects the appliance from the high-voltage live wire, making it safe.

---

# 6. Fuse

## Definition

A **fuse** is a safety device that protects electrical appliances from excessive current.

---

## Working Principle

Works on the **heating effect of electric current**.

---

## Characteristics

- Low melting point.
- High resistance.
- Connected in series.

---

## Function

When excessive current flows:

```
Fuse melts

↓

Circuit breaks

↓

Appliance protected
```

---

# 7. Miniature Circuit Breaker (MCB)

## Definition

An **MCB** is an automatic electrical safety device that switches off the circuit during overload or short circuit.

---

## Advantages Over Fuse

- Reusable.
- Operates automatically.
- Faster response.
- More reliable.
- No replacement needed.

---

# Difference Between Fuse and MCB

| Fuse                  | MCB                 |
| --------------------- | ------------------- |
| Melts during overload | Trips automatically |
| Must be replaced      | Can be reset        |
| Cheaper               | More expensive      |
| Slower                | Faster              |

---

# 8. Earthing

## Definition

**Earthing** is the process of connecting the metal body of an electrical appliance to the earth through a low-resistance wire.

---

## Purpose

- Prevents electric shock.
- Protects appliances.
- Provides a safe path for leakage current.

---

## Earthing Diagram

```
Appliance Body
      │
 Earth Wire
      │
 Earth Plate
      │
 Ground
```

---

# 9. Short Circuit

## Definition

A **short circuit** occurs when the live wire comes into direct contact with the neutral wire, causing a large current to flow.

---

## Causes

- Damaged insulation.
- Loose connections.
- Faulty appliances.
- Wet conditions.

---

## Effects

- Fire.
- Fuse melts.
- MCB trips.
- Damage to appliances.

---

# 10. Overloading

## Definition

**Overloading** occurs when the current drawn exceeds the safe carrying capacity of the circuit.

---

## Causes

- Too many appliances connected to one socket.
- High-power appliances used simultaneously.

---

## Prevention

- Use separate circuits.
- Install MCBs.
- Avoid multiple adapters.
- Use correct fuse ratings.

---

# Difference Between Short Circuit and Overloading

| Short Circuit        | Overloading                   |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Live touches neutral | Too many appliances connected |
| Very high current    | Current exceeds safe limit    |
| Sudden fault         | Gradual increase in load      |

---

# 11. Electric Shock

## Causes

- Touching a live wire.
- Wet hands.
- Damaged insulation.
- Faulty appliances.

---

## Safety Measures

- Use insulated wires.
- Never touch switches with wet hands.
- Use proper earthing.
- Install MCBs and fuses.
- Switch off the main supply before repairs.

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

What is the domestic electric supply in India?

### Solution

Domestic electricity is supplied as alternating current.

### Answer

```
230 V, 50 Hz AC
```

---

## Example 2

### Question

Why is the switch connected in the live wire?

### Solution

Disconnecting the live wire cuts off the high-voltage supply to the appliance.

### Answer

To disconnect the appliance from the live supply and ensure user safety.

---

## Example 3

### Question

State the function of the earth wire.

### Solution

The earth wire carries leakage current safely to the ground.

### Answer

It protects users from electric shock by providing a low-resistance path to earth.

---

## Example 4

### Question

Name one advantage of an MCB over a fuse.

### Solution

An MCB operates automatically and can be reused.

### Answer

It trips automatically and can be reset without replacement.

---

## Example 5

### Question

Differentiate between short circuit and overloading.

### Solution

A short circuit is caused by direct contact between live and neutral wires, while overloading occurs when excessive current is drawn due to too many appliances.

### Answer

- **Short Circuit:** Live wire touches neutral wire, causing a sudden large current.
- **Overloading:** Excessive current flows because the connected load exceeds the circuit's capacity.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing the functions of **live**, **neutral**, and **earth** wires.
- Assuming the **switch is connected in the neutral wire** (it must be connected in the **live wire**).
- Thinking that a **fuse** and an **MCB** work in the same way.
- Ignoring the importance of **earthing**.
- Connecting too many appliances to a single socket, leading to overloading.
- Forgetting that domestic electricity in India is **230 V AC at 50 Hz**.

# Chapter Summary

- A **household circuit** safely distributes electrical energy from the power supply to various appliances through **live, neutral, and earth wires**.
- In India, domestic electricity is supplied as **230 V, 50 Hz alternating current (AC)**.
- The **live wire** carries current to the appliance, the **neutral wire** returns it to the source, and the **earth wire** protects users by safely carrying leakage current to the ground.
- **Switches**, **fuses**, and **Miniature Circuit Breakers (MCBs)** are essential safety devices that prevent damage due to excessive current, short circuits, and overloading.
- **Earthing** plays a crucial role in preventing electric shocks and ensuring the safe operation of electrical appliances.
- Understanding household circuits helps in the safe use, maintenance, and efficient management of electrical systems in homes, schools, industries, and public buildings.
