# Introduction

An **ecosystem** is a functional unit of nature where living organisms interact with each other and with the physical environment.

---

# Definition

According to ecology:

An ecosystem consists of:

```
Living Components + Non-living Components
```

interacting through:

- Energy flow
- Nutrient cycling

---

# 1. Components of Ecosystem

An ecosystem has two major components:

```
              Ecosystem

                  │

      ┌───────────┴───────────┐

      ▼                       ▼

 Abiotic Components      Biotic Components
```

---

# 2. Abiotic Components

## Definition

Non-living physical and chemical factors of an ecosystem.

---

# Examples

## Physical Factors

- Temperature
- Light
- Water
- Soil

---

## Chemical Factors

- pH
- Minerals
- Oxygen
- Carbon dioxide

---

# 3. Biotic Components

Living organisms of an ecosystem.

---

Types:

```
Biotic Components

        │

 ┌──────┼──────┐

 ▼      ▼      ▼

Producers Consumers Decomposers
```

---

# 4. Producers

## Definition

Organisms that prepare their own food through photosynthesis.

---

Examples:

- Green plants
- Algae

---

Function:

Convert:

```
Solar Energy → Chemical Energy
```

---

# 5. Consumers

Organisms dependent on other organisms for food.

---

Types:

## Primary Consumers

Herbivores.

Examples:

- Deer
- Rabbit

---

## Secondary Consumers

Primary carnivores.

Examples:

- Frog
- Small fish

---

## Tertiary Consumers

Top carnivores.

Examples:

- Lion
- Eagle

---

# 6. Decomposers

Organisms that break down dead organic matter.

Examples:

- Bacteria
- Fungi

---

Functions:

- Release nutrients
- Maintain nutrient cycles

---

                Ecosystem

# 9. Energy Flow in Ecosystem

Energy flow occurs through food chains.

---

# Source of Energy

```
Sun
 ↓
Plants
 ↓
Animals
```

---

# 10. Food Chain

## Definition

A sequence showing transfer of food and energy from one organism to another.

---

Example:

```
Grass
 ↓
Grasshopper
 ↓
Frog
 ↓
Snake
 ↓
Eagle
```

---

# Types of Food Chain

## 1. Grazing Food Chain

Starts from green plants.

Example:

```
Grass → Deer → Tiger
```

---

## 2. Detritus Food Chain

Starts from dead organic matter.

Example:

```
Dead Leaves → Earthworm → Bird
```

---

# 11. Food Web

## Definition

A network of interconnected food chains.

---

Importance:

- Maintains ecosystem stability.
- Provides alternative food sources.

---

# 12. Ecological Trophic Levels

Each feeding level in a food chain is called a trophic level.

---

Example:

```
1st Trophic Level
      ↓
Producers

2nd Trophic Level
      ↓
Herbivores

3rd Trophic Level
      ↓
Carnivores

4th Trophic Level
      ↓
Top Carnivores
```

---

# 13. Energy Transfer

According to:

## Ten Percent Law

Proposed by:

### Raymond Lindeman

---

Only:

```
10%
```

of energy passes from one trophic level to the next.

---

Example:

```
Plants
10000 J

     ↓

Herbivores
1000 J

     ↓

Carnivores
100 J
```

---

# 14. Ecological Pyramids

## Definition

Graphical representation of trophic structure.

---

Types:

```
Ecological Pyramid

        │

 ┌──────┼──────┐

 ▼      ▼      ▼

Number Biomass Energy
```

---

# 15. Pyramid of Numbers

Represents:

Number of organisms at each trophic level.

---

Example:

Many plants → Few herbivores → One carnivore

---

# 16. Pyramid of Biomass

Represents:

Total mass of organisms.

---

Can be:

- Upright
- Inverted

Example:

Aquatic ecosystem may show inverted biomass pyramid.

---

# 17. Pyramid of Energy

Represents energy flow.

---

Always:

```
Upright
```

because energy decreases at each level.

---

# 18. Productivity

## Definition

Rate of biomass production in an ecosystem.

---

Types:

```
Productivity

      │

 ┌────┴────┐

 ▼         ▼

Primary   Secondary
```

---

# 19. Primary Productivity

Production of biomass by producers.

---

Types:

## Gross Primary Productivity (GPP)

Total energy captured by plants.

---

## Net Primary Productivity (NPP)

Energy remaining after respiration.

Formula:

\[
NPP = GPP - Respiration
\]

---

# 20. Secondary Productivity

Rate of biomass formation by consumers.

---

# 22. Humus Formation

Formation of dark-coloured organic matter called humus.

---

# 26. Ecological Succession

## Definition

Gradual replacement of one community by another over time.

---

# Types

```
Succession

    │

 ┌──┴──┐

 ▼     ▼

```

---

# Primary Succession

Occurs in:

- Bare areas without life.

Example:

Newly formed rocks.

---

# Secondary Succession

Occurs where previous community existed.

Example:

After forest fire.

---

# 27. Pioneer Species

First organisms to colonize an area.

Example:

Lichens

---

# 28. Climax Community

Final stable community formed after succession.

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. Define ecosystem.

**Answer:**

An ecosystem is a functional unit where living organisms interact with each other and with the physical environment.

---

## 2. State the 10% law.

**Answer:**

Only about 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next.

---

## 3. Differentiate GPP and NPP.

| GPP                     | NPP                                |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Total energy produced   | Energy available after respiration |
| Before respiration loss | After respiration loss             |

---

## 4. What are decomposers?

**Answer:**

Decomposers are organisms like bacteria and fungi that break down dead organic matter.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing food chain and food web.
- Forgetting energy pyramid is always upright.
- Mixing GPP and NPP.
- Confusing primary and secondary succession.
- Writing decomposers as consumers.

# Chapter Summary

- **Ecosystem** explains interactions between organisms and their physical environment.
- Energy flows through food chains and trophic levels, with only 10% energy transferred between levels.
- Producers, consumers, and decomposers maintain ecosystem functioning.
- Productivity measures biomass production in ecosystems.
- Decomposition and nutrient cycles recycle essential elements.
- Ecological succession describes the gradual development of communities over time.
