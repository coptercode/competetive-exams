# Introduction

Chemical reactions involve the transformation of one or more substances into new substances with different properties. Water, known as the universal solvent, plays a vital role in many chemical processes. This chapter covers the types of chemical reactions, precipitation, neutralization, exothermic and endothermic reactions, catalysts, solubility, and water of crystallization.

---

# 1. Chemical Reactions

## Chemical Reaction

A **chemical reaction** is a process in which one or more substances (reactants) are converted into new substances (products).

---

## Indicators of a Chemical Reaction

- Change in colour
- Evolution of gas
- Formation of precipitate
- Change in temperature
- Change in smell
- Change in state

---

## Types of Chemical Reactions

### A. Combination Reaction

Two or more substances combine to form a single product.

**General Equation**

```
A + B → AB
```

**Example**

```
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO
```

---

### B. Decomposition Reaction

A single compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances.

**General Equation**

```
AB → A + B
```

**Example**

```
CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
```

---

### C. Displacement Reaction

A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound.

**General Equation**

```
A + BC → AC + B
```

**Example**

```
Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
```

---

### D. Double Decomposition (Double Displacement) Reaction

Two compounds exchange their ions to form two new compounds.

**General Equation**

```
AB + CD → AD + CB
```

**Example**

```
AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃
```

---

# 2. Solution Processes

## Solution

A **solution** is a homogeneous mixture of a solute dissolved in a solvent.

### Components

- **Solute:** Substance being dissolved.
- **Solvent:** Substance in which the solute dissolves.

---

## Precipitation Reaction

A **precipitation reaction** occurs when two aqueous solutions react to form an insoluble solid called a precipitate.

### Example

```
Pb(NO₃)₂ + 2KI → PbI₂ + 2KNO₃
```

Here,

```
PbI₂
```

is the yellow precipitate.

---

## Neutralization Reaction

A **neutralization reaction** occurs when an acid reacts with a base to produce salt and water.

**General Equation**

```
Acid + Base → Salt + Water
```

### Example

```
HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
```

---

# 3. Thermal Energy Changes

Chemical reactions may absorb or release heat.

---

## Exothermic Reaction

An **exothermic reaction** releases heat to the surroundings.

### Characteristics

- Temperature increases.
- Heat is evolved.

### Examples

- Burning of fuels.
- Respiration.
- Neutralization.

---

## Endothermic Reaction

An **endothermic reaction** absorbs heat from the surroundings.

### Characteristics

- Temperature decreases.
- Heat is absorbed.

### Examples

- Photosynthesis.
- Thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate.

---

## Catalyst

A **catalyst** is a substance that changes the rate of a chemical reaction without itself undergoing permanent chemical change.

### Characteristics

- Speeds up or slows down reactions.
- Remains chemically unchanged.
- Required in small quantities.

### Examples

| Catalyst                 | Reaction                           |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| Manganese dioxide (MnO₂) | Decomposition of hydrogen peroxide |
| Iron (Fe)                | Haber process                      |
| Platinum (Pt)            | Catalytic converters               |

---

# 4. Solubility

## Solubility

**Solubility** is the maximum amount of solute that can dissolve in a given amount of solvent at a specified temperature.

---

## Types of Solutions

### Unsaturated Solution

Contains less solute than the maximum amount that can dissolve.

---

### Saturated Solution

Contains the maximum amount of dissolved solute at a given temperature.

---

### Supersaturated Solution

Contains more dissolved solute than a saturated solution at the same temperature.

It is unstable and excess solute crystallizes on disturbance.

---

## Factors Affecting Solubility

- Temperature
- Nature of solute
- Nature of solvent
- Pressure (mainly for gases)

---

## Solubility Curve

A graph showing the variation of solubility with temperature.

### Observations

- Most solids become more soluble with increasing temperature.
- Gases generally become less soluble with increasing temperature.

---

# 5. Hydrated Crystals

## Water of Crystallization

The fixed number of water molecules chemically combined with one molecule of a salt is called **water of crystallization**.

---

## Examples

| Compound            | Formula      |
| ------------------- | ------------ |
| Copper(II) Sulphate | CuSO₄·5H₂O   |
| Washing Soda        | Na₂CO₃·10H₂O |
| Gypsum              | CaSO₄·2H₂O   |

---

## Efflorescence

**Efflorescence** is the loss of water of crystallization from a hydrated salt when exposed to air.

### Example

```
Na₂CO₃·10H₂O
```

loses water and becomes powdery.

---

## Deliquescence

**Deliquescence** is the process by which a substance absorbs moisture from the atmosphere until it dissolves in the absorbed water.

### Examples

- Sodium hydroxide (NaOH)
- Calcium chloride (CaCl₂)

---

## Hygroscopic Substances

Hygroscopic substances absorb moisture from the atmosphere without dissolving.

### Examples

- Concentrated sulphuric acid
- Silica gel
- Phosphorus pentoxide

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **combination** and **double decomposition** reactions.
- Assuming all decomposition reactions require heating.
- Forgetting to balance chemical equations.
- Confusing **efflorescence** with **deliquescence**.
- Assuming hygroscopic substances dissolve in absorbed water.
- Confusing **exothermic** and **endothermic** reactions.
- Forgetting the role of catalysts.
- Ignoring the effect of temperature on solubility.

# Chapter Summary

- A **chemical reaction** converts reactants into products with new properties.
- The main types of reactions are **combination**, **decomposition**, **displacement**, and **double decomposition**.
- **Precipitation reactions** produce insoluble solids, while **neutralization reactions** produce salt and water.
- **Exothermic reactions** release heat, whereas **endothermic reactions** absorb heat.
- **Catalysts** alter the rate of chemical reactions without being consumed.
- **Solubility** depends on temperature, pressure (for gases), and the nature of the solute and solvent.
- Solutions may be **unsaturated**, **saturated**, or **supersaturated**.
- **Water of crystallization** gives hydrated salts their characteristic crystalline structure.
- **Efflorescent**, **deliquescent**, and **hygroscopic** substances interact differently with atmospheric moisture.
- Understanding chemical reactions and the properties of water is essential in chemistry, industry, environmental science, and daily life.
