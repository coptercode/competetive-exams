# Introduction

**Kinetic Theory of Gases** explains the macroscopic properties of gases such as **pressure, temperature, and volume** in terms of the microscopic motion of gas molecules.

It connects the behavior of individual molecules with measurable quantities and provides the foundation for understanding **ideal gases, gas laws, molecular speeds, and degrees of freedom**.

---

# 1. Molecular Nature of Matter

Matter is made up of tiny particles called **atoms and molecules**.

---

# Evidence for Molecular Motion

- Diffusion of gases.
- Brownian motion.
- Osmosis.
- Evaporation.

---

# Brownian Motion

## Definition

The random motion of microscopic particles suspended in a fluid due to collisions with molecules of the fluid.

---

# Importance

- Proves molecular motion.
- Supports kinetic theory.

---

# 2. Ideal Gas

## Definition

An ideal gas is a hypothetical gas that obeys gas laws perfectly under all conditions.

---

# Assumptions of Ideal Gas

1. Gas consists of a large number of molecules.
2. Molecules are point masses.
3. Molecular volume is negligible compared to gas volume.
4. Molecules move randomly.
5. Collisions are perfectly elastic.
6. No intermolecular forces exist except during collisions.

---

# 3. Gas Laws

---

# Boyle's Law

## Statement

At constant temperature, pressure is inversely proportional to volume.

```
P ∝ 1/V
```

or

```
PV = Constant
```

---

# Charles' Law

## Statement

At constant pressure, volume is directly proportional to absolute temperature.

```
V ∝ T
```

or

```
V/T = Constant
```

---

# Gay-Lussac's Law

## Statement

At constant volume, pressure is directly proportional to absolute temperature.

```
P ∝ T
```

---

# Combined Gas Equation

```
PV/T = Constant
```

---

# Ideal Gas Equation

```
PV = nRT
```

Where:

- P = Pressure
- V = Volume
- n = Number of moles
- R = Gas constant
- T = Absolute temperature

---

# Universal Gas Constant

```
R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹
```

---

# 4. Pressure of an Ideal Gas

Pressure arises due to collisions of gas molecules with the walls of the container.

---

# Formula

```
P = 1/3 ρv²
```

Where

- ρ = Density of gas
- v = Root mean square velocity

---

# Derivation Concept

Molecular collisions produce momentum change.

Rate of momentum change gives force.

Force per unit area gives pressure.

---

# 5. Kinetic Interpretation of Temperature

Temperature represents the average kinetic energy of gas molecules.

---

# Average Kinetic Energy

```
KE = 3/2 kT
```

Where

- k = Boltzmann constant

---

# Boltzmann Constant

```
k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹
```

---

# Important Result

Higher temperature →

Greater molecular speed →

Greater kinetic energy.

---

# 6. Molecular Speeds

Gas molecules do not move with the same velocity.

Three important velocities are:

- Most probable velocity
- Average velocity
- Root mean square velocity

---

# Most Probable Velocity

The velocity possessed by the maximum number of molecules.

```
vmp = √(2RT/M)
```

---

# Average Velocity

Mean velocity of molecules.

```
vavg = √(8RT/πM)
```

---

# Root Mean Square Velocity

## Definition

The square root of the average of squares of molecular velocities.

---

# Formula

```
vrms = √(3RT/M)
```

---

# Relation

```
vrms > vavg > vmp
```

---

# Effect of Temperature

As temperature increases:

- Molecular speed increases.
- Kinetic energy increases.

---

# 7. Degrees of Freedom

## Definition

The number of independent ways in which a molecule can possess energy.

---

# Types

## Translational

Motion in three directions.

```
f = 3
```

---

## Rotational

Rotation about axes.

---

## Vibrational

Oscillation of atoms.

---

# Degrees of Freedom of Different Gases

| Gas        | Degrees of Freedom |
| ---------- | ------------------ |
| Monoatomic | 3                  |
| Diatomic   | 5                  |
| Polyatomic | 6                  |

---

# 8. Law of Equipartition of Energy

## Statement

Energy is equally distributed among all degrees of freedom.

---

# Energy Per Degree of Freedom

```
1/2 kT
```

---

# Total Energy

```
E = f/2 kT
```

---

# For One Mole

```
E = f/2 RT
```

---

# 9. Specific Heat Capacities of Gases

---

# Heat Capacity at Constant Volume

```
Cv
```

---

# Heat Capacity at Constant Pressure

```
Cp
```

---

# Relation

```
Cp - Cv = R
```

---

# Ratio of Heat Capacities

```
γ = Cp/Cv
```

---

# Values of γ

| Gas Type   | γ   |
| ---------- | --- |
| Monoatomic | 5/3 |
| Diatomic   | 7/5 |
| Polyatomic | 4/3 |

---

# 10. Mean Free Path

## Definition

The average distance travelled by a molecule between two successive collisions.

---

# Formula

```
λ = 1/√2πd²n
```

Where:

- d = Diameter of molecule
- n = Number density

---

# Factors Affecting Mean Free Path

- Temperature.
- Pressure.
- Molecular size.

---

---


---

# Number of Molecules

```

```

---

# 12. Boltzmann Constant Relation

```

```

---

# Importance

Connects microscopic and macroscopic properties.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Find the kinetic energy of a gas molecule at 300 K.

(Given: k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K)

### Solution

```
KE = 3/2 kT

= 3/2 × 1.38×10⁻²³ × 300

= 6.21×10⁻²¹ J
```

### Answer

**6.21 × 10⁻²¹ J**

---

## Example 2

### Question

Find the RMS velocity of oxygen molecules at 300 K.

(Given: R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹K⁻¹, M = 0.032 kg/mol)

### Solution

```
vrms = √(3RT/M)

= √(3×8.314×300/0.032)

≈ 484 m/s
```

### Answer

**484 m/s**

---

## Example 3

### Question

A gas occupies 2 m³ at pressure 5 × 10⁵ Pa. Find pressure if volume becomes 5 m³ at constant temperature.

### Solution

Using Boyle's Law:

```
P₁V₁=P₂V₂

P₂=(P₁V₁)/V₂

=(5×10⁵×2)/5

=2×10⁵ Pa
```

### Answer

**2 × 10⁵ Pa**

---

## Example 4

### Question

Find the number of molecules in 2 moles of gas.

### Solution

```
N=nNA

=2×6.022×10²³

=1.204×10²⁴
```

### Answer

**1.204 × 10²⁴ molecules**

---

## Example 5

### Question

Find the value of γ for a monoatomic gas.

### Solution

For monoatomic gas:

```
Cp/Cv = 5/3
```

### Answer

**γ = 5/3**

# Common Mistakes

- Using Celsius instead of Kelvin in gas equations.
- Confusing average speed with RMS speed.
- Forgetting that pressure is caused by molecular collisions.
- Using wrong degrees of freedom.
- Confusing R and k values.
- Ignoring unit conversion of molar mass.

# Chapter Summary

- **Kinetic theory** explains gas behavior by considering gases as collections of rapidly moving molecules.
- Gas laws such as **Boyle's law, Charles' law, and Gay-Lussac's law** combine to form the ideal gas equation **PV=nRT**.
- The microscopic motion of molecules produces **gas pressure**, while temperature represents their average kinetic energy.
- Molecular speeds are described using **most probable, average, and RMS velocities**, with RMS velocity being the most commonly used in calculations.
- The concepts of **degrees of freedom and equipartition of energy** explain the heat capacities of gases.
- Kinetic theory provides the foundation for **thermal physics, atmospheric science, gas dynamics, engineering systems, and modern molecular physics**.
