# Introduction

**Transpiration** is the process by which plants lose water in the form of water vapour from their aerial parts, mainly through the **stomata** present on leaves. Although it results in water loss, transpiration is essential for the **ascent of sap**, cooling of plants, transport of minerals, and maintenance of water balance. It is one of the most important physiological processes in plants.

---

# 1. Transpiration

## Definition

**Transpiration** is the loss of water as water vapour from the aerial parts of a plant, mainly through the stomata.

---

## Sites of Transpiration

- Stomata (Major route)
- Cuticle
- Lenticels

---

# 2. Types of Transpiration

## (i) Stomatal Transpiration

Occurs through the **stomata** of leaves.

### Characteristics

- About **80–90%** of total transpiration.
- Most common type.
- Controlled by guard cells.

---

## (ii) Cuticular Transpiration

Occurs through the waxy **cuticle** covering leaves.

### Characteristics

- Very little water loss.
- More common in young leaves.

---

## (iii) Lenticular Transpiration

Occurs through **lenticels** present on woody stems.

### Characteristics

- Small amount of water loss.
- Continuous process.
- Not controlled by guard cells.

---

# 3. Stomata

## Definition

**Stomata** are tiny pores present mainly on the lower surface of leaves that regulate gaseous exchange and transpiration.

---

## Structure of a Stoma

A stoma consists of:

- Stomatal pore
- Two guard cells
- Epidermal cells
- Chloroplasts (in guard cells)

---

# 4. Guard Cells

## Function

Guard cells regulate the opening and closing of stomata by changing their turgidity.

---

## Opening of Stomata

- Guard cells absorb water.
- Cells become turgid.
- Stomatal pore opens.

---

## Closing of Stomata

- Guard cells lose water.
- Cells become flaccid.
- Stomatal pore closes.

---

# 5. Role of Potassium Ions (K⁺)

The movement of **potassium ions (K⁺)** controls the turgidity of guard cells.

### Mechanism

- K⁺ ions enter guard cells.
- Water enters by osmosis.
- Guard cells become turgid.
- Stomata open.

When K⁺ ions leave:

- Water moves out.
- Guard cells become flaccid.
- Stomata close.

# 7. Ascent of Sap

## Definition

**Ascent of Sap** is the upward movement of water and dissolved minerals from the roots to the leaves through the xylem.

---

## Forces Responsible

### Transpiration Pull

- Created due to evaporation of water from leaves.
- Main force responsible for ascent of sap.

---

### Root Pressure

- Pushes water upward from roots.
- More effective at night or when transpiration is low.

---

### Cohesion

Water molecules attract one another, forming a continuous water column.

---

### Adhesion

Water molecules stick to the walls of xylem vessels, helping maintain the water column.

---

# 8. Factors Affecting Transpiration

## Light

- Bright light → Increases transpiration.
- Darkness → Decreases transpiration.

---

## Temperature

- High temperature → Increases evaporation.
- Low temperature → Reduces transpiration.

---

## Humidity

- High humidity → Decreases transpiration.
- Low humidity → Increases transpiration.

---

## Wind

- Moderate wind removes moist air around leaves.
- Increases transpiration.

---

## Soil Water Availability

- Adequate water → Normal transpiration.
- Water shortage → Reduced transpiration.

---

# 9. Advantages of Transpiration

- Produces transpiration pull.
- Helps in ascent of sap.
- Transports minerals.
- Cools the plant.
- Maintains cell turgidity.
- Removes excess water.

---

# 10. Disadvantages of Transpiration

- Excessive water loss.
- Wilting during drought.
- Reduced growth.
- May lead to death if water loss is severe.

---

# 11. Guttation

## Definition

**Guttation** is the loss of liquid water droplets from the margins or tips of leaves through **hydathodes**.

---

## Characteristics

- Occurs at night or early morning.
- Water is in liquid form.
- Caused by root pressure.

---

# 12. Bleeding

## Definition

**Bleeding** is the oozing of sap from cut or injured plant parts.

---

## Examples

- Sugarcane
- Grapevine
- Rubber plant

---

# 13. Difference Between Transpiration, Guttation and Bleeding

| Feature        | Transpiration               | Guttation             | Bleeding            |
| -------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------- |
| Water Form     | Vapour                      | Liquid                | Sap                 |
| Occurs Through | Stomata, Cuticle, Lenticels | Hydathodes            | Cut or injured stem |
| Time           | Mostly daytime              | Night / Early morning | After injury        |
| Main Cause     | Evaporation                 | Root pressure         | Injury              |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Transpiration** with **Guttation**.
- Assuming transpiration occurs only through stomata.
- Forgetting that **stomatal transpiration** accounts for most water loss.
- Mixing up **Hydathodes** and **Stomata**.
- Ignoring the role of **potassium ions** in stomatal opening.
- Confusing **Root Pressure** with **Transpiration Pull**.
- Assuming transpiration is always harmful.
- Forgetting that **bleeding** occurs through injured plant parts.

# Chapter Summary

- **Transpiration** is the loss of water vapour from the aerial parts of plants, mainly through the **stomata**.
- There are three types of transpiration: **stomatal**, **cuticular**, and **lenticular**, with stomatal transpiration accounting for the majority of water loss.
- **Guard cells** regulate the opening and closing of stomata by changing their turgidity, which is controlled by the movement of **potassium ions (K⁺)**.
- Transpiration creates **transpiration pull**, the primary force responsible for the **ascent of sap** through the xylem.
- Environmental factors such as **light, temperature, humidity, wind, and soil water availability** significantly influence the rate of transpiration.
- Although excessive transpiration can lead to **wilting**, it plays vital roles in cooling the plant, transporting minerals, maintaining cell turgidity, and supporting water movement.
- **Guttation** is the loss of liquid water through **hydathodes**, while **bleeding** is the oozing of sap from injured plant tissues.
- Transpiration is essential for healthy plant growth and has important applications in agriculture, forestry, irrigation, and environmental management.
