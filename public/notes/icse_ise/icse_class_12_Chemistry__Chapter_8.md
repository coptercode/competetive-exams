# Introduction

Aldehydes and ketones are carbonyl compounds containing the functional group:

\[

> C=O
> \]

called the **carbonyl group**.

---

# 1. Classification of Carbonyl Compounds

```
             Carbonyl Compounds

                    │

        ┌───────────┴───────────┐

        ▼                       ▼

    Aldehydes              Ketones

       R-CHO                R-CO-R'
```

---

# 2. Aldehydes

## Definition

Organic compounds containing a carbonyl group attached to at least one hydrogen atom.

General formula:

\[
R-CHO
\]

---

Examples:

## Methanal

\[
HCHO
\]

## Ethanal

\[
CH_3CHO
\]

---

# 3. Ketones

## Definition

Organic compounds containing a carbonyl group attached to two carbon groups.

General formula:

\[
R-CO-R'
\]

---

Examples:

## Propanone (Acetone)

\[
CH_3COCH_3
\]

---

# 4. Nomenclature of Aldehydes

Rules:

1. Select longest chain containing -CHO group.
2. Replace "e" of alkane with "al".

---

Examples:

\[
CH_3CHO
\]

Name:

```
Ethanal
```

---

\[
CH_3CH_2CHO
\]

Name:

```
Propanal
```

---

# 5. Nomenclature of Ketones

Rules:

1. Select longest chain containing carbonyl group.
2. Replace "e" with "one".
3. Give carbonyl group lowest number.

---

Example:

\[
CH_3COCH_3
\]

Name:

```
Propanone
```

---

\[
CH_3COCH_2CH_3
\]

Name:

```
Butan-2-one
```

---

# 6. Structure of Carbonyl Group

The carbonyl carbon is:

```
sp² hybridised
```

Geometry:

```
Trigonal planar
```

Bond angle:

\[
120^\circ
\]

---

# 7. Nature of Carbonyl Group

The C=O bond is polar.

Structure:

\[
C^{\delta+}=O^{\delta-}
\]

---

Reason:

Oxygen is more electronegative than carbon.

---

# 8. Preparation of Aldehydes and Ketones

---

# 1. Oxidation of Alcohols

## Primary Alcohol

Forms aldehyde.

\[
RCH_2OH
\rightarrow
RCHO
\]

---

## Secondary Alcohol

Forms ketone.

\[
R_2CHOH
\rightarrow
R_2CO
\]

---

# 2. Dehydrogenation of Alcohols

Using copper catalyst.

Primary alcohol:

\[
RCH_2OH
\rightarrow
RCHO+H_2
\]

---

Secondary alcohol:

\[
R_2CHOH
\rightarrow
R_2CO+H_2
\]

---

# 3. Ozonolysis of Alkenes

Alkenes react with ozone to form carbonyl compounds.

Example:

\[
CH_2=CH_2
\rightarrow
2HCHO
\]

---

# 4. Rosenmund Reduction

Preparation of aldehydes from acid chlorides.

Reaction:

\[
RCOCl+H_2
\rightarrow
RCHO+HCl
\]

Catalyst:

\[
Pd/BaSO_4
\]

---

# 5. Stephen Reaction

Preparation of aldehydes from nitriles.

\[
RCN
\rightarrow
RCHO
\]

---

# 9. Physical Properties

---

# 1. Boiling Point

Aldehydes and ketones have higher boiling points than hydrocarbons.

Reason:

Dipole-dipole attraction.

---

# 2. Solubility

Lower aldehydes and ketones are soluble in water.

Reason:

Hydrogen bonding with water.

---

# 10. Chemical Reactions of Aldehydes and Ketones

Main reactions:

```
Carbonyl Reactions

        │

 ┌──────┼──────┐

 ▼      ▼      ▼

Addition Oxidation Reduction
```

---

# 11. Nucleophilic Addition Reaction

## Definition

Addition of nucleophile to carbonyl carbon.

---

General reaction:

\[
RCHO+Nu^-
\rightarrow
RCH(OH)Nu
\]

---

# Reactivity Order

\[
HCHO>RCHO>RCOR'
\]

---

Reason:

Aldehydes have less steric hindrance.

---

# 12. Addition of Hydrogen Cyanide (HCN)

Forms cyanohydrin.

Reaction:

\[
RCHO+HCN
\rightarrow
RCH(OH)CN
\]

---

# 13. Addition of Sodium Bisulphite

Forms bisulphite addition compound.

Used for purification.

---

# 14. Addition of Alcohols

Aldehydes form acetals.

Reaction:

\[
RCHO+2ROH
\rightarrow
RCH(OR)_2+H_2O
\]

---

# 15. Reaction with Ammonia Derivatives

Forms oximes, hydrazones, etc.

---

Example:

With hydroxylamine:

\[
Carbonyl+NH_2OH
\rightarrow
Oxime
\]

---

# 16. Oxidation Reactions

---

# Aldehydes

Easily oxidised to acids.

\[
RCHO
\rightarrow
RCOOH
\]

---

# Ketones

Resist oxidation.

---

# 17. Tests for Aldehydes

---

# Tollens Test

## Reagent:

Ammoniacal silver nitrate.

---

Observation:

```
Silver mirror formation
```

---

# Fehling Test

Produces:

```
Brick red Cu₂O precipitate
```

---

# 18. Reduction Reactions

Carbonyl compounds reduce to alcohols.

---

Aldehyde:

\[
RCHO
\rightarrow
RCH_2OH
\]

---

Ketone:

\[
RCOR'
\rightarrow
RCHOHR'
\]

---

Reducing agents:

- H₂/Ni
- NaBH₄
- LiAlH₄

---

# 19. Clemmensen Reduction

Carbonyl compounds reduced to hydrocarbons.

Reagent:

\[
Zn-Hg/HCl
\]

Reaction:

\[
RCO-R'
\rightarrow
RCH_2R'
\]

---

# 20. Wolff-Kishner Reduction

Reagents:

- Hydrazine
- KOH

Reaction:

\[
RCO-R'
\rightarrow
RCH_2R'
\]

---

# 21. Aldol Condensation

## Definition

Aldehydes or ketones having α-hydrogen undergo condensation.

Example:

Ethanal:

\[
2CH_3CHO
\rightarrow
CH_3CH(OH)CH_2CHO
\]

---

# 22. Cannizzaro Reaction

## Definition

Aldehydes without α-hydrogen undergo self oxidation-reduction.

Example:

Formaldehyde:

\[
2HCHO
\rightarrow
CH_3OH+HCOOH
\]

---

# 23. Haloform Reaction

Given by methyl ketones.

General reaction:

\[
RCOCH_3
\rightarrow
CHI_3
\]

---

Observation:

Yellow precipitate of iodoform.

---

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. Why are aldehydes more reactive than ketones?

**Answer:**

Aldehydes have less steric hindrance and only one electron-donating alkyl group, making the carbonyl carbon more electrophilic.

---

## 2. Explain Tollens test.

**Answer:**

Aldehydes reduce ammoniacal silver nitrate to metallic silver, producing a silver mirror.

---

## 3. What is aldol condensation?

**Answer:**

A reaction in which aldehydes or ketones containing α-hydrogen undergo condensation to form β-hydroxy aldehydes or ketones.

---

## 4. Differentiate aldehydes and ketones.

| Aldehyde        | Ketone               |
| --------------- | -------------------- |
| R-CHO           | R-CO-R               |
| Easily oxidised | Difficult to oxidise |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing aldehyde and ketone structures.
- Forgetting aldehydes give Tollens test.
- Mixing Clemmensen and Wolff-Kishner reactions.
- Forgetting α-hydrogen requirement for aldol reaction.
- Writing ketones as easily oxidised.

# Chapter Summary

- **Aldehydes and Ketones** are important carbonyl compounds containing the C=O group.
- They undergo nucleophilic addition reactions due to the polarity of the carbonyl bond.
- Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones and are easily oxidised.
- Important reactions include aldol condensation, Cannizzaro reaction, haloform reaction, and reduction reactions.
- These compounds have wide industrial and biological applications.
