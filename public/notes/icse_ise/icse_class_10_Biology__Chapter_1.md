# Introduction

The **cell** is the structural and functional unit of life. Every living organism grows, repairs damaged tissues, and reproduces through **cell division**. During cell division, the genetic material (DNA) is accurately distributed into daughter cells with the help of **chromosomes**. Understanding chromosome structure and the different stages of cell division is essential for studying heredity, growth, and reproduction.

---

# 1. Cell

## Definition

A **Cell** is the basic structural, functional, and biological unit of all living organisms.

### Types of Cells

- Plant Cell
- Animal Cell

---

# 2. Chromosomes

## Definition

**Chromosomes** are thread-like structures present in the nucleus that carry hereditary information in the form of genes.

---

## Characteristics

- Present inside the nucleus.
- Made of DNA and proteins.
- Become visible during cell division.
- Carry genes responsible for inheritance.
- Human body cells contain **46 chromosomes (23 pairs)**.

---

# 3. Structure of a Chromosome

A chromosome consists of the following parts:

### Chromatid

One of the two identical halves of a replicated chromosome.

---

### Sister Chromatids

Two identical chromatids joined together at the centromere.

---

### Centromere

The constricted region joining the two sister chromatids.

**Functions**

- Holds sister chromatids together.
- Attaches spindle fibres during cell division.

---

### Telomere

The protective ends of chromosomes.

**Functions**

- Prevent chromosome damage.
- Protect DNA during replication.

---

### Gene

A segment of DNA that controls a particular inherited characteristic.

Examples

- Eye colour
- Blood group
- Hair colour

---

# 4. DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)

## Definition

DNA is the hereditary material present in chromosomes that carries genetic information.

---

## Structure

- Double Helix
- Two polynucleotide strands
- Sugar-Phosphate Backbone
- Nitrogen Bases

### Nitrogen Bases

- Adenine (A)
- Thymine (T)
- Guanine (G)
- Cytosine (C)

---

## Base Pairing Rule

```
A ↔ T
```

```
G ↔ C
```

---

# 5. Histones and Nucleosomes

## Histones

Proteins around which DNA is wrapped.

---

## Nucleosome

A DNA molecule wrapped around histone proteins.

### Function

- Packages DNA into chromosomes.
- Protects genetic material.
- Helps regulate gene expression.

---

# 6. Cell Cycle

## Definition

The **Cell Cycle** is the sequence of events through which a cell grows, duplicates its DNA, and divides into two daughter cells.

---

## Phases of the Cell Cycle

```
Interphase
        ↓
Mitosis / Meiosis
        ↓
Cytokinesis
```

---

# 7. Interphase

Interphase is the longest phase of the cell cycle.

It consists of three stages.

---

## G₁ Phase (Growth Phase)

- Cell grows.
- Organelles increase in number.
- Proteins are synthesized.

---

## S Phase (Synthesis Phase)

- DNA replication occurs.
- Each chromosome forms two sister chromatids.

---

## G₂ Phase

- Further cell growth.
- Preparation for cell division.
- Spindle proteins are synthesized.

---

# 8. Cell Division

## Definition

Cell division is the process by which one parent cell divides to produce new daughter cells.

There are two types:

- Mitosis
- Meiosis

---

# 9. Mitosis

## Definition

Mitosis is the division of a somatic (body) cell producing **two genetically identical diploid daughter cells**.

---

## Characteristics

- Occurs in body cells.
- One nuclear division.
- Chromosome number remains unchanged.
- Produces two daughter cells.

---

## Stages of Mitosis

### (i) Prophase

- Chromosomes condense.
- Nuclear membrane disappears.
- Spindle fibres appear.
- Centrioles move to opposite poles (animal cells).

---

### (ii) Metaphase

- Chromosomes arrange at the equator.
- Spindle fibres attach to centromeres.

---

### (iii) Anaphase

- Centromeres split.
- Sister chromatids separate.
- Chromatids move to opposite poles.

---

### (iv) Telophase

- Chromosomes uncoil.
- Nuclear membrane reforms.
- Two nuclei are formed.

---

# 10. Cytokinesis

## Definition

Cytokinesis is the division of the cytoplasm after nuclear division.

---

## Animal Cell

- Cleavage furrow develops.
- Cell divides inward.

---

## Plant Cell

- Cell plate forms.
- New cell wall develops.

---

# 11. Importance of Mitosis

- Growth
- Repair of tissues
- Replacement of worn-out cells
- Asexual reproduction
- Maintains chromosome number

---

# 12. Meiosis

## Definition

Meiosis is a special type of cell division that produces **four genetically different haploid cells** from one diploid cell.

---

## Characteristics

- Occurs in reproductive organs.
- Two successive divisions.
- Chromosome number is reduced to half.
- Produces gametes.

---

## Meiosis I

### Prophase I

- Homologous chromosomes pair.
- Crossing over occurs.
- Genetic recombination takes place.

---

### Metaphase I

- Homologous chromosomes arrange at the equator.

---

### Anaphase I

- Homologous chromosomes separate.

---

### Telophase I

- Two haploid cells are formed.

---

## Meiosis II

Similar to mitosis.

Results in four haploid daughter cells.

---

# 13. Differences Between Mitosis and Meiosis

| Mitosis                     | Meiosis                           |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| One division                | Two divisions                     |
| Two daughter cells          | Four daughter cells               |
| Diploid cells produced      | Haploid cells produced            |
| No crossing over            | Crossing over occurs              |
| Growth and repair           | Formation of gametes              |
| Chromosome number unchanged | Chromosome number reduced by half |

---

# 14. Significance of Meiosis

- Formation of gametes
- Maintains chromosome number in a species
- Introduces genetic variation
- Essential for sexual reproduction
- Promotes evolution

---

# 15. Chromosome Number in Humans

| ------------ | ----------------: |
| Somatic Cell |     46 (23 pairs) |
| Sperm Cell   |                23 |
| Ovum         |                23 |
| Zygote       |                46 |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **DNA** with **Chromosomes**.
- Assuming mitosis produces haploid cells.
- Forgetting that DNA replication occurs during the **S Phase**.
- Mixing up the stages of mitosis.
- Confusing sister chromatids with homologous chromosomes.
- Believing crossing over occurs in mitosis (it occurs only in **Prophase I of meiosis**).
- Forgetting that cytokinesis differs in plant and animal cells.
- Writing the chromosome number of gametes as **46** instead of **23**.

# Chapter Summary

- The **cell** is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
- **Chromosomes** are made of DNA and proteins and contain genes that determine inherited characteristics.
- DNA is organized into **nucleosomes** by wrapping around **histone proteins**, allowing it to fit inside the nucleus.
- The **Cell Cycle** consists of **Interphase (G₁, S, G₂)** followed by **cell division** and **cytokinesis**.
- **Mitosis** produces two genetically identical diploid daughter cells for growth, repair, and asexual reproduction.
- **Meiosis** consists of two successive divisions that produce four genetically different haploid gametes, ensuring genetic variation and maintaining chromosome number across generations.
- **Crossing over** during **Prophase I of meiosis** increases genetic diversity.
- In humans, **somatic cells contain 46 chromosomes**, while **gametes contain 23 chromosomes**, and fertilization restores the diploid number.
