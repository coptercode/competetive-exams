# Introduction

**Analytical Chemistry** is the branch of chemistry that deals with the **identification and analysis of chemical substances**. In qualitative analysis, different reagents are used to identify the presence of specific **cations (positive ions)** and **anions (negative ions)** by observing changes such as the formation of precipitates, colour changes, or gas evolution. At the ICSE Class 10 level, emphasis is placed on the use of **Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH)** and **Ammonium Hydroxide (NH₄OH)** as analytical reagents.

---

# 1. Analytical Chemistry

## Definition

**Analytical Chemistry** is the branch of chemistry that deals with the identification and analysis of substances based on their chemical properties.

---

## Objectives

- Identify unknown salts.
- Detect metal ions.
- Observe precipitate colours.
- Differentiate between similar ions.
- Study chemical reactions.

---

# 2. Reagents

## Definition

A **Reagent** is a chemical substance used to test or identify another substance.

---

## Common Reagents

- Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH)
- Ammonium Hydroxide (NH₄OH)
- Dilute Hydrochloric Acid
- Barium Chloride
- Silver Nitrate

---

# 3. Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH) Test

## Purpose

Used to identify metal ions by observing the colour and solubility of precipitates.

---

## Observations

| Ion  | Observation with NaOH        | Excess NaOH |
| ---- | ---------------------------- | ----------- |
| Cu²⁺ | Light blue precipitate       | Insoluble   |
| Fe²⁺ | Dirty green precipitate      | Insoluble   |
| Fe³⁺ | Reddish-brown precipitate    | Insoluble   |
| Zn²⁺ | White gelatinous precipitate | Soluble     |
| Al³⁺ | White gelatinous precipitate | Soluble     |
| Pb²⁺ | White precipitate            | Soluble     |
| Ca²⁺ | White precipitate            | Insoluble   |

---

# 4. Ammonium Hydroxide (NH₄OH) Test

## Purpose

Used to distinguish metal ions based on precipitate colour and solubility.

---

## Observations

| Ion  | Observation with NH₄OH                     | Excess NH₄OH                         |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Cu²⁺ | Light blue precipitate                     | Dissolves to form deep blue solution |
| Fe²⁺ | Dirty green precipitate                    | Insoluble                            |
| Fe³⁺ | Reddish-brown precipitate                  | Insoluble                            |
| Zn²⁺ | White gelatinous precipitate               | Soluble (colourless solution)        |
| Al³⁺ | White precipitate                          | Insoluble                            |
| Pb²⁺ | White precipitate                          | Insoluble                            |
| Ca²⁺ | No precipitate or slight white precipitate | Insoluble                            |

---

# 5. Amphoteric Oxides

## Definition

**Amphoteric Oxides** are oxides that react with **both acids and bases**.

---

## Examples

- Zinc Oxide (ZnO)
- Aluminium Oxide (Al₂O₃)
- Lead Oxide (PbO)

---

## Characteristics

- Dissolve in acids.
- Dissolve in strong alkalis.
- Form salts in both reactions.

---

# 6. Action of Alkalis on Ammonium Salts

## Reaction

When ammonium salts are heated with a strong alkali, **ammonia gas** is evolved.

---

## Example

```
NH₄Cl + NaOH →

NaCl + NH₃↑ + H₂O
```

---

## Test for Ammonia

- Colourless gas.
- Pungent smell.
- Turns **moist red litmus blue**.
- Forms dense white fumes with hydrochloric acid.

---

# 7. Identification of Important Metal Ions

---

## Copper (Cu²⁺)

### NaOH Test

Light blue precipitate.

### NH₄OH Test

Light blue precipitate dissolves in excess to form a **deep blue solution**.

---

## Iron (II) (Fe²⁺)

### NaOH Test

Dirty green precipitate.

### NH₄OH Test

Dirty green precipitate.

---

## Iron (III) (Fe³⁺)

### NaOH Test

Reddish-brown precipitate.

### NH₄OH Test

Reddish-brown precipitate.

---

## Zinc (Zn²⁺)

### NaOH Test

White gelatinous precipitate soluble in excess.

### NH₄OH Test

White gelatinous precipitate soluble in excess.

---

## Aluminium (Al³⁺)

### NaOH Test

White gelatinous precipitate soluble in excess.

### NH₄OH Test

White precipitate insoluble in excess.

---

## Lead (Pb²⁺)

### NaOH Test

White precipitate soluble in excess.

### NH₄OH Test

White precipitate insoluble.

---

## Calcium (Ca²⁺)

### NaOH Test

White precipitate.

### NH₄OH Test

Usually no precipitate or a slight white precipitate.

---

# Difference Between NaOH and NH₄OH Tests

| Sodium Hydroxide                     | Ammonium Hydroxide             |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Strong alkali                        | Weak alkali                    |
| Identifies many metal ions           | Helps distinguish similar ions |
| Some precipitates dissolve in excess | Different solubility patterns  |
| Used in qualitative analysis         | Used as a confirmatory reagent |

Copper(II) ions form a characteristic blue precipitate.

### Answer

```

```

---

## Example 2

### Question

Which ion forms a deep blue solution in excess ammonium hydroxide?

### Solution

The blue precipitate dissolves due to the formation of a complex ion.

### Answer

```
Copper (Cu²⁺)
```

---

## Example 3

### Question

Name one amphoteric oxide.

### Solution

Some metal oxides react with both acids and alkalis.

### Answer


---

### Question

What gas is evolved when ammonium chloride is heated with sodium hydroxide?

### Solution

Ammonium salts release a characteristic alkaline gas.

### Answer

```
Ammonia (NH₃)
```

### Question

Which ion gives a reddish-brown precipitate with both NaOH and NH₄OH?

### Solution

Iron(III) compounds show a distinctive reddish-brown precipitate.

### Answer

```

```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Cu²⁺** and **Fe²⁺** precipitate colours.
- Forgetting that **Cu²⁺** forms a **deep blue solution** in excess NH₄OH.
- Mixing up amphoteric oxides with basic oxides.
- Assuming all white precipitates behave the same in excess reagent.
- Forgetting that NaOH is a **strong alkali** and NH₄OH is a **weak alkali**.
- Confusing Fe²⁺ (dirty green) with Fe³⁺ (reddish-brown).
- Ignoring the importance of precipitate solubility.

---

# Quick Revision

- Analytical Chemistry → Identification of substances.
- NaOH → Strong alkali reagent.
- NH₄OH → Weak alkali reagent.
- Cu²⁺ → Light blue ppt; deep blue solution in excess NH₄OH.
- Fe²⁺ → Dirty green ppt.
- Fe³⁺ → Reddish-brown ppt.
- Zn²⁺ → White ppt soluble in excess NaOH and NH₄OH.
- Al³⁺ → White ppt soluble in NaOH but insoluble in NH₄OH.
- Amphoteric oxides → ZnO, Al₂O₃, PbO.
- NH₄Cl + NaOH → NH₃ gas.

# Chapter Summary

- **Analytical Chemistry** involves identifying substances using characteristic chemical reactions and observations.
- **Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH)** and **Ammonium Hydroxide (NH₄OH)** are important reagents used to identify metal ions based on the **colour and solubility of precipitates**.
- **Copper, iron, zinc, aluminium, lead, and calcium ions** each produce characteristic precipitates that help distinguish them during qualitative analysis.
- **Amphoteric oxides** such as **ZnO, Al₂O₃, and PbO** react with both acids and alkalis.
- Heating **ammonium salts** with a strong alkali produces **ammonia gas**, which can be identified by its pungent smell and its ability to turn **moist red litmus paper blue**.
- Analytical chemistry plays a vital role in **laboratories, industries, environmental monitoring, medicine, and quality control**, making it an essential branch of chemistry.
