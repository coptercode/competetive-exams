# Introduction

**Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants, algae, and some bacteria prepare their own food by utilizing **light energy**, **carbon dioxide**, and **water** in the presence of **chlorophyll**. It is the primary source of energy for almost all living organisms and is responsible for maintaining the balance of oxygen and carbon dioxide in the atmosphere.

The overall chemical equation for photosynthesis is:

```
6CO₂ + 12H₂O  →  C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O
        Light
     Chlorophyll
```

---

# Importance of Photosynthesis

- Produces food for all living organisms.
- Releases oxygen into the atmosphere.
- Converts solar energy into chemical energy.
- Maintains atmospheric CO₂ and O₂ balance.
- Provides raw materials for growth.
- Forms the basis of food chains.

Photosynthetic pigments are molecules that absorb light energy required for photosynthesis.

## A. Chlorophyll a

### Characteristics

- Primary photosynthetic pigment.
- Blue-green in colour.
- Directly involved in light reactions.

---

## B. Chlorophyll b

### Characteristics

- Yellow-green pigment.
- Accessory pigment.
- Transfers absorbed energy to chlorophyll a.

---

## C. Carotenoids

### Types

- Carotenes
- Xanthophylls

### Functions

- Protect chlorophyll from photooxidation.
- Absorb light of different wavelengths.

| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |

# 3. Photosystems

Two photosystems participate in photosynthesis.

---

## Photosystem I (PS I)

Reaction centre pigment

```
P700
```

Functions

- NADPH formation.
- Cyclic photophosphorylation.

---

## Photosystem II (PS II)

Reaction centre pigment

```
P680
```

Functions

- Water splitting.
- Oxygen evolution.
- Non-cyclic photophosphorylation.

---

# Difference Between PS I and PS II

| PS I             | PS II           |
| ---------------- | --------------- |
| P700             | P680            |
| Forms NADPH      | Splits water    |
| Cyclic possible  | Non-cyclic only |
| Acts after PS II | Acts first      |

## Definition

Splitting of water molecules using light energy.

Equation

```
2H₂O

↓

4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂
```

Occurs in

```
Photosystem II
```

---

# Photophosphorylation

## Definition

Formation of ATP using light energy.

---

# Types

```
Photophosphorylation
         │
   ┌─────┴─────┐
   ▼           ▼
Cyclic     Non-Cyclic
```

---

## Cyclic Photophosphorylation

Characteristics

- Only PS I involved.
- ATP produced.
- No oxygen released.
- No NADPH formed.

---

## Non-Cyclic Photophosphorylation

Characteristics

- PS I and PS II involved.
- ATP produced.
- NADPH produced.
- Oxygen released.

---

# Difference Between Cyclic and Non-Cyclic Photophosphorylation

| Cyclic           | Non-Cyclic              |
| ---------------- | ----------------------- |
| PS I only        | PS I and PS II          |
| ATP only         | ATP + NADPH             |
| No O₂            | O₂ released             |
| Electrons return | Electrons do not return |

---

# 5. Dark Reaction (Calvin Cycle)

## Definition

The light-independent reaction occurs in the **stroma** where carbon dioxide is converted into carbohydrates using ATP and NADPH.

---

## Site

```
Stroma
```

---

## Calvin Cycle

Proposed by

```
Melvin Calvin
```

---

# Three Phases

```
Calvin Cycle
      │
 ┌────┼─────────────┐
 ▼    ▼             ▼
Carboxylation
Reduction
Regeneration
```

---

## A. Carboxylation

- CO₂ combines with RuBP.
- Enzyme:

```
RuBisCO
```

- Forms 3-PGA.

---

## B. Reduction

- ATP and NADPH convert 3-PGA into G3P.

---

## C. Regeneration

- RuBP regenerated.
- Cycle repeats.

---

# Importance of Calvin Cycle

- Glucose synthesis.
- Carbon fixation.
- Basis of plant productivity.

---

| C₃ Plants                      | C₄ Plants                    |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| First product: 3-PGA           | First product: OAA           |
| RuBisCO                        | PEP Carboxylase + RuBisCO    |
| No Kranz anatomy               | Kranz anatomy present        |

# 8. Photorespiration

## Definition

Photorespiration is the process in which **RuBisCO** acts as an oxygenase and oxygen is consumed instead of carbon dioxide.

---

## Characteristics

- Occurs mainly in C₃ plants.
- Decreases photosynthetic efficiency.
- No ATP produced.

---

# Importance

- Protects plants under stress.
- Reduces productivity.

---

# Blackman's Law of Limiting Factors

## Statement

When a process depends on several factors, the rate is controlled by the factor that is present in the least favourable amount.

Example

If CO₂ is limiting, increasing light intensity alone will not increase photosynthesis.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **light reaction** with **dark reaction**.
- Mixing up **PS I (P700)** and **PS II (P680)**.
- Assuming the dark reaction occurs only at night.
- Confusing **RuBisCO** with **PEP carboxylase**.
- Mixing up **C₃** and **C₄** pathways.
- Forgetting that oxygen is released from the photolysis of water, not from carbon dioxide.

# Chapter Summary

- **Photosynthesis** is the fundamental process by which green plants convert **solar energy into chemical energy**, producing carbohydrates and releasing oxygen.
- Light energy is captured by **chlorophyll** in the **thylakoid membranes**, where the **light reaction** generates **ATP, NADPH, and oxygen** through photolysis and photophosphorylation.
- The **Calvin cycle**, occurring in the **stroma**, uses ATP and NADPH to fix atmospheric carbon dioxide into glucose.
- Plants utilize either the **C₃** or **C₄** pathway for carbon fixation, with **C₄ plants** showing greater efficiency under high temperatures due to reduced photorespiration.
- **Photorespiration** lowers photosynthetic efficiency in C₃ plants, while **Blackman's Law of Limiting Factors** explains how environmental factors regulate the rate of photosynthesis.
- Understanding photosynthesis is essential for **agriculture, crop improvement, food production, climate regulation, and sustainable ecosystem management**, making it one of the most important biological processes on Earth.
