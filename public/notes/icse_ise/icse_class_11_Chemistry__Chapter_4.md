# Introduction

Atoms combine with one another to attain **stability** by achieving the electronic configuration of noble gases. The attractive force that holds atoms together is called a **chemical bond**. Understanding chemical bonding helps explain the structure, geometry, polarity, and properties of molecules.

The major theories explaining chemical bonding include:

- Octet Rule
- Ionic Bonding
- Covalent Bonding
- VSEPR Theory
- Valence Bond Theory (VBT)
- Hybridization
- Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT)

---

# 1. Chemical Bond

## Definition

A **chemical bond** is the attractive force that holds two or more atoms together to form a stable molecule or compound.

---

# Types of Chemical Bonds

```
             Chemical Bond
                    │
     ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
     ▼              ▼              ▼
 Ionic         Covalent      Coordinate
                    │
                    ▼
              Metallic Bond
```

---

# Octet Rule

## Statement

Atoms combine by **gaining, losing, or sharing electrons** to achieve **eight electrons** in their outermost shell.

---

## Exceptions

- Hydrogen (Duplet rule)
- Helium
- BeCl₂ (Incomplete octet)
- BF₃ (Incomplete octet)
- PCl₅ (Expanded octet)
- SF₆ (Expanded octet)

---

# Limitations of Octet Rule

- Cannot explain odd-electron molecules (NO, NO₂).
- Cannot explain expanded octets.
- Cannot explain molecular shapes.
- Cannot explain transition metal compounds.

---

# 2. Ionic Bond

## Definition

An ionic bond is formed by the **complete transfer of electrons** from one atom to another, resulting in oppositely charged ions held together by electrostatic attraction.

---

# Formation of NaCl

```
Na → Na⁺ + e⁻

Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻

Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl
```

---

# Characteristics of Ionic Compounds

- High melting point.
- High boiling point.
- Hard and brittle.
- Conduct electricity in molten or aqueous state.
- Soluble in polar solvents.

---

# Factors Favoring Ionic Bond Formation

- Low ionization enthalpy.
- High electron gain enthalpy.
- High lattice enthalpy.

---

# Lattice Enthalpy

## Definition

Energy released when one mole of an ionic solid is formed from its gaseous ions.

---

# Difference Between Ionic and Covalent Compounds

| Ionic Compound               | Covalent Compound   |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Electron transfer            | Electron sharing    |
| High melting point           | Lower melting point |
| Conduct electricity (molten) | Poor conductor      |
| Polar                        | Usually non-polar   |

---

# 3. Covalent Bond

## Definition

A covalent bond is formed by the **sharing of one or more pairs of electrons** between atoms.

---

# Types of Covalent Bonds

| Bond        | Shared Electron Pairs |
| ----------- | --------------------- |
| Single Bond | 1                     |
| Double Bond | 2                     |
| Triple Bond | 3                     |

---

# Lewis Electron Dot Structure

## Rules

- Represent valence electrons as dots.
- Shared pair forms a bond.
- Lone pairs remain around atoms.

---

### Example: Water (H₂O)

```
H : O : H

(O has two lone pairs)
```

---

### Example: Methane (CH₄)

```
      H
      |
H — C — H
      |
      H
```

---

# Coordinate (Dative) Bond

## Definition

A covalent bond in which **both shared electrons are contributed by the same atom**.

---

## Examples

- NH₄⁺
- H₃O⁺
- CO

---

# Difference Between Covalent and Coordinate Bond

| Covalent Bond                   | Coordinate Bond                     |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Both atoms contribute electrons | One atom contributes both electrons |
| Ordinary shared pair            | Donor-acceptor bond                 |

---

# 4. Bond Parameters

---

## Bond Length

Distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms.

---

## Bond Angle

Angle between two covalent bonds.

---

## Bond Enthalpy

Energy required to break one mole of a specific bond in gaseous state.

---

## Bond Order

Number of bonds between two atoms.

Examples

| Molecule | Bond Order |
| -------- | ---------- |
| H₂       | 1          |
| O₂       | 2          |
| N₂       | 3          |

---

# Relation Between Bond Order and Bond Length

```
Bond Order ↑

↓

Bond Length ↓

↓

Bond Strength ↑
```

# 5. VSEPR Theory

## Full Form

Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion Theory

---

## Principle

Electron pairs around the central atom repel each other and arrange themselves to minimize repulsion.

---

# Molecular Shapes

| Molecule | Shape                | Bond Angle |
| -------- | -------------------- | ---------- |
| BeCl₂    | Linear               | 180°       |
| BF₃      | Trigonal Planar      | 120°       |
| CH₄      | Tetrahedral          | 109.5°     |
| NH₃      | Trigonal Pyramidal   | 107°       |
| H₂O      | Bent                 | 104.5°     |
| PCl₅     | Trigonal Bipyramidal | 90°,120°   |
| SF₆      | Octahedral           | 90°        |

---

# Order of Electron Pair Repulsion

```
Lone Pair–Lone Pair

>

Lone Pair–Bond Pair

>

Bond Pair–Bond Pair
```

---

# Difference Between Electron Geometry and Molecular Geometry

| Electron Geometry            | Molecular Geometry          |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Considers all electron pairs | Considers bonded atoms only |
| Includes lone pairs          | Excludes lone pairs         |

---

# 6. Valence Bond Theory (VBT)

## Principle

A covalent bond is formed by the **overlap of half-filled atomic orbitals**.

---

# Types of Overlap

```
Orbital Overlap

↓

Head-on

↓

Sidewise
```

---

# Sigma (σ) Bond

- Formed by head-on overlap.
- Stronger bond.
- Cylindrical symmetry.

---

# Pi (π) Bond

- Formed by sideways overlap.
- Weaker than σ bond.
- Exists along with σ bond.

---

# Difference Between Sigma and Pi Bonds

| Sigma Bond        | Pi Bond          |
| ----------------- | ---------------- |
| Head-on overlap   | Sidewise overlap |
| Stronger          | Weaker           |
| First bond formed | Additional bond  |

---

# 7. Hybridization

## Definition

Hybridization is the mixing of atomic orbitals of nearly equal energy to form equivalent hybrid orbitals.

---

# Types of Hybridization

| Hybridization | Geometry        | Example     |
| ------------- | --------------- | ----------- |
| sp            | Linear          | BeCl₂       |

| sp³           | Tetrahedral     | CH₄         |
| dsp²          | Square Planar   | [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ |
| d²sp³         | Octahedral      | SF₆         |

```

2
```

```
Bond Order > 0

```

# 10. Hydrogen Bonding

## Definition

The attraction between hydrogen attached to **F, O, or N** and another electronegative atom.

---

# Types

### Intermolecular

Between different molecules.

Examples

- Water
- HF

---

### Intramolecular

Within the same molecule.

Examples

- o-Nitrophenol
- Salicylic acid

---

# Effects of Hydrogen Bonding

- Higher boiling point.
- Higher melting point.
- Greater solubility.
- Increased viscosity.

---

# Difference Between Intermolecular and Intramolecular Hydrogen Bonding

| Intermolecular       | Intramolecular                    |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Between molecules    | Within one molecule               |
| Raises boiling point | Reduces intermolecular attraction |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Predict the geometry of CH₄.

### Solution

Carbon undergoes sp³ hybridization with four bond pairs and no lone pairs.

### Answer

**Tetrahedral (109.5°)**

---

## Example 2

### Question

Calculate the bond order of O₂.

### Solution

Using MOT:

```
Bond Order = (10 − 6)/2 = 2
```

### Answer

**2**

---

## Example 3

### Question

Why is NH₃ pyramidal?

### Solution

Nitrogen has one lone pair and three bond pairs. Lone pair-bond pair repulsion reduces the bond angle.

### Answer

**Trigonal pyramidal due to one lone pair.**

---

## Example 4

### Question

Which bond is stronger: σ or π?

### Solution

Head-on overlap in σ bonds is greater than sidewise overlap in π bonds.

### Answer

**Sigma (σ) bond**

---

## Example 5

### Question

Why is water polar?

### Solution

Water has a bent shape and unequal distribution of charge, producing a net dipole moment.

### Answer

**Because of its bent geometry and polar O–H bonds.**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing ionic bonds with coordinate bonds.
- Mixing up σ and π bonds.
- Forgetting lone pairs while predicting molecular geometry.
- Assuming all tetrahedral molecules are polar.
- Incorrectly calculating bond order in MOT.
- Confusing intermolecular and intramolecular hydrogen bonding.

# Chapter Summary

- Chemical bonding explains how atoms combine to form stable molecules by **transferring or sharing electrons**.
- **Ionic bonds** arise from electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions, while **covalent bonds** result from electron sharing. **Coordinate bonds** involve a shared electron pair contributed by one atom.
- **VSEPR theory** predicts molecular shapes based on electron pair repulsions, whereas **Valence Bond Theory** explains bond formation through orbital overlap and **hybridization**.
- **Molecular Orbital Theory** describes electrons as occupying molecular orbitals and explains bond order, stability, and magnetic properties of molecules.
- Concepts such as **bond length, bond angle, bond enthalpy, dipole moment, and hydrogen bonding** determine the physical and chemical behavior of substances.
- A clear understanding of chemical bonding is essential for studying **molecular structure, reactivity, coordination compounds, organic chemistry, and materials science**.
