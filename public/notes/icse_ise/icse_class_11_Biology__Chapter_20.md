# Introduction

Living organisms exhibit various types of movements in response to internal and external stimuli. In humans, movement is made possible by the coordinated action of the **skeletal system, muscles, joints, and nervous system**. These movements enable locomotion, maintenance of posture, protection of organs, and execution of daily activities.

---

# 1. Movement and Locomotion

## Definition of Movement

Movement is the **change in position of any part of the body**.

---

## Definition of Locomotion

Locomotion is the **movement of the entire body from one place to another**.

---

# Difference Between Movement and Locomotion

| Movement                        | Locomotion                      |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Change in position of body part | Movement of whole organism      |
| May not involve change of place | Always involves change of place |
| Example: Blinking               | Walking                         |

---


## Principle

During muscle contraction,

- Actin slides over myosin.
- Sarcomere shortens.
- Muscle contracts.

### Solution

Ligaments connect **bone to bone**, whereas tendons connect **muscle to bone**.

ATP supplies energy for muscle contraction and relaxation.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing movement with locomotion.
- Mixing up ligaments and tendons.
- Assuming cartilage is harder than bone.
- Confusing skeletal, smooth, and cardiac muscles.
- Forgetting that the sarcomere is the functional unit of muscle contraction.
- Mixing up ball-and-socket and hinge joints.

# Chapter Summary

- **Movement** refers to a change in the position of body parts, while **locomotion** involves movement of the entire organism from one place to another.
- The **skeletal system**, consisting of **206 bones**, provides support, protection, mineral storage, blood cell formation, and acts as a framework for movement.
- Bones articulate through different types of **joints**, with **synovial joints** allowing the greatest range of movement.
- The **muscular system** includes **skeletal, smooth, and cardiac muscles**, each specialized for different functions.
- The **Sliding Filament Theory** explains muscle contraction through the interaction of **actin and myosin filaments**, powered by ATP and regulated by calcium ions.
- Disorders such as **arthritis, osteoporosis, muscular dystrophy, and myasthenia gravis** affect the locomotor system and highlight the importance of maintaining healthy bones, joints, and muscles.
