# Introduction

The **s-block elements** are those in which the last electron enters the **s-orbital** of the outermost shell. They occupy **Groups 1 and 2** of the Modern Periodic Table.

These elements are highly electropositive and readily lose electrons to form **positive ions**. They are excellent reducing agents and are highly reactive due to their low ionization enthalpies.

The chapter covers the properties, occurrence, preparation, compounds, anomalies, and industrial importance of **alkali metals** and **alkaline earth metals**.

# Difference Between Group 1 and Group 2 Elements

| Group 1       | Group 2       |
| ------------- | ------------- |
| ns¹           | ns²           |
| Valency = 1   | Valency = 2   |
| Softer        | Harder        |
| More reactive | Less reactive |
| Form M⁺ ions  | Form M²⁺ ions |

# 2. General Characteristics of s-Block Elements

- Highly electropositive.
- Low ionization enthalpy.
- Good reducing agents.
- Form ionic compounds.
- Soft metals.
- Low density.
- High thermal and electrical conductivity.

| Property            | Down the Group      |

| Ionic radius        | Increases           |
| Density             | Generally increases |

| Reactivity          | Increases           |

# Anomalous Behaviour of Lithium

Lithium differs from other alkali metals due to

- Small size.
- High charge density.
- High polarizing power.

---

## Differences

- Forms oxide (Li₂O) instead of peroxide.
- Reacts directly with nitrogen.
- Carbonate decomposes on heating.
- Chloride is more covalent.

---

# Diagonal Relationship

Lithium resembles magnesium because of

- Similar ionic size.
- Similar charge density.

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Why are alkali metals highly reactive?

### Solution

They possess a single valence electron which is easily lost due to low ionization enthalpy.

### Answer

**Because they have one loosely held valence electron.**

---

## Example 2

### Question

Name the process used to manufacture sodium carbonate.

### Solution

Industrial production is carried out by the Solvay process.

### Answer

**Solvay process.**

---

## Example 3

### Question

What is the chemical formula of Plaster of Paris?

### Solution

Plaster of Paris is calcium sulfate hemihydrate.

### Answer

**CaSO₄·½H₂O**

---

## Example 4

### Question

Why does lithium show anomalous behavior?

### Solution

Lithium has the smallest atomic size and the highest polarizing power among alkali metals.

### Answer

**Due to its very small size and high charge density.**

---

## Example 5

### Question

Write the reaction of calcium oxide with water.

### Solution

```
CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂
```

### Answer

**Calcium hydroxide (slaked lime) is formed.**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O)** with **baking soda (NaHCO₃)**.
- Mixing up **quick lime (CaO)** and **slaked lime [Ca(OH)₂]**.
- Forgetting the anomalous behavior of **lithium** and **beryllium**.
- Confusing **gypsum** with **Plaster of Paris**.
- Assuming all Group 2 metals react with water at the same rate.
- Writing incorrect formulas for important sodium and calcium compounds.

# Chapter Summary

- The **s-block elements** include the **alkali metals (Group 1)** and **alkaline earth metals (Group 2)**, characterized by the presence of valence electrons in the **s-orbital**.
- These elements are highly electropositive, form ionic compounds readily, and exhibit systematic trends in atomic size, ionization enthalpy, and reactivity.
- **Lithium** and **beryllium** show anomalous behavior due to their small size and high polarizing power and exhibit diagonal relationships with **magnesium** and **aluminium**, respectively.
- The chapter highlights important compounds such as **sodium hydroxide, washing soda, baking soda, quick lime, slaked lime, gypsum, and Plaster of Paris**, along with their preparation, properties, and applications.
- The biological importance of **sodium, potassium, magnesium, and calcium** emphasizes their essential roles in living organisms.
- Knowledge of s-block elements is fundamental for understanding **industrial chemistry, metallurgy, agriculture, medicine, construction, and environmental applications**.
