# Introduction

All living organisms require **oxygen** for cellular respiration and must eliminate **carbon dioxide**, a waste product of metabolism. In humans, this exchange of gases occurs through the **respiratory system**, which consists of the respiratory tract and lungs.

Breathing ensures a continuous supply of oxygen to body tissues and removal of carbon dioxide, maintaining the body's metabolic activities.

---

# 1. Breathing

## Definition

**Breathing** (Ventilation) is the mechanical process of taking in oxygen-rich air (**inspiration**) and expelling carbon dioxide-rich air (**expiration**).

---

# Difference Between Breathing and Respiration

| Breathing          | Respiration         |
| ------------------ | ------------------- |
| Mechanical process | Biochemical process |
| Occurs in lungs    | Occurs in cells     |
| Exchange of gases  | Release of ATP      |
| No energy produced | Energy produced     |

# 5. Mechanism of Breathing

Breathing consists of

```
Breathing

↓

Inspiration

↓

Expiration
```

---

# A. Inspiration

## Process

- Diaphragm contracts.
- External intercostal muscles contract.
- Thoracic cavity expands.
- Lung volume increases.
- Pressure decreases.
- Air enters lungs.

---

# B. Expiration

## Process

- Diaphragm relaxes.
- Intercostal muscles relax.
- Thoracic cavity decreases.
- Lung volume decreases.
- Pressure increases.
- Air expelled.

---

# Difference Between Inspiration and Expiration

| Inspiration               | Expiration                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Air enters lungs          | Air leaves lungs          |
| Diaphragm contracts       | Diaphragm relaxes         |
| Thoracic volume increases | Thoracic volume decreases |
| Pressure decreases        | Pressure increases        |

---

# 6. Respiratory Volumes

## Tidal Volume (TV)

Amount of air inhaled or exhaled during normal breathing.

```
≈ 500 mL
```

---

## Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV)

Extra air inhaled after normal inspiration.

```
≈ 2500–3000 mL
```

---

## Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV)

Extra air exhaled after normal expiration.

```
≈ 1000–1100 mL
```

---

## Residual Volume (RV)

Air remaining in lungs after forceful expiration.

```
≈ 1100–1200 mL
```

---

# Respiratory Capacities

## Inspiratory Capacity (IC)

```
IC = TV + IRV
```

---

## Functional Residual Capacity (FRC)

```
FRC = ERV + RV
```

---

## Vital Capacity (VC)

Maximum air exhaled after maximum inspiration.

```
VC = TV + IRV + ERV
```

---

## Total Lung Capacity (TLC)

```
TLC = VC + RV
```

---

# Difference Between Lung Volumes and Capacities

| Lung Volumes       | Lung Capacities            |
| ------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Single measurement | Sum of two or more volumes |
| TV, IRV, ERV, RV   | IC, FRC, VC, TLC           |

# Factors Affecting Oxygen Dissociation

- Increased CO₂
- Increased temperature
- Decreased pH
- Increased H⁺ concentration

---

# Bohr Effect

## Definition

The Bohr Effect states that **an increase in carbon dioxide concentration or decrease in pH reduces the affinity of haemoglobin for oxygen**, promoting oxygen release to tissues.

---

# Importance

- Efficient oxygen delivery.
- Supports active tissues.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Where does the exchange of gases occur in humans?

### Solution

Gas exchange occurs across the thin walls of the **alveoli**.

### Answer

Alveoli.

---

## Example 2

### Question

Name the respiratory pigment in human blood.

### Solution

Haemoglobin binds oxygen and transports it through the bloodstream.

### Answer

Haemoglobin.

---

## Example 3

### Question

What is the normal tidal volume of an adult?

### Solution

During normal breathing, approximately **500 mL** of air is inhaled or exhaled.

### Answer

500 mL.

---

## Example 4

### Question

In which form is most carbon dioxide transported in blood?

### Solution

Most carbon dioxide is transported as **bicarbonate ions (HCO₃⁻)**.

### Answer

Bicarbonate ions.

---

## Example 5

### Question

State the Bohr Effect.

### Solution

An increase in CO₂ concentration or decrease in blood pH reduces haemoglobin's affinity for oxygen, facilitating oxygen release to tissues.

### Answer

Increased CO₂ or decreased pH promotes oxygen dissociation from haemoglobin.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing breathing with cellular respiration.
- Mixing up inspiration and expiration.
- Forgetting that gas exchange occurs in the alveoli.
- Confusing lung volumes with lung capacities.
- Assuming oxygen is mainly transported dissolved in plasma.
- Mixing up the transport forms of oxygen and carbon dioxide.

# Chapter Summary

- **Breathing** is the mechanical process of inhalation and exhalation, while **respiration** is the cellular process of ATP production.
- The **human respiratory system** includes the respiratory tract and lungs, with **alveoli** serving as the primary site of gaseous exchange.
- Breathing is achieved through coordinated contractions and relaxations of the **diaphragm** and **intercostal muscles**, altering thoracic volume and pressure.
- Oxygen is transported mainly as **oxyhaemoglobin**, whereas carbon dioxide is transported predominantly as **bicarbonate ions**.
- The **oxygen-haemoglobin dissociation curve** and the **Bohr Effect** explain efficient oxygen loading in the lungs and unloading in metabolically active tissues.
- Proper regulation of respiration and healthy lung function are essential for maintaining **cellular metabolism, acid-base balance, and overall physiological homeostasis**.
