# Introduction

**Reproductive health** refers to the condition of complete physical, mental, and social well-being in all matters related to reproduction.

It includes:

- Safe reproductive practices
- Awareness about reproductive systems
- Prevention of sexually transmitted diseases
- Family planning
- Maternal and child health

---

# 1. Importance of Reproductive Health

Good reproductive health ensures:

- Healthy sexual life
- Safe pregnancy
- Reduced maternal mortality
- Prevention of sexually transmitted infections
- Control of population growth
- Healthy offspring

---

# 2. Reproductive Health Problems

Major reproductive health issues include:

- Population explosion
- Sexually transmitted infections (STIs)
- Unwanted pregnancies
- Infertility
- Lack of awareness
- Unsafe abortions

---

# 3. Population Explosion

## Definition

A rapid increase in human population beyond available resources is called population explosion.

---

# Causes

- Increased birth rate
- Decreased death rate
- Improved medical facilities
- Increased life expectancy

---

# Effects

- Shortage of resources
- Environmental degradation
- Unemployment
- Poverty
- Pressure on healthcare systems

---

# Population Control Measures

Methods:

- Family planning
- Awareness programmes
- Contraceptive use
- Delayed marriage
- Small family concept

---

# 4. Contraception

## Definition

Methods used to prevent pregnancy are called contraceptive methods.

---

# Ideal Characteristics of Contraceptives

An ideal contraceptive should be:

- Effective
- Safe
- Easy to use
- Reversible
- Affordable
- Have minimal side effects

---

# 5. Natural Methods

These methods prevent pregnancy by avoiding fertilization naturally.

---

## 1. Periodic Abstinence

Avoiding intercourse during fertile period.

Fertile period:

```
10th - 17th day
```

of menstrual cycle.

---

## 2. Withdrawal Method

Removal of penis before ejaculation.

Also called:

**Coitus interruptus**

---

## 3. Lactational Amenorrhea

Absence of menstruation during breastfeeding.

Effective for:

Approximately 6 months after childbirth.

---

# 6. Barrier Methods

Prevent contact between sperm and ovum.

---

# Male Condom

Example:

- Nirodh

Function:

- Prevents sperm entry.
- Protects against STIs.

---

# Female Condom

Placed inside vagina to prevent sperm entry.

---

# Diaphragm

A device placed over cervix.

---

# Cervical Caps

Cover cervix and prevent sperm movement.

---

# 7. Intrauterine Devices (IUDs)

Devices placed inside uterus.

---

# Types

## 1. Non-medicated IUDs

Example:

- Lippes loop

---

## 2. Copper-Releasing IUDs

Examples:

- CuT
- Cu7

---

Function:

- Reduce sperm motility.
- Prevent fertilization.

---

## 3. Hormone-Releasing IUDs

Examples:

- LNG-20
- Progestasert

---

Function:

- Make uterus unsuitable for implantation.

---

# 8. Hormonal Methods

Use hormones to prevent ovulation.

---

# Oral Contraceptive Pills

Contain:

- Estrogen
- Progesterone

---

# Mechanism

They prevent:

- Ovulation
- Implantation

---

# Emergency Contraception

Used after unprotected intercourse.

Examples:

- Hormonal pills

Effective within:

```
72 hours
```

---

# 9. Surgical Methods (Sterilization)

Permanent methods of contraception.

---

# Vasectomy

Performed in males.

Procedure:

- Cutting and tying vas deferens.

Result:

- Sperms cannot enter semen.

---

# Tubectomy

Performed in females.

Procedure:

- Cutting and tying fallopian tubes.

Result:

- Prevents egg and sperm meeting.

---

# 10. Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)

## Definition

Diseases transmitted through sexual contact are called sexually transmitted infections.

---

# Common STIs

| Disease        | Causative Organism |
| -------------- | ------------------ |
| Gonorrhoea     | Bacteria           |
| Syphilis       | Bacteria           |
| AIDS           | HIV                |
| Genital herpes | Virus              |
| Hepatitis-B    | Virus              |
| Chlamydia      | Bacteria           |

---

# Symptoms

- Abnormal discharge
- Pain during urination
- Genital itching
- Swelling
- Fever

---

# Prevention

- Safe sexual practices
- Use of condoms
- Avoid multiple partners
- Early diagnosis
- Proper treatment

---

# 11. HIV/AIDS

## Causative Agent

Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)

---

# Target Cells

HIV attacks:

```
Helper T-lymphocytes
```

---

# Transmission

Through:

- Sexual contact
- Blood transfusion
- Sharing infected needles
- Mother to child

---

# Not Transmitted Through

- Handshake
- Hugging
- Sharing food
- Mosquito bites

---

# Effects

Reduction of immunity leading to:

**AIDS**

(Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome)

---

# 12. Infertility

## Definition

The inability to conceive even after regular unprotected intercourse is called infertility.

---

# Causes of Infertility

## Male Causes

- Low sperm count
- Poor sperm motility
- Hormonal problems

---

## Female Causes

- Ovulation disorders
- Blocked fallopian tubes
- Uterine problems

---

# 13. Assisted Reproductive Technologies (ART)

## Definition

Medical techniques used to help infertile couples produce children.

---

# 1. IVF (In Vitro Fertilization)

## Definition

Fertilization occurs outside the body in laboratory conditions.

---

Process:

```
Egg Collection
      ↓
Sperm Collection
      ↓
Fertilization in Lab
      ↓
Embryo Formation
      ↓
Transfer to Uterus
```

---

# 2. ZIFT

## Zygote Intrafallopian Transfer

Transfer of zygote into fallopian tube.

---

# 3. IUT

## Intrauterine Transfer

Transfer of embryo into uterus.

---

# 4. GIFT

## Gamete Intrafallopian Transfer

Transfer of gametes into fallopian tube.

---

# 5. ICSI

## Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection

Direct injection of sperm into ovum.

---

# 14. Amniocentesis

## Definition

A prenatal diagnostic technique used to detect genetic disorders.

---

# Procedure

Analysis of:

- Amniotic fluid
- Fetal cells

---

# Applications

Detection of:

- Chromosomal abnormalities
- Genetic disorders

---

# Misuse

Sex determination using amniocentesis is illegal in many countries.

---

# 15. Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP)

## Definition

Intentional termination of pregnancy before full term is called MTP.

---

# Reasons for MTP

- Unwanted pregnancy
- Failure of contraception
- Risk to mother
- Genetic abnormalities

---

# Importance

- Prevents unsafe abortions.
- Protects maternal health.

---

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. What is reproductive health?

**Answer:**

Reproductive health is the state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being related to reproductive functions.

---

## 2. Explain vasectomy.

**Answer:**

Vasectomy is a male sterilization method where vas deferens is cut and tied to prevent sperm transport.

---

## 3. What is IVF?

**Answer:**

IVF is a technique where fertilization occurs outside the body in laboratory conditions and the embryo is transferred into the uterus.

---

## 4. Name two STIs.

**Answer:**

- AIDS
- Gonorrhoea

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing contraception with abortion.
- Writing HIV as the disease instead of virus.
- Confusing IVF and GIFT.
- Forgetting that condoms prevent STIs.
- Mixing vasectomy and tubectomy.

---

- Contraception prevents pregnancy.
- IUDs are placed in uterus.
- Vasectomy → Male.
- Tubectomy → Female.
- HIV attacks helper T-cells.
- IVF helps infertile couples.
- ART includes IVF, ZIFT, GIFT, ICSI.

# Chapter Summary

- **Reproductive Health** deals with maintaining healthy reproductive systems and preventing reproductive problems.
- Population control and contraception help manage family planning.
- Barrier, hormonal, IUD, and surgical methods are important contraceptive techniques.
- Sexually transmitted infections can be prevented through awareness and safe practices.
- Assisted reproductive technologies like IVF help infertile couples achieve pregnancy.
- Awareness, education, and healthcare services are essential for improving reproductive health.
