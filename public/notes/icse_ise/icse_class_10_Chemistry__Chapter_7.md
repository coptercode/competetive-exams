# Introduction

**Metallurgy** is the branch of chemistry that deals with the **extraction of metals from their ores**, their purification, and the preparation of alloys. Since most metals occur in nature as compounds, they must be extracted and refined before use. Metallurgy plays a vital role in industries such as construction, transportation, electrical engineering, and manufacturing.

---

# 1. Metallurgy

## Definition

**Metallurgy** is the science and process of extracting metals from their ores and refining them for practical use.

---

## Objectives

- Extract metals from ores.
- Purify metals.
- Manufacture alloys.
- Improve the properties of metals.

---

# 2. Minerals

## Definition

**Minerals** are naturally occurring substances in the Earth's crust containing metals or their compounds.

---

## Examples

- Bauxite
- Haematite
- Magnetite
- Galena
- Zinc Blende

---

# 3. Ores

## Definition

An **Ore** is a mineral from which a metal can be extracted economically.

---

## Examples

| Metal     | Ore                     |
| --------- | ----------------------- |
| Aluminium | Bauxite (Al₂O₃·2H₂O)    |
| Iron      | Haematite (Fe₂O₃)       |
| Zinc      | Zinc Blende (ZnS)       |
| Lead      | Galena (PbS)            |
| Copper    | Copper Pyrites (CuFeS₂) |

---

# Difference Between Minerals and Ores

| Minerals                            | Ores                                    |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Naturally occurring compounds       | Minerals used for metal extraction      |
| May or may not contain useful metal | Contain sufficient metal for extraction |
| Not always economical               | Economical to extract                   |

---

# 4. Concentration of Ore (Ore Dressing)

## Definition

The process of removing impurities (gangue) from an ore is called **Concentration of Ore** or **Ore Dressing**.

---

## Gangue

**Gangue** refers to unwanted impurities such as sand, clay, and rocks present in an ore.

---

## Methods of Concentration

### (i) Hydraulic Washing (Gravity Separation)

Based on the difference in densities of ore and impurities.

---

### (ii) Magnetic Separation

Used when either the ore or impurities are magnetic.

---

### (iii) Froth Flotation

Used for **sulphide ores**.

---

### (iv) Leaching

Used for ores soluble in suitable solvents.

---

# 5. Calcination

## Definition

**Calcination** is the process of heating an ore in the **absence or limited supply of air**.

---

## Purpose

- Removes moisture.
- Removes volatile impurities.
- Converts carbonates into oxides.

---

## Example

```
ZnCO₃

Heat

↓

ZnO + CO₂↑
```

---

# 6. Roasting

## Definition

**Roasting** is the process of heating **sulphide ores** in the **presence of excess air**.

---

## Purpose

- Converts sulphides into oxides.
- Removes sulphur as sulfur dioxide.

---

## Example

```
2ZnS + 3O₂

↓

2ZnO + 2SO₂↑
```

---

# Difference Between Calcination and Roasting

| Calcination             | Roasting               |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Absence of air          | Excess air             |
| Used for carbonate ores | Used for sulphide ores |
| Removes CO₂             | Removes SO₂            |
| Produces metal oxide    | Produces metal oxide   |

---

# 7. Reduction

## Definition

**Reduction** is the removal of oxygen from a metal oxide to obtain the metal.

---

## Example

```
Fe₂O₃ + 3CO

↓

2Fe + 3CO₂
```

---

# 8. Extraction of Aluminium

---

## Ore

```
Bauxite
```

---

## Concentrated Ore

```
Alumina (Al₂O₃)
```

---

## Method

Electrolysis (Hall-Héroult Process)

---

## Electrolyte

Molten alumina dissolved in **cryolite (Na₃AlF₆)**.

---

## Purpose of Cryolite

- Lowers melting point.
- Improves electrical conductivity.

---

## Cathode Reaction

```
Al³⁺ + 3e⁻

↓

Al
```

---

## Anode Reaction

```
2O²⁻

↓

O₂ + 4e⁻
```

---

## Overall Observation

Pure molten aluminium is collected at the bottom of the electrolytic cell.

---

# 9. Alloys

## Definition

An **Alloy** is a homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or a metal with a non-metal, prepared to improve properties.

---

## Advantages

- Greater strength.
- Better corrosion resistance.
- Increased hardness.
- Improved durability.

---

## Common Alloys

| Alloy           | Composition                                | Uses                                  |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Brass           | Copper + Zinc                              | Utensils, Screws                      |
| Bronze          | Copper + Tin                               | Statues, Medals                       |
| Duralumin       | Aluminium + Copper + Magnesium + Manganese | Aircraft                              |
| Stainless Steel | Iron + Chromium + Nickel                   | Kitchen utensils, Medical instruments |
| Solder          | Lead + Tin                                 | Electrical work                       |

---

# 10. Corrosion

## Definition

**Corrosion** is the gradual destruction of metals due to chemical reactions with the environment.

---

## Example

Rusting of iron.

---

## Prevention

- Painting.
- Galvanizing.
- Electroplating.
- Alloying.
- Oiling and greasing.

Only minerals that can economically yield metals are called ores.

Aluminium is mainly extracted from one important ore.

## Example 3

### Question

Differentiate between calcination and roasting.

### Solution

Calcination is carried out without air, whereas roasting requires excess air.

### Answer

Calcination is heating in the **absence or limited supply of air**, while roasting is heating in the **presence of excess air**.

---

## Example 4

### Question

Why is cryolite added during the extraction of aluminium?

### Solution

Pure alumina has a very high melting point and poor conductivity.

### Answer

Cryolite lowers the melting point of alumina and improves its electrical conductivity.

Brass

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **minerals** with **ores**.
- Mixing up **calcination** and **roasting**.
- Forgetting that **froth flotation** is used for sulphide ores.
- Assuming all metals are extracted by electrolysis.
- Forgetting the role of **cryolite** in aluminium extraction.
- Confusing **alloys** with pure metals.
- Assuming corrosion only affects iron.

# Quick Revision

- Metallurgy → Extraction and purification of metals.
- Ore → Economically useful mineral.
- Gangue → Unwanted impurities.
- Ore Dressing → Removal of gangue.
- Calcination → Heating without air.
- Roasting → Heating with excess air.
- Reduction → Removal of oxygen.
- Aluminium Ore → Bauxite.
- Cryolite → Lowers melting point of alumina.
- Alloy → Mixture of metals (or a metal with a non-metal).

# Chapter Summary

- **Metallurgy** involves the extraction, purification, and processing of metals from their ores.
- **Minerals** are naturally occurring compounds, while **ores** are minerals from which metals can be extracted economically.
- The removal of impurities from ores is called **ore dressing** or **concentration**, using methods such as hydraulic washing, magnetic separation, froth flotation, and leaching.
- **Calcination** and **roasting** convert ores into oxides, which are then reduced to obtain pure metals.
- Aluminium is extracted from **bauxite** using the **Hall-Héroult electrolytic process**, where **cryolite** lowers the melting point and increases conductivity.
- **Alloys** such as **brass, bronze, duralumin, and stainless steel** are produced to improve the strength, hardness, and corrosion resistance of metals.
- Metallurgy is essential in industries such as **construction, transportation, aerospace, electronics, medicine, and manufacturing**, contributing significantly to modern technological development.
