# Introduction

The **Circulatory System** is the transport system of the human body. It carries **oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and other essential substances** to all body cells and removes **carbon dioxide and metabolic wastes**. The circulatory system consists of the **heart, blood, and blood vessels**. Humans have a **closed, double circulatory system**, ensuring efficient transport and preventing the mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood.

# 2. Blood

## Definition

**Blood** is a fluid connective tissue that transports oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and waste materials throughout the body.

---

## Composition of Blood

Blood consists of:

- Plasma
- Red Blood Cells (RBCs)
- White Blood Cells (WBCs)
- Platelets

---

### Plasma

**Definition**

Plasma is the straw-coloured liquid part of blood.

### Functions

- Transports nutrients.
- Carries hormones.
- Transports carbon dioxide.
- Carries waste products.
- Maintains blood pressure.

---

### Red Blood Cells (RBCs)

**Scientific Name**

Erythrocytes

### Characteristics

- Biconcave shape.
- Contain haemoglobin.
- Lack nucleus in mammals.
- Produced in red bone marrow.

### Functions

- Transport oxygen.
- Carry a small amount of carbon dioxide.

---

### White Blood Cells (WBCs)

**Scientific Name**

Leucocytes

### Characteristics

- Larger than RBCs.
- Have a nucleus.
- Can change shape.
- Fewer in number.

### Functions

- Protect the body from infections.
- Destroy disease-causing microorganisms.
- Produce antibodies.

---

### Platelets

**Scientific Name**

Thrombocytes

### Characteristics

- Small cell fragments.
- Produced in bone marrow.

### Functions

- Help in blood clotting.
- Prevent excessive blood loss.

# 4. Blood Clotting

## Definition

**Blood Clotting** is the process of preventing excessive blood loss from injured blood vessels.

---

## Steps

```
Injury
   │
Platelets break
   │
Release Thromboplastin
   │
Prothrombin → Thrombin
   │
Fibrinogen → Fibrin
   │
Blood Clot
```

# 5. Heart

## Definition

The **Heart** is a muscular organ that pumps blood throughout the body.

---

## Location

- Thoracic cavity
- Between the lungs
- Slightly towards the left side

---

## Covering

**Pericardium**

A double-layered membrane enclosing the heart.

---

## Chambers of the Heart

The human heart has **four chambers**.

- Right Atrium
- Right Ventricle
- Left Atrium
- Left Ventricle

---

## Septum

A muscular wall separating the right and left sides of the heart.

---

# 6. Heart Valves

## Tricuspid Valve

Between

- Right Atrium
- Right Ventricle

---

## Bicuspid (Mitral) Valve

Between

- Left Atrium
- Left Ventricle

---

## Semilunar Valves

Present at the openings of

- Aorta
- Pulmonary Artery

---

## Function of Valves

Prevent the backflow of blood.

---

# 7. Blood Vessels

## Arteries

### Characteristics

- Carry blood away from the heart.
- Thick elastic walls.
- High blood pressure.
- No valves (except near the heart).

### Exception

Pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood.

---

## Veins

### Characteristics

- Carry blood towards the heart.
- Thin walls.
- Low blood pressure.
- Contain valves.

### Exception

Pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood.

---

## Capillaries

### Characteristics

- Smallest blood vessels.
- Walls are one cell thick.
- Site of exchange of gases, nutrients, and wastes.

---

# 8. Double Circulation

## Definition

Blood passes through the heart **twice** during one complete circulation.

---

## Types

### Pulmonary Circulation

```
Heart
   │
Lungs
   │
Heart
```

Function

Exchange of gases.

---

### Systemic Circulation

```
Heart
   │
Body
   │
Heart
```

Function

Supplies oxygen and nutrients to body tissues.

# 10. Hepatic Portal System

## Definition

The **Hepatic Portal System** is a network of veins that carries nutrient-rich blood from the digestive organs to the liver before it enters the general circulation.

---

## Importance

- Nutrient processing.
- Storage of glucose as glycogen.
- Detoxification of harmful substances.

---

# 11. Lymphatic System

## Definition

The **Lymphatic System** is a network of lymph vessels and lymph nodes that transports lymph and supports immunity.

---

## Components

- Lymph
- Lymph vessels
- Lymph nodes
- Spleen
- Thymus

---

## Functions

- Returns tissue fluid to blood.
- Absorbs fats from the intestine.
- Produces lymphocytes.
- Defends against infections.

---

# 12. Difference Between Arteries and Veins

| Arteries                    | Veins                     |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Carry blood away from heart | Carry blood towards heart |
| Thick walls                 | Thin walls                |
| No valves                   | Valves present            |
| High pressure               | Low pressure              |
| Usually oxygenated          | Usually deoxygenated      |

---

# 13. Difference Between RBCs and WBCs

| RBC                 | WBC             |
| ------------------- | --------------- |
| Carry oxygen        | Fight infection |
| Contain haemoglobin | No haemoglobin  |
| No nucleus          | Nucleus present |
| More numerous       | Less numerous   |
| Red in colour       | Colourless      |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **arteries** with **veins**.
- Forgetting that the **pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood**.
- Forgetting that the **pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood**.
- Mixing up the **tricuspid** and **bicuspid (mitral)** valves.
- Assuming WBCs transport oxygen (they fight infections).
- Confusing plasma with lymph.
- Forgetting the sequence of blood flow through the heart.
- Mixing up pulmonary and systemic circulation.

# Chapter Summary

- The **circulatory system** consists of the **heart, blood, blood vessels, and lymphatic system**, ensuring transport of oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and wastes.
- **Blood** is made up of **plasma, red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets**, each performing specialized functions.
- The **heart** is a four-chambered muscular organ protected by the **pericardium**, with valves ensuring one-way blood flow.
- Humans have a **closed, double circulatory system**, comprising **pulmonary circulation** (heart–lungs–heart) and **systemic circulation** (heart–body–heart).
- **Arteries** generally carry oxygenated blood away from the heart, while **veins** generally return deoxygenated blood to the heart, with the pulmonary vessels being important exceptions.
- The **hepatic portal system** transports nutrient-rich blood from the digestive organs to the liver, while the **lymphatic system** maintains fluid balance and provides immune protection.
- Proper functioning of the circulatory system is essential for maintaining homeostasis, supporting metabolism, and ensuring the survival of all body tissues.
