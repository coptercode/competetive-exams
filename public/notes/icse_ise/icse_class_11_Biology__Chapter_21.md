# Introduction

The human body constantly receives information from the surroundings and responds appropriately. This coordination is achieved by the **nervous system**, which works along with the endocrine system to regulate body functions. The nervous system detects stimuli, processes information, and initiates responses through nerve impulses.

The human nervous system consists of:

- Central Nervous System (CNS)
- Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)
- Autonomic Nervous System (ANS)

---

# 1. Nervous System

## Definition

The nervous system is a network of specialized tissues that **receives, processes, and transmits information** through nerve impulses.

---

# Functions

- Detects stimuli.
- Conducts nerve impulses.
- Coordinates body activities.
- Controls voluntary and involuntary actions.
- Maintains homeostasis.

# Parts of a Neuron

## Dendrites

- Receive nerve impulses.
- Short and branched.

---

## Cell Body (Cyton)

Contains

- Nucleus
- Cytoplasm
- Nissl granules

---

## Axon

- Conducts impulses away from the cell body.
- May be covered by myelin sheath.

---

## Myelin Sheath

Produced by

- Schwann cells (PNS)
- Oligodendrocytes (CNS)

Functions

- Insulates axon.
- Increases impulse speed.

---

## Nodes of Ranvier

Gaps in the myelin sheath that enable **saltatory conduction**.

---

# Difference Between Dendrites and Axon

| Dendrites                       | Axon                    |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Carry impulses toward cell body | Carry impulses away     |
| Short and branched              | Usually long and single |
| Receive signals                 | Transmit signals        |

# 3. Nerve Impulse

## Definition

A nerve impulse is an **electrical signal** transmitted along a neuron.

---

# Resting Potential

- Inside of neuron is negative.
- Outside is positive.
- Maintained by sodium-potassium pump.

---

# Action Potential

Occurs when

- Sodium ions enter the neuron.
- Membrane depolarizes.
- Impulse is generated.

---

# Repolarization

- Potassium ions move out.
- Resting potential restored.

---

# Difference Between Resting Potential and Action Potential

| Resting Potential  | Action Potential     |
| ------------------ | -------------------- |
| Polarized membrane | Depolarized membrane |
| No impulse         | Impulse transmitted  |

---

## Example 1

### Question

What is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system?

### Solution

The neuron is specialized for receiving and transmitting nerve impulses.

### Answer

Neuron.

---

## Example 2

### Question

Which part of the brain controls balance and posture?

### Solution

The cerebellum coordinates muscular movements and maintains balance.

### Answer

Cerebellum.

---

## Example 3

### Question

Which neurotransmitter is commonly released at chemical synapses?

### Solution

Acetylcholine is one of the most common neurotransmitters in the human nervous system.

### Answer

Acetylcholine.

---

## Example 4

### Question

Name the natural pathway of a reflex action.

### Solution

A reflex follows the sequence: receptor → sensory neuron → interneuron → motor neuron → effector.

### Answer

Reflex arc.

---

## Example 5

### Question

Differentiate between sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems.

### Solution

The sympathetic system prepares the body for emergencies ("fight or flight"), whereas the parasympathetic system restores normal body functions ("rest and digest").

### Answer

Sympathetic → Fight or Flight; Parasympathetic → Rest and Digest.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing dendrites with axons.
- Mixing up the functions of the cerebrum and cerebellum.
- Assuming reflex actions are controlled by the cerebrum.
- Confusing sensory neurons with motor neurons.
- Mixing up sympathetic and parasympathetic functions.
- Forgetting that the myelin sheath speeds up impulse conduction.

# Chapter Summary

- The **nervous system** coordinates and regulates body activities by transmitting electrical impulses through specialized cells called **neurons**.
- A neuron consists of **dendrites, a cell body, and an axon**, while impulses are transmitted across **synapses** using neurotransmitters such as **acetylcholine**.
- The **Central Nervous System (CNS)** includes the **brain and spinal cord**, whereas the **Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)** connects the CNS with the rest of the body.
- The brain is divided into the **forebrain, midbrain, and hindbrain**, each responsible for specific functions such as intelligence, coordination, reflexes, and regulation of involuntary activities.
- The **Autonomic Nervous System (ANS)** regulates involuntary body functions through its **sympathetic** and **parasympathetic** divisions.
- Understanding neural control is essential for diagnosing neurological disorders, improving medical treatments, and appreciating how the human body maintains coordination, communication, and homeostasis.
