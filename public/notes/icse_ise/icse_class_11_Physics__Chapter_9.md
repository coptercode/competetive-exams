# Introduction

**Thermal Properties of Matter** deals with the behavior of substances when they gain or lose heat. It explains concepts such as **temperature, heat, thermal expansion, specific heat capacity, calorimetry, change of state, heat transfer, and thermal conductivity**.

These principles are widely used in **engineering, construction, refrigeration, meteorology, power plants, and household appliances**.

---

# 1. Heat

## Definition

Heat is a form of energy that flows from a body at higher temperature to a body at lower temperature.

---

# SI Unit

```
Joule (J)
```

---

# Other Unit

```
1 calorie = 4.186 Joules
```

---

# Characteristics

- Heat is energy in transit.
- Flows naturally from hot to cold.
- Measured using calorimetry.

---

# Difference Between Heat and Temperature

| Heat            | Temperature                   |
| --------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Form of energy  | Degree of hotness or coldness |
| Unit: Joule     | Unit: Kelvin                  |
| Depends on mass | Independent of mass           |

---

# 2. Temperature

## Definition

Temperature is the measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of a body.

---

# SI Unit

```
Kelvin (K)
```

---

# Temperature Scales

- Celsius (°C)
- Fahrenheit (°F)
- Kelvin (K)

---

# Conversion Formulae

```
K = °C + 273
```

---

```
°F = (9/5 × °C) + 32
```

---

```
°C = (5/9)(°F − 32)
```

---

# Difference Between Celsius and Kelvin

| Celsius               | Kelvin                |
| --------------------- | --------------------- |
| Ice point = 0°C       | Ice point = 273 K     |
| Boiling point = 100°C | Boiling point = 373 K |

---

# 3. Thermal Expansion

## Definition

Thermal expansion is the increase in dimensions of a body due to rise in temperature.

---

# Types

- Linear Expansion
- Superficial Expansion
- Cubical Expansion

---

# Linear Expansion

## Formula

```
ΔL = αLΔT
```

Where

- α = Coefficient of linear expansion

---

# Superficial Expansion

```
ΔA = βAΔT
```

---

# Cubical Expansion

```
ΔV = γVΔT
```

---

# Relations

```
β = 2α
```

---

```
γ = 3α
```

---

# SI Unit of Expansion Coefficient

```
K⁻¹
```

---

# Applications

- Railway tracks.
- Bridges.
- Electric wires.
- Expansion joints.

---

# Difference Between Linear and Cubical Expansion

| Linear Expansion | Cubical Expansion |
| ---------------- | ----------------- |
| Change in length | Change in volume  |
| α                | γ                 |

---

# 4. Anomalous Expansion of Water

## Definition

Water behaves differently between **0°C and 4°C**.

---

# Characteristics

- Water contracts when heated from 0°C to 4°C.
- Water expands above 4°C.
- Maximum density at 4°C.

---

# Importance

- Lakes freeze from the top.
- Aquatic life survives in winter.

---

# 5. Specific Heat Capacity

## Definition

Specific heat capacity is the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of **1 kg** of a substance by **1 K**.

---

# Formula

```
      Q
c = --------
      mΔT
```

---

# SI Unit

```
J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹
```

---

# Heat Capacity

## Formula

```
C = mc
```

---

# Difference Between Heat Capacity and Specific Heat Capacity

| Heat Capacity | Specific Heat Capacity |
| ------------- | ---------------------- |
| Whole body    | Unit mass              |
| J K⁻¹         | J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹             |

---

# 6. Calorimetry

## Principle

Heat lost by the hot body equals the heat gained by the cold body.

---

# Formula

```
Heat Lost

=

Heat Gained
```

---

```
m₁c₁(T₁−T)

=

m₂c₂(T−T₂)
```

---

# Applications

- Measuring specific heat.
- Mixing liquids.
- Industrial heating.

---

---

# Latent Heat

## Definition

The heat required to change the state of a substance without changing its temperature.

---

# Formula

```
Q = mL
```

---

# Types

### Latent Heat of Fusion

Solid → Liquid

---

### Latent Heat of Vaporization

Liquid → Gas

---

# Difference Between Fusion and Vaporization

| Fusion           | Vaporization     |
| ---------------- | ---------------- |
| Solid to Liquid  | Liquid to Gas    |
| At melting point | At boiling point |

# 9. Newton's Law of Cooling

## Statement

The rate of loss of heat of a body is directly proportional to the temperature difference between the body and its surroundings for small temperature differences.

---

# Formula

```
dT/dt

∝

(T − Ts)
```

---

# Applications

- Cooling of beverages.
- Thermometers.
- Refrigeration.

---

# 10. Stefan-Boltzmann Law

## Statement

The energy radiated per second is proportional to the fourth power of absolute temperature.

---

# Formula

```
P = σAT⁴
```

Where

- σ = Stefan's constant

---

# Applications

- Stars.
- Furnaces.
- Radiation studies.

---

# 11. Wien's Displacement Law

## Formula

```
λmaxT = Constant
```

---

# Application

Determination of star temperatures.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A rod of length 2 m expands by 2 mm when heated. Find the linear strain.

### Solution

```
ΔL = 2 mm = 0.002 m

Linear Strain

= ΔL/L

= 0.002/2

= 0.001
```

### Answer

**0.001**

---

## Example 2

### Question

Calculate the heat required to raise the temperature of 2 kg of water by 10 K.

(Given: c = 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹)

### Solution

```
Q = mcΔT

= 2 × 4200 × 10

= 84000 J
```

### Answer

**84000 J**

---

## Example 3

### Question

Find the heat required to melt 5 kg of ice.

(Given: Latent heat of fusion = 3.36 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹)

### Solution

```
Q = mL

= 5 × 3.36×10⁵

= 1.68×10⁶ J
```

### Answer

**1.68 × 10⁶ J**

---

## Example 4

### Question

Convert 27°C into Kelvin.

### Solution

```
K = °C + 273

= 27 + 273

= 300 K
```

### Answer

**300 K**

---

## Example 5

### Question

A body has a heat capacity of 400 J K⁻¹. If its mass is 2 kg, find its specific heat capacity.

### Solution

```
c = C/m

= 400/2

= 200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹
```

### Answer

**200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **heat** with **temperature**.
- Forgetting to convert Celsius into Kelvin where required.
- Using incorrect units for specific heat capacity.
- Ignoring latent heat during phase changes.
- Mixing up conduction, convection, and radiation.
- Using α instead of β or γ in expansion problems.

# Chapter Summary

- **Heat** is a form of energy transferred due to temperature difference, while **temperature** measures the degree of hotness or coldness of a body.
- Materials undergo **thermal expansion** when heated, characterized by linear, superficial, and cubical expansion coefficients.
- **Specific heat capacity** and **calorimetry** explain the absorption and transfer of heat between substances, while **latent heat** describes energy involved in phase changes without temperature change.
- Heat is transferred by **conduction, convection, and radiation**, each governed by distinct physical principles and laws.
- **Newton's law of cooling**, **Stefan-Boltzmann law**, and **Wien's displacement law** describe important aspects of thermal radiation and cooling processes.
- Understanding thermal properties is essential in **engineering, climate science, power generation, refrigeration, construction, and modern industrial applications**.
