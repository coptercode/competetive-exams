# Introduction

**Fluid Mechanics** is the branch of physics that studies the behavior of **liquids and gases** at rest and in motion. It explains phenomena such as **pressure, buoyancy, viscosity, surface tension, capillary action, fluid flow, and Bernoulli's principle**.

Fluid mechanics has numerous applications in **hydraulic systems, aviation, shipbuilding, medicine, engineering, and meteorology**.

---

# 1. Fluid

## Definition

A **fluid** is a substance that can flow and continuously changes its shape under the action of an external force.

---

# Types of Fluids

- Liquids
- Gases

---

# Characteristics of Fluids

- Have no fixed shape.
- Flow under applied force.
- Exert pressure in all directions.
- Cannot withstand shear stress.

---

# Difference Between Solids and Fluids

| Solids              | Fluids                     |
| ------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Fixed shape         | No fixed shape             |
| Resist shear stress | Cannot resist shear stress |
| Do not flow         | Flow easily                |

# 5. Archimedes' Principle

## Statement

A body wholly or partially immersed in a fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the displaced fluid.

---

# Buoyant Force

```
FB = ρVg
```

Where

- ρ = Density of fluid
- V = Volume displaced
- g = Acceleration due to gravity

---

# Conditions for Floating

```
Weight

=

Buoyant Force
```

---

# Conditions for Sinking

```
Weight

>

Buoyant Force
```

---

# Applications

- Ships
- Submarines
- Hydrometers
- Balloons

---

# Difference Between Floating and Sinking

| Floating                | Sinking                    |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Weight = Upthrust       | Weight > Upthrust          |
| Density less than fluid | Density greater than fluid |

# 11. Stokes' Law

## Statement

The viscous force acting on a small sphere moving through a viscous fluid is

```
F = 6πηrv
```

Where

- η = Coefficient of viscosity
- r = Radius
- v = Velocity

---

# Terminal Velocity

## Definition

The constant maximum velocity attained by a body falling through a viscous medium.

---

# Formula

```
         2r²(ρ−σ)g
Vt = ----------------
           9η
```

Where

- ρ = Density of body
- σ = Density of fluid

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A liquid has a mass of 10 kg and occupies a volume of 2 m³. Find its density.

### Solution

```
ρ = m/V

= 10/2

= 5 kg m⁻³
```

### Answer

**5 kg m⁻³**

---

## Example 2

### Question

A force of 100 N acts on an area of 0.02 m². Find the pressure.

### Solution

```
P = F/A

= 100/0.02

= 5000 Pa
```

### Answer

**5000 Pa**

---

## Example 3

### Question

Calculate the pressure at a depth of 5 m in water (ρ = 1000 kg m⁻³, g = 9.8 m s⁻²).

### Solution

```
P = ρgh

= 1000 × 9.8 × 5

= 49000 Pa
```

### Answer

**49000 Pa**

---

## Example 4

### Question

A body displaces 0.02 m³ of water. Find the buoyant force.

### Solution

```
FB = ρVg

= 1000 × 0.02 × 9.8

= 196 N
```

### Answer

**196 N**

---

## Example 5

### Question

Water flows through a pipe of area 4 m² at 2 m s⁻¹. Find the velocity when the area becomes 2 m².

### Solution

```
A₁V₁ = A₂V₂

4 × 2 = 2 × V₂

V₂ = 4 m s⁻¹
```

### Answer

**4 m s⁻¹**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **pressure** with **force**.
- Using incorrect units for **surface tension** and **viscosity**.
- Forgetting atmospheric pressure when calculating total pressure.
- Applying Bernoulli's equation to non-ideal fluids.
- Ignoring the condition of streamline flow in Bernoulli's theorem.
- Using incorrect radius in capillary action problems.

# Chapter Summary

- **Fluids** are substances that can flow and take the shape of their containers, exhibiting properties such as **density, pressure, viscosity, and surface tension**.
- **Pascal's law** explains the transmission of pressure in enclosed fluids, forming the basis of hydraulic machines, while **Archimedes' principle** explains buoyancy and flotation.
- **Surface tension** and **capillary action** arise from intermolecular forces and are responsible for many natural and industrial phenomena.
- **Viscosity** represents the internal friction in fluids, and **Stokes' law** describes the resistive force experienced by bodies moving through viscous fluids.
- **Bernoulli's principle** and the **equation of continuity** govern the flow of ideal fluids and have widespread applications in aviation, medicine, and engineering.
- Fluid mechanics is essential in **civil engineering, aerospace, hydraulics, marine engineering, biomedical sciences, meteorology, and industrial processes**.
