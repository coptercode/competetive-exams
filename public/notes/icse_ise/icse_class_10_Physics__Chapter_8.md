# Introduction

**Current Electricity** is the branch of physics that deals with the flow of electric charge through conductors. Electric current powers almost every modern device, from household appliances to industrial machines. This chapter introduces **electric current, potential difference, resistance, Ohm's Law, electrical power, electrical energy, heating effect of current, and household electrical devices**.

---

# 1. Electric Current

## Definition

**Electric Current** is the rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor.

---

## Formula

```
          Charge
Current = --------
           Time

I = Q/t
```

Where,

- **I** = Current (Ampere)
- **Q** = Charge (Coulomb)
- **t** = Time (second)

---

## SI Unit

```
Ampere (A)
```

---

## One Ampere

One ampere is the current when **1 coulomb** of charge flows through a conductor in **1 second**.

```
1 A = 1 C/s
```

---

# 2. Electric Charge

## Definition

Electric charge is the basic property of matter responsible for electrical phenomena.

---

## SI Unit

```
Coulomb (C)
```

---

## Charge Formula

```
Q = It
```

---

# 3. Potential Difference (Voltage)

## Definition

The **Potential Difference** between two points is the work done in moving a unit positive charge from one point to another.

---

## Formula

```
         Work Done
V = ----------------
          Charge

V = W/Q
```

---

## SI Unit

```
Volt (V)
```

---

## One Volt

One volt is the potential difference when **1 joule** of work is done to move **1 coulomb** of charge.

```
1 V = 1 J/C
```

---

# 4. Resistance

## Definition

**Resistance** is the property of a conductor that opposes the flow of electric current.

---

## Formula

```
      V
R = ----
      I
```

---

## SI Unit

```
Ohm (Ω)
```

---

## Factors Affecting Resistance

Resistance depends on:

- Length of conductor (L)
- Cross-sectional area (A)
- Nature of material
- Temperature

---

## Formula

```
        ρL
R = ----------
         A
```

Where,

- **ρ** = Resistivity

---

# 5. Ohm's Law

## Statement

At constant temperature, the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it.

---

## Mathematical Form

```
V ∝ I
```

or

```
V = IR
```

---

## Ohm's Law Graph

```

│
│
│
│
│
└──────────── I

Straight Line
```

# 6. Combination of Resistors

---

## (i) Series Combination

### Characteristics

- Same current flows through all resistors.
- Voltage divides.

---

### Equivalent Resistance

```
R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃
```

---

### Current

```
Same
```

---

## (ii) Parallel Combination

### Characteristics

- Same voltage across each resistor.
- Current divides.

---

### Equivalent Resistance

```
1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃
```

---

# Difference Between Series and Parallel

| Series                     | Parallel                        |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Current is same            | Voltage is same                 |
| Voltage divides            | Current divides                 |
| Total resistance increases | Total resistance decreases      |
| One failure breaks circuit | Other branches continue working |

# 9. Heating Effect of Electric Current

## Joule's Law of Heating

### Statement

The heat produced in a conductor is directly proportional to:

- Square of current.
- Resistance.
- Time.

---

## Formula

```
H = I²Rt
```

---

## SI Unit

```
Joule (J)
```

---

## Applications

- Electric iron.
- Heater.
- Toaster.
- Electric kettle.
- Fuse.

---

# 10. Fuse

## Definition

A **Fuse** is a safety device that protects electrical appliances from excessive current.

---

## Characteristics

- Low melting point.
- High resistance.
- Connected in series.

---

## Function

When excessive current flows, the fuse wire melts and breaks the circuit.

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A charge of **60 C** flows through a conductor in **12 s**. Calculate the current.

### Solution

```
I = Q/t

= 60/12

= 5 A
```

### Answer

```
5 A
```

---

## Example 2

### Question

A resistor has a voltage of **24 V** across it and a current of **3 A**. Find its resistance.

### Solution

```
R = V/I

= 24/3

= 8 Ω
```

### Answer

```
8 Ω
```

---

## Example 3

### Question

An electric appliance operates at **220 V** and draws **2 A** of current. Calculate its power.

### Solution

```
P = VI

= 220 × 2

= 440 W
```

### Answer

```
440 W
```

---

## Example 4

### Question

A heater of **1000 W** operates for **2 hours**. Calculate the electrical energy consumed in **kWh**.

### Solution

```
Power = 1 kW

Time = 2 h

Energy = Pt

= 1 × 2

= 2 kWh
```

### Answer

```
2 kWh
```

---

## Example 5

### Question

A conductor carries **4 A** of current through a **5 Ω** resistor for **10 s**. Calculate the heat produced.

### Solution

Using Joule's Law,

```
H = I²Rt

= 4² × 5 × 10

= 16 × 50

= 800 J
```

### Answer

```
800 J
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **current** with **voltage**.
- Forgetting the SI units (**Ampere**, **Volt**, **Ohm**).
- Using the wrong power formula.
- Mixing up **series** and **parallel** resistor combinations.
- Forgetting that an **ammeter is connected in series** and a **voltmeter in parallel**.
- Ignoring the conversion:

```
1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J
```

- Forgetting to square the current in **Joule's Law of Heating**.

# Chapter Summary

- **Electric current** is the flow of electric charge through a conductor and is measured in **amperes (A)**.
- The relationship between **current, voltage, and resistance** is explained by **Ohm's Law (V = IR)**, which forms the basis of electrical circuit analysis.
- Resistors can be connected in **series** or **parallel**, each affecting the total resistance and current distribution differently.
- **Electrical power** is the rate of electrical energy consumption and is calculated using **P = VI**, while **electrical energy** is measured commercially in **kilowatt-hours (kWh)**.
- According to **Joule's Law of Heating (H = I²Rt)**, heat produced in a conductor depends on the square of the current, the resistance, and the time for which current flows.
- Electrical measuring instruments such as the **ammeter** and **voltmeter**, along with safety devices like the **fuse**, are essential components of electrical systems used in homes, industries, and power distribution.
