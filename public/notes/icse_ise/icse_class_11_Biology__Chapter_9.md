# Introduction

All living organisms are made up of **chemical substances called biomolecules**. These molecules are essential for maintaining life processes such as metabolism, growth, reproduction, respiration, and heredity.

Biomolecules are broadly classified into:

- Carbohydrates
- Proteins
- Lipids
- Nucleic Acids

Apart from these, living cells also contain water, minerals, vitamins, pigments, and enzymes.

---

# 1. Biomolecules

## Definition

Biomolecules are naturally occurring organic and inorganic compounds present in living organisms that perform structural, metabolic, and regulatory functions.

# 2. Carbohydrates

## Definition

Carbohydrates are organic compounds composed of **carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen**, generally represented by the formula:

```
(CH₂O)n
```

They are the primary source of energy in living organisms.

---

## Functions

- Immediate source of energy.
- Food storage.
- Structural components.
- Formation of nucleic acids.
- Cell recognition.

---

# Classification of Carbohydrates

```
Carbohydrates
      │
 ┌────┼───────────┐
 ▼    ▼           ▼
Mono  Di       Polysaccharides
```

---

## A. Monosaccharides

Simplest carbohydrates.

### Characteristics

- Sweet
- Soluble in water
- Cannot be hydrolyzed

Examples

- Glucose
- Fructose
- Galactose

---

## B. Disaccharides

Formed by joining two monosaccharides through a

```
Glycosidic Bond
```

Examples

| Disaccharide | Components          |
| ------------ | ------------------- |
| Sucrose      | Glucose + Fructose  |
| Maltose      | Glucose + Glucose   |
| Lactose      | Glucose + Galactose |

---

## C. Polysaccharides

Long chains of monosaccharides.

Examples

| Polysaccharide | Function                  |
| -------------- | ------------------------- |
| Starch         | Plant storage             |
| Glycogen       | Animal storage            |
| Cellulose      | Plant cell wall           |
| Chitin         | Exoskeleton of arthropods |

---

# Glycosidic Bond

A **glycosidic bond** is the covalent bond formed between two monosaccharides by the removal of a water molecule (condensation reaction).

---

# 3. Proteins

## Definition

Proteins are polymers of amino acids linked together by

```
Peptide Bonds
```

---

# Amino Acid Structure

General structure

```
       H
       |
H₂N — C — COOH
       |
       R
```

Where **R** is the side chain.

---

# Essential Components

- Amino group (-NH₂)
- Carboxyl group (-COOH)
- Hydrogen atom
- Variable R group

---

# Peptide Bond

A peptide bond is formed between the amino group of one amino acid and the carboxyl group of another.

---

# Levels of Protein Structure

## Primary Structure

Linear sequence of amino acids.

---

## Secondary Structure

Regular folding.

Types

- α-Helix
- β-Pleated Sheet

---

## Tertiary Structure

Three-dimensional folding.

---

## Quaternary Structure

Association of two or more polypeptide chains.

Example

```
Haemoglobin
```

---

# Functions of Proteins

- Enzymes
- Hormones
- Transport
- Structural support
- Immunity
- Muscle contraction

---

# Difference Between Fibrous and Globular Proteins

| Fibrous              | Globular             |
| -------------------- | -------------------- |
| Long and thread-like | Spherical            |
| Structural role      | Functional role      |
| Insoluble            | Soluble              |
| Example: Keratin     | Example: Haemoglobin |

# 6. Enzymes

## Definition

Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up biochemical reactions without being consumed.

Most enzymes are proteins.

---

# Properties

- Highly specific.
- Work in small quantities.
- Reusable.
- Lower activation energy.

---

# Components

```
Enzyme
    │
 ┌──┴────────┐
 ▼           ▼
Apoenzyme  Cofactor
```

---

## Apoenzyme

Protein part.

---

## Cofactor

Non-protein component.

Types

- Metal ions
- Coenzymes

---

# Mechanism of Enzyme Action

```
Enzyme + Substrate

↓

Enzyme-Substrate Complex

↓

Product + Enzyme
```

---

# Lock and Key Model

Proposed by

```
Emil Fischer
```

The substrate fits exactly into the enzyme's active site.

---

# Induced Fit Model

Proposed by

```
Daniel Koshland
```

The enzyme changes its shape slightly to fit the substrate.

---

# Michaelis-Menten Concept

The rate of enzyme activity increases with substrate concentration until all active sites become saturated.

---

# Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity

- Temperature
- pH
- Enzyme concentration
- Substrate concentration
- Inhibitors

---

# Enzyme Inhibition

## Competitive Inhibition

- Inhibitor competes with substrate.
- Binds to active site.

---

## Non-Competitive Inhibition

- Inhibitor binds elsewhere.
- Changes enzyme shape.

---

# Difference Between Competitive and Non-Competitive Inhibition

| Competitive                             | Non-Competitive           |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Active site occupied                    | Active site unchanged     |
| Substrate competition                   | No competition            |
| Can be overcome by increasing substrate | Cannot be overcome easily |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Which bond joins amino acids in proteins?

### Solution

Amino acids are linked by **peptide bonds**.

### Answer

Peptide bond.

---

## Example 2

### Question

What is the storage carbohydrate in animals?

### Solution

Animals store carbohydrates in the form of **glycogen**.

### Answer

Glycogen.

---

## Example 3

### Question

Differentiate between DNA and RNA.

### Solution

DNA is double-stranded and contains thymine, whereas RNA is usually single-stranded and contains uracil.

### Answer

DNA: Double-stranded with thymine; RNA: Single-stranded with uracil.

---

## Example 4

### Question

Why are enzymes called biological catalysts?

### Solution

Enzymes accelerate biochemical reactions without being consumed in the process.

### Answer

Because they increase the rate of biochemical reactions without undergoing permanent change.

---

## Example 5

### Question

Name the phospholipid component responsible for cell membrane formation.

### Solution

Phospholipids have a hydrophilic head and hydrophobic tails, enabling them to form the lipid bilayer.

### Answer

Phospholipids.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **glycosidic** and **peptide** bonds.
- Mixing up saturated fats and unsaturated oils.
- Confusing DNA bases with RNA bases.
- Assuming all enzymes function at the same optimum pH and temperature.
- Mixing up competitive and non-competitive inhibition.
- Forgetting that phospholipids are amphipathic.

# Chapter Summary

- **Biomolecules** are the chemical compounds that form the structural and functional basis of all living organisms.
- **Carbohydrates** serve as the primary energy source and include mono-, di-, and polysaccharides linked by **glycosidic bonds**.
- **Proteins**, composed of amino acids joined by **peptide bonds**, perform structural, catalytic, transport, regulatory, and defensive functions.
- **Lipids** are energy-rich hydrophobic molecules essential for energy storage, membrane structure, insulation, and hormone synthesis, with **phospholipids** forming the cell membrane.
- **Nucleic acids (DNA and RNA)** store, transmit, and express genetic information through nucleotides connected by **phosphodiester bonds**.
- **Enzymes** are highly specific biological catalysts that lower activation energy and regulate metabolic pathways, making biomolecules fundamental to every cellular process in living organisms.
