# PART A: ATOMS

---

# 1. Discovery of Atom

Atoms contain:

```
Atom

 │

 ├── Electrons (-ve)
 │
 ├── Protons (+ve)
 │
 └── Neutrons (0)
```

---

# 2. Thomson's Atomic Model

## Description

According to Thomson:

- Atom is a positively charged sphere.
- Electrons are embedded inside it.

---

Known as:

```
Plum Pudding Model
```

---

# Limitation

Could not explain:

- Rutherford scattering results
- Stability of atom

---

# 3. Rutherford's Atomic Model

Based on:

```
Alpha particle scattering experiment
```

---

# Observations

1. Most particles passed straight through.
2. Few particles were deflected.
3. Very few bounced back.

---

# Conclusions

1. Atom has a small, dense, positively charged nucleus.
2. Most of the atom is empty space.
3. Electrons revolve around nucleus.

---

# Limitations

According to classical theory:

Moving electrons should radiate energy and collapse into nucleus.

Could not explain:

- Stability of atom
- Line spectra

---

# 4. Bohr's Atomic Model

Bohr proposed:

Electrons revolve around nucleus only in certain allowed orbits.

---

# Bohr's Postulates

---

## 1. Stationary Orbits

Electrons revolve without radiating energy.

---

## 2. Quantisation of Angular Momentum

\[
mvr=\frac{nh}{2\pi}
\]

Where:

- n = Principal quantum number

---

## 3. Radiation During Transition

Electron emits or absorbs energy when it jumps between energy levels.

---

Energy:

\[
h\nu=E_2-E_1
\]

---

# 5. Radius of Bohr Orbit

For hydrogen atom:

\[
r_n=n^2r_1
\]

---

Where:

\[
r_1=0.529\AA
\]

---

# 6. Velocity of Electron

\[
v_n=\frac{2.18\times10^6}{n}m/s
\]

---

# 7. Energy of Electron

Energy in nth orbit:

\[
E_n=
-\frac{13.6}{n^2}eV
\]

---

For ground state:

\[
E_1=-13.6eV
\]

---

# 8. Hydrogen Spectrum

When electrons transition between energy levels, spectral lines are produced.

---

# Hydrogen Series

```
Hydrogen Spectrum

       │

 ┌─────┼─────┐

 ▼     ▼     ▼

Lyman Balmer Paschen
```

---

# 1. Lyman Series

Region:

```
Ultraviolet
```

Transition ends at:

\[
n=1
\]

---

# 2. Balmer Series

Region:

```
Visible
```

Transition ends at:

\[
n=2
\]

---

# 3. Paschen Series

Region:

```
Infrared
```

Transition ends at:

\[
n=3
\]

---

# 9. Rydberg Equation

For hydrogen spectrum:

\[
\frac1\lambda
=

R
\left(
\frac1{n_1^2}
-

\frac1{n_2^2}
\right)
\]

---

Where:

- R = Rydberg constant

\[
R=1.097\times10^7m^{-1}
\]

---

# PART B: NUCLEI

---

# 10. Atomic Nucleus

## Definition

The central part of an atom containing protons and neutrons is called nucleus.

---

Nucleus contains:

```
Protons + Neutrons
```

Together called:

```
Nucleons
```

---

# 11. Nuclear Notation

\[
^A_ZX
\]

Where:

- X = Element symbol
- Z = Atomic number
- A = Mass number

---

Relationship:

\[
A=Z+N
\]

Where:

N = Number of neutrons

---

# 12. Nuclear Size

Radius of nucleus:

\[
R=R_0A^{1/3}
\]

---

Where:

\[
R_0=1.2\times10^{-15}m
\]

---

# 13. Nuclear Force

## Definition

The force that holds nucleons together inside the nucleus is called nuclear force.

---

Properties:

1. Very strong force.
2. Short range.
3. Independent of charge.

---

# 14. Mass Defect

## Definition

The difference between the mass of nucleus and the sum of masses of its nucleons is called mass defect.

---

Formula:

\[
\Delta m=
(Zm_p+Nm_n)-M
\]

---

Where:

- mp = Mass of proton
- mn = Mass of neutron
- M = Nuclear mass

---

# 15. Binding Energy

## Definition

The energy required to separate a nucleus into its individual nucleons is called binding energy.

---

Formula:

\[
BE=\Delta mc^2
\]

---

Using atomic mass unit:

\[
1u=931.5MeV
\]

---

# 16. Binding Energy Per Nucleon

Formula:

\[
\frac{BE}{A}
\]

---

It indicates:

```
Stability of nucleus
```

---

Higher binding energy per nucleon:

```
More stable nucleus
```

---

# 17. Nuclear Stability

Stability depends on:

- Neutron-proton ratio
- Binding energy

---

Light nuclei:

\[
N/Z\approx1
\]

---

Heavy nuclei:

\[
N/Z>1
\]

---

# 18. Nuclear Fission

## Definition

The splitting of a heavy nucleus into two lighter nuclei with release of energy is called nuclear fission.

---

Example:

\[
^{235}U
\]

---

Process:

```
Heavy nucleus

      ↓

Two lighter nuclei

      ↓

Energy release
```

---

# 19. Chain Reaction

A reaction where released neutrons cause further fission reactions.

---

Applications:

- Nuclear reactors
- Atomic bombs

---

# 20. Nuclear Fusion

## Definition

The combination of two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus with release of energy.

---

Example:

Hydrogen nuclei combine to form helium.

---

Occurs in:

```
Sun and stars
```

---

# Difference Between Fission and Fusion

| Fission                  | Fusion                         |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Heavy nucleus splits     | Light nuclei combine           |
| Used in nuclear reactors | Occurs in stars                |
| Chain reaction           | Requires very high temperature |

---

# 21. Nuclear Reactor

## Definition

A device used to produce controlled nuclear fission reactions.

---

# Main Parts

---

## 1. Fuel

Example:

\[
U^{235}
\]

---

## 2. Moderator

Slows down neutrons.

Examples:

- Graphite
- Heavy water

---

## 3. Control Rods

Control reaction rate.

Example:

- Cadmium

---

## 4. Coolant

Removes heat.

---

# 22. Nuclear Energy

Energy produced from nuclear reactions.

---

Uses:

- Electricity generation
- Medical applications
- Research

# Board Important Questions

## 1. State Bohr's postulates.

**Answer:**

1. Electrons revolve in stable orbits without radiation.
2. Angular momentum is quantised.

\[
mvr=\frac{nh}{2\pi}
\]

3. Radiation occurs during transitions.

---

## 2. What is mass defect?

**Answer:**

The difference between the mass of nucleus and the sum of masses of nucleons is called mass defect.

---

## 3. Define binding energy.

**Answer:**

The energy required to separate a nucleus into individual nucleons is called binding energy.

---

## 4. Explain nuclear fission.

**Answer:**

Nuclear fission is the splitting of a heavy nucleus into lighter nuclei with release of energy.

---

## 5. Differentiate fission and fusion.

| Fission              | Fusion               |
| -------------------- | -------------------- |
| Heavy nucleus splits | Light nuclei combine |
| Reactor process      | Stellar process      |

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing mass number and atomic number.
- Forgetting negative sign in Bohr energy equation.
- Mixing fission and fusion.
- Forgetting binding energy represents nuclear stability.
- Confusing nucleons with electrons.
