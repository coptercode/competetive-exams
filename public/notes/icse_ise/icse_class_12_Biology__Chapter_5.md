# Introduction

**Inheritance** is the process by which genetic information is transferred from parents to offspring.

**Variation** refers to differences among individuals of the same species.

These two concepts form the foundation of:

- Genetics
- Evolution
- Breeding programmes
- Medical genetics

---

# 1. Genetics

## Definition

Genetics is the branch of biology that deals with:

- Heredity
- Variation
- Transmission of characters

---

# Father of Genetics

## Gregor Johann Mendel

Mendel performed experiments on:

```
Garden Pea (Pisum sativum)
```

He proposed the basic laws of inheritance.

---

# 2. Mendel's Experimental Organism

## Why Pea Plant?

Mendel selected pea plants because:

- They have clear contrasting traits.
- They have short life cycles.
- Self-pollination occurs naturally.
- Cross-pollination can be controlled.

---

# 3. Important Genetic Terms

## Gene

A segment of DNA that carries information for a particular trait.

---

## Allele

Alternative forms of a gene.

Example:

```
T = Tall
t = Dwarf
```

---

## Character

A heritable feature.

Example:

Height of plant.

---

## Trait

Specific expression of a character.

Example:

Tall or dwarf.

---

## Genotype

Genetic constitution of an organism.

Example:

```
TT, Tt, tt
```

---

## Phenotype

Observable characteristics.

Example:

Tall plant.

---

## Homozygous

Both alleles are identical.

Examples:

```
TT
tt
```

---

## Heterozygous

Both alleles are different.

Example:

```
Tt
```

---

# 4. Mendel's Monohybrid Cross

A cross involving one pair of contrasting characters.

Example:

Tall plant × Dwarf plant

---

# Parental Generation (P)

```
TT × tt
```

---

# First Filial Generation (F₁)

All offspring:

```
Tt
```

Phenotype:

All tall

---

# Second Filial Generation (F₂)

Self-cross:

```
Tt × Tt
```

---

# Punnett Square

|     | T   | t   |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T   | TT  | Tt  |
| t   | Tt  | tt  |

---

# Genotypic Ratio

```
1 : 2 : 1
```

TT : Tt : tt

---

# Phenotypic Ratio

```
3 : 1
```

Tall : Dwarf

---

# 5. Mendel's Laws of Inheritance

Mendel proposed three laws:

```
Law of Dominance
Law of Segregation
Law of Independent Assortment
```

---

# Law of Dominance

## Statement

When two contrasting alleles are present together, only one expresses itself.

---

Example:

```
Tt

T = Dominant
t = Recessive
```

Tallness appears.

---

# Law of Segregation

## Statement

The two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation.

Also called:

**Law of purity of gametes**

---

Example:

```
Tt

Gametes:
T and t
```

---

# Law of Independent Assortment

## Statement

Genes controlling different characters assort independently during gamete formation.

---

# 6. Dihybrid Cross

Cross involving two pairs of contrasting characters.

Seed shape and seed colour.

---


---

F₁:


---

F₂ Phenotypic Ratio:

# 7. Test Cross

## Definition

Cross between an individual with dominant phenotype and homozygous recessive individual.

---

Example:

```
T_ × tt
```

---

# Purpose:

To determine unknown genotype.

---

# 8. Back Cross

## Definition

Cross between offspring and one of its parents.

---

Types:

- Dominant back cross
- Recessive back cross

---

# 9. Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance

Proposed by:

- Sutton
- Boveri

---

# Concept

Genes are located on chromosomes.

Chromosomes carry hereditary information.

---

# Relationship

```
Genes → Chromosomes → DNA → Traits
```

---

# 10. Linkage

## Definition

The tendency of genes located close together on the same chromosome to be inherited together.

---

Discovered by:

## Thomas Hunt Morgan

---

# Types of Linkage

## Complete Linkage

Genes inherited together without crossing over.

---

## Incomplete Linkage

Genes may separate due to crossing over.

---

# 11. Crossing Over

## Definition

Exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes.

---

Occurs during:

```
Pachytene stage of Prophase I
```

of meiosis.

---

# Importance

- Produces genetic variation.
- Creates new gene combinations.

---

# 12. Recombination

## Definition

Formation of new combinations of genes due to crossing over.

---

Importance:

- Genetic diversity
- Evolution

---

# 16. Mutation

## Definition

A sudden heritable change in genetic material is called mutation.

---

# Types of Mutation

```
Mutation
    │
 ┌──┴──┐
 ▼     ▼
Gene   Chromosomal
Mutation Mutation
```

---

# Gene Mutation

Changes in DNA sequence.

Example:

Sickle cell anaemia.

---

# Chromosomal Mutation

Changes in chromosome number or structure.

Examples:

- Down syndrome
- Turner syndrome

---

# 17. Genetic Disorders

---

# 1. Mendelian Disorders

Caused by mutation in single genes.

Examples:

- Haemophilia
- Thalassemia
- Sickle cell anaemia

---

# 2. Chromosomal Disorders

Caused by abnormal chromosome number.

Examples:

- Down syndrome
- Klinefelter syndrome
- Turner syndrome

---

# 18. Sickle Cell Anaemia

## Cause

Mutation in β-globin gene.

---

Normal:

```
Glutamic acid
```

replaced by:

```
Valine
```

---

Effect:

Abnormal haemoglobin:

```
HbS
```

---

# Inheritance

Autosomal recessive disorder.

---

# 19. Haemophilia

## Definition

A disorder where blood fails to clot properly.

---

Cause:

Deficiency of clotting factor.

---

Inheritance:

X-linked recessive.

---

# 20. Thalassemia

## Definition

A genetic disorder affecting haemoglobin production.

---

Types:

- Alpha thalassemia
- Beta thalassemia

---

# 21. Down Syndrome

```
47
```

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. State Mendel's laws.

**Answer:**

- Law of Dominance
- Law of Segregation
- Law of Independent Assortment

---

## 2. Explain monohybrid cross ratio.

**Answer:**

Phenotypic ratio:

```
3:1
```

Genotypic ratio:

```
1:2:1
```

---

## 3. Why is father responsible for sex determination in humans?

**Answer:**

Because sperm contains either X or Y chromosome, while egg always contains X chromosome.

---

## 4. What causes Down syndrome?

**Answer:**

Presence of an extra chromosome 21 (Trisomy 21).

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing genotype and phenotype.
- Writing incorrect Mendelian ratios.
- Confusing linkage and crossing over.
- Forgetting chromosome numbers in disorders.
- Mixing X-linked and autosomal disorders.
