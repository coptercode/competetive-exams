# 2. Abiotic Factors

## Definition

Non-living components of the environment are called abiotic factors.

# Major Abiotic Factors

## 1. Temperature

Affects:

- Metabolism
- Growth
- Reproduction

---

## 2. Water

Essential for:

- Survival
- Physiological processes

---

## 3. Light

Important for:

- Photosynthesis
- Biological rhythms

---

## 4. Soil

Provides:

- Minerals
- Support
- Water

---

# 3. Responses to Abiotic Factors

Organisms respond differently to environmental changes.

---

# 1. Regulation

Organisms maintain internal conditions despite external changes.

Example:

- Humans maintain body temperature.

---

# 2. Conformation

Internal conditions change according to environment.

Example:

- Many aquatic animals

---

# 3. Migration

Movement to suitable habitats.

Example:

- Birds migrating during winter.

---

# 4. Suspension

Organisms reduce activity during unfavourable conditions.

Examples:

- Hibernation
- Aestivation
- Diapause

---

# 7. Population

## Definition

A group of individuals of the same species living in a particular area is called a population.

---

Examples:

- Human population
- Deer population

---

# Population Attributes

Individual organisms do not have:

- Birth rate
- Death rate
- Sex ratio
- Population density

These are characteristics of populations.

---

# 8. Population Density

## Definition

Number of individuals of a species per unit area or volume.

---

Formula:

\[
Population\ Density =
\frac{Number\ of\ individuals}{Area}
\]

---

Example:

500 plants in 100 m²

\[
Density=5\ plants/m^2
\]

---

# 9. Population Growth

Population size changes due to:

```
Births
Deaths
Immigration
Emigration
```

---

# Formula

\[
N_t=N_0+(B+D+I-E)

- Nt = Population size after time

# 11. Death Rate (Mortality)

# 14. Exponential Growth

Occurs when resources are unlimited.

---

Formula:

\[
\frac{dN}{dt}=rN
\]

Where:

- N = Population size
- r = Growth rate

---

Graph:

```
Population

   |
   |        /
   |      /
   |    /
   |___/________ Time
```

Called:

## J-shaped curve

---

# 15. Logistic Growth

Occurs when resources are limited.

---

Formula:

\[
\frac{dN}{dt}=rN\frac{(K-N)}{K}
\]

---

Where:

- K = Carrying capacity

---

Graph:

```
Population

    |
 K  |────────────
    |       ____
    |    __/
    |___/________ Time
```

Called:

## S-shaped curve

---

# 16. Carrying Capacity

# 18. Mutualism

## Definition

Interaction where both organisms benefit.

---

Examples:

## Lichen

Algae + Fungus

---

Benefits:

Algae:

- Food production

Fungus:

- Shelter and minerals

---

## Mycorrhiza

Fungus + Plant roots

---

# 19. Competition

## Definition

Interaction where organisms compete for limited resources.

---

Example:

Two plant species competing for sunlight.

---

# Competitive Exclusion Principle

Proposed by:

## Gause

---

Statement:

Two species competing for exactly the same resources cannot coexist permanently.

---

# 20. Predation

## Definition

One organism kills and consumes another organism.

---

Examples:

- Lion and deer
- Snake and rat

---

# Importance

- Controls population size
- Maintains ecosystem balance

---

# 21. Parasitism

## Definition

One organism benefits while harming another.

---

Types:

## Ectoparasites

Live outside host.

Example:

- Lice

---

## Endoparasites

Live inside host.

Example:

- Tapeworm

---

# 22. Host-Parasite Adaptations

Parasites show:

- Loss of unnecessary organs
- High reproductive capacity
- Special attachment structures

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. Define population density.

**Answer:**

Population density is the number of individuals of a species present per unit area or volume.

---

## 2. Explain exponential growth.

**Answer:**

Exponential growth occurs when resources are unlimited and population increases rapidly, producing a J-shaped growth curve.

---

## 3. What is carrying capacity?

**Answer:**

Carrying capacity is the maximum population size that an environment can support sustainably.

---

## 4. Differentiate mutualism and parasitism.

| Mutualism              | Parasitism                 |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Both organisms benefit | One benefits, other harmed |
| Example: Lichen        | Example: Tapeworm          |

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing population and community.
- Mixing exponential and logistic growth.
- Forgetting carrying capacity in logistic growth.
- Confusing mutualism and parasitism.
- Writing adaptations as temporary changes.

# Chapter Summary

- **Organisms and Populations** explains how organisms interact with their environment and with other organisms.
- Abiotic factors influence survival, growth, and distribution.
- Organisms develop adaptations to survive environmental conditions.
- Population characteristics include density, growth rate, age structure, and interactions.
- Population growth is controlled by resource availability and carrying capacity.
- Interactions such as mutualism, competition, predation, and parasitism maintain ecological balance.
