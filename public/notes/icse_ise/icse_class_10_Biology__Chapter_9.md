# Introduction

The **Nervous System** is the control and coordination system of the human body. It receives information from the surroundings, processes it, and sends appropriate responses to different organs. It works together with the **endocrine system** to maintain body coordination. The basic structural and functional unit of the nervous system is the **neuron (nerve cell)**.

---

# 1. Nervous System

## Definition

The **Nervous System** is an organ system that controls, coordinates, and regulates all body activities by transmitting nerve impulses.

---

## Functions

- Controls voluntary actions.
- Controls involuntary actions.
- Receives sensory information.
- Coordinates body movements.
- Maintains balance and posture.
- Helps in thinking, learning, and memory.

---

# 2. Neuron (Nerve Cell)

## Definition

A **Neuron** is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system that transmits nerve impulses.

---

## Structure of a Neuron

A neuron consists of:

- Dendrites
- Cell Body (Cyton)
- Nucleus
- Axon
- Myelin Sheath
- Nodes of Ranvier
- Axon Terminals (Synaptic Knobs)

---

### Dendrites

Receive nerve impulses from receptors or other neurons.

---

### Cell Body (Cyton)

Contains the nucleus and controls the activities of the neuron.

---

### Axon

A long projection that carries nerve impulses away from the cell body.

---

### Myelin Sheath

A fatty insulating layer surrounding the axon.

#### Function

- Protects the axon.
- Increases the speed of impulse transmission.

---

### Nodes of Ranvier

Small gaps in the myelin sheath that allow rapid conduction of nerve impulses.

---

### Synapse

## Definition

A **Synapse** is the junction between two neurons or between a neuron and an effector organ.

---

## Function

- Transfers nerve impulses using neurotransmitters.

---

# 3. Types of Neurons

## Sensory Neuron

Carries impulses from receptors to the central nervous system.

---

## Motor Neuron

Carries impulses from the central nervous system to muscles or glands.

---

## Relay (Interneuron)

Connects sensory and motor neurons within the central nervous system.

---

# 4. Central Nervous System (CNS)

## Definition

The **Central Nervous System (CNS)** consists of the **brain** and the **spinal cord**.

---

## Functions

- Receives information.
- Processes information.
- Coordinates responses.
- Controls body functions.

---

# 5. Brain

## Definition

The **Brain** is the chief coordinating organ of the body.

---

## Parts of the Brain

- Cerebrum
- Cerebellum
- Medulla Oblongata

---

### Cerebrum

The largest part of the brain.

#### Functions

- Intelligence
- Thinking
- Memory
- Learning
- Speech
- Voluntary movements
- Interpretation of sensory information

---

### Cerebellum

Located below the cerebrum.

#### Functions

- Maintains body balance.
- Coordinates muscular movements.
- Maintains posture.

---

### Medulla Oblongata

Connects the brain to the spinal cord.

#### Functions

Controls involuntary activities such as:

- Breathing
- Heartbeat
- Swallowing
- Blood pressure
- Coughing
- Sneezing

---

# 6. Spinal Cord

## Definition

The **Spinal Cord** is a long cylindrical structure extending from the medulla oblongata through the vertebral column.

---

## Functions

- Conducts nerve impulses between the brain and body.
- Controls reflex actions.

---

# 7. Reflex Action

## Definition

A **Reflex Action** is a quick, automatic, and involuntary response to a stimulus.

---

## Characteristics

- Immediate response.
- No conscious thinking.
- Protects the body from injury.

---

## Examples

- Withdrawing hand from a hot object.
- Blinking.
- Sneezing.
- Knee-jerk reflex.

---

# 8. Reflex Arc

## Definition

A **Reflex Arc** is the pathway followed by a nerve impulse during a reflex action.

---

## Sequence

```
Stimulus
      │
      ▼
Receptor
      │
      ▼
Sensory Neuron
      │
      ▼
Relay Neuron
      │
      ▼
Motor Neuron
      │
      ▼
Effector
      │
      ▼
Response
```

---

# 9. Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)

## Definition

The **Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)** consists of all nerves outside the brain and spinal cord.

---

## Components

- Cranial nerves
- Spinal nerves

---

## Functions

- Connects the CNS with body organs.
- Carries sensory and motor impulses.

---

# 10. Divisions of the Peripheral Nervous System

## Somatic Nervous System

### Functions

- Controls voluntary actions.
- Supplies skeletal muscles.

---

## Autonomic Nervous System (ANS)

### Functions

- Controls involuntary activities.

---

### Divisions of ANS

#### Sympathetic Nervous System

Prepares the body for emergency ("Fight or Flight").

Effects

- Increases heart rate.
- Dilates pupils.
- Increases breathing rate.
- Inhibits digestion.

---

#### Parasympathetic Nervous System

Restores the body to normal ("Rest and Digest").

Effects

- Slows heart rate.
- Constricts pupils.
- Stimulates digestion.
- Conserves energy.

---

# 11. Sense Organs

## Definition

Sense organs receive external stimuli and convert them into nerve impulses.

---

## Major Sense Organs

- Eyes
- Ears
- Nose
- Tongue
- Skin

---

## Functions

| Sense Organ | Function                           |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Eye         | Vision                             |
| Ear         | Hearing and Balance                |
| Nose        | Smell                              |
| Tongue      | Taste                              |
| Skin        | Touch, Pressure, Pain, Temperature |

---

# 12. Difference Between Voluntary and Involuntary Actions

| Voluntary Actions       | Involuntary Actions           |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Under conscious control | Automatic                     |
| Controlled by cerebrum  | Controlled by medulla and ANS |
| Example: Walking        | Example: Heartbeat            |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Neuron** with **Nerve**.
- Mixing up the **Cerebrum** and **Cerebellum**.
- Assuming reflex actions involve conscious thinking.
- Forgetting that the **medulla oblongata** controls involuntary actions.
- Confusing the **Sympathetic** and **Parasympathetic** nervous systems.
- Mixing up **Sensory** and **Motor** neurons.
- Forgetting the correct sequence of the **Reflex Arc**.
- Assuming all actions are controlled by the cerebrum.

# Chapter Summary

- The **Nervous System** coordinates and controls all body activities by transmitting **nerve impulses** through specialized cells called **neurons**.
- A **neuron** consists of **dendrites, cell body, axon, myelin sheath, nodes of Ranvier, and synaptic terminals**, each playing a specific role in impulse transmission.
- The **Central Nervous System (CNS)** includes the **brain and spinal cord**, while the **Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)** consists of cranial and spinal nerves connecting the CNS to the rest of the body.
- The **cerebrum** controls intelligence, memory, thinking, and voluntary actions; the **cerebellum** coordinates balance and posture; and the **medulla oblongata** regulates involuntary activities such as breathing and heartbeat.
- A **reflex action** is a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus, carried through a **reflex arc** involving sensory, relay, and motor neurons.
- The **Autonomic Nervous System** is divided into the **sympathetic** ("fight or flight") and **parasympathetic** ("rest and digest") divisions, which regulate involuntary body functions.
- The nervous system works closely with the sense organs to receive stimuli, process information, and produce appropriate responses, making it essential for survival, coordination, and adaptation.
