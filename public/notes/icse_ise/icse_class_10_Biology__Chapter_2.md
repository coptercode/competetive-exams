# Introduction

**Genetics** is the branch of biology that deals with the study of **heredity** and **variation**. Heredity is the transmission of characteristics from parents to their offspring through genes. The fundamental principles of genetics were established by **Gregor Johann Mendel**, who is known as the **Father of Genetics**. His experiments on pea plants led to the formulation of the basic laws of inheritance.

---

# 1. Genetics

## Definition

**Genetics** is the branch of biology that studies heredity, variation, genes, and inheritance.

---

# 2. Heredity

## Definition

**Heredity** is the transmission of genetic characteristics from parents to offspring through genes.

### Examples

- Eye colour
- Blood group
- Hair texture
- Height

---

# 3. Variation

## Definition

**Variation** refers to the differences in characteristics among individuals of the same species.

---

## Importance of Variation

- Helps organisms adapt to changing environments.
- Increases chances of survival.
- Promotes evolution.
- Produces biodiversity.

---

# 4. Gregor Johann Mendel

## Contributions

- Conducted experiments on **Garden Pea (Pisum sativum)**.
- Studied inheritance of traits.
- Proposed the **Laws of Inheritance**.
- Introduced the concept of dominant and recessive traits.

---

# 5. Important Genetic Terms

## Gene

A **Gene** is the unit of heredity located on a chromosome.

---

## Alleles

Alternative forms of the same gene controlling the same characteristic.

Example

```
T → Tall

t → Dwarf
```

---

## Dominant Trait

A trait that expresses itself even in the presence of its alternative allele.

Example

```
Tall (T)
```

---

## Recessive Trait

A trait that expresses itself only when both alleles are recessive.

Example

```
Dwarf (t)
```

---

## Genotype

The genetic makeup of an organism.

Examples

```
TT

Tt

tt
```

---

## Phenotype

The observable physical appearance of an organism.

Examples

- Tall Plant
- Dwarf Plant
- Purple Flower

---

## Homozygous

Both alleles are identical.

Examples

```
TT

tt
```

---

## Heterozygous

The two alleles are different.

Example

```
Tt
```

---

# 6. Mendel's Laws of Inheritance

---

## Law of Dominance

### Statement

When two contrasting alleles are present together, only the dominant allele expresses itself, while the recessive allele remains hidden.

---

### Example

```
TT × tt

↓

All Tt

↓

Tall Plants
```

---

## Law of Segregation

### Statement

The two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation so that each gamete receives only one allele.

---

### Importance

- Explains purity of gametes.
- Prevents mixing of genes permanently.

---

## Law of Independent Assortment

### Statement

The inheritance of one pair of contrasting characters is independent of the inheritance of another pair.

---

### Importance

- Produces new combinations.
- Increases genetic variation.

---

# 7. Monohybrid Cross

## Definition

A **Monohybrid Cross** is a cross involving only one pair of contrasting characters.

---

## Example

Tall Plant

```
TT
```

×

Dwarf Plant

```
tt
```

---

### Parental Generation (P)

```
TT × tt
```

---

### Gametes

```
T

t
```

---

### F₁ Generation

| T   | T      |
| --- | ------ |
| t   | **Tt** | **Tt** |
| t   | **Tt** | **Tt** |

All offspring

```
Tall (Tt)
```

---

### Selfing of F₁

```
Tt × Tt
```

---

### F₂ Generation (Punnett Square)

|       | T   | t   |
| ----- | --- | --- |
| **T** | TT  | Tt  |
| **t** | Tt  | tt  |

---

### Phenotypic Ratio

```
3 Tall : 1 Dwarf
```

---

### Genotypic Ratio

```
1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt
```

---

# 8. Dihybrid Cross

## Definition

A **Dihybrid Cross** involves the inheritance of two pairs of contrasting characters.

---

### Example

Seed Shape

```
Round (R)

Wrinkled (r)
```

Seed Colour

```
Yellow (Y)

Green (y)
```

---

### Cross

```
RRYY × rryy
```

---

### F₁ Generation

```
All RrYy
```

---

### F₂ Phenotypic Ratio

```
9 : 3 : 3 : 1
```

---

### Meaning

- 9 Round Yellow
- 3 Round Green
- 3 Wrinkled Yellow
- 1 Wrinkled Green

# 10. Sex-Linked Inheritance

## Definition

Traits controlled by genes located on the **X chromosome** are called **Sex-linked Traits**.

---

## Characteristics

- Mostly affect males.
- Females are often carriers.
- Passed from mother to son.

---

## Examples

### Colour Blindness

An X-linked recessive disorder affecting colour vision.

---

### Haemophilia

A hereditary disorder in which blood fails to clot normally.

---

# 11. Mutation

## Definition

A **Mutation** is a sudden change in the genetic material (DNA) or chromosome.

---

## Causes

- Radiation
- Chemicals
- Errors during DNA replication

---

## Importance

### Useful

- Produces variation.
- Helps evolution.

### Harmful

- Genetic disorders.
- Cancer.
- Birth defects.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Genotype** with **Phenotype**.
- Mixing up **Dominant** and **Recessive** traits.
- Writing the F₂ phenotypic ratio of a monohybrid cross as **1 : 2 : 1** instead of **3 : 1**.
- Forgetting that the **genotypic ratio** is **1 : 2 : 1**.
- Assuming the mother determines the sex of the child; it is determined by the father's X or Y chromosome.
- Confusing **Autosomes** with **Sex Chromosomes**.
- Assuming all genetic disorders are sex-linked.
- Mixing up **Homozygous** and **Heterozygous** genotypes.

# Chapter Summary

- **Genetics** is the study of heredity and variation, explaining how traits are transmitted from parents to offspring.
- **Gregor Johann Mendel**, through his experiments on pea plants, established the **Laws of Dominance, Segregation, and Independent Assortment**, forming the basis of modern genetics.
- **Genes**, located on chromosomes, exist in different forms called **alleles**, which may be **dominant** or **recessive**.
- A **Monohybrid Cross** results in a **3 : 1 phenotypic ratio** and a **1 : 2 : 1 genotypic ratio**, while a **Dihybrid Cross** produces the characteristic **9 : 3 : 3 : 1 phenotypic ratio**.
- In humans, females possess **XX** sex chromosomes and males possess **XY**. The **father determines the sex of the child** by contributing either an X or a Y chromosome.
- **Sex-linked inheritance** explains the transmission of disorders such as **Colour Blindness** and **Haemophilia**, which are usually inherited through the X chromosome.
- **Mutations** introduce genetic variation, contributing to evolution but may also lead to genetic disorders.
- Genetics has extensive applications in medicine, agriculture, biotechnology, forensic science, and genetic engineering.
