# Introduction

Pressure is the force exerted per unit area. In fluids (liquids and gases), pressure acts in all directions and increases with depth. The atmosphere surrounding the Earth also exerts pressure due to the weight of air. This chapter explains the concepts of thrust, pressure, fluid pressure, Pascal's Law, atmospheric pressure, barometers, altimeters, and their practical applications.

---

# 1. Thrust and Pressure

## Thrust

**Thrust** is the force acting **perpendicular (normal)** to a surface.

### SI Unit

```
newton (N)
```

### Examples

- A person standing on the floor exerts thrust.
- A nail driven into wood exerts thrust.
- Water pressing against the walls of a dam.

---

## Pressure

**Pressure** is the thrust acting per unit area.

### Formula

```
Pressure = Thrust / Area

P = F / A
```

where

- **P** = Pressure
- **F** = Thrust (Force)
- **A** = Area

### SI Unit

```
pascal (Pa)
```

### Definition of One Pascal

One Pascal is the pressure produced when a force of **1 N** acts normally on an area of **1 m²**.

```
1 Pa = 1 N/m²
```

---

## Factors Affecting Pressure

Pressure depends on:

- Magnitude of thrust
- Area of contact

### Relationship

- Greater thrust → Greater pressure
- Smaller area → Greater pressure
- Larger area → Smaller pressure

### Everyday Examples

- Sharp knives cut easily because they have a smaller contact area.
- Broad tyres reduce pressure on the road.
- School bags have wide straps to reduce pressure on shoulders.
- Camels have broad feet to walk on sand.

---

# 2. Pressure in Fluids

A **fluid** is a substance that can flow.

Examples:

- Water
- Oil
- Air
- Mercury

Unlike solids, fluids exert pressure in all directions.

---

## Characteristics of Fluid Pressure

- Acts equally in all directions.
- Acts perpendicular to the surface.
- Increases with depth.
- Depends on the density of the fluid.
- Does not depend on the shape of the container.

---

## Pressure Due to a Liquid Column

The pressure exerted by a liquid at a depth is given by:

```
P = hρg
```

where

- **P** = Pressure
- **h** = Depth of liquid
- **ρ (rho)** = Density of the liquid
- **g** = Acceleration due to gravity

### Observations

- Greater depth → Greater pressure.
- Denser liquid → Greater pressure.
- Higher value of **g** → Greater pressure.

---

## Applications

- Dams are thicker at the bottom.
- Divers experience greater pressure underwater.
- Water jets from lower holes of a container travel farther than those from upper holes.

---

# 3. Pascal's Law

## Statement

> **Pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted equally and undiminished in all directions throughout the fluid.**

---

## Principle

When pressure is applied to a liquid enclosed in a container, the pressure is transmitted equally to every part of the liquid and to the walls of the container.

---

## Hydraulic Machines

Pascal's Law is the working principle of hydraulic devices.

### Hydraulic Jack

Used to lift heavy vehicles.

### Hydraulic Lift

Used in:

- Car service stations
- Elevators
- Factories

### Hydraulic Brakes

Used in automobiles.

Advantages:

- Equal braking force on all wheels.
- Smooth and efficient braking.

---

# 4. Atmospheric Pressure

The Earth is surrounded by a thick layer of air called the **atmosphere**.

The weight of this air exerts pressure on everything on the Earth's surface.

This pressure is called **atmospheric pressure**.

---

## Standard Atmospheric Pressure

At sea level,

```
1 atmosphere (atm)

= 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa
```

---

## Effects of Atmospheric Pressure

- Drinking through a straw.
- Syringe operation.
- Suction cups sticking to smooth surfaces.
- Rise of liquid in a dropper.

---

# 5. Mercury Barometer

A **Mercury Barometer** is used to measure atmospheric pressure.

---

## Construction

It consists of:

- A long glass tube closed at one end.
- Filled completely with mercury.
- Inverted into a mercury trough.

A vacuum is formed at the top of the tube called the **Torricellian Vacuum**.

---

## Working

Atmospheric pressure pushes the mercury in the trough upward into the tube.

At sea level,

```
Height of Mercury Column

= 76 cm

= 760 mm
```

---

## Why Mercury is Used

- High density.
- Does not wet glass.
- Low vapour pressure.
- Easily visible.

---

## Why Water is Not Used

Water has low density.

A water barometer would require a tube approximately:

```
10.3 metres
```

long.

Hence it is impractical.

# 6. Altimeter and Weather Forecasting

## Altimeter

An altimeter measures the altitude (height above sea level).

---

## Principle

Atmospheric pressure decreases with increasing altitude.

Higher altitude:

- Lower pressure.

Lower altitude:

- Higher pressure.

---

## Applications

- Aircraft navigation.
- Mountain climbing.
- Surveying.
- Weather stations.

---

## Atmospheric Pressure and Weather

### High Pressure

Usually indicates:

- Clear skies.
- Dry weather.

### Low Pressure

Usually indicates:

- Rainfall.
- Storms.
- Cloud formation.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A force of **250 N** acts on an area of **5 m²**.

Find the pressure.

### Solution

```
P = F / A

= 250 / 5

= 50 Pa
```

### Answer

```
50 Pa
```

---

## Example 2

### Question

Calculate the pressure exerted by water at a depth of **4 m**.

Given:

```
ρ = 1000 kg/m³

g = 9.8 m/s²
```

### Solution

```
P = hρg

= 4 × 1000 × 9.8

= 39200 Pa
```

### Answer

```
39200 Pa
```

---

## Example 3

### Question

A hydraulic press has a small piston area of **0.02 m²**. A force of **100 N** is applied.

Find the pressure produced.

### Solution

```
P = F / A

= 100 / 0.02

= 5000 Pa
```

### Answer

```
5000 Pa
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **thrust** with **pressure**.
- Forgetting that pressure acts **perpendicular** to the surface.
- Using incorrect SI units.
- Assuming fluid pressure depends on the shape of the container.
- Forgetting that pressure increases with depth.
- Using water instead of mercury in barometer-related calculations.
- Confusing atmospheric pressure with fluid pressure.
- Ignoring density while applying the formula **P = hρg**.

# Chapter Summary

- **Thrust** is the force acting normally on a surface.
- **Pressure** is thrust per unit area and is measured in **Pascals (Pa)**.
- Pressure increases when the contact area decreases.
- Fluids exert pressure in all directions and perpendicular to the container walls.
- Pressure in a liquid depends on its **depth**, **density**, and **gravitational acceleration**.
- **Pascal's Law** states that pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.
- Hydraulic machines such as **hydraulic lifts, jacks, and brakes** work on Pascal's Law.
- **Atmospheric pressure** is caused by the weight of the air surrounding the Earth.
- A **Mercury Barometer** measures atmospheric pressure, while an **Altimeter** measures altitude.
- Changes in atmospheric pressure help predict weather conditions.
