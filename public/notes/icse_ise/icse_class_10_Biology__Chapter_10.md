# Introduction

The **Endocrine System** is a system of **ductless glands** that secretes chemical messengers called **hormones** directly into the bloodstream. Hormones regulate growth, metabolism, reproduction, water balance, stress responses, and many other body functions. The endocrine system works closely with the **nervous system** to maintain **homeostasis**.

---

# 1. Endocrine System

## Definition

The **Endocrine System** is a collection of ductless glands that produce hormones to regulate various physiological activities.

---

## Characteristics

- Glands have no ducts.
- Hormones are released directly into the blood.
- Hormones act only on specific target organs.
- Helps maintain internal balance (homeostasis).

---

# 2. Hormones

## Definition

**Hormones** are chemical messengers produced by endocrine glands and transported by blood to target organs.

---

## Characteristics

- Produced in very small quantities.
- Highly specific in action.
- Regulate body functions.
- Slow but long-lasting effects.

---

# 3. Major Endocrine Glands

- Pituitary Gland
- Thyroid Gland
- Parathyroid Glands
- Adrenal Glands
- Pancreas (Islets of Langerhans)
- Testes
- Ovaries

---

# 4. Pituitary Gland

## Definition

The **Pituitary Gland** is known as the **Master Gland** because it controls the activity of many other endocrine glands.

---

## Location

Base of the brain, below the hypothalamus.

---

## Hormones Secreted

### Growth Hormone (GH)

#### Functions

- Stimulates growth of bones and muscles.
- Promotes protein synthesis.

---

### Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH)

#### Function

Stimulates the thyroid gland to secrete thyroxine.

---

### Anti-Diuretic Hormone (ADH)

#### Function

Regulates water reabsorption by the kidneys.

---

### Gonadotropic Hormones

#### Functions

- Stimulate testes and ovaries.
- Control reproductive functions.

---

# 5. Thyroid Gland

## Definition

The **Thyroid Gland** is a butterfly-shaped gland located in the neck.

---

## Hormone

### Thyroxine

---

## Requirements

Iodine is essential for the production of thyroxine.

---

## Functions

- Regulates metabolism.
- Controls growth and development.
- Maintains body temperature.
- Supports normal brain development.

---

# 6. Disorders of the Thyroid Gland

## Goitre

### Cause

Deficiency of iodine.

### Symptoms

- Swelling of the neck.
- Enlarged thyroid gland.

---

## Cretinism

### Cause

Deficiency of thyroxine during childhood.

### Symptoms

- Stunted growth.
- Mental retardation.
- Delayed physical development.

---

## Myxoedema

### Cause

Deficiency of thyroxine in adults.

### Symptoms

- Slow metabolism.
- Weight gain.
- Fatigue.
- Dry skin.

---

# 7. Parathyroid Glands

## Hormone

### Parathormone (PTH)

---

## Functions

- Regulates calcium levels in blood.
- Maintains healthy bones and teeth.

---

# 8. Adrenal Glands

## Location

Situated above each kidney.

---

## Hormone

### Adrenaline (Epinephrine)

---

## Functions

- Increases heart rate.
- Raises blood pressure.
- Increases breathing rate.
- Increases blood glucose level.
- Prepares the body for emergencies.

---

## Fight or Flight Response

Adrenaline prepares the body to respond quickly during:

- Fear
- Stress
- Anger
- Danger

---

# 9. Pancreas

## Definition

The **Pancreas** is both an endocrine and exocrine gland.

---

## Endocrine Part

**Islets of Langerhans**

---

## Hormones

### Insulin

#### Functions

- Lowers blood glucose.
- Converts glucose into glycogen.
- Promotes glucose uptake by cells.

---

### Glucagon

#### Functions

- Raises blood glucose.
- Converts glycogen into glucose.

---

# 10. Diabetes Mellitus

## Definition

A disorder caused by insufficient insulin secretion or ineffective insulin action.

---

## Symptoms

- Frequent urination
- Excessive thirst
- Increased hunger
- Weight loss
- Fatigue

---

## Treatment

- Insulin injections
- Healthy diet
- Regular exercise

---

# 11. Testes

## Hormone

### Testosterone

---

## Functions

- Development of male reproductive organs.
- Secondary sexual characteristics.
- Sperm production.

---

# 12. Ovaries

## Hormones

- Oestrogen
- Progesterone

---

## Functions of Oestrogen

- Development of female reproductive organs.
- Secondary sexual characteristics.

---

## Functions of Progesterone

- Maintains pregnancy.
- Regulates the menstrual cycle.

---

# 13. Difference Between Endocrine and Exocrine Glands

| Endocrine Glands             | Exocrine Glands                   |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Ductless                     | Have ducts                        |
| Hormones released into blood | Secretions released through ducts |
| Act on distant organs        | Act locally                       |
| Example: Pituitary           | Example: Salivary gland           |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Hormones** with **Enzymes**.
- Forgetting that endocrine glands are **ductless**.
- Mixing up **Insulin** and **Glucagon**.
- Assuming **Adrenaline** lowers blood pressure (it increases it).
- Confusing **Goitre** with **Diabetes Mellitus**.
- Forgetting that **iodine deficiency** causes goitre.
- Mixing up **Growth Hormone** with **Thyroxine**.
- Assuming the pancreas is only an endocrine gland (it is both endocrine and exocrine).

# Chapter Summary

- The **Endocrine System** is made up of **ductless glands** that release **hormones** directly into the bloodstream to regulate body functions.
- The **Pituitary Gland**, known as the **Master Gland**, controls growth and regulates the activity of several other endocrine glands through hormones such as **Growth Hormone (GH)**, **TSH**, **ADH**, and **gonadotropic hormones**.
- The **Thyroid Gland** secretes **thyroxine**, which regulates metabolism, growth, and development. Deficiency of iodine can lead to **goitre**, while insufficient thyroxine may cause **cretinism** or **myxoedema**.
- The **Adrenal Glands** produce **adrenaline**, which prepares the body for emergencies through the **fight-or-flight response**.
- The **Pancreas** secretes **insulin** and **glucagon**, which regulate blood glucose levels. Insulin deficiency results in **Diabetes Mellitus**.
- The **Testes** produce **testosterone**, while the **Ovaries** produce **oestrogen** and **progesterone**, regulating reproductive functions and secondary sexual characteristics.
- The endocrine system works in coordination with the nervous system to maintain **homeostasis**, ensuring normal growth, metabolism, reproduction, and overall health.
