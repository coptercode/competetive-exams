# Introduction

**Ray Optics** deals with the study of light propagation using the concept of rays.

It explains:

- Reflection of light
- Refraction of light
- Image formation
- Mirrors
- Lenses
- Optical instruments

---

# 1. Nature of Light

Light travels in the form of rays.

A ray represents:

```
Direction of propagation of light
```

---

# 2. Reflection of Light

## Definition

The bouncing back of light into the same medium after striking a surface is called reflection.

---

# Laws of Reflection

## First Law

The incident ray, reflected ray, and normal lie in the same plane.

---

## Second Law

Angle of incidence is equal to angle of reflection.

\[
i=r
\]

---

# Types of Reflection

```
Reflection

    │

 ┌──┴──┐

 ▼     ▼

Regular  Diffuse
```

---

# Regular Reflection

Occurs on smooth surfaces.

Example:

- Mirror

---

# Diffuse Reflection

Occurs on rough surfaces.

Example:

- Wall

---

# 3. Spherical Mirrors

A spherical mirror is a reflecting surface that forms part of a sphere.

---

Types:

```
Spherical Mirrors

       │

 ┌─────┴─────┐

 ▼           ▼

Concave     Convex
Mirror      Mirror
```

---

# 4. Terms Related to Spherical Mirrors

## Pole (P)

Centre of mirror surface.

---

## Centre of Curvature (C)

Centre of sphere from which mirror is formed.

---

## Radius of Curvature (R)

Distance between P and C.

\[
R=PC
\]

---

## Principal Axis

Line joining pole and centre of curvature.

---

## Focus (F)

Point where parallel rays meet after reflection.

---

Relation:

\[
f=\frac R2
\]

---

# 5. Mirror Formula

For spherical mirrors:

\[
\frac1f=\frac1v+\frac1u
\]

Where:

- f = Focal length
- v = Image distance
- u = Object distance

---

# 6. Magnification of Mirror

\[
m=\frac{h_i}{h_o}
\]

Also:

\[
m=-\frac vu
\]

---

Where:

- hi = Image height
- ho = Object height

---

# 7. Sign Convention (Cartesian)

Rules:

1. All distances measured from pole.
2. Direction of incident light is positive.
3. Opposite direction is negative.
4. Heights above axis are positive.
5. Heights below axis are negative.

---

# 8. Image Formation by Concave Mirror

| Object Position | Image                    |
| --------------- | ------------------------ |
| At infinity     | At focus, real, inverted |
| Beyond C        | Between C and F          |
| At C            | At C                     |
| Between C and F | Beyond C                 |
| At F            | At infinity              |
| Between F and P | Behind mirror, virtual   |

---

# 9. Convex Mirror

- Always forms virtual image
- Always erect
- Always diminished

---

# Mirror Formula Applications

- Vehicle rear-view mirrors

- Reflecting telescopes

# 10. Refraction of Light

## Definition

The bending of light when it passes from one medium to another is called refraction.

---

Reason:

Change in speed of light.

---

# Laws of Refraction (Snell's Law)

## First Law

Incident ray, refracted ray, and normal lie in same plane.

---

## Second Law

\[
\frac{\sin i}{\sin r}=constant
\]

---

This constant is called:

```
Refractive index
```

---

# 11. Refractive Index

Formula:

\[
\mu=\frac cv
\]

Where:

- c = Speed of light in vacuum
- v = Speed of light in medium

---

# Relative Refractive Index

\[
\mu_{21}=
\frac{\mu_2}{\mu_1}
\]

---

# 12. Refraction Through Glass Slab

When light passes through a glass slab:

- It bends towards normal while entering.
- It bends away while leaving.

---

The emergent ray is:

```
Parallel to incident ray
```

---

# 13. Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

## Definition

Complete reflection of light back into the denser medium when it travels from denser to rarer medium.

---

# Conditions for TIR

1. Light must travel from denser to rarer medium.
2. Angle of incidence must be greater than critical angle.

---

# Critical Angle

Angle of incidence at which angle of refraction becomes 90°.

---

Formula:

\[
\sin C=\frac1\mu
\]

---

# Applications of TIR

- Optical fibres
- Diamond brilliance
- Prisms

---

# 14. Optical Fibre

An optical fibre works on:

```
Total Internal Reflection
```

---

Uses:

# 15. Refraction at Spherical Surface

\frac{\mu_2-\mu_1}{R}

# 20. Power of Lens

## Definition

Ability of lens to converge or diverge light.

---

Formula:

\[

\]

---

Unit:

\[

\]

---

# Combination of Lenses

\[

\]

---

# 21. Lens Maker's Formula

\[
\frac1f=
(\mu-1)
\left(
\frac1{R_1}
-

\frac1{R_2}
\right)
\]

# 22. Prism

A prism is a transparent refracting medium bounded by two inclined surfaces.

---

# Angle of Deviation

The angle between incident and emergent rays is called deviation.

---

Formula:

\[
\delta=i+e-A
\]

---

# Minimum Deviation

At minimum deviation:

\[
i=e
\]

---

# Refractive Index of Prism

\[
\mu=
\frac{\sin\frac{A+D_m}{2}}
{\sin\frac A2}
\]

---

# Board Important Questions

## 1. State laws of reflection.

**Answer:**

1. Incident ray, reflected ray, and normal lie in same plane.
2. Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection.

\[
i=r
\]

---

## 2. Explain total internal reflection.

**Answer:**

Total internal reflection occurs when light travelling from denser to rarer medium is completely reflected back when angle of incidence exceeds critical angle.

---

## 3. Derive lens formula.

Important derivation:

\[
\frac1f=\frac1v-\frac1u
\]

---

## 4. What is power of lens?

**Answer:**

Power is the reciprocal of focal length in metres.

\[
P=\frac1f
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing mirror and lens formulas.
- Forgetting sign convention.
- Mixing convex and concave lens properties.
- Forgetting TIR conditions.
- Confusing critical angle and angle of deviation.
