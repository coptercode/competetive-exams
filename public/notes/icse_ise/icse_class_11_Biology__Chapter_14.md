# Introduction

**Respiration** is the biochemical process by which living cells release energy from food molecules. Plants continuously respire throughout their life to obtain **ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate)**, which powers cellular activities such as growth, transport, synthesis, and repair.

Unlike photosynthesis, respiration occurs in **all living cells**, both during the day and night.

---

# Definition

Respiration is the **oxidation of organic food (mainly glucose)** to release energy in the form of ATP.

General Equation (Aerobic Respiration)

```
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

↓

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy (ATP)
```

---

# Importance of Respiration

- Releases ATP for cellular activities.
- Supports growth and development.
- Provides intermediates for biosynthesis.
- Maintains metabolism.
- Essential for active transport.
- Produces metabolic water.

# 1. Aerobic Respiration

## Definition

Respiration occurring **in the presence of oxygen**, resulting in the complete oxidation of glucose.

---

## Characteristics

- Requires oxygen.
- Complete oxidation of glucose.
- Produces maximum ATP.
- Occurs mainly in mitochondria.
- End products:
  - Carbon dioxide
  - Water

---

## Overall Equation

```
Glucose + Oxygen

↓

Carbon Dioxide + Water + ATP
```

---

# 2. Anaerobic Respiration

## Definition

Respiration occurring **without oxygen**, leading to partial breakdown of glucose.

---

## Characteristics

- Oxygen absent.
- Less ATP produced.
- Occurs in cytoplasm.
- Incomplete oxidation.

---

## Alcoholic Fermentation (Yeast)

```
Glucose

↓

Ethanol + CO₂ + ATP
```

---

## Lactic Acid Fermentation

Occurs in

- Muscle cells
- Certain bacteria

```
Glucose

↓

Lactic Acid + ATP
```

---

# Difference Between Aerobic and Anaerobic Respiration

| Aerobic Respiration   | Anaerobic Respiration        |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Oxygen required       | Oxygen not required          |
| Complete oxidation    | Incomplete oxidation         |
| Mitochondria involved | Cytoplasm only               |
| 36–38 ATP             | 2 ATP                        |
| CO₂ + H₂O produced    | Ethanol/Lactic acid produced |

# 3. Glycolysis

## Definition

Glycolysis is the **ten-step oxygen-independent pathway** in which one glucose molecule is converted into **two molecules of pyruvate**.

---

## Site

```
Cytoplasm
```

---

## Discovered By

```
Embden-Meyerhof-Parnas (EMP) Pathway
```

---

# Summary of Glycolysis

```
1 Glucose

↓

2 Pyruvate

↓

2 ATP (Net)

+

2 NADH
```

---

# Major Steps

1. Glucose phosphorylation.
2. Formation of fructose-1,6-bisphosphate.
3. Cleavage into two triose phosphates.
4. Oxidation of glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate.
5. ATP formation.
6. Formation of pyruvate.

---

# Significance

- Common pathway for aerobic and anaerobic respiration.
- Produces ATP directly.
- Produces NADH.

---

# 4. Oxidative Decarboxylation

Conversion of pyruvate into **Acetyl-CoA** before entering the Krebs cycle.

---

## Site

```

---

## Reaction

```
Pyruvate

Acetyl-CoA

+

---

## Enzyme

```
Pyruvate Dehydrogenase Complex
```

---

# Importance

- Links glycolysis with Krebs cycle.
- Produces NADH.
- Releases CO₂.

# 6. Electron Transport Chain (ETC)

## Definition

The Electron Transport Chain is a series of protein complexes located on the **inner mitochondrial membrane** that transfer electrons from NADH and FADH₂ to oxygen, producing ATP.

---

## Site

```
Inner Mitochondrial Membrane (Cristae)
```

---

# Components

```
Complex I

↓

Complex II

↓

Coenzyme Q

↓

Complex III

↓

Cytochrome c

↓

Complex IV

↓

Oxygen
```

---

# Final Electron Acceptor

```
Oxygen
```

---

# End Product

```
Water
```

---

# Importance

- Produces maximum ATP.
- Reoxidizes NADH and FADH₂.
- Maintains cellular energy supply.

---

# Chemiosmotic Theory

## Proposed By

```
Peter Mitchell
```

---

## Explanation

- Electron transport pumps H⁺ ions across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- A proton gradient is formed.
- ATP synthase uses this gradient to synthesize ATP.

---

# ATP Synthase

```
ADP + Pi

↓

ATP
```

# 8. Amphibolic Pathway

## Definition

Respiration acts as both a **catabolic** and **anabolic** pathway.

Hence it is called an

```
Amphibolic Pathway
```

---

# Why Amphibolic?

Catabolism

- Breakdown of glucose.

Anabolism

- Intermediates used for synthesis of amino acids, fats, and nucleotides.

---

# 9. Respiratory Quotient (RQ)

## Definition

Respiratory Quotient is the ratio of the **volume of carbon dioxide evolved** to the **volume of oxygen consumed** during respiration.

---

## Formula

```
RQ = CO₂ Released

      ─────────────

      O₂ Consumed
```

---

# RQ Values

| Substrate     | RQ      |
| ------------- | ------- |
| Carbohydrates | 1.0     |
| Proteins      | 0.8–0.9 |
| Fats          | 0.7     |
| Organic Acids | >1      |

---

# Significance

- Identifies respiratory substrate.
- Helps measure metabolic activity.

## Example 1

### Question

Where does glycolysis occur?

### Solution

Glycolysis takes place in the **cytoplasm** of all living cells.

### Answer

Cytoplasm.

---

## Example 2

### Question

Which stage of respiration produces the maximum ATP?

### Solution

The **Electron Transport Chain** generates most of the ATP through oxidative phosphorylation.

### Answer

Electron Transport Chain.

---

## Example 3

### Question

Who proposed the Chemiosmotic Theory?

### Solution

The Chemiosmotic Theory was proposed by **Peter Mitchell**.

### Answer

Peter Mitchell.

---

## Example 4

### Question

What is the Respiratory Quotient (RQ) for carbohydrates?

### Solution

For carbohydrates, the volume of CO₂ released equals the volume of O₂ consumed.

### Answer

RQ = 1.0.

---

## Example 5

### Question

Why is respiration called an amphibolic pathway?

### Solution

Respiration not only breaks down glucose to release energy (catabolism) but also provides intermediates for the synthesis of biomolecules (anabolism).

### Answer

Because it functions in both catabolism and anabolism.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **photosynthesis** with **respiration**.
- Assuming glycolysis requires oxygen.
- Mixing up the sites of glycolysis, Krebs cycle, and ETC.
- Forgetting that oxygen is the **final electron acceptor** in the ETC.
- Confusing ATP production in glycolysis with total ATP yield.
- Mixing up RQ values for carbohydrates, fats, and proteins.

# Chapter Summary

- **Respiration in Plants** is the process by which glucose is oxidized to release energy in the form of ATP, supporting all cellular activities.
- Aerobic respiration consists of **glycolysis, oxidative decarboxylation, the Krebs cycle, and the Electron Transport Chain**, resulting in the complete oxidation of glucose.
- **Glycolysis** occurs in the cytoplasm and produces pyruvate, while the **Krebs cycle** and **ETC** occur in mitochondria, generating the majority of ATP.
- The **Chemiosmotic Theory**, proposed by **Peter Mitchell**, explains ATP synthesis through a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- The **Respiratory Quotient (RQ)** helps identify the type of respiratory substrate being metabolized by comparing CO₂ released to O₂ consumed.
- Respiration is considered an **amphibolic pathway** because it functions in both the breakdown of biomolecules for energy and the synthesis of essential cellular compounds, making it central to plant metabolism, growth, and survival.
