# Introduction

Plants require **essential mineral elements** for their growth, development, metabolism, and reproduction. Besides carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen obtained from air and water, plants absorb various **mineral nutrients from the soil** through their roots.

Mineral nutrition plays an important role in:

- Growth and development
- Photosynthesis
- Respiration
- Protein synthesis
- Enzyme activation
- Flowering and fruiting

---

# 1. Mineral Nutrition

## Definition

**Mineral nutrition** is the process by which plants absorb, transport, and utilize essential mineral elements from the soil.

---

# 2. Essential Elements

## Definition

Essential elements are mineral nutrients that are indispensable for the normal growth and reproduction of plants.

---

# Criteria of Essentiality (Arnon and Stout, 1939)

An element is considered essential if:

- A plant cannot complete its life cycle without it.
- Its deficiency cannot be replaced by another element.
- It is directly involved in plant metabolism.

---

| Macronutrients              | Micronutrients            |

| Example: N, P, K            | Example: Fe, Zn, Cu       |

# 6. Deficiency Symptoms

## Definition

Deficiency symptoms are visible abnormalities caused by the lack of essential mineral nutrients.

---

# Common Symptoms

- Chlorosis
- Necrosis
- Stunted growth
- Delayed flowering
- Premature leaf fall
- Reduced fruit production

---

# Chlorosis

Yellowing of leaves due to lack of chlorophyll.

Commonly caused by deficiency of:

- Nitrogen
- Magnesium
- Iron

---

# Necrosis

Death of plant tissues.

Occurs due to deficiency of:

- Calcium
- Potassium
- Copper

---

# Inhibition of Cell Division

Occurs due to deficiency of:

- Calcium
- Boron

---

# Delayed Flowering

Usually caused by phosphorus deficiency.

---

# Difference Between Chlorosis and Necrosis

| Chlorosis                  | Necrosis             |
| -------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Yellowing of leaves        | Death of tissues     |
| Chlorophyll deficiency     | Tissue destruction   |
| Reversible in early stages | Usually irreversible |

---

# 7. Mobility of Mineral Elements

## Mobile Elements

Can move from older leaves to younger leaves.

Examples

- Nitrogen
- Phosphorus
- Potassium
- Magnesium

Deficiency appears first in

```
Older Leaves
```

---

## Immobile Elements

Cannot move easily.

Examples

- Calcium
- Boron
- Iron

Deficiency appears first in

```
Young Leaves
```

---

# Difference Between Mobile and Immobile Elements

| Mobile                   | Immobile                   |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Move within plant        | Do not move easily         |
| Deficiency in old leaves | Deficiency in young leaves |
| N, P, K                  | Ca, Fe, B                  |

---

---

Nitrogen is required for:

- DNA
- RNA
- Chlorophyll

---

# Difference Between Nitrogen Fixation and Nitrification

| Nitrogen Fixation            | Nitrification                           |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Converts N₂ to NH₃           | Converts NH₃ to NO₂⁻ and NO₃⁻           |
| Performed by Rhizobium       | Performed by Nitrosomonas & Nitrobacter |
| First step of nitrogen cycle | Intermediate step                       |

---

# Difference Between Nitrification and Denitrification

| Nitrification            | Denitrification               |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| Produces nitrates        | Produces atmospheric nitrogen |
| Aerobic process          | Anaerobic process             |
| Increases soil fertility | Decreases soil fertility      |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Name the bacterium responsible for symbiotic nitrogen fixation.

### Solution

Rhizobium lives in the root nodules of leguminous plants and fixes atmospheric nitrogen.

### Answer

Rhizobium.

---

## Example 2

### Question

Which mineral element is the central atom of chlorophyll?

### Solution

Magnesium forms the central atom of the chlorophyll molecule.

### Answer

Magnesium (Mg).

---

## Example 3

### Question

What is hydroponics?

### Solution

Hydroponics is the cultivation of plants in a nutrient solution without soil.

### Answer

Growing plants without soil using nutrient solutions.

---

## Example 4

### Question

Which bacterium converts nitrite into nitrate?

### Solution

Nitrobacter oxidizes nitrite (NO₂⁻) into nitrate (NO₃⁻).

### Answer

Nitrobacter.

---

## Example 5

### Question

Why does nitrogen deficiency reduce plant growth?

### Solution

Nitrogen is required for the synthesis of proteins, nucleic acids, chlorophyll, and enzymes. Its deficiency limits growth and causes chlorosis.

### Answer

Because nitrogen is essential for protein and chlorophyll synthesis.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **macronutrients** with **micronutrients**.
- Mixing up **chlorosis** and **necrosis**.
- Confusing **nitrogen fixation** with **nitrification**.
- Forgetting the sequence of the nitrogen cycle.
- Assuming all deficiency symptoms appear in older leaves.
- Mixing up the functions of magnesium and iron.

# Chapter Summary

- **Mineral Nutrition** deals with the absorption, transport, and utilization of essential mineral elements required for plant growth and metabolism.
- Essential nutrients are classified into **macronutrients** and **micronutrients** based on the quantity required by plants.
- Deficiency of mineral nutrients produces characteristic symptoms such as **chlorosis, necrosis, stunted growth, and delayed flowering**, helping diagnose nutrient deficiencies.
- **Hydroponics** demonstrates that plants can grow successfully without soil when supplied with an appropriate nutrient solution.
- The **nitrogen cycle** continuously circulates nitrogen through fixation, nitrification, assimilation, ammonification, and denitrification, ensuring the availability of nitrogen for plants.
- Understanding mineral nutrition is fundamental to **agriculture, soil management, crop improvement, sustainable farming, and environmental conservation**, enabling efficient nutrient management and improved crop productivity.
