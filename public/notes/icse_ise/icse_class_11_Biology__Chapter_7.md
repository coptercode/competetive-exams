# Introduction

The **Structural Organisation in Animals** deals with the arrangement of cells into tissues, organs, and organ systems. Animals possess highly specialized tissues that perform specific functions such as protection, movement, support, transport, and coordination.

This chapter also includes the detailed study of two representative animals:

- **Cockroach (Periplaneta americana)** – An insect representing Arthropoda.
- **Frog (Rana tigrina/Hoplobatrachus tigerinus)** – An amphibian representing Vertebrata.

Understanding their morphology and anatomy provides insight into the organization and functioning of higher animals.

---

## Definition

# 2. Epithelial Tissue

## Definition

Epithelial tissue forms the outer covering of the body and lines internal organs and cavities.

---

## Characteristics

- Cells are closely packed.
- Very little intercellular material.
- Rests on a basement membrane.
- No blood vessels.
- Rapid regeneration.

---

# Types of Epithelial Tissue

## A. Simple Squamous Epithelium

- Thin and flat cells.
- Allows diffusion.

Examples

- Alveoli
- Blood capillaries

---

## B. Cuboidal Epithelium

- Cube-shaped cells.
- Secretion and absorption.

Examples

- Kidney tubules
- Glands

---

## C. Columnar Epithelium

- Tall cells.
- Absorption and secretion.

Examples

- Stomach
- Intestine

---

## D. Ciliated Epithelium

- Columnar cells with cilia.
- Moves mucus or ova.

Examples

- Trachea
- Fallopian tube

---

## E. Glandular Epithelium

- Specialized for secretion.

Examples

- Salivary glands
- Sweat glands

---

# Functions

- Protection
- Secretion
- Absorption
- Excretion
- Sensory reception

---

---

## Smooth Muscle

- Involuntary
- Non-striated

---

## Cardiac Muscle

- Striated

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Which tissue connects muscles to bones?

### Solution

Tendons are dense connective tissues that attach muscles to bones.

### Answer

Tendon.

---

## Example 2

### Question

Name the excretory organ of the cockroach.

### Solution

Cockroaches remove nitrogenous wastes through **Malpighian tubules**.

### Answer

Malpighian tubules.

---

## Example 3

### Question

How many chambers are present in the frog's heart?

### Solution

The frog has a three-chambered heart consisting of two atria and one ventricle.

### Answer

Three chambers.

---

## Example 4

### Question

Which type of muscle is present in the heart?

### Solution

The heart is composed of **cardiac muscle**, which is involuntary, striated, and branched.

### Answer

Cardiac muscle.

---

## Example 5

### Question

State one adaptation that helps frogs live both on land and in water.

### Solution

The moist skin enables **cutaneous respiration**, allowing gas exchange in water and on land.

### Answer

Moist skin for cutaneous respiration.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing tendons with ligaments.
- Mixing up voluntary and involuntary muscles.
- Assuming cockroaches have a closed circulatory system.
- Forgetting that insects respire through tracheae, not lungs.
- Confusing hepatic portal circulation with renal portal circulation.
- Assuming frogs respire only through lungs.

# Chapter Summary

- **Structural Organisation in Animals** explains how cells are organized into **epithelial, connective, muscular, and nervous tissues**, each specialized for specific functions such as protection, support, movement, and coordination.
- The **cockroach** serves as a representative arthropod, illustrating an **open circulatory system, tracheal respiration, Malpighian tubules for excretion, and a chitinous exoskeleton**.
- The **frog** represents amphibians and demonstrates adaptations for both aquatic and terrestrial life, including **cutaneous and pulmonary respiration, a three-chambered heart, and portal circulation**.
- Comparative study of cockroach and frog highlights the increasing complexity of animal organization and the evolutionary adaptations of different groups.
- Knowledge of animal tissues and organ systems forms the foundation for advanced studies in **zoology, physiology, medicine, veterinary science, biotechnology, and evolutionary biology**.
