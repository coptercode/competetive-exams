# Introduction

**Work, Energy, and Power** are fundamental concepts in mechanics that describe how forces produce motion, how energy is transferred, and how quickly work is done. These concepts explain the operation of machines, engines, vehicles, and everyday activities. This chapter also introduces the **Work-Energy Theorem** and the **Law of Conservation of Energy**.

---

# 1. Work

## Definition

**Work** is said to be done when a force acting on a body causes displacement in the direction of the force.

---

## Conditions for Work

- A force must act on the body.
- The body must be displaced.
- The displacement should have a component in the direction of the applied force.

---

## Formula

```
Work = Force × Displacement × cos θ

W = Fd cosθ
```

Where,

- W = Work (J)
- F = Force (N)
- d = Displacement (m)
- θ = Angle between force and displacement

---

## SI Unit

```
Joule (J)
```

### 1 Joule

One joule is the work done when a force of **1 N** moves a body through **1 m** in the direction of the force.

---

## Types of Work

### Positive Work

Force and displacement are in the same direction.

```
θ = 0°

W = +Fd
```

Example:

- Pulling a cart.

---

### Negative Work

Force acts opposite to displacement.

```
θ = 180°

W = -Fd
```

Example:

- Friction.

---

### Zero Work

No displacement or force is perpendicular to displacement.

```
θ = 90°

W = 0
```

Example:

- Carrying a bag on a horizontal road.

# 3. Potential Energy (PE)

## Definition

**Potential Energy** is the energy possessed by a body due to its position or configuration.

---

## Gravitational Potential Energy

### Formula

```
PE = mgh
```

Where,

- m = Mass (kg)
- g = Acceleration due to gravity (9.8 m/s²)
- h = Height (m)

---

## SI Unit

```
Joule (J)
```

---

## Factors Affecting PE

- Mass.
- Height.
- Gravitational acceleration.

---

# 4. Kinetic Energy (KE)

## Definition

**Kinetic Energy** is the energy possessed by a body due to its motion.

---

## Formula

```
      1
KE = --- mv²
      2
```

Where,

- m = Mass (kg)
- v = Velocity (m/s)

---

## SI Unit

```
Joule (J)
```

---

## Factors Affecting KE

- Mass.
- Velocity (depends on the square of velocity).

---

# 5. Mechanical Energy

## Definition

The sum of kinetic energy and potential energy.

---

## Formula

```
Mechanical Energy

=

PE + KE
```

or

```
E = U + K
```

---

# 6. Conservation of Energy

## Law

**Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be transformed from one form to another.**

---

## Free Fall of a Body

### At the Highest Point

```
PE = Maximum

KE = Zero
```

---

### During Fall

```
PE decreases

↓

KE increases
```

---

### Just Before Reaching Ground

```
PE ≈ Zero

KE = Maximum
```

---

### Total Mechanical Energy

```
PE + KE

=

Constant
```

---

# 7. Work-Energy Theorem

## Statement

**The work done on a body is equal to the change in its kinetic energy.**

---

## Mathematical Form

```
Work Done

=

Change in Kinetic Energy
```

or

```
W = ΔKE
```

---

# 8. Power

## Definition

**Power** is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred.

---

## Formula

```
Power = Work / Time

P = W/t
```

---

## Alternative Formula

```
P = F × v
```

Where,

- P = Power (W)
- F = Force (N)
- v = Velocity (m/s)

---

## SI Unit

```
Watt (W)
```

---

## One Watt

A power of **1 watt** means **1 joule of work is done in 1 second**.

---

## Larger Units

```
1 kW = 1000 W

1 MW = 10⁶ W
```

---

# Difference Between Potential Energy and Kinetic Energy

| Potential Energy         | Kinetic Energy          |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Due to position          | Due to motion           |
| Formula: PE = mgh        | Formula: KE = ½mv²      |
| Can exist without motion | Exists only when moving |
| Depends on height        | Depends on velocity     |

| ------------------ | ------------------ |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A force of **25 N** moves an object through **4 m** in the direction of the force. Calculate the work done.

### Solution

Given,

```
F = 25 N

d = 4 m

θ = 0°
```

Using

```
W = Fd cosθ

= 25 × 4 × cos0°

= 100 J
```

### Answer

```
100 J
```

---

## Example 2

### Question

Calculate the potential energy of a **5 kg** object placed **8 m** above the ground.

### Solution

```
m = 5 kg

g = 9.8 m/s²

h = 8 m
```

```
PE = mgh

= 5 × 9.8 × 8

= 392 J
```

### Answer

```
392 J
```

---

## Example 3

### Question

Calculate the kinetic energy of a **2 kg** object moving at **6 m/s**.

### Solution

```
KE = ½mv²

= ½ × 2 × 6²

= 36 J
```

### Answer

```
36 J
```

---

## Example 4

### Question

A machine performs **2400 J** of work in **60 s**. Calculate its power.

### Solution

```
P = W/t

= 2400 / 60

= 40 W
```

### Answer

```
40 W
```

---

## Example 5

### Question

State the Law of Conservation of Energy.

### Solution

Energy changes from one form to another without being lost.

### Answer

**Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be transformed from one form to another.**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **work** with **force**.
- Forgetting the **cosθ** term in the work formula.
- Using **mass instead of weight** in potential energy calculations.
- Ignoring that **kinetic energy depends on the square of velocity**.
- Confusing **power** with **energy**.
- Forgetting SI units (**Joule** and **Watt**).
- Assuming energy is lost during transformation instead of converted.

# Chapter Summary

- **Work** is done when a force causes displacement and is calculated using **W = Fd cosθ**.
- **Energy** is the ability to do work and exists in many forms, with **potential energy** depending on position (**PE = mgh**) and **kinetic energy** depending on motion (**KE = ½mv²**).
- The **Work-Energy Theorem** states that the work done on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy.
- **Mechanical energy** is the sum of potential and kinetic energies and remains constant in an isolated system according to the **Law of Conservation of Energy**.
- During **free fall**, potential energy decreases while kinetic energy increases, but the total mechanical energy remains constant.
- **Power** is the rate of doing work and is measured in **watts**, with formulas **P = W/t** and **P = Fv**.
- These concepts explain the operation of machines, vehicles, power plants, and countless everyday mechanical systems.
