# Introduction

**Environmental Chemistry** is the branch of chemistry that deals with the study of **chemical processes occurring in the environment**, the effects of pollutants on living organisms, and methods to prevent environmental pollution.

The environment consists of four major components:

- Atmosphere
- Hydrosphere
- Lithosphere
- Biosphere

Rapid industrialization, urbanization, and population growth have increased pollution, making environmental protection an important global concern.

---

# 1. Environment

## Definition

The **environment** is the sum total of all living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) surroundings that influence organisms.

# 2. Pollution

## Definition

**Pollution** is the undesirable change in the physical, chemical, or biological characteristics of the environment due to harmful substances called **pollutants**.

---

# Pollutants

## Definition

Substances that adversely affect the environment.

---

# Types of Pollutants

### Primary Pollutants

Released directly into the atmosphere.

Examples

- CO
- SO₂
- NO
- Dust

---

### Secondary Pollutants

Formed by chemical reactions in the atmosphere.

Examples

- Ozone (O₃)
- PAN
- Acid rain components

---

# Difference Between Primary and Secondary Pollutants

| Primary Pollutants | Secondary Pollutants |
| ------------------ | -------------------- |
| Emitted directly   | Formed in atmosphere |
| CO, SO₂            | O₃, PAN              |

---

# 4. Smog

## Definition

Smog is a mixture of **smoke and fog** containing harmful pollutants.

---

# Types of Smog

---

## Classical Smog

Occurs in cold and humid climate.

Contains

- SO₂
- Smoke

---

## Photochemical Smog

Occurs in warm climate.

Contains

- NOₓ
- Hydrocarbons
- Ozone
- PAN

---

# Difference Between Classical and Photochemical Smog

| Classical Smog  | Photochemical Smog |
| --------------- | ------------------ |
| Reducing nature | Oxidizing nature   |
| SO₂ + Smoke     | NOₓ + Hydrocarbons |
| Cold climate    | Sunny climate      |

### Question

What is acid rain?

### Solution

Rainwater having a pH below 5.6 due to dissolved sulfuric and nitric acids.

### Answer

**Rain with pH less than 5.6 caused by acidic oxides in the atmosphere.**

## Example 2

### Question

Name one greenhouse gas.

### Solution

Carbon dioxide traps infrared radiation.

### Answer

**Carbon dioxide (CO₂).**

---

## Example 3

### Question

What causes ozone depletion?

### Solution

Chlorofluorocarbons release chlorine radicals that destroy ozone.

### Answer

**Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).**

---

## Example 4

### Question

What is eutrophication?

### Solution

It is caused by excess nutrients in water leading to algal growth.

### Answer

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing the **greenhouse effect** with **ozone depletion**.
- Assuming **CO₂** is poisonous like **CO**.
- Mixing up **classical smog** and **photochemical smog**.
- Forgetting that **acid rain has a pH below 5.6**.
- Confusing **stratospheric ozone** (beneficial) with **tropospheric ozone** (harmful).
- Assuming all fertilizers are pollutants under normal usage.

# Chapter Summary

- **Environmental chemistry** focuses on understanding the chemical processes occurring in the environment and developing methods to reduce pollution.
- Major forms of pollution include **air, water, and soil pollution**, caused by industrialization, transportation, agriculture, and human activities.
- Environmental issues such as **acid rain, photochemical smog, greenhouse effect, global warming, ozone depletion, and eutrophication** significantly affect ecosystems and human health.
- Effective pollution control involves **waste management, cleaner technologies, renewable energy, water treatment, emission control, and sustainable development**.
- **Green chemistry** promotes environmentally friendly chemical processes by minimizing hazardous substances, conserving energy, and reducing waste generation.
- Understanding environmental chemistry is essential for protecting natural resources, maintaining ecological balance, and achieving sustainable development for future generations.
