# Introduction

Compounds are pure substances formed by the chemical combination of two or more elements in a fixed proportion by mass. Studying important compounds helps us understand their preparation, physical and chemical properties, uses, and safety precautions. In this chapter, we study **Hydrogen, Oxygen, Carbon Dioxide, Ammonia, Hydrochloric Acid, Nitric Acid, Sulphuric Acid, Sodium Hydroxide, and Calcium Hydroxide**.

---

# 1. Hydrogen (H₂)

## Introduction

Hydrogen is the **lightest and most abundant element** in the universe. It is a colorless, odorless, tasteless, and highly combustible gas.

---

## Laboratory Preparation

Hydrogen is prepared by reacting **zinc granules** with **dilute hydrochloric acid**.

### Chemical Equation

```
Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂↑
```

---

## Physical Properties

- Colourless gas
- Odourless
- Tasteless
- Lightest gas
- Slightly soluble in water

---

## Chemical Properties

### Combustion

```
2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
```

### Reducing Nature

Hydrogen reduces metal oxides to metals.

Example:

```
CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O
```

---

## Uses

- Manufacture of ammonia
- Hydrogen fuel
- Hydrogenation of vegetable oils
- Oxy-hydrogen flame for welding

---

# 2. Oxygen (O₂)

## Introduction

Oxygen supports combustion and respiration.

---

## Laboratory Preparation

Heating potassium chlorate in the presence of manganese dioxide.

```
2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂↑
```

(MnO₂ acts as a catalyst.)

---

## Physical Properties

- Colourless
- Odourless
- Slightly soluble in water

---

## Chemical Properties

### Supports Combustion

```
C + O₂ → CO₂
```

### Respiration

Oxygen is essential for respiration.

---

## Uses

- Medical oxygen
- Welding
- Steel industry
- Rocket oxidizer

---

# 3. Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)

## Laboratory Preparation

```
CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂↑
```

---

## Physical Properties

- Colourless
- Odourless
- Denser than air
- Does not support combustion

---

## Chemical Properties

### Lime Water Test

```
CO₂ + Ca(OH)₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O
```

Lime water turns **milky** due to calcium carbonate formation.

---

## Uses

- Fire extinguishers
- Carbonated drinks
- Refrigeration (dry ice)
- Photosynthesis

---

# 4. Ammonia (NH₃)

## Laboratory Preparation

Heating ammonium chloride with calcium hydroxide.

```
2NH₄Cl + Ca(OH)₂

→ CaCl₂ + 2NH₃ + 2H₂O
```

---

## Physical Properties

- Colourless
- Pungent smell
- Highly soluble in water
- Lighter than air

---

## Chemical Properties

### Burns in Oxygen

```
4NH₃ + 3O₂ → 2N₂ + 6H₂O
```

---

## Uses

- Fertilizer manufacture
- Refrigeration
- Nitric acid manufacture
- Cleaning agents

---

# 5. Hydrochloric Acid (HCl)

## Properties

- Colourless
- Strong acid
- Corrosive
- Turns blue litmus red

---

## Reactions

### With Zinc

```
Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂
```

### With Sodium Hydroxide

```
HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
```

---

## Uses

- Cleaning metals
- Laboratory reagent
- Preparation of chloride salts

---

# 6. Nitric Acid (HNO₃)

## Properties

- Strong acid
- Colourless
- Oxidizing agent
- Corrosive

---

## Reactions

### With Copper

```
Cu + 4HNO₃

→ Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO₂ + 2H₂O
```

---

## Uses

- Fertilizers
- Explosives
- Dyes
- Pharmaceuticals

---

# 7. Sulphuric Acid (H₂SO₄)

## Properties

- Dense liquid
- Strong acid
- Hygroscopic
- Dehydrating agent

---

## Reactions

### Neutralization

```
H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH

→ Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O
```

---

## Uses

- Car batteries
- Fertilizers
- Chemical industry
- Petroleum refining

---

# 8. Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH)

## Introduction

Also called **Caustic Soda**.

---

## Properties

- White solid
- Highly soluble in water
- Strong alkali
- Corrosive

---

## Uses

- Soap manufacture
- Paper industry
- Textile industry
- Drain cleaners

---

# 9. Calcium Hydroxide [Ca(OH)₂]

## Introduction

Also called **Slaked Lime**.

---

## Preparation

```
CaO + H₂O

→ Ca(OH)₂
```

---

## Uses

- Whitewashing
- Manufacture of bleaching powder
- Water treatment
- Testing carbon dioxide

---

# Difference Between Acids and Bases

| Acids                | Bases                |
| -------------------- | -------------------- |
| Sour taste           | Bitter taste         |
| Turn blue litmus red | Turn red litmus blue |
| Release H⁺ ions      | Release OH⁻ ions     |
| pH less than 7       | pH greater than 7    |


# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **quick lime (CaO)** with **slaked lime [Ca(OH)₂]**.
- Forgetting that **CO₂ turns lime water milky**.
- Assuming oxygen burns instead of supporting combustion.
- Mixing up the laboratory preparation methods of hydrogen and oxygen.
- Confusing the uses of nitric acid and sulphuric acid.
- Forgetting that **NaOH** is an alkali and highly corrosive.
- Using incorrect chemical formulae for common compounds.
- Writing unbalanced chemical equations.

# Chapter Summary

- A **compound** is a pure substance formed by the chemical combination of elements in a fixed ratio.
- **Hydrogen** is the lightest gas and is widely used as a fuel and reducing agent.
- **Oxygen** supports combustion and is essential for respiration.
- **Carbon dioxide** does not support combustion and turns **lime water milky**.
- **Ammonia** is a pungent gas used in fertilizer production and refrigeration.
- **Hydrochloric acid**, **nitric acid**, and **sulphuric acid** are important mineral acids with numerous industrial applications.
- **Sodium hydroxide (caustic soda)** is a strong alkali used in soap, paper, and textile industries.
- **Calcium hydroxide (slaked lime)** is used in whitewashing, water treatment, and testing for carbon dioxide.
- Understanding the preparation, properties, reactions, and uses of common compounds is essential for both laboratory work and industrial chemistry.
