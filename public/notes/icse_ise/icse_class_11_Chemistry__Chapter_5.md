# Introduction

**Thermodynamics** is the branch of chemistry that deals with the study of **energy changes** accompanying physical and chemical processes. It helps predict whether a reaction is feasible, how much heat is evolved or absorbed, and how energy is converted from one form to another.

The study of thermodynamics is based on the **First Law**, **Second Law**, **Entropy**, and **Gibbs Free Energy**.

---

# 1. Basic Terminology

## System

The part of the universe under study.

---

## Surroundings

Everything outside the system.

---

## Universe

```
Universe

=

System

+

Surroundings
```

---

# Types of Systems

## Open System

- Exchanges matter and energy.

Example

```
Boiling water in an open vessel
```

---

## Closed System

- Exchanges energy only.
- No exchange of matter.

Example

```
Gas inside a sealed cylinder
```

---

## Isolated System

- No exchange of matter or energy.

Example

```
Thermos flask (approximately)
```

---

# Difference Between Open, Closed and Isolated Systems

| Open     | Closed   | Isolated |
| -------- | -------- | -------- |
| Matter ✔ | Matter ✖ | Matter ✖ |
| Energy ✔ | Energy ✔ | Energy ✖ |

---

# Types of Properties

## Intensive Properties

Independent of amount.

Examples

- Temperature
- Pressure
- Density

---

## Extensive Properties

Depend on amount.

Examples

- Mass
- Volume
- Internal energy

---

# Difference Between Intensive and Extensive Properties

| Intensive               | Extensive           |
| ----------------------- | ------------------- |
| Independent of quantity | Depends on quantity |
| Temperature             | Mass                |
| Pressure                | Volume              |

---

# State Functions and Path Functions

## State Functions

Depend only on initial and final states.

Examples

- Internal energy (U)
- Enthalpy (H)
- Entropy (S)
- Gibbs free energy (G)

---

## Path Functions

Depend on the path followed.

Examples

- Heat (q)
- Work (w)

---

# Difference Between State and Path Functions

| State Function   | Path Function  |
| ---------------- | -------------- |
| Path independent | Path dependent |
| U, H, G, S       | q, w           |

---

# 2. Internal Energy (U)

## Definition

The total energy possessed by a system due to the motion and interactions of its particles.

---

# Change in Internal Energy

```
ΔU

=

U(final)

−

U(initial)
```

---

# Sign Convention

| Process             | Sign |
| ------------------- | ---- |
| Heat absorbed       | +q   |
| Heat released       | -q   |
| Work done on system | +w   |
| Work done by system | -w   |

---

# 3. First Law of Thermodynamics

## Statement

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It can only be transformed from one form to another.

---

# Mathematical Expression

```
ΔU

=

q + w
```

---

Where

- ΔU = Change in internal energy
- q = Heat absorbed
- w = Work done on the system

---

# Expansion Work

```
w

=

−PΔV
```

Where

- P = Pressure
- ΔV = Change in volume

---

# Special Cases

## Constant Volume

```
ΔV = 0

↓

w = 0

↓

ΔU = q
```

---

## Constant Pressure

```
Heat = ΔH
```

---

# 4. Enthalpy (H)

## Definition

Enthalpy is the total heat content of a system.

---

# Formula

```
H

=

U + PV
```

---

# Change in Enthalpy

```
ΔH

=

ΔU + PΔV
```

---

# Exothermic Reaction

```
ΔH < 0
```

Heat is released.

Example

```
CH₄ + 2O₂

↓

CO₂ + 2H₂O + Heat
```

---

# Endothermic Reaction

```
ΔH > 0
```

Heat is absorbed.

Example

```
CaCO₃

↓

CaO + CO₂
```

---

# Difference Between Exothermic and Endothermic Reactions

| Exothermic               | Endothermic               |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Heat released            | Heat absorbed             |
| ΔH negative              | ΔH positive               |
| Products lower in energy | Products higher in energy |

---

# 5. Thermochemical Equations

A balanced chemical equation showing the heat change during the reaction.

Example

```
H₂ + ½O₂

↓

H₂O

ΔH = -286 kJ mol⁻¹
```

---

# Types of Enthalpy Changes

| Type                       | Definition                      |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Enthalpy of Formation      | Formation of 1 mole of compound |
| Enthalpy of Combustion     | Complete combustion of 1 mole   |
| Enthalpy of Neutralization | Formation of 1 mole of water    |
| Enthalpy of Atomization    | Formation of gaseous atoms      |
| Bond Enthalpy              | Breaking 1 mole of bonds        |

---

# 6. Hess's Law

## Statement

The total enthalpy change of a reaction is independent of the pathway followed.

---

# Application

Used to calculate

- Heat of formation
- Heat of combustion
- Bond enthalpy

# 7. Bond Enthalpy

## Definition

The energy required to break one mole of a specific covalent bond in the gaseous state.

---

# Formula

```
ΔH

=

Σ(Bond Broken)

−

Σ(Bond Formed)
```

# 8. Second Law of Thermodynamics

## Statement

The entropy of the universe increases in every spontaneous process.

---

# Entropy (S)

## Definition

Entropy is the measure of randomness or disorder in a system.

---

# Entropy Change

```
ΔS

=

S(final)

−

S(initial)
```

---

# Factors Affecting Entropy

- Increase in temperature.
- Increase in volume.
- Change from solid → liquid → gas.
- Increase in number of particles.

---

# Order of Entropy

```
Gas

>

Liquid

>

Solid
```

---

# Difference Between Enthalpy and Entropy

| Enthalpy     | Entropy     |
| ------------ | ----------- |
| Heat content | Disorder    |
| H            | S           |
| kJ mol⁻¹     | J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ |

---

# 9. Gibbs Free Energy

## Definition

The energy available to perform useful work.

---

# Gibbs Equation

```
ΔG

=

ΔH − TΔS
```

Where

- ΔG = Gibbs free energy
- ΔH = Enthalpy change
- T = Temperature (Kelvin)
- ΔS = Entropy change

---

# Conditions for Spontaneity

| ΔG       | Nature          |
| -------- | --------------- |
| Negative | Spontaneous     |
| Positive | Non-spontaneous |
| Zero     | Equilibrium     |

---

# Relation Between ΔH, ΔS and ΔG

| ΔH  | ΔS  | Spontaneity        |
| --- | --- | ------------------ |
| -   | +   | Always spontaneous |
| +   | -   | Never spontaneous  |
| -   | -   | Low temperature    |
| +   | +   | High temperature   |

---

# Difference Between Spontaneous and Non-Spontaneous Processes

| Spontaneous      | Non-Spontaneous          |
| ---------------- | ------------------------ |
| Occurs naturally | Requires external energy |
| ΔG < 0           | ΔG > 0                   |

---

# 10. Calorimetry

## Definition

Measurement of heat changes during physical or chemical processes.

---

# Heat Equation

```
q

=

m × c × ΔT
```

Where

- m = Mass
- c = Specific heat capacity
- ΔT = Temperature change

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

State the First Law of Thermodynamics.

### Solution

Energy is conserved and can only change from one form to another.

### Answer

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed.

---

## Example 2

### Question

Calculate the heat absorbed by 200 g of water heated from 25°C to 35°C. (Specific heat = 4.2 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹)

### Solution

```
q = mcΔT

= 200 × 4.2 × 10

= 8400 J
```

### Answer

**8400 J**

---

## Example 3

### Question

A reaction has ΔH = -120 kJ and ΔS = +80 J K⁻¹. Is the reaction spontaneous?

### Solution

Since ΔH is negative and ΔS is positive, ΔG will always be negative.

### Answer

**Yes, the reaction is spontaneous.**

---

## Example 4

### Question

Which process has higher entropy: ice or steam?

### Solution

Gases have greater molecular randomness than solids.

### Answer

**Steam**

---

## Example 5

### Question

What is the sign of ΔH for an endothermic reaction?

### Solution

Heat is absorbed by the system.

### Answer

**Positive (ΔH > 0)**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **internal energy (U)** with **enthalpy (H)**.
- Using Celsius instead of Kelvin in Gibbs free energy calculations.
- Forgetting the negative sign in expansion work (**w = −PΔV**).
- Mixing up exothermic and endothermic reactions.
- Confusing state functions with path functions.
- Assuming every exothermic reaction is spontaneous without considering entropy.

# Chapter Summary

- **Chemical thermodynamics** studies the energy changes accompanying physical and chemical processes.
- A **system** exchanges matter and/or energy with its surroundings depending on whether it is **open, closed, or isolated**.
- The **First Law of Thermodynamics** expresses the conservation of energy through the equation **ΔU = q + w**, while **enthalpy (H)** represents the heat content of a system.
- **Hess's Law** states that the total enthalpy change is independent of the reaction pathway, enabling calculation of unknown enthalpy changes.
- The **Second Law of Thermodynamics** introduces **entropy (S)** as a measure of disorder, explaining the direction of spontaneous processes.
- **Gibbs Free Energy (ΔG = ΔH − TΔS)** combines enthalpy and entropy to predict whether a reaction is **spontaneous**, making it one of the most important criteria in chemical thermodynamics.
