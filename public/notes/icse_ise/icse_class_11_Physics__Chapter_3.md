# Introduction

The **Laws of Motion**, formulated by **Sir Isaac Newton**, explain the relationship between **force** and **motion**. These laws form the foundation of **classical mechanics** and are used to analyze the motion of objects under the action of forces.

This chapter introduces the concepts of **force, inertia, momentum, impulse, friction, circular motion, banking of roads, pulley systems, and equilibrium of forces**.

---

# 1. Force

## Definition

A **force** is a push or pull that changes or tends to change the state of rest or motion of a body.

---

## SI Unit

```
Newton (N)
```

---

## Formula

```
F = ma
```

---

## Dimensional Formula

```
[MLT⁻²]
```

---

# Types of Forces

### Contact Forces

- Friction
- Normal Reaction
- Tension
- Muscular Force

---

### Non-Contact Forces

- Gravitational Force
- Electrostatic Force
- Magnetic Force

---

# Difference Between Contact and Non-Contact Forces

| Contact Force             | Non-Contact Force       |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Requires physical contact | Acts without contact    |
| Friction, Tension         | Gravity, Magnetic force |

---

# 2. Inertia

## Definition

**Inertia** is the property of a body by which it resists any change in its state of rest or uniform motion.

---

# Types of Inertia

### Inertia of Rest

A body at rest tends to remain at rest.

---

### Inertia of Motion

A moving body continues to move with constant velocity.

---

### Inertia of Direction

A body resists any change in its direction of motion.

---

# Mass and Inertia

```
Greater Mass

↓

Greater Inertia
```

---

# 3. Newton's First Law of Motion

## Statement

Every body continues in its state of rest or uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force.

---

# Significance

- Defines force.
- Explains inertia.
- Introduces equilibrium.

---

# Examples

- Passengers move forward when a bus stops suddenly.
- Dust leaves a carpet when beaten.
- Fruits fall when branches are shaken.

---

# 4. Momentum

## Definition

Momentum is the product of the mass and velocity of a body.

---

## Formula

```
p = mv
```

---

## SI Unit

```
kg m s⁻¹
```

---

## Dimensional Formula

```
[MLT⁻¹]
```

---

# Difference Between Momentum and Inertia

| Momentum                     | Inertia              |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Depends on mass and velocity | Depends only on mass |
| Vector quantity              | Property of matter   |

---

# 5. Newton's Second Law of Motion

## Statement

The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied force and occurs in the direction of the force.

---

# Mathematical Form

```
F = dp/dt
```

For constant mass,

```
F = ma
```

---

# Applications

- Rocket launch.
- Vehicle acceleration.
- Sports.
- Machinery.

---

# 6. Impulse

## Definition

Impulse is the product of force and the time for which it acts.

---

## Formula

```
Impulse = FΔt
```

---

## Relation

```
Impulse = Change in Momentum
```

---

## SI Unit

```
Newton-second (N s)
```

---

# Impulse-Momentum Theorem

```
FΔt = Δp
```

---

# Examples

- Airbags in cars.
- Catching a ball by moving hands backward.
- Helmets reduce injuries.

---

# Difference Between Force and Impulse

| Force          | Impulse        |
| -------------- | -------------- |
| Acts instantly | Acts over time |
| Unit: N        | Unit: N s      |

---

# 7. Newton's Third Law of Motion

## Statement

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

---

# Characteristics

- Equal magnitude.
- Opposite direction.
- Act on different bodies.
- Simultaneous.

---

# Examples

- Gun recoil.
- Rocket propulsion.
- Walking.
- Swimming.

---

# Difference Between Action and Reaction

| Action             | Reaction             |
| ------------------ | -------------------- |
| Acts on one body   | Acts on another body |
| Equal magnitude    | Equal magnitude      |
| Opposite direction | Opposite direction   |

---

# 8. Conservation of Linear Momentum

## Principle

If no external force acts on a system, the total linear momentum remains constant.

---

# Mathematical Form

```
Initial Momentum

=

Final Momentum
```

---

For two bodies,

```
m₁u₁ + m₂u₂

=

m₁v₁ + m₂v₂
```

---

# Applications

- Explosion.
- Collision.
- Rocket motion.
- Recoil of gun.

---

# 9. Collision

---

## Elastic Collision

- Momentum conserved.
- Kinetic energy conserved.

---

## Inelastic Collision

- Momentum conserved.
- Kinetic energy not conserved.

---

# Coefficient of Restitution

```
e

=

Relative Velocity of Separation

──────────────────────────

Relative Velocity of Approach
```

---

# Difference Between Elastic and Inelastic Collision

| Elastic                  | Inelastic                |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Momentum conserved       | Momentum conserved       |
| Kinetic energy conserved | Kinetic energy decreases |
| e = 1                    | 0 < e < 1                |

---

# 10. Friction

## Definition

Friction is the force that opposes the relative motion between two surfaces in contact.

---

# Types of Friction

- Static friction
- Limiting friction
- Kinetic friction
- Rolling friction

---

# Static Friction

Acts before motion begins.

---

# Limiting Friction

Maximum static friction.

```
Fs(max)

=

μsN
```

---

# Kinetic Friction

Acts after motion starts.

```
Fk

=

μkN
```

---

# Rolling Friction

Smallest type of friction.

---

# Laws of Friction

- Friction opposes motion.
- Proportional to normal reaction.
- Independent of contact area.
- Depends on surface nature.

---

# Coefficient of Friction

```
μ

=

F/N
```

---

# Angle of Friction

```
tanθ = μ
```

---

# Angle of Repose

The angle at which a body just begins to slide.

```
θ = tan⁻¹μ
```

---

# Difference Between Static and Kinetic Friction

| Static Friction | Kinetic Friction |
| --------------- | ---------------- |
| Before motion   | During motion    |
| Greater         | Smaller          |

---

# Advantages of Friction

- Walking.
- Braking.
- Writing.
- Driving.

---

# Disadvantages

- Wear and tear.
- Heat generation.
- Energy loss.

---

# Methods to Reduce Friction

- Lubrication.
- Ball bearings.
- Polishing.
- Streamlining.

---

# 11. Banking of Roads

## Definition

Roads are inclined on curves to avoid skidding.

---

# Banking Angle

```
tanθ

=

v²/rg
```

Where

- v = Speed
- r = Radius
- g = Acceleration due to gravity

---

# Advantages

- Safe turning.
- Less dependence on friction.
- Prevents overturning.

# 13. Equilibrium of Concurrent Forces

## Definition

When several forces acting at one point balance each other.

---

# Conditions of Equilibrium

```
ΣFx = 0

ΣFy = 0
```

---

# Lami's Theorem

If three concurrent forces keep a body in equilibrium,

```
F₁/sinα

=

F₂/sinβ

=

F₃/sinγ
```

---

# Applications

- Suspended bodies.
- Crane systems.
- Rope problems.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A force of 20 N acts on a 5 kg body. Find the acceleration.

### Solution

```
F = ma

20 = 5a

a = 4 m s⁻²
```

### Answer

**4 m s⁻²**

---

## Example 2

### Question

Find the momentum of a 3 kg body moving with a velocity of 5 m s⁻¹.

### Solution

```
p = mv

= 3 × 5

= 15 kg m s⁻¹
```

### Answer

**15 kg m s⁻¹**

---

## Example 3

### Question

A force of 10 N acts for 2 s. Find the impulse.

### Solution

```
Impulse = FΔt

= 10 × 2

= 20 N s
```

### Answer

**20 N s**

---

## Example 4

### Question

A 10 kg body moves in a circle of radius 5 m with a speed of 10 m s⁻¹. Find the centripetal force.

### Solution

```
Fc = mv²/r

= 10 × 100/5

= 200 N
```

### Answer

**200 N**

---

## Example 5

### Question

A block experiences a normal reaction of 100 N and the coefficient of friction is 0.3. Find the frictional force.

### Solution

```
F = μN

= 0.3 × 100

= 30 N
```

### Answer

**30 N**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **mass** with **weight**.
- Assuming action and reaction act on the same body.
- Forgetting that momentum is a **vector** quantity.
- Using static friction instead of kinetic friction after motion starts.
- Ignoring free body diagrams in force analysis.
- Applying momentum conservation when external forces are present.

# Chapter Summary

- **Newton's three laws of motion** explain the behavior of bodies under the influence of forces and form the basis of classical mechanics.
- **Momentum** and **impulse** are closely related through the impulse-momentum theorem, while the **law of conservation of linear momentum** governs collisions and explosions.
- **Friction** opposes relative motion and plays a crucial role in daily life, engineering applications, and vehicle dynamics.
- **Banking of roads** and **uniform circular motion** involve centripetal force, ensuring safe motion along curved paths.
- The analysis of **equilibrium**, **pulley systems**, and **free body diagrams** enables accurate solution of force-related problems.
- A thorough understanding of these principles is essential for advanced topics such as **work and energy, rotational dynamics, gravitation, and engineering mechanics**.
