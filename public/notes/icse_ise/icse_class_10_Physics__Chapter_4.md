# Introduction

**Refraction** is the phenomenon in which light changes its direction when it passes from one transparent medium to another due to a change in its speed. Refraction explains many natural phenomena such as the apparent bending of a pencil in water, the apparent depth of ponds, mirages, and the working of optical instruments. This chapter covers **Snell's Law, Refraction through Glass Slabs and Prisms, Refractive Index, Apparent Depth, Critical Angle, and Total Internal Reflection (TIR).**

---

# 1. Refraction of Light

## Definition

**Refraction** is the change in the direction of light when it passes obliquely from one transparent medium to another because its speed changes.

---

## Causes of Refraction

- Change in the speed of light.
- Change in the optical density of the medium.

---

## Rules of Refraction

### (i) From Rarer to Denser Medium

- Speed decreases.
- Light bends **towards the normal**.

```
Air → Glass

      \
       \
        \
---------|---------
         |\
         | \
         |  \
      Normal
```

---

### (ii) From Denser to Rarer Medium

- Speed increases.
- Light bends **away from the normal**.

```
Glass → Air

         /
        /
       /
---------|---------
        |
        |
     Normal
```

---

# 2. Refractive Index

## Definition

The **Refractive Index (μ)** of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to its speed in that medium.

---

## Formula

```
      c
μ = ----
      v
```

Where,

- μ = Refractive Index
- c = Speed of light in vacuum
- v = Speed of light in the medium

---

## SI Unit

```
No Unit (Dimensionless)
```

---

# 3. Snell's Law

## Statement

The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant for a given pair of media.

---

## Formula

```
          sin i
μ = ----------------
          sin r
```

or

```
μ = c/v
```

---

## Important Points

- Valid for transparent media.
- Explains bending of light.
- Used to calculate refractive index.

---

# 4. Refraction Through a Glass Slab

## Characteristics

- Light bends towards the normal on entering.
- Light bends away from the normal on leaving.
- Emergent ray is **parallel** to the incident ray.
- A **lateral displacement** is produced.

---

## Ray Diagram

```
Incident Ray
      \
       \
        \
─────────│──────────
         │\
         │ \
         │  \
         │   \
─────────│────\──────
             \
              \
         Emergent Ray
 (Parallel to Incident Ray)
```

---

# 5. Refraction Through a Prism

## Prism

A prism is a transparent refracting medium bounded by two plane inclined surfaces.

---

## Angle of Prism

```
A
```

---

## Angle of Deviation

The angle between the direction of the incident ray and the emergent ray.

---

## Formula

```
δ = i₁ + i₂ − A
```

Where,

- δ = Angle of deviation
- i₁ = Angle of incidence
- i₂ = Angle of emergence
- A = Angle of prism

---

## Observation

Light always bends **towards the base** of the prism.

---

# 6. Real Depth and Apparent Depth

## Definition

Due to refraction, an object inside water appears **closer to the surface** than it actually is.

---

## Formula

```
             Real Depth
μ = ------------------------------
          Apparent Depth
```

---

## Observation

```
Real Depth

>

Apparent Depth
```

---

## Applications

- Swimming pools appear shallower.
- Fish appear nearer to the surface.

---

# 7. Critical Angle

## Definition

The **Critical Angle (C)** is the angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction in the rarer medium is **90°**.

---

## Formula

```
        1
sin C = ----
         μ
```

Where,

- C = Critical Angle
- μ = Refractive Index

---

## Conditions

- Light travels from denser to rarer medium.
- Angle of refraction becomes 90°.

---

# 8. Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

## Definition

**Total Internal Reflection** is the complete reflection of light back into the denser medium when the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle.

---

## Conditions for TIR

1. Light must travel from a denser medium to a rarer medium.
2. Angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle.

---

## Ray Diagram

```
Denser Medium

        \
         \
          \
-----------\------------
            \
             \
              X

(No refracted ray)

Light reflects back.
```

---

## Applications

- Optical fibres.
- Endoscopes.
- Right-angled prisms.
- Binoculars.
- Periscopes.
- Diamond sparkle.

---

# Difference Between Reflection and Refraction

| Reflection                               | Refraction              |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Light bounces back                       | Light changes direction |
| Same medium                              | Different media         |
| Speed remains constant                   | Speed changes           |
| Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection | Obeys Snell's Law       |

---

# Difference Between Refraction and Total Internal Reflection

| Refraction                          | Total Internal Reflection        |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Part of light enters second medium  | Entire light reflects back       |
| Occurs in any two transparent media | Only from denser to rarer medium |
| Requires no critical angle          | Requires angle > critical angle  |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A ray passes from air into glass. How does it bend?

### Solution

Glass is optically denser than air.

### Answer

```
Towards the normal.
```

---

## Example 2

### Question

A medium has a refractive index of **1.5**. Calculate the speed of light in the medium.

### Solution

Given,

```
μ = 1.5

c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
```

Using,

```
v = c/μ

= (3 × 10⁸)/1.5

= 2 × 10⁸ m/s
```

### Answer

```
2 × 10⁸ m/s
```

---

## Example 3

### Question

A pond has a real depth of **3 m** and a refractive index of **1.5**. Find its apparent depth.

### Solution

```
Apparent Depth = Real Depth/μ

= 3/1.5

= 2 m
```

### Answer

```
2 m
```

---

## Example 4

### Question

Define the critical angle.

### Solution

The angle is measured in the denser medium.

### Answer

The angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction in the rarer medium is **90°**.

---

## Example 5

### Question

State two conditions necessary for Total Internal Reflection.

### Solution

The phenomenon occurs only under specific conditions.

### Answer

1. Light must travel from a denser to a rarer medium.
2. The angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **reflection** with **refraction**.
- Forgetting that light bends **towards the normal** when entering a denser medium.
- Using the wrong formula for **refractive index**.
- Assuming apparent depth is greater than real depth.
- Confusing **critical angle** with **angle of incidence**.
- Forgetting that **Total Internal Reflection occurs only from denser to rarer medium**.
- Drawing incorrect ray diagrams for prisms and glass slabs.

# Quick Revision

- Refraction → Change in direction due to change in speed.
- Snell's Law → **μ = sin i / sin r**.
- Refractive Index → **μ = c/v**.
- Glass slab → Emergent ray parallel to incident ray.
- Prism → Light deviates towards the base.
- Apparent Depth → **Real Depth / Apparent Depth = μ**.
- Critical Angle → **sin C = 1/μ**.
- TIR → Complete internal reflection.
- Conditions → Denser → Rarer and **i > C**.

# Chapter Summary

- **Refraction** is the bending of light when it travels between transparent media of different optical densities due to a change in its speed.
- The amount of bending depends on the **refractive index**, which is related to the speed of light by **μ = c/v**, and follows **Snell's Law (μ = sin i / sin r)**.
- In a **glass slab**, the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray but undergoes **lateral displacement**, while a **prism** causes deviation of light toward its base.
- Refraction also explains the phenomenon of **apparent depth**, making objects under water appear closer to the surface than they actually are.
- When light travels from a denser to a rarer medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the **critical angle**, **Total Internal Reflection (TIR)** occurs.
- TIR has important applications in **optical fibres, medical endoscopes, binoculars, periscopes, prisms, and communication systems**, making refraction one of the most important concepts in optics.
