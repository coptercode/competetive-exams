# Introduction

Sound is a form of energy produced by vibrating objects. It travels as a mechanical wave through a material medium such as solids, liquids, or gases. Sound cannot travel through a vacuum. This chapter explains the production and propagation of sound, wave characteristics, the wave equation, frequency ranges, and the applications of ultrasound.

---

# 1. Acoustic Production (Production of Sound)

## Sound

Sound is a form of energy that produces the sensation of hearing.

---

## Source of Sound

Sound is produced by the **vibration of objects**.

Examples:

- Tuning fork
- Guitar string
- Drum membrane
- Loudspeaker diaphragm
- Human vocal cords

---

## Vibrations

A vibration is the rapid to-and-fro motion of an object about its mean position.

When an object vibrates:

- It compresses the particles of the medium.
- It creates regions of **compressions** and **rarefactions**.
- These disturbances travel through the medium as sound waves.

---

## Medium Required for Sound

Sound requires a **material medium** to travel.

It can travel through:

- Solids
- Liquids
- Gases

It **cannot travel through a vacuum** because there are no particles to transmit vibrations.

---

## Speed of Sound in Different Media

| Medium | Approximate Speed |
| ------ | ----------------: |
| Air    |           343 m/s |
| Water  |          1480 m/s |
| Steel  |          5960 m/s |

### Observation

Sound travels:

- Fastest in solids.
- Slower in liquids.
- Slowest in gases.

---

# 2. Wave Attributes

A **wave** is a disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another without transporting matter.

---

## Types of Waves

### Longitudinal Waves

In longitudinal waves, particles vibrate **parallel** to the direction of wave propagation.

Sound waves in air are longitudinal waves.

---

### Transverse Waves

In transverse waves, particles vibrate **perpendicular** to the direction of wave propagation.

Examples:

- Water surface waves (approximate)
- Light waves
- Electromagnetic waves

---

## Compressions

Regions where particles are close together.

Characteristics:

- High pressure
- High density

---

## Rarefactions

Regions where particles are far apart.

Characteristics:

- Low pressure
- Low density

---

## Characteristics of Sound Waves

### Amplitude (A)

Maximum displacement of particles from their mean position.

Unit:

```
metre (m)
```

Higher amplitude produces **louder sound**.

---

### Wavelength (λ)

Distance between two successive compressions or two successive rarefactions.

Unit:

```
metre (m)
```

---

### Frequency (f)

Number of vibrations produced in one second.

Formula

```
Frequency = Number of Vibrations / Time
```

SI Unit

```
Hertz (Hz)
```

---

### Time Period (T)

Time taken for one complete vibration.

Formula

```
T = 1/f
```

Unit

```
second (s)
```

---

### Pitch

Pitch depends on the frequency of sound.

- Higher frequency → Higher pitch
- Lower frequency → Lower pitch

---

### Loudness

Loudness depends on the amplitude of vibration.

- Greater amplitude → Louder sound
- Smaller amplitude → Softer sound

---

### Quality (Timbre)

Quality distinguishes sounds produced by different sources having the same pitch and loudness.

Example:

- Piano
- Violin
- Flute

---

# 3. The Wave Equation

The speed of sound depends on its frequency and wavelength.

### Wave Equation

```
v = fλ
```

where:

- **v** = Speed of sound
- **f** = Frequency
- **λ** = Wavelength

---

## Rearranged Forms

```
f = v/λ
```

```
λ = v/f
```

---

## SI Units

| Quantity   | Unit |
| ---------- | ---- |
| Speed      | m/s  |
| Frequency  | Hz   |
| Wavelength | m    |

---

# 4. Acoustic Frequency Limits

The human ear can hear only a limited range of frequencies.

---

## Infrasonic Sound

Frequency less than:

```
20 Hz
```

Examples:

- Earthquakes
- Elephants
- Whales

Applications:

- Earthquake monitoring
- Animal communication studies

---

## Audible Sound

Frequency range:

```
20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz)
```

This is the normal hearing range of healthy human beings.

---

## Ultrasonic Sound

Frequency greater than:

```
20,000 Hz
```

Examples:

- Bats
- Dolphins
- Dogs (partially)

---

# 5. Ultrasound Applications

Ultrasound refers to sound waves having frequencies above **20 kHz**.

---

## Medical Applications

### Ultrasonography

Used to examine:

- Foetus during pregnancy
- Kidneys
- Liver
- Heart
- Internal organs

---

### Breaking Kidney Stones

High-intensity ultrasound is used to break kidney stones into smaller pieces.

---

## Industrial Applications

Used for:

- Detecting cracks in metal blocks.
- Testing machine parts.
- Cleaning delicate electronic components.
- Cleaning jewellery.

---

## SONAR (Sound Navigation and Ranging)

SONAR uses ultrasonic waves to detect underwater objects.

Applications:

- Measuring sea depth.
- Detecting submarines.
- Locating underwater obstacles.
- Finding shoals of fish.

---

## Echo

Echo is the repetition of sound caused by reflection from a distant surface.

### Conditions for Hearing an Echo

- Minimum distance from obstacle:

```
17 metres
```

(approximately)

- Time gap between original sound and reflected sound:

```
0.1 second
```

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A sound wave has a frequency of **500 Hz** and a wavelength of **0.68 m**.

Find its speed.

### Solution

Using

```
v = fλ
```

```
v = 500 × 0.68

= 340 m/s
```

### Answer

```
340 m/s
```

---

## Example 2

### Question

A wave travels at **330 m/s** with a frequency of **660 Hz**.

Find its wavelength.

### Solution

```
λ = v/f

= 330/660

= 0.5 m
```

### Answer

```
0.5 m
```

---

## Example 3

### Question

Find the time period of a sound wave having a frequency of **200 Hz**.

### Solution

```
T = 1/f

= 1/200

= 0.005 s
```

### Answer

```
0.005 second
```

---

# Common Mistakes

- Assuming sound can travel through a vacuum.
- Confusing frequency with pitch.
- Confusing amplitude with loudness.
- Using incorrect units in the wave equation.
- Mixing longitudinal and transverse waves.
- Forgetting the audible frequency range.
- Using incorrect formulas for wavelength and frequency.
- Assuming ultrasound is harmful in all situations.

# Chapter Summary

- **Sound** is produced by vibrating objects and requires a material medium for propagation.
- Sound travels as a **longitudinal wave** consisting of compressions and rarefactions.
- The **speed of sound** depends on the medium and is greatest in solids, lower in liquids, and least in gases.
- **Amplitude** determines the loudness of sound, while **frequency** determines its pitch.
- The **wave equation (v = fλ)** relates speed, frequency, and wavelength.
- Humans can hear sounds in the frequency range of **20 Hz to 20 kHz**.
- Sounds below **20 Hz** are called **infrasonic**, while those above **20 kHz** are called **ultrasonic**.
- **Ultrasound** has important applications in medicine, industry, and SONAR technology.
- **Echoes** are produced by the reflection of sound and are useful in measuring distances and detecting objects.
