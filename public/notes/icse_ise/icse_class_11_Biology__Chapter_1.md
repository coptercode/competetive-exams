# Introduction

Biology is the branch of science that deals with the study of **living organisms** and their interactions with one another and the environment. The Earth is home to millions of different organisms that vary in size, shape, habitat, structure, and function.

To study this enormous diversity systematically, scientists classify organisms into groups based on their similarities, differences, and evolutionary relationships. This chapter introduces the **characteristics of living organisms**, **taxonomy**, **systematics**, **binomial nomenclature**, **taxonomic hierarchy**, and **taxonomical aids**.

---

# 1. What is Life?

## Definition

**Life** is the characteristic that distinguishes living organisms from non-living matter by the presence of biological processes such as metabolism, growth, reproduction, and response to stimuli.

---

# 2. Characteristics of Living Organisms

## A. Growth

Growth is a permanent increase in the size, volume, or mass of an organism.

### In Plants

- Growth occurs throughout life.
- Due to **meristematic tissues**.

### In Animals

- Growth occurs only up to a certain age.

---

## B. Reproduction

Reproduction is the process by which organisms produce offspring of their own kind.

### Types

#### Asexual Reproduction

One parent produces offspring.

Examples

- Amoeba → Binary fission
- Hydra → Budding
- Yeast → Budding

---

#### Sexual Reproduction

Two parents produce genetically different offspring.

Examples

- Humans
- Flowering plants
- Birds

---

## C. Metabolism

### Definition

Metabolism is the sum total of all biochemical reactions occurring inside living cells.

It consists of

```
Metabolism
      │
 ┌────┴────┐
 ▼         ▼
Anabolism  Catabolism
```

---

### Anabolism

- Building reactions
- Energy is consumed

Examples

- Photosynthesis
- Protein synthesis

---

### Catabolism

- Breakdown reactions
- Energy is released

Examples

- Respiration
- Digestion

---

### Importance

Metabolism is considered the **most important defining characteristic of life**.

---

## D. Cellular Organisation

All living organisms are made up of one or more cells.

Examples

| Organism   | Number of Cells |
| ---------- | --------------- |
| Amoeba     | Unicellular     |
| Bacteria   | Unicellular     |
| Human      | Multicellular   |
| Mango Tree | Multicellular   |

---

## E. Consciousness (Response to Stimuli)

Living organisms respond to internal and external stimuli.

Examples

- Touch
- Light
- Temperature
- Chemicals
- Sound

Example

```
Touch Me Not Plant
(Mimosa pudica)

↓

Leaves fold on touching.
```

---

# 3. Defining and Non-Defining Characteristics

## Defining Characteristics

These are characteristics present in all living organisms.

- Cellular organization
- Metabolism
- Consciousness

---

## Non-Defining Characteristics

These characteristics are generally present but are not universal.

- Growth
- Reproduction
- Movement

Examples

- Mule cannot reproduce.
- Sterile worker bees cannot reproduce.
- Seeds remain dormant without visible growth.

---

# Difference Between Living and Non-Living Things

| Living Organisms      | Non-Living Objects             |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Made of cells         | Not made of cells              |
| Show metabolism       | No metabolism                  |
| Respond to stimuli    | No response                    |
| Grow biologically     | Grow by external addition only |
| Reproduce (generally) | Cannot reproduce               |
| Have life span        | No life processes              |

# 4. Biodiversity

## Definition

Biodiversity refers to the variety and variability of living organisms found on Earth.

---

## Types of Biodiversity

### Genetic Diversity

Variation within the same species.

Example

Different varieties of rice.

---

### Species Diversity

Different species living in an ecosystem.

Example

Forest ecosystem.

---

### Ecosystem Diversity

Different ecosystems within a region.

Examples

- Forest
- Desert
- Grassland
- Ocean

# 9. Nomenclature

## Definition

Nomenclature is the process of assigning scientific names to organisms.

---

## Scientific Name

Each organism has two names

```
Genus

+

Species
```

called **Binomial Nomenclature**.

---

# 10. Binomial Nomenclature

Developed by

**Carolus Linnaeus**

---

## Rules

1. Two words are used.
2. First word is the **Genus**.
3. Second word is the **Species**.
4. Genus begins with a capital letter.
5. Species begins with a small letter.
6. Printed in _italics_.
7. If handwritten, both words are underlined separately.
8. Author's name is written after the scientific name.

---

## Examples

| Common Name | Scientific Name    |
| ----------- | ------------------ |
| Human       | _Homo sapiens_     |
| Mango       | _Mangifera indica_ |
| Tiger       | _Panthera tigris_  |
| Lion        | _Panthera leo_     |
| Rice        | _Oryza sativa_     |

---

# 11. Taxonomic Hierarchy

The taxonomic hierarchy arranges organisms from higher to lower categories.

```
Kingdom
   │
Phylum (Division in Plants)
   │
Class
   │
Order
   │
Family
   │
Genus
   │
Species
```

---

## Characteristics

### Kingdom

Largest taxonomic category.

Example

Animalia

---

### Phylum

Group of related classes.

Example

Chordata

---

### Class

Group of related orders.

Example

Mammalia

---

### Order

Group of related families.

Example

Primates

---

### Family

Group of related genera.

Example

Hominidae

---

### Genus

Group of closely related species.

Example

_Homo_

---

### Species

Smallest taxonomic unit.

Members can interbreed and produce fertile offspring.

Example

_Homo sapiens_

---

# Example of Taxonomic Hierarchy

| Category | Human          |
| -------- | -------------- |
| Kingdom  | Animalia       |
| Phylum   | Chordata       |
| Class    | Mammalia       |
| Order    | Primates       |
| Family   | Hominidae      |
| Genus    | _Homo_         |
| Species  | _Homo sapiens_ |

# 12. Taxonomical Aids

Taxonomical aids help in identification, classification, and preservation of organisms.

---

## Herbarium

A herbarium is a collection of dried and preserved plant specimens mounted on sheets.

### Uses

- Plant identification
- Research
- Teaching

---

## Botanical Garden

A garden where living plants are grown for study and conservation.

Examples

- Indian Botanical Garden, Howrah
- Lalbagh Botanical Garden, Bengaluru

---

## Museum

A museum stores preserved plant and animal specimens.

Methods

- Preserved in formalin
- Skeletons
- Stuffed animals
- Dry specimens

---

## Zoological Park

A place where wild animals are maintained in protected environments.

Purpose

- Conservation
- Public education
- Research

---

## Key

A key is a taxonomic tool used for identification through contrasting characteristics.

Example

```
Leaves Parallel

↓

Monocot

Leaves Reticulate

↓

Dicot
```

---

# Difference Between Taxonomy and Systematics

| Taxonomy                                  | Systematics                                    |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Identification, naming and classification | Includes taxonomy and evolution                |
| Based mainly on similarities              | Based on similarities and evolutionary history |
| Narrow field                              | Broader field                                  |

---

# Difference Between Herbarium and Botanical Garden

| Herbarium                  | Botanical Garden                   |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Dried plants               | Living plants                      |
| Indoor storage             | Outdoor cultivation                |
| Used for preserved records | Used for conservation and research |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **taxonomy** with **systematics**.
- Writing scientific names without following capitalization and italicization rules.
- Interchanging **genus** and **species**.
- Assuming reproduction is present in every living organism.
- Confusing botanical gardens with herbaria.
- Forgetting the correct order of taxonomic hierarchy.

---

# Chapter Summary

- The **Living World** introduces the fundamental characteristics that distinguish living organisms from non-living matter, emphasizing **metabolism**, **cellular organization**, and **response to stimuli** as defining features.
- **Taxonomy** provides a systematic method for identifying, naming, and classifying organisms, while **systematics** extends this by incorporating evolutionary relationships and phylogeny.
- The **binomial nomenclature** system developed by **Carolus Linnaeus** ensures that every organism has a unique and universally accepted scientific name.
- Organisms are arranged in a **taxonomic hierarchy** from **Kingdom** down to **Species**, enabling easy identification and comparison.
- **Taxonomical aids** such as **herbaria, botanical gardens, museums, zoological parks, and identification keys** are essential for the study, preservation, and classification of biodiversity.
- Understanding the living world forms the foundation for all subsequent topics in biology, including classification, evolution, ecology, genetics, and biodiversity conservation.
