# Introduction

Many bodies in nature undergo **rotational motion**, where they rotate about a fixed axis while translating or remaining stationary. Examples include **wheels, fans, turbines, planets, and spinning tops**.

This chapter introduces the concepts of **centre of mass, torque, angular momentum, moment of inertia, rolling motion, and rotational equilibrium**, which are essential for understanding rotational dynamics.

---

# 1. System of Particles

## Definition

A **system of particles** is a collection of two or more particles considered together for the analysis of motion.

---

# Types

- Two-particle system
- Multi-particle system
- Rigid body

---

# Rigid Body

A rigid body is an object in which the distance between any two particles remains constant.

---

# Difference Between Particle and Rigid Body

| Particle                  | Rigid Body               |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Size neglected            | Finite size considered   |
| Only translational motion | Translation and rotation |

---

# 2. Centre of Mass (CM)

## Definition

The **centre of mass** is the point at which the entire mass of a system may be assumed to be concentrated.

---

# Formula for Two Particles

```
xCM

=

(m₁x₁ + m₂x₂)

────────────────

m₁ + m₂
```

---

# Formula in Three Dimensions

```
xCM = Σmixi / Σmi

yCM = Σmiyi / Σmi

zCM = Σmizi / Σmi
```

---

# Centre of Mass of Uniform Bodies

| Body     | Position of CM |
| -------- | -------------- |
| Rod      | Midpoint       |
| Ring     | Centre         |
| Disc     | Centre         |
| Sphere   | Centre         |
| Cylinder | Centre         |

---

# Properties

- Depends on mass distribution.
- May lie inside or outside the body.
- Moves as though the entire mass is concentrated at that point.

---

# Difference Between Centre of Mass and Centre of Gravity

| Centre of Mass                       | Centre of Gravity                |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| Depends only on mass distribution    | Depends on gravity               |
| Independent of gravitational field   | Depends on gravitational field   |
| Coincides with CG in a uniform field | May differ in non-uniform fields |

---

# 3. Linear Momentum of a System

## Total Momentum

```
P = Σmivi
```

---

# Motion of Centre of Mass

```
Fexternal = M aCM
```

Where

- M = Total mass
- aCM = Acceleration of centre of mass

---

# Applications

- Rocket motion.
- Explosion.
- Collision.
- Sports.

# 4. Rotational Motion

## Definition

Motion of a body about a fixed axis.

---

# Angular Displacement

```
θ = s/r
```

---

# Angular Velocity

Rate of change of angular displacement.

```
ω = dθ/dt
```

---

# SI Unit

```
rad s⁻¹
```

---

# Angular Acceleration

Rate of change of angular velocity.

```
α = dω/dt
```

---

# SI Unit

```
rad s⁻²
```

---

# Rotational Equations of Motion

```
ω = ω₀ + αt
```

---

```
θ = ω₀t + ½αt²
```

---

```
ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ
```

---

# Difference Between Linear and Angular Quantities

| Linear           | Angular                  |
| ---------------- | ------------------------ |
| Displacement (s) | Angular displacement (θ) |
| Velocity (v)     | Angular velocity (ω)     |
| Acceleration (a) | Angular acceleration (α) |

---

# Relation Between Linear and Angular Quantities

| Linear Quantity | Angular Quantity         |
| --------------- | ------------------------ |
| v = rω          | Linear velocity          |
| aₜ = rα         | Tangential acceleration  |
| aₙ = v²/r = rω² | Centripetal acceleration |

---

# 5. Torque (Moment of Force)

## Definition

Torque is the turning effect of a force acting on a body.

---

# Formula

```
τ = r × F
```

Magnitude

```
τ = rF sinθ
```

---

# SI Unit

```
N m
```

---

# Dimensional Formula

```
[ML²T⁻²]
```

---

# Direction

Determined by the **Right-Hand Rule**.

---

# Applications

- Opening a door.
- Tightening bolts.
- Steering wheel.
- Spanner.

---

# Difference Between Force and Torque

| Force                | Torque            |
| -------------------- | ----------------- |
| Produces translation | Produces rotation |
| F = ma               | τ = rF sinθ       |

---

# 6. Moment of Inertia (MOI)

## Definition

The moment of inertia is the rotational analogue of mass and measures the resistance of a body to rotational motion.

---

# Formula

```
I = Σmir²
```

---

# SI Unit

```
kg m²
```

---

# Dimensional Formula

```
[ML²]
```

---

# Factors Affecting MOI

- Mass
- Shape
- Size
- Position of axis

---

# Standard Moments of Inertia

| Body           | Axis           | Moment of Inertia |
| -------------- | -------------- | ----------------- |
| Thin Ring      | Through centre | MR²               |
| Solid Disc     | Through centre | ½MR²              |
| Solid Sphere   | Through centre | 2/5MR²            |
| Hollow Sphere  | Through centre | 2/3MR²            |
| Solid Cylinder | Axis           | ½MR²              |
| Thin Rod       | Centre         | 1/12ML²           |
| Thin Rod       | End            | 1/3ML²            |

---

# Radius of Gyration

## Definition

The distance from the axis at which the entire mass may be assumed to be concentrated without changing the moment of inertia.

---

# Formula

```
I = Mk²
```

---

```
k = √(I/M)
```

---

# Difference Between Mass and Moment of Inertia

| Mass                      | Moment of Inertia      |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Resistance to translation | Resistance to rotation |
| Unit: kg                  | Unit: kg m²            |

---

# 7. Theorems of Moment of Inertia

---

# Parallel Axis Theorem

If

```
Icm
```

is the MOI about the centre of mass,

```
I = Icm + Md²
```

Where

- d = Distance between axes

---

# Perpendicular Axis Theorem

Applicable to plane lamina.

```
Iz = Ix + Iy
```

---

# Difference Between Parallel and Perpendicular Axis Theorems

| Parallel Axis                  | Perpendicular Axis              |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| Shift to parallel axis         | Sum of two perpendicular axes   |
| Applicable to all rigid bodies | Applicable only to plane lamina |

---

# 8. Angular Momentum

## Definition

Angular momentum is the rotational analogue of linear momentum.

---

# Formula

```
L = Iω
```

---

Also,

```
L = r × p
```

---

# SI Unit

```
kg m² s⁻¹
```

---

# Conservation of Angular Momentum

If no external torque acts,

```
Linitial = Lfinal
```

---

# Applications

- Ice skater spinning.
- Planetary motion.
- Diving.
- Gyroscope.

---

# Difference Between Linear and Angular Momentum

| Linear Momentum | Angular Momentum |
| --------------- | ---------------- |
| p = mv          | L = Iω           |
| Translation     | Rotation         |

---

# 9. Rotational Kinetic Energy

## Formula

```
KE = ½Iω²
```

---

# Total Kinetic Energy of Rolling Body

```
KE

=

½mv²

+

½Iω²
```

---

# 10. Rolling Motion

## Definition

Rolling motion is the combination of translational and rotational motion.

---

# Condition for Pure Rolling

```
v = rω
```

---

# Examples

- Bicycle wheel.
- Rolling ball.
- Car tyres.

---

# Difference Between Rolling and Sliding

| Rolling                | Sliding          |
| ---------------------- | ---------------- |
| Translation + Rotation | Only translation |
| Static friction        | Kinetic friction |

---

# 11. Equilibrium of Rigid Bodies

## Conditions

### Translational Equilibrium

```
ΣF = 0
```

---

### Rotational Equilibrium

```
Στ = 0
```

---

# Principle of Moments

For equilibrium,

```
Clockwise Moments

=

Anticlockwise Moments
```

---

# Applications

- Seesaw.
- Beam balance.
- Bridge design.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Find the torque produced by a force of 20 N acting perpendicular to a spanner of length 0.5 m.

### Solution

```
τ = rF

= 0.5 × 20

= 10 N m
```

### Answer

**10 N m**

---

## Example 2

### Question

A disc has a moment of inertia of 4 kg m² and rotates with an angular velocity of 5 rad s⁻¹. Find its angular momentum.

### Solution

```
L = Iω

= 4 × 5

= 20 kg m² s⁻¹
```

### Answer

**20 kg m² s⁻¹**

---

## Example 3

### Question

A wheel rotates with an angular velocity of 10 rad s⁻¹ and radius 0.2 m. Find its linear speed.

### Solution

```
v = rω

= 0.2 × 10

= 2 m s⁻¹
```

### Answer

**2 m s⁻¹**

---

## Example 4

### Question

Find the rotational kinetic energy of a body having I = 2 kg m² and ω = 6 rad s⁻¹.

### Solution

```
KE = ½Iω²

= ½ × 2 × 36

= 36 J
```

### Answer

**36 J**

---

## Example 5

### Question

A rolling ball has a translational kinetic energy of 40 J and rotational kinetic energy of 10 J. Find its total kinetic energy.

### Solution

```
Total KE

= 40 + 10

= 50 J
```

### Answer

**50 J**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **torque** with **force**.
- Using incorrect standard formulas for **moment of inertia**.
- Forgetting that **angular momentum is conserved only when external torque is zero**.
- Ignoring the rotational kinetic energy while solving rolling motion problems.
- Applying the **perpendicular axis theorem** to three-dimensional bodies.
- Forgetting the condition **v = rω** for pure rolling.

# Chapter Summary

- A **system of particles** can undergo both translational and rotational motion, with the **centre of mass** representing the effective point of concentration of mass.
- **Torque** is the turning effect of a force, while the **moment of inertia** measures a body's resistance to rotational acceleration and depends on its mass distribution.
- The **parallel axis theorem** and **perpendicular axis theorem** simplify the calculation of the moment of inertia for different axes.
- **Angular momentum** is conserved in the absence of external torque and plays a crucial role in rotating systems such as gyroscopes, planets, and spinning athletes.
- **Rolling motion** combines translation and rotation, with the condition **v = rω** for pure rolling without slipping.
- Understanding rotational motion is essential for studying **mechanics, engineering, machine design, robotics, aerospace systems, and advanced dynamics**.
