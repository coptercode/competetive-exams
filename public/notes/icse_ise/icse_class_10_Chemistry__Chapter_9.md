# Introduction

**Ammonia (NH₃)** is a colourless gas with a characteristic **pungent smell**. It is highly soluble in water and forms a weak alkali called **ammonium hydroxide (NH₄OH)**. Ammonia is one of the most important industrial chemicals and is extensively used in the manufacture of **fertilizers, nitric acid, explosives, medicines, refrigeration systems, and cleaning agents**. Industrially, ammonia is manufactured by the **Haber Process**.

---

# 1. Ammonia

## Definition

**Ammonia** is a colourless, alkaline gas consisting of one nitrogen atom and three hydrogen atoms.

---

## Molecular Formula

```
NH₃
```

---

## Nature

- Covalent compound.
- Colourless gas.
- Pungent smell.
- Highly soluble in water.
- Lighter than air.
- Weakly alkaline.

---

# 2. Laboratory Preparation of Ammonia

## Principle

Ammonia is prepared by heating an **ammonium salt** with a **strong alkali**.

---

## Chemical Equation

```
2NH₄Cl + Ca(OH)₂

↓

CaCl₂ + 2NH₃↑ + 2H₂O
```

---

## Apparatus Required

- Round-bottom flask.
- Thistle funnel.
- Delivery tube.
- Dry gas jar.

---

## Drying Agent

```
Quicklime (CaO)
```

### Why Quicklime?

Quicklime absorbs moisture without reacting with ammonia.

---

## Collection of Gas

Ammonia is collected by **downward displacement of air** because:

- It is lighter than air.
- Highly soluble in water.

---

# 3. Industrial Preparation (Haber Process)

## Principle

Nitrogen reacts with hydrogen to produce ammonia under suitable conditions.

---

## Chemical Equation

```
N₂ + 3H₂

⇌

2NH₃ + Heat
```

---

## Conditions

| Condition   | Value                        |
| ----------- | ---------------------------- |
| Temperature | 450°C                        |
| Pressure    | 200 atm                      |
| Catalyst    | Finely divided Iron          |
| Promoters   | Molybdenum / Potassium oxide |

---

# 4. Physical Properties

- Colourless gas.
- Characteristic pungent smell.
- Highly soluble in water.
- Lighter than air.
- Poisonous in high concentration.
- Easily liquefied.

---

# 5. Chemical Properties

---

## (i) Reaction with Water

```
NH₃ + H₂O

⇌

NH₄OH
```

Forms a weak alkali.

---

## (ii) Reaction with Hydrochloric Acid

```
NH₃ + HCl

↓

NH₄Cl
```

### Observation

Dense white fumes of **ammonium chloride** are formed.

---

## (iii) Combustion

```
4NH₃ + 3O₂

↓

2N₂ + 6H₂O
```

Ammonia burns in oxygen to form nitrogen and water.

---

## (iv) Oxidation (Catalytic)

```
4NH₃ + 5O₂

↓

4NO + 6H₂O
```

Catalyst: Platinum

This reaction is used in the manufacture of nitric acid.

---

# 6. Tests for Ammonia Gas

---

## (i) Smell Test

Characteristic pungent smell.

---

## (ii) Moist Red Litmus Test

Turns **moist red litmus blue**.

---

## (iii) Hydrochloric Acid Test

Produces **dense white fumes** of ammonium chloride.

# Difference Between Ammonia and Ammonium Hydroxide

| Ammonia (NH₃)                 | Ammonium Hydroxide (NH₄OH)    |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Gas                           | Aqueous solution              |
| Covalent compound             | Weak alkali                   |
| Colourless gas                | Colourless liquid             |
| Does not contain OH⁻ directly | Produces OH⁻ ions in solution |

# Difference Between Laboratory and Industrial Preparation

| Laboratory Preparation                       | Haber Process                               |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Uses ammonium chloride and calcium hydroxide | Uses nitrogen and hydrogen                  |
| Small-scale preparation                      | Large-scale industrial production           |
| Simple heating                               | High temperature and pressure with catalyst |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **ammonia (NH₃)** with **ammonium hydroxide (NH₄OH)**.
- Forgetting that ammonia is **lighter than air**.
- Assuming ammonia can be collected over water (it cannot because it is highly soluble).
- Using **concentrated sulfuric acid** to dry ammonia (it reacts with NH₃; **quicklime** is used instead).
- Mixing up the conditions of the **Haber Process**.
- Forgetting that ammonia is a **weak base**, not a strong alkali.
- Confusing the laboratory preparation with the industrial method.

# Chapter Summary

- **Ammonia (NH₃)** is a colourless, pungent-smelling gas that is highly soluble in water and forms **ammonium hydroxide**, a weak alkali.
- In the laboratory, ammonia is prepared by heating **ammonium chloride** with **calcium hydroxide**, dried using **quicklime**, and collected by **downward displacement of air**.
- Industrially, ammonia is manufactured by the **Haber Process**, in which **nitrogen and hydrogen react at 450°C and about 200 atm in the presence of an iron catalyst**.
- Ammonia reacts with **water** to form ammonium hydroxide, with **hydrochloric acid** to produce dense white fumes of ammonium chloride, and with **oxygen** during combustion and catalytic oxidation.
- The gas can be identified by its **pungent smell**, its ability to **turn moist red litmus paper blue**, and by forming **white fumes with hydrochloric acid**.
- Ammonia is widely used in the manufacture of **fertilizers, nitric acid, explosives, pharmaceuticals, refrigeration systems, and cleaning products**, making it one of the most important industrial chemicals.
