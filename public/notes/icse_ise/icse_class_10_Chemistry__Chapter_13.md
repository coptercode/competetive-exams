# Introduction

**Sulphuric Acid (H₂SO₄)** is one of the most important industrial chemicals and is often called the **"King of Chemicals"** because of its extensive use in industries. It is a **strong, non-volatile, highly corrosive, and hygroscopic acid** used in the manufacture of fertilizers, detergents, explosives, petroleum products, dyes, and batteries. Industrially, sulphuric acid is manufactured by the **Contact Process**.

---

# 1. Sulphuric Acid

## Definition

**Sulphuric Acid** is a strong mineral acid consisting of hydrogen, sulfur, and oxygen.

---

## Molecular Formula

```
H₂SO₄
```

---

## Nature

- Strong acid.
- Dibasic acid (contains two replaceable hydrogen atoms).
- Highly corrosive.
- Non-volatile.
- Strong dehydrating agent.
- Hygroscopic.
- Strong oxidizing agent (when concentrated).

---

# 2. Laboratory Preparation

Sulphuric acid is **not prepared in the laboratory** because it is manufactured economically on a large scale by the **Contact Process**.

---

# 3. Industrial Preparation – Contact Process

## Step 1: Formation of Sulfur Dioxide

Sulfur is burned in air.

```
S + O₂

↓

SO₂
```

---

## Step 2: Oxidation of Sulfur Dioxide

```
2SO₂ + O₂

⇌

2SO₃
```

### Conditions

| Factor      | Value                    |
| ----------- | ------------------------ |
| Catalyst    | Vanadium(V) Oxide (V₂O₅) |
| Temperature | About 450°C              |
| Pressure    | 1–2 atm                  |

---

## Step 3: Formation of Oleum

```
SO₃ + H₂SO₄

↓

H₂S₂O₇ (Oleum)
```

---

## Step 4: Formation of Sulphuric Acid

```
H₂S₂O₇ + H₂O

↓

2H₂SO₄
```

---

# 4. Physical Properties

- Colourless, oily liquid.
- Odourless.
- Highly viscous.
- Highly soluble in water.
- Highly corrosive.
- High boiling point.
- Dense liquid.

> **Safety Note:** Always add **acid to water**, never water to acid, to prevent violent splashing.

---

# 5. Chemical Properties

---

## (i) Ionization in Water

```
H₂SO₄ + 2H₂O

↓

2H₃O⁺ + SO₄²⁻
```

---

## (ii) Reaction with Metals

Example:

```
Zn + H₂SO₄ (Dil.)

↓

ZnSO₄ + H₂↑
```

---

## (iii) Reaction with Bases

```
H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH

↓

Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O
```

---

## (iv) Reaction with Carbonates

```
CaCO₃ + H₂SO₄

↓

CaSO₄ + CO₂↑ + H₂O
```

---

# 6. Dehydrating Nature

## Definition

Concentrated sulphuric acid removes water from many substances.

---

## Example

When concentrated sulphuric acid is added to sugar:

```
C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁

↓

12C + 11H₂O
```

### Observation

- Sugar turns black.
- Steam is evolved.
- A porous mass of carbon is formed.

---

# 7. Hygroscopic Nature

## Definition

A **hygroscopic substance** absorbs moisture from the atmosphere without changing its chemical composition.

---

## Application

Concentrated sulphuric acid is used as a **drying agent** for gases that do not react with it.

---

# 8. Oxidizing Nature

Concentrated sulphuric acid acts as an oxidizing agent at high temperatures.

### Example

```
Cu + 2H₂SO₄ (Conc.)

↓

CuSO₄ + SO₂↑ + 2H₂O
```

### Observation

Sulfur dioxide gas is evolved.

---

# 9. Uses of Sulphuric Acid

- Manufacture of fertilizers.
- Lead-acid batteries.
- Petroleum refining.
- Manufacture of detergents.
- Manufacture of explosives.
- Dyes and paints.
- Chemical laboratories.
- Metal cleaning (pickling).

---

# Difference Between Dilute and Concentrated Sulphuric Acid

| Dilute H₂SO₄                            | Concentrated H₂SO₄                     |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Strong acid                             | Strong acid + dehydrating agent        |
| Reacts with active metals to produce H₂ | Strong oxidizing and dehydrating agent |
| Less corrosive                          | Highly corrosive                       |
| Used in neutralization                  | Used as drying agent                   |

---

# Difference Between Sulphuric Acid and Nitric Acid

| Sulphuric Acid  | Nitric Acid            |
| --------------- | ---------------------- |
| Formula: H₂SO₄  | Formula: HNO₃          |
| Dibasic acid    | Monobasic acid         |
| Contact Process | Ostwald Process        |
| Drying agent    | Strong oxidizing agent |

Sulphuric acid is produced on a large scale by a catalytic oxidation process.


---

## Example 3

### Question

Which catalyst is used in the Contact Process?

### Solution

Sulfur dioxide is oxidized using a suitable catalyst.

### Answer

```

```

---

## Example 4

### Question

Why is concentrated sulphuric acid called a dehydrating agent?

### Solution

It removes water from many compounds.

### Answer

Because it removes water from substances, causing dehydration.

---

## Example 5

### Question

Why should acid always be added to water?

### Solution

Dilution of concentrated sulphuric acid is highly exothermic.

### Answer

To prevent violent splashing due to the large amount of heat produced.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing the **Contact Process** with the **Ostwald Process**.
- Forgetting that sulphuric acid is a **dibasic acid**.
- Mixing up **hygroscopic** and **dehydrating** properties.
- Writing the wrong catalyst (**V₂O₅**, not platinum).
- Forgetting to **add acid to water**, not water to acid.
- Assuming concentrated sulphuric acid always liberates hydrogen gas with metals.
- Confusing **oleum (H₂S₂O₇)** with sulphuric acid.

# Chapter Summary

- **Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄)** is a strong, dibasic mineral acid widely known as the **King of Chemicals** because of its immense industrial importance.
- It is manufactured commercially by the **Contact Process**, where sulfur dioxide is converted into sulfur trioxide using **vanadium(V) oxide (V₂O₅)** as a catalyst, followed by the formation of **oleum** and finally sulphuric acid.
- Sulphuric acid reacts with **metals, bases, and carbonates**, producing salts and characteristic products such as hydrogen gas, water, and carbon dioxide.
- Concentrated sulphuric acid exhibits **dehydrating**, **hygroscopic**, and **oxidizing** properties, making it valuable as a drying agent and industrial reagent.
- It is extensively used in the manufacture of **fertilizers, lead-acid batteries, detergents, explosives, petroleum products, dyes, and pharmaceuticals**.
- Proper handling is essential because sulphuric acid is highly corrosive, and **acid should always be added to water** during dilution to ensure safety.
