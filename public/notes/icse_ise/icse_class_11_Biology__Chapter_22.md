# Introduction

Besides the nervous system, the human body is regulated by the **endocrine system**, which controls body functions through **chemical messengers called hormones**. Hormones regulate growth, metabolism, reproduction, water balance, stress response, and homeostasis.

The endocrine system consists of **ductless glands** that release hormones directly into the bloodstream.

---

# 1. Endocrine System

## Definition

The endocrine system is a network of **ductless glands** that secrete hormones directly into the blood to regulate various physiological activities.

---

# Characteristics of Hormones

- Secreted by endocrine glands.
- Released directly into blood.
- Act in very small quantities.
- Specific target organs.
- Regulate body functions.
- Maintain homeostasis.

---

# Difference Between Endocrine and Exocrine Glands

| Endocrine Glands             | Exocrine Glands                   |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Ductless                     | Have ducts                        |
| Hormones secreted into blood | Secretions released through ducts |
| Example: Pituitary           | Example: Salivary gland           |

# Feedback Mechanism

## Negative Feedback

Maintains hormone levels within normal limits.

Example

- Thyroxine regulation.

---

## Positive Feedback

Enhances hormone secretion.

Example

- Oxytocin during childbirth.

---

# Difference Between Positive and Negative Feedback

| Negative Feedback         | Positive Feedback           |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Reduces hormone secretion | Increases hormone secretion |
| Maintains homeostasis     | Amplifies response          |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Which gland is known as the master gland?

### Solution

The pituitary gland controls the activity of several other endocrine glands.

### Answer

Pituitary gland.

---

## Example 2

### Question

Which hormone lowers blood glucose level?

### Solution

Insulin promotes the uptake and storage of glucose, thereby reducing blood glucose concentration.

### Answer

Insulin.

---

## Example 3

### Question

Name the hormone secreted by the adrenal medulla.

### Solution

The adrenal medulla secretes adrenaline and noradrenaline, which prepare the body for emergency situations.

### Answer

Adrenaline (Epinephrine).

---

## Example 4

### Question

What is the cause of goitre?

### Solution

Goitre commonly develops due to iodine deficiency, which reduces thyroxine synthesis and enlarges the thyroid gland.

### Answer

Iodine deficiency.

---

## Example 5

### Question

Differentiate between insulin and glucagon.

### Solution

Insulin decreases blood glucose levels, whereas glucagon increases blood glucose levels.

### Answer

Insulin → Lowers blood glucose; Glucagon → Raises blood glucose.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing endocrine glands with exocrine glands.
- Mixing up the functions of insulin and glucagon.
- Assuming calcitonin and parathyroid hormone have the same effect.
- Confusing gigantism with acromegaly.
- Forgetting that the posterior pituitary stores and releases ADH and oxytocin.
- Mixing up hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism.

# Chapter Summary

- The **endocrine system** regulates body functions through **hormones**, which are chemical messengers released by ductless glands into the bloodstream.
- Major endocrine glands include the **pituitary, thyroid, parathyroid, adrenal glands, pancreas, pineal gland, thymus, testes, and ovaries**, each secreting specific hormones with distinct physiological roles.
- Hormones regulate **growth, metabolism, reproduction, calcium balance, stress responses, blood glucose levels, water balance, and immunity**, helping maintain internal stability.
- Hormone secretion is controlled by **feedback mechanisms**, especially **negative feedback**, which maintains homeostasis.
- Disorders such as **diabetes mellitus, goitre, gigantism, acromegaly, dwarfism, Addison's disease, and Cushing's syndrome** result from hormonal imbalance.
- Understanding chemical coordination is fundamental for **medicine, endocrinology, biotechnology, and maintaining overall physiological homeostasis**.
