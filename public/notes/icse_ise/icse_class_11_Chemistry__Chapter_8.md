# Introduction

Hydrogen is the **lightest and most abundant element in the universe**. It occupies a unique position in the Periodic Table because it exhibits similarities with both **alkali metals (Group 1)** and **halogens (Group 17)**. Hydrogen exists mainly as a **diatomic molecule (H₂)** and forms numerous compounds with almost all elements.

This chapter deals with the occurrence, preparation, properties, isotopes, hydrides, water, hydrogen peroxide, and the importance of hydrogen as a clean fuel.

---

# 1. Position of Hydrogen in the Periodic Table

## Electronic Configuration

```
Atomic Number = 1

Electronic Configuration

1s¹
```

---

## Similarity with Alkali Metals

- One valence electron.
- Forms H⁺ ion.
- Electropositive nature.
- Forms ionic compounds.

---

## Similarity with Halogens

- Requires one electron to complete duplet.
- Forms H⁻ ion.
- Exists as a diatomic molecule (H₂).
- Forms covalent compounds.

---

## Unique Nature of Hydrogen

- Smallest atom.
- High ionization enthalpy.
- Forms both ionic and covalent compounds.
- Acts as both oxidizing and reducing agent.

---

# Difference Between Hydrogen and Alkali Metals

| Hydrogen           | Alkali Metals          |
| ------------------ | ---------------------- |
| Non-metal          | Metals                 |
| Gas                | Solids                 |
| Covalent compounds | Mostly ionic compounds |

---

# Difference Between Hydrogen and Halogens

| Hydrogen                          | Halogens                |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| One electron                      | Seven valence electrons |
| Can lose or gain electron         | Usually gains electron  |
| Electropositive & electronegative | Mainly electronegative  |

# 3. Isotopes of Hydrogen

Hydrogen has **three isotopes**.

| Isotope   | Symbol  | Protons | Neutrons |
| --------- | ------- | ------- | -------- |
| Protium   | ¹H      | 1       | 0        |
| Deuterium | ²H or D | 1       | 1        |
| Tritium   | ³H or T | 1       | 2        |

---

# Difference Between the Isotopes

| Protium    | Deuterium   | Tritium      |
| ---------- | ----------- | ------------ |
| Stable     | Stable      | Radioactive  |
| No neutron | One neutron | Two neutrons |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Name the three isotopes of hydrogen.

### Solution

Hydrogen exists as three isotopes.

### Answer

**Protium, Deuterium, and Tritium.**

---

## Example 2

### Question

Which isotope of hydrogen is radioactive?

### Solution

Among the three isotopes, only tritium undergoes radioactive decay.

### Answer

**Tritium (³H).**

---

## Example 3

### Question

Write the balanced equation for the combustion of hydrogen.

### Solution

```
2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
```

### Answer

**2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O**

---

## Example 4

### Question

What causes temporary hardness of water?

### Solution

Temporary hardness is caused by bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium.

### Answer

**Ca(HCO₃)₂ and Mg(HCO₃)₂**

---

## Example 5

### Question

Why is hydrogen considered a clean fuel?

### Solution

Hydrogen burns to produce water without emitting carbon dioxide.

### Answer

**Because it produces only water as the main product of combustion.**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **protium, deuterium, and tritium**.
- Assuming hydrogen behaves only like alkali metals or only like halogens.
- Mixing up **temporary** and **permanent hardness**.
- Confusing **water (H₂O)** with **hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂)**.
- Forgetting that hydrogen peroxide acts as both an oxidizing and reducing agent.
- Writing unbalanced equations for hydrogen reactions.

# Chapter Summary

- **Hydrogen** is the lightest element and occupies a **unique position** in the periodic table due to its resemblance to both alkali metals and halogens.
- It exists naturally in combined form and has **three isotopes**—**protium, deuterium, and tritium**—with distinct applications in science and industry.
- Hydrogen is prepared by **laboratory methods, electrolysis of water, and steam reforming**, and it exhibits important physical and chemical properties, including reducing behavior.
- It forms **ionic, covalent, and metallic hydrides**, each with characteristic properties and uses.
- The chapter also covers **water, hardness of water, heavy water, and hydrogen peroxide**, emphasizing their preparation, properties, and industrial significance.
- Hydrogen is regarded as a **clean and renewable fuel**, making it an important energy source for future sustainable technologies.
