# Introduction

**Electrochemistry** is the branch of chemistry that deals with the relationship between:

- Chemical energy
- Electrical energy

It involves reactions where electrons are transferred.

---

# 1. Redox Reactions

Electrochemical reactions are based on oxidation and reduction.

---

# Oxidation

## Definition

Loss of electrons is called oxidation.

Example:

\[
Zn \rightarrow Zn^{2+}+2e^-
\]

Zinc loses electrons.

---

# Reduction

## Definition

Gain of electrons is called reduction.

Example:

\[
Cu^{2+}+2e^- \rightarrow Cu
\]

Copper ions gain electrons.

---

# 2. Electrochemical Cells

## Definition

A device that converts chemical energy into electrical energy or electrical energy into chemical energy.

---

# 3. Galvanic Cell

## Definition

A cell that converts chemical energy into electrical energy through a spontaneous redox reaction.

---

# Example:

## Daniell Cell

Reaction:

\[
Zn + Cu^{2+} \rightarrow Zn^{2+}+Cu
\]

---

# Structure of Daniell Cell

```
      Zn Electrode              Cu Electrode

          │                         │

       ZnSO₄                     CuSO₄

          │                         │

          └──── Salt Bridge ────────┘
```

---

# Components

## 1. Anode

Electrode where oxidation occurs.

In Daniell cell:

```
Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻
```

Zinc electrode acts as anode.

---

## 2. Cathode

Electrode where reduction occurs.

Reaction:

```
Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu
```

Copper electrode acts as cathode.

---

# 4. Salt Bridge

## Definition

A U-shaped tube containing electrolyte that connects two half cells.

---

# Functions

### 1. Maintains electrical neutrality

### 2. Completes electrical circuit

### 3. Prevents direct mixing of solutions

---

# 5. Cell Notation

Representation of an electrochemical cell.

---

Daniell Cell:

\[
Zn|Zn^{2+}||Cu^{2+}|Cu
\]

---

Meaning:

```
Anode | Anode solution || Cathode solution | Cathode
```

---

# 6. Electrode Potential

## Definition

The tendency of an electrode to lose or gain electrons when placed in electrolyte.

---

# Standard Electrode Potential (E°)

Measured under standard conditions:

- Temperature = 298 K
- Pressure = 1 bar
- Concentration = 1 M

---

# Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)

Reference electrode.

Its potential:

\[
E^\circ = 0V
\]

---

# 7. EMF of Cell

## Definition

The maximum potential difference between two electrodes of an electrochemical cell.

---

Formula:

\[
E_{cell}=E_{cathode}-E_{anode}
\]

---

# For spontaneous cell:

\[
E_{cell}>0
\]

---

# 8. Electrochemical Series

## Definition

Arrangement of electrodes according to their standard reduction potentials.

---

# Applications

Used to predict:

- Oxidising strength
- Reducing strength
- Feasibility of reactions

---

# 9. Gibbs Energy and Cell Potential

Relationship:

\[
\Delta G=-nFE_{cell}
\]

Where:

- n = Number of electrons
- F = Faraday constant
- Ecell = Cell potential

---

# Conditions

If:

\[
E_{cell}>0
\]

Then:

\[
\Delta G<0
\]

Reaction is spontaneous.

---

# 10. Nernst Equation

## Definition

Equation used to calculate electrode potential under non-standard conditions.

---

General equation:

\[
E=E^\circ-\frac{0.0591}{n}\log Q
\]

(at 298 K)

---

Where:

- E = Cell potential
- E° = Standard potential
- n = Number of electrons
- Q = Reaction quotient

---

# For Daniell Cell:

Reaction:

\[
Zn+Cu^{2+}\rightarrow Zn^{2+}+Cu
\]

Reaction quotient:

\[
Q=\frac{[Zn^{2+}]}{[Cu^{2+}]}
\]

---

# 11. Relation Between E° and Equilibrium Constant

At equilibrium:

\[
E_{cell}=0
\]

Equation:

\[
E^\circ_{cell}
=

\frac{0.0591}{n}\log K
\]

---

Where:

K = Equilibrium constant

---

## Definition

A cell where electrical energy is converted into chemical energy.

---

Electrolysis of molten NaCl.

---

# Electrolysis

Chemical decomposition caused by passing electricity through an electrolyte.

---

# Components


# 13. Conductance of Electrolytic Solutions

Resistance:

\[
R=\frac{V}{I}
\]

---

Conductance:

\[
G=\frac{1}{R}
\]

---

Unit:

```
Siemens (S)
```

---

# 14. Specific Conductivity (κ)

## Definition

Conductance of a solution placed between electrodes of unit length and unit area.

---

Formula:

\[
\kappa=G\frac{l}{A}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
S\,cm^{-1}
\]

---

# 15. Molar Conductivity (Λm)

## Definition

Conductance of all ions produced by one mole of electrolyte.

---

Formula:

\[
\Lambda_m=
\frac{\kappa \times1000}{M}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}
\]

---

# 16. Variation of Conductivity with Dilution

## Strong Electrolytes

Examples:

- HCl
- NaCl

Conductivity increases due to increased ion mobility.

---

## Weak Electrolytes

Examples:

- CH₃COOH

Conductivity increases greatly due to increased ionisation.

---

# 17. Kohlrausch's Law

## Statement

At infinite dilution, each ion contributes independently to total molar conductivity.

---

Formula:

\[
\Lambda_m^\circ=
\lambda^\circ_+ + \lambda^\circ_-
\]

---

# Applications

## 1. Calculate conductivity of weak electrolytes.

## 2. Determine degree of dissociation.

## 3. Calculate solubility of sparingly soluble salts.

# 18. Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis

---

# First Law

## Statement

The amount of substance deposited is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity passed.

---

Formula:

\[
m \propto Q
\]

Since:

\[
Q=It
\]

Therefore:

\[
m=ZIt
\]

---

Where:

- m = Mass deposited
- I = Current
- t = Time
- Z = Electrochemical equivalent

---

# Second Law

## Statement

When the same quantity of electricity passes through different electrolytes, deposited masses are proportional to their equivalent masses.

---

Formula:

\[
\frac{m_1}{m_2}
=

\frac{E_1}{E_2}
\]

---

# 19. Faraday Constant

One Faraday:

\[
96500C
\]

is the charge required to deposit one gram equivalent of a substance.

---

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. Write Nernst equation.

**Answer:**

\[
E=E^\circ-\frac{0.0591}{n}\log Q
\]

---

## 2. Explain salt bridge functions.

**Answer:**

Salt bridge maintains electrical neutrality, completes circuit, and prevents direct mixing of electrolytes.

---

## 3. State Faraday's first law.

**Answer:**

Mass deposited during electrolysis is directly proportional to quantity of electricity passed.

---

## 4. What is Kohlrausch's law?

**Answer:**

At infinite dilution, each ion contributes independently to molar conductivity.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing anode and cathode reactions.
- Writing oxidation at cathode.
- Forgetting sign convention of Ecell.
- Mixing conductivity and molar conductivity.
- Ignoring number of electrons in Nernst equation.
