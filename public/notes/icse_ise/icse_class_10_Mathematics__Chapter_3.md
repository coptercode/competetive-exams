# Introduction

A **share** represents a unit of ownership in a company. Companies raise capital by issuing shares to investors. In return, shareholders receive a portion of the company's profits known as **dividends**. This chapter explains the concepts of **face value**, **market value**, **premium**, **discount**, **investment**, **dividend**, and **rate of return**, along with problems involving the buying and selling of shares.

---

# 1. Shares

## Definition

A **share** is a unit of ownership in a company.

A person who owns shares is called a **shareholder**.

---

## Why Companies Issue Shares?

Companies issue shares to:

- Raise capital
- Expand business
- Purchase assets
- Develop new projects

---

## Types of Values

### Face Value (FV)

The original value printed on a share certificate.

Examples

```
₹10

₹50

₹100
```

---

### Market Value (MV)

The current price of a share in the stock market.

It may be

- Equal to Face Value
- Greater than Face Value
- Less than Face Value

# 4. Dividend

## Definition

A **Dividend** is the share of profit distributed by a company to its shareholders.

Dividend is always calculated on the **Face Value**, not on the Market Value.

---

## Formula

```
Dividend

=

Number of Shares

×

Face Value

Dividend %
```

# Common Mistakes

- Calculating the **dividend** on the **market value** instead of the **face value**.
- Confusing **face value** with **market value**.
- Using the investment amount instead of the number of shares while finding the dividend.
- Forgetting that the **yield** is calculated on the **investment**, not on the face value.
- Mixing up **premium**, **discount**, and **at par**.
- Using the wrong market value while calculating the number of shares purchased.
- Forgetting to convert the dividend percentage into the correct calculation.
- Making calculation errors during share interchange problems.

# Formula Sheet

| Formula                                                         | Description      |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| **Investment = Number of Shares × Market Value**                | Total Investment |
| **Number of Shares = Investment / Market Value**                | Shares Purchased |
| **Dividend = Number of Shares × Face Value × Dividend % / 100** | Annual Dividend  |
| **Yield = Dividend / Investment × 100**                         | Rate of Return   |
| **Yield = Dividend % × Face Value / Market Value**              | Shortcut Formula |
| **Market Value = Face Value**                                   | At Par           |
| **Market Value > Face Value**                                   | At Premium       |
| **Market Value < Face Value**                                   | At Discount      |

# Chapter Summary

- A **share** represents a unit of ownership in a company, and a **shareholder** earns a portion of the company's profits as **dividend**.
- **Face Value** is the nominal value of a share, whereas **Market Value** is the current trading price in the stock market.
- Shares may be issued **at par**, **at premium**, or **at discount**, depending on the relationship between the market value and face value.
- The **investment** required to purchase shares depends on the **market value**, while the **dividend** is always calculated using the **face value**.
- The **rate of return (yield)** measures the profitability of an investment as a percentage of the amount invested.
- In **share interchange** problems, the proceeds from selling one company's shares are used to purchase another company's shares.
- Accurate use of formulas and a clear understanding of the difference between **face value** and **market value** are essential for solving ICSE Class 10 examination questions.
