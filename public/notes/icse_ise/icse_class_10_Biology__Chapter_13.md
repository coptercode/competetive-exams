# Introduction

**Pollution** is the undesirable change in the physical, chemical, or biological characteristics of the environment due to the addition of harmful substances called **pollutants**. Pollution affects **air, water, soil, and living organisms**, leading to serious health and environmental problems. Preventing pollution is essential for protecting biodiversity and ensuring sustainable development.

---

# 1. Pollution

## Definition

**Pollution** is the contamination of the environment by harmful substances that adversely affect living organisms and natural resources.

---

## Pollutants

### Definition

**Pollutants** are harmful substances that cause pollution.

---

## Types of Pollutants

- Biodegradable pollutants
- Non-biodegradable pollutants

---

### Biodegradable Pollutants

Can be decomposed by microorganisms.

#### Examples

- Vegetable waste
- Paper
- Cotton
- Food waste

---

### Non-Biodegradable Pollutants

Cannot be decomposed naturally.

#### Examples

- Plastic
- Glass
- Heavy metals
- Pesticides
- Radioactive waste

---

# 2. Air Pollution

## Definition

**Air Pollution** is the contamination of the atmosphere by harmful gases, smoke, dust, and particulate matter.

---

## Major Air Pollutants

- Carbon monoxide (CO)
- Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
- Sulphur dioxide (SO₂)
- Nitrogen oxides (NOₓ)
- Particulate matter (PM)
- Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)

---

## Sources

- Industries
- Vehicles
- Burning of fossil fuels
- Forest fires
- Thermal power plants

---

## Effects

- Respiratory diseases
- Asthma
- Global warming
- Acid rain
- Smog
- Damage to crops
- Climate change

---

# 3. Water Pollution

## Definition

**Water Pollution** is the contamination of water bodies by harmful substances, making water unsafe for living organisms.

---

## Sources

- Domestic sewage
- Industrial waste
- Agricultural runoff
- Oil spills
- Plastic waste

---

## Effects

- Water-borne diseases
- Death of aquatic organisms
- Eutrophication
- Shortage of drinking water
- Loss of biodiversity

---

# 4. Soil Pollution

## Definition

**Soil Pollution** is the contamination of soil by harmful chemicals and wastes.

---

## Sources

- Excessive use of fertilizers
- Pesticides
- Industrial waste
- Plastic waste
- Mining activities

---

## Effects

- Reduced soil fertility
- Crop contamination
- Groundwater pollution
- Loss of microorganisms
- Health hazards

---

# 5. Noise Pollution

## Definition

**Noise Pollution** is the presence of excessive or unwanted sound that disturbs normal life.

---

## Sources

- Vehicles
- Factories
- Loudspeakers
- Construction work
- Aircraft

---

## Effects

- Hearing loss
- Stress
- Sleep disturbance
- High blood pressure
- Reduced concentration

---

# 6. Radioactive Pollution

## Definition

**Radioactive Pollution** is the contamination of the environment by radioactive substances.

---

## Sources

- Nuclear power plants
- Nuclear accidents
- Radioactive waste
- Nuclear weapon testing

---

## Effects

- Cancer
- Genetic mutations
- Birth defects
- Damage to ecosystems

---

# 7. Greenhouse Effect

## Definition

The **Greenhouse Effect** is the warming of the Earth due to the trapping of heat by greenhouse gases.

---

## Greenhouse Gases

- Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
- Methane (CH₄)
- Nitrous oxide (N₂O)
- Water vapour
- CFCs

---

## Effects

- Global warming
- Climate change
- Melting glaciers
- Rising sea level

---

# 8. Global Warming

## Definition

**Global Warming** is the gradual increase in the Earth's average temperature due to increased greenhouse gases.

---

## Effects

- Heat waves
- Floods
- Droughts
- Cyclones
- Habitat loss
- Extinction of species

---

# 9. Ozone Layer

## Definition

The **Ozone Layer** is a layer of ozone gas in the stratosphere that absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV) rays.

---

## Ozone Depletion

### Cause

Mainly due to **Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).**

---

## Effects

- Skin cancer
- Cataracts
- Reduced crop yield
- Damage to marine organisms

---

# 10. Acid Rain

## Definition

**Acid Rain** is rainfall with a low pH caused by sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides reacting with water vapour.

---

## Effects

- Damages buildings
- Harms crops
- Acidifies lakes and rivers
- Kills aquatic organisms

---

# 11. Eutrophication

## Definition

**Eutrophication** is the excessive growth of algae due to nutrient enrichment of water bodies.

---

## Causes

- Fertilizers
- Sewage discharge

---

## Effects

- Oxygen depletion
- Death of aquatic animals
- Bad odour
- Poor water quality

---

# 12. Waste Management

## Methods

- Recycling
- Reuse
- Reduce
- Composting
- Proper disposal
- Waste segregation

---

# 13. Control of Pollution

## Air Pollution

- Use clean fuels.
- Plant more trees.
- Use public transport.
- Install filters in factories.

---

## Water Pollution

- Treat sewage before discharge.
- Avoid dumping waste into rivers.
- Reduce plastic use.

---

## Soil Pollution

- Reduce pesticide use.
- Promote organic farming.
- Proper disposal of industrial waste.

---

## Noise Pollution

- Use silencers.
- Restrict loudspeakers.
- Plant green belts.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Global Warming** with the **Greenhouse Effect**.
- Assuming all pollutants are non-biodegradable.
- Mixing up **Ozone Depletion** and **Global Warming**.
- Forgetting that **CFCs** damage the ozone layer.
- Confusing **Acid Rain** with normal rainfall.
- Assuming eutrophication increases dissolved oxygen (it decreases it).
- Ignoring the importance of the **3Rs** (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle).

# Chapter Summary

- **Pollution** is the contamination of the environment by harmful substances called **pollutants**, which may be **biodegradable** or **non-biodegradable**.
- Major types of pollution include **air, water, soil, noise, and radioactive pollution**, each affecting living organisms and ecosystems in different ways.
- **Air pollution** contributes to respiratory diseases, acid rain, and climate change, while **water pollution** causes water-borne diseases and harms aquatic life.
- **Soil pollution** reduces soil fertility, and **noise pollution** affects hearing and mental health. **Radioactive pollution** can lead to cancer and genetic mutations.
- The **Greenhouse Effect** and **Global Warming** are caused by the accumulation of greenhouse gases, whereas **ozone layer depletion** is mainly caused by **CFCs**, allowing harmful ultraviolet rays to reach the Earth's surface.
- **Acid rain** damages plants, buildings, and aquatic ecosystems, while **eutrophication** reduces oxygen levels in water bodies due to excessive algal growth.
- Pollution can be controlled through proper **waste management**, the **3Rs (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle)**, afforestation, cleaner technologies, and sustainable environmental practices to protect natural resources and human health.
