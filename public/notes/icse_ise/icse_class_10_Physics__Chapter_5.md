# Introduction

A **lens** is a transparent optical medium bounded by two refracting surfaces, at least one of which is spherical. Lenses refract light to form images and are widely used in spectacles, cameras, microscopes, telescopes, projectors, and many optical instruments. This chapter explains **convex and concave lenses, image formation, ray diagrams, lens formula, magnification, and power of a lens**.

---

# 1. Lens

## Definition

A **lens** is a transparent refracting medium bounded by two curved surfaces or one curved and one plane surface.

---

## Types of Lenses

### (i) Convex Lens

Also called a **Converging Lens**.

```
      ()
```

- Thicker at the centre.
- Thinner at the edges.
- Converges parallel rays.

---

### (ii) Concave Lens

Also called a **Diverging Lens**.

```
      )(
```

- Thinner at the centre.
- Thicker at the edges.
- Diverges parallel rays.

---

# 2. Parts of a Lens

## Principal Axis

The straight line passing through the optical centre and the centres of curvature.

---

## Optical Centre (O)

The point through which a ray passes without deviation.

---

## Principal Focus (F)

The point where rays parallel to the principal axis either:

- Meet after refraction (Convex Lens).
- Appear to diverge from (Concave Lens).

---

## Focal Length (f)

Distance between the optical centre and the principal focus.

---

## Twice the Focal Length (2F)

A point located at twice the focal length from the optical centre.

---

# 5. Ray Rules for a Concave Lens

### Rule 1

Parallel rays diverge after refraction.

---

### Rule 2

Ray through optical centre passes undeviated.

---

### Rule 3

Ray directed towards principal focus emerges parallel.

---

| ----------- | --------------- |
| Nature      | Virtual         |
| Orientation | Erect           |

# 7. Lens Formula

## Formula

```
      1      1      1
     --- = --- - ---
      f      v      u
```

Where,

- f = Focal length
- v = Image distance
- u = Object distance

---

## Cartesian Sign Convention

### Positive Direction

Direction of incident light.

### Rules

- Right side → Positive
- Left side → Negative
- Heights above axis → Positive
- Heights below axis → Negative

---

# 8. Magnification

## Definition

Magnification is the ratio of the height of the image to the height of the object.

---

## Formula

```
      Image Height
m = ----------------
      Object Height
```

Also,

```
      v
m = ----
      u
```

---

## Interpretation

- m > 1 → Enlarged image
- m = 1 → Same size
- m < 1 → Diminished image

---

# 9. Power of a Lens

## Definition

The **Power of a Lens** is its ability to converge or diverge light.

---

## Formula

```
      1
P = ----
      f
```

Where,

- P = Power (Dioptre)
- f = Focal length (metres)

---

## SI Unit

```
Dioptre (D)
```

---

## Sign Convention

### Convex Lens

```
Positive Power
```

---

### Concave Lens

```
Negative Power
```

# Difference Between Convex and Concave Lens

| Convex Lens                     | Concave Lens                |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Thicker at centre               | Thinner at centre           |
| Converges light                 | Diverges light              |
| Positive focal length           | Negative focal length       |
| May form real or virtual images | Always forms virtual images |

---

# Difference Between Real and Virtual Image

| Real Image                | Virtual Image                |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Can be obtained on screen | Cannot be obtained on screen |
| Inverted                  | Erect                        |
| Formed by actual rays     | Formed by apparent rays      |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Define the principal focus of a convex lens.

### Solution

Parallel rays converge after refraction.

### Answer

The point where rays parallel to the principal axis meet after passing through a convex lens.

---

## Example 2

### Question

A lens has a focal length of **0.5 m**. Find its power.

### Solution

```
P = 1/f

= 1/0.5

= 2 D
```

### Answer

```
+2 Dioptres
```

---

## Example 3

### Question

State the nature of the image formed by a concave lens.

### Solution

A concave lens always diverges light.

### Answer

**Virtual, erect, and diminished.**

---

## Example 4

### Question

Write the lens formula.

### Solution

The relation connects focal length, object distance, and image distance.

### Answer

```
1/f = 1/v − 1/u
```

---

## Example 5

### Question

Where is the image formed when an object is placed at **2F** of a convex lens?

### Solution

The image forms symmetrically on the opposite side.

### Answer

At **2F** on the other side; the image is **real, inverted, and of the same size**.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **convex** and **concave** lenses.
- Forgetting the **Cartesian sign convention** while solving numerical problems.
- Using focal length in **centimetres** instead of **metres** while calculating power.
- Assuming a concave lens can form a real image.
- Mixing up **magnification** and **power**.
- Drawing incorrect ray diagrams.
- Forgetting that a convex lens forms a **virtual image only when the object is placed between the optical centre and the focus**.

# Quick Revision

- Convex Lens → Converging lens.
- Concave Lens → Diverging lens.
- Lens Formula → **1/f = 1/v − 1/u**.
- Magnification → **m = v/u = hᵢ/h₀**.
- Power → **P = 1/f**.
- Unit of Power → **Dioptre (D)**.
- Convex Lens → Positive power.
- Concave Lens → Negative power.
- Concave Lens → Always virtual, erect, diminished image.
- Convex Lens → Can form both real and virtual images.

# Chapter Summary

- A **lens** is a transparent optical medium that refracts light to form images and is classified as either a **convex (converging)** or **concave (diverging)** lens.
- A **convex lens** can produce both **real and virtual images** depending on the position of the object, whereas a **concave lens** always forms a **virtual, erect, and diminished image**.
- Image formation is studied using standard **ray diagrams**, based on rays passing through the **principal focus** and **optical centre**.
- The **Lens Formula (1/f = 1/v − 1/u)** relates the object distance, image distance, and focal length using the Cartesian sign convention.
- **Magnification (m = v/u)** indicates whether the image is enlarged or diminished, while the **Power of a Lens (P = 1/f)** measures its converging or diverging ability in **dioptres**.
- Lenses are essential components of **spectacles, cameras, microscopes, telescopes, binoculars, projectors, and medical optical instruments**, making them indispensable in science and technology.
