# Introduction

The **cell** is the **basic structural, functional, and biological unit of life**. Every living organism is composed of one or more cells, and all life processes such as growth, metabolism, reproduction, and inheritance occur within cells.

The discovery of the cell revolutionized biology and led to the formulation of the **Cell Theory**, which states that all living organisms are composed of cells and that cells arise only from pre-existing cells.

This chapter explains the structure and functions of **prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells**, the organization of cell organelles, the endomembrane system, the cytoskeleton, and the nucleus.

# A. Prokaryotic Cells

## Characteristics

- Primitive cells
- No true nucleus
- No membrane-bound organelles
- Circular DNA
- Small (1–10 μm)
- Ribosomes: 70S

---

## Examples

- Bacteria
- Cyanobacteria
- Mycoplasma

---

# B. Eukaryotic Cells

## Characteristics

- True nucleus
- Membrane-bound organelles
- Linear chromosomes
- Larger cells
- Ribosomes: 80S

---

## Examples

- Plants
- Animals
- Fungi
- Protists

---

# Difference Between Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

| Prokaryotic                  | Eukaryotic           |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------- |
| No true nucleus              | True nucleus present |
| No membrane-bound organelles | Organelles present   |
| Circular DNA                 | Linear chromosomes   |
| 70S ribosomes                | 80S ribosomes        |
| Small                        | Larger               |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Who proposed the Fluid Mosaic Model of the plasma membrane?

### Solution

The Fluid Mosaic Model was proposed by **Singer and Nicolson** in 1972.

### Answer

Singer and Nicolson.

---

## Example 2

### Question

Why are mitochondria called the powerhouse of the cell?

### Solution

They produce ATP through cellular respiration, supplying energy for cellular activities.

### Answer

Because they generate ATP.

---

## Example 3

### Question

Name the organelle responsible for protein synthesis.

### Solution

Ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis. Rough ER also participates because ribosomes are attached to it.

### Answer

Ribosomes.

---

## Example 4

### Question

Differentiate between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.

### Solution

Prokaryotic cells lack a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, whereas eukaryotic cells possess both.

### Answer

Prokaryotic: No true nucleus; Eukaryotic: True nucleus present.

---

## Example 5

### Question

What is the function of the nucleolus?

### Solution

The nucleolus synthesizes ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and assembles ribosomal subunits.

### Answer

Synthesis of rRNA and ribosome formation.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **70S** and **80S** ribosomes.
- Assuming lysosomes are present in all mature plant cells.
- Mixing up the functions of RER and SER.
- Confusing mitochondria with chloroplasts.
- Forgetting that centrioles are generally absent in higher plant cells.
- Mixing up chromosome types based on centromere position.

# Chapter Summary

- The **cell** is the fundamental unit of life, and the **Cell Theory** establishes that all living organisms are composed of cells arising from pre-existing cells.
- Cells are broadly classified into **prokaryotic** and **eukaryotic** types based on the presence or absence of a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
- The **plasma membrane**, explained by the **Fluid Mosaic Model**, regulates the movement of substances into and out of the cell.
- The **endomembrane system**—comprising the **endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, and vacuoles**—coordinates synthesis, modification, transport, and storage of cellular materials.
- **Mitochondria** and **chloroplasts** are double-membrane organelles possessing their own DNA and ribosomes, reflecting their semi-autonomous nature.
- The **nucleus** houses genetic material and controls cellular activities, while the **cytoskeleton** maintains cell shape, movement, and intracellular organization, making the cell a highly coordinated and efficient unit of life.
