# Introduction

Heat is a form of energy that flows from a hotter body to a colder body due to a temperature difference. It plays an essential role in daily life, weather, industries, and biological processes. This chapter explains the concepts of heat, temperature, thermal expansion, anomalous expansion of water, Hope's Experiment, and the law of conservation of energy.

---

# 1. Temperature and Heat

## Heat

Heat is a form of energy transferred from a hotter object to a colder object.

### SI Unit

```
joule (J)
```

Other Unit

```
calorie (cal)
```

### Conversion

```
1 calorie = 4.186 joules
```

---

## Temperature

Temperature is the measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of a body.

### SI Unit

```
kelvin (K)
```

Common Units

- Celsius (°C)
- Fahrenheit (°F)

---

## Difference Between Heat and Temperature

| Heat                   | Temperature                    |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Form of energy         | Measure of hotness or coldness |
| Measured in Joule      | Measured in Kelvin or Celsius  |
| Flows from hot to cold | Does not flow                  |
| Depends on mass        | Independent of mass            |

---

## Modes of Heat Transfer

### 1. Conduction

Heat transfer without the movement of particles.

Examples

- Heating one end of a metal rod.
- Ironing clothes.

Best Conductors

- Silver
- Copper
- Aluminium

Poor Conductors (Insulators)

- Wood
- Plastic
- Rubber
- Air

---

### 2. Convection

Heat transfer through the movement of fluids (liquids and gases).

Examples

- Boiling water.
- Sea breeze.
- Land breeze.
- Chimneys.

---

### 3. Radiation

Heat transfer without any material medium.

Examples

- Heat from the Sun.
- Campfire warmth.
- Electric heater.

Characteristics

- Travels through vacuum.
- Moves in straight lines.
- Travels at the speed of light.

---

# 2. Thermal Expansion

Thermal expansion is the increase in the dimensions of a substance when heated.

When heated:

- Particles move faster.
- Intermolecular spaces increase.
- The body expands.

---

## Types of Thermal Expansion

### Linear Expansion

Increase in the length of a solid.

Example

- Railway tracks.
- Metal rods.

---

### Superficial Expansion

Increase in the surface area of a solid.

Example

- Metal plates.

---

### Cubical Expansion

Increase in the volume of a solid.

Example

- Steel balls.
- Metal blocks.

---

## Expansion of Liquids

Liquids expand only in volume.

Examples

- Mercury in thermometers.
- Petrol tanks are not filled completely.

---

## Expansion of Gases

Gases expand more than solids and liquids because intermolecular spaces are larger.

---

## Applications of Thermal Expansion

- Expansion joints in bridges.
- Gaps between railway tracks.
- Electric transmission wires are left slightly loose.
- Hot air balloons.
- Bimetallic strips in thermostats.

---

# 3. Anomalous Expansion of Water

Normally, substances expand on heating and contract on cooling.

Water behaves differently between **0°C and 4°C**.

---

## Behaviour of Water

### From 0°C to 4°C

- Water contracts.
- Density increases.

### At 4°C

- Density becomes maximum.
- Volume becomes minimum.

### Above 4°C

- Water expands normally.

---

## Importance

- Lakes freeze only at the surface.
- Aquatic animals survive in winter.
- Water below remains at approximately 4°C.

---

# 4. Hope's Experiment

Hope's Experiment demonstrates the anomalous expansion of water.

---

## Apparatus

- Tall cylindrical vessel.
- Water.
- Freezing mixture.
- Thermometers.

---

## Procedure

1. Fill the cylinder with water.
2. Surround the middle portion with a freezing mixture.
3. Record temperatures at the top and bottom.
4. Observe the temperature changes.

---

## Observation

- Water at **4°C** sinks because it is denser.
- Water below **4°C** rises because it becomes less dense.

---

## Conclusion

Water has maximum density at **4°C**, proving its anomalous expansion.

# 5. Energy and Energy Conversion

## Energy

Energy is the capacity to do work.

### SI Unit

```
joule (J)
```

---

## Forms of Energy

### Mechanical Energy

- Kinetic Energy
- Potential Energy

---

### Heat Energy

Energy due to the motion of molecules.

---

### Chemical Energy

Stored in fuels and food.

Examples

- Coal
- Petrol
- Batteries

---

### Electrical Energy

Energy due to moving electric charges.

Examples

- Fans
- Bulbs
- Motors

---

### Light Energy

Produced by luminous objects.

Examples

- Sun
- Bulbs
- LEDs

---

### Sound Energy

Produced by vibrating objects.

Examples

- Musical instruments.
- Loudspeakers.

---

### Nuclear Energy

Produced during nuclear reactions.

Examples

- Nuclear power plants.
- Atomic bombs.

---

# Energy Conversion

Energy can change from one form to another.

Examples

| Device        | Energy Conversion            |
| ------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Electric Bulb | Electrical → Light + Heat    |
| Electric Fan  | Electrical → Mechanical      |
| Generator     | Mechanical → Electrical      |
| Solar Cell    | Light → Electrical           |
| Battery       | Chemical → Electrical        |
| Car Engine    | Chemical → Mechanical + Heat |

---

# Law of Conservation of Energy

## Statement

> **Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It can only be transformed from one form into another.**

---

## Examples

- Hydroelectric power stations.
- Solar panels.
- Wind turbines.
- Pendulum motion.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing heat with temperature.
- Assuming all liquids behave like water near 0°C.
- Forgetting that water has maximum density at **4°C**.
- Confusing conduction with convection.
- Believing radiation requires a material medium.
- Ignoring SI units during numerical problems.
- Assuming energy can be destroyed.
- Forgetting the conversion factor between calories and joules.

# Chapter Summary

- **Heat** is a form of energy transferred due to a temperature difference.
- **Temperature** measures the degree of hotness or coldness of a body.
- Heat is transferred by **conduction**, **convection**, and **radiation**.
- Solids, liquids, and gases expand when heated due to increased molecular motion.
- Water shows **anomalous expansion** between **0°C and 4°C**, attaining maximum density at **4°C**.
- **Hope's Experiment** demonstrates the anomalous expansion of water.
- **Energy** exists in many forms, including mechanical, heat, electrical, chemical, light, sound, and nuclear energy.
- Energy can be converted from one form to another but cannot be created or destroyed, as stated by the **Law of Conservation of Energy**.
- Understanding heat transfer and energy conversion is essential in everyday life, engineering, and environmental science.
