# Introduction

**Electrostatics** is the branch of physics that deals with:

- Electric charges at rest
- Electric fields
- Electric potential
- Capacitance

---

# 1. Electric Charge

## Definition

Electric charge is a fundamental property of matter responsible for electrical interactions.

---

# Types of Charges

```
Electric Charge

      │

 ┌────┴────┐

 ▼         ▼

Positive  Negative
Charge    Charge
```

---

# Properties of Electric Charge

## 1. Additivity of Charge

Total charge:

\[
q=q_1+q_2+q_3+...
\]

---

## 2. Conservation of Charge

Charge can neither be created nor destroyed.

It can only be transferred.

---

## 3. Quantisation of Charge

Charge exists in discrete packets.

Formula:

\[
q=ne
\]

Where:

- n = Integer
- e = Charge of electron

\[
e=1.6\times10^{-19}C
\]

---

# 2. Coulomb's Law

## Statement

The force of attraction or repulsion between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of their charges and inversely proportional to the square of distance between them.

---

Formula:

\[
F=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\frac{q_1q_2}{r^2}
\]

---

Where:

- F = Electrostatic force
- q₁, q₂ = Charges
- r = Distance between charges
- ε₀ = Permittivity of free space

---

# In Vacuum:

\[
\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}=9\times10^9 Nm^2/C^2
\]

---

# Nature of Force

Like charges:

```
Repulsion
```

Unlike charges:

```
Attraction
```

---

# Coulomb's Law in Vector Form

\[
\vec{F}
=

\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\frac{q_1q_2}{r^2}
\hat{r}
\]

---

# 3. Principle of Superposition

## Statement

The net force on a charge due to several charges is the vector sum of individual forces.

---

Formula:

\[
\vec{F}
=

\vec{F_1} +
\vec{F_2} +
\vec{F_3}
+...
\]

---

# 4. Electric Field

## Definition

The region around a charged particle where another charge experiences force is called electric field.

---

Electric field intensity:

\[
E=\frac{F}{q_0}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
NC^{-1}
\]

or

\[
Vm^{-1}
\]

---

# Electric Field Due to Point Charge

\[
E=
\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\frac{q}{r^2}
\]

---

Direction:

Positive charge:

```
Away from charge
```

Negative charge:

```
Towards charge
```

---

# 5. Electric Field Lines

## Properties

1. Field lines start from positive charge.
2. End at negative charge.
3. Never intersect.
4. Density represents field strength.

---

Diagram:

```
      +q

    ↗ ↑ ↖

  ←     →

    ↘ ↓ ↙
```

---

# 6. Electric Dipole

## Definition

A pair of equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance.

---

Representation:

\[
(+q)\quad\quad(-q)
\]

Distance:

\[
2a
\]

---

# Dipole Moment

Formula:

\[
p=q(2a)
\]

---

Direction:

From negative charge to positive charge.

---

Unit:

\[
Cm
\]

---

# Electric Field Due to Dipole

## On Axial Position

\[
E=
\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\frac{2p}{r^3}
\]

---

## On Equatorial Position

\[
E=
\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\frac{p}{r^3}
\]

Direction opposite to dipole moment.

---

# 7. Electric Flux

## Definition

Measure of electric field passing through a surface.

---

Formula:

\[
\phi=E.A
\]

For angle θ:

\[
\phi=EA\cos\theta
\]

---

Unit:

\[
Nm^2/C
\]

---

# 8. Gauss's Law

## Statement

The total electric flux through a closed surface is equal to the charge enclosed divided by permittivity.

---

Formula:

\[
\phi=\frac{q}{\epsilon_0}
\]

---

Mathematical form:

\[
\oint E.dA
=

\frac{q}{\epsilon_0}
\]

---

# Applications of Gauss's Law

---

# 1. Electric Field Due to Infinite Line Charge

Linear charge density:

\[
\lambda=\frac{q}{L}
\]

Electric field:

\[
E=
\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0r}
\]

---

# 2. Electric Field Due to Infinite Plane Sheet

\[
E=
\frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0}
\]

Where:

\[
\sigma=\frac{q}{A}
\]

---

# 3. Electric Field Inside a Charged Sphere

Inside conductor:

\[
E=0
\]

---

Outside:

\[
E=
\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\frac{Q}{r^2}
\]

---

# 9. Electric Potential

## Definition

Work done per unit positive charge in bringing a test charge from infinity to a point.

---

Formula:

\[
V=\frac{W}{q}
\]

---

Unit:

Volt (V)

---

# Potential Due to Point Charge

\[
V=
\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\frac{q}{r}
\]

---

# Relation Between Electric Field and Potential

\[
E=-\frac{dV}{dr}
\]

---

Meaning:

Electric field acts from higher potential to lower potential.

---

# 10. Potential Energy

## Definition

Energy possessed by charges due to their positions.

---

Two Charge System:

\[
U=
\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\frac{q_1q_2}{r}
\]

---

# Electric Potential Energy of Dipole

\[
U=-pE\cos\theta
\]

---

# 11. Conductors in Electrostatic Equilibrium

Properties:

---

## 1. Electric Field Inside Conductor

\[
E=0
\]

---

## 2. Charge Distribution

Charge resides on outer surface.

---

## 3. Potential

Potential remains constant throughout conductor.

---

# 12. Capacitance

## Definition

Ability of a conductor to store electric charge.

---

Formula:

\[
C=\frac{Q}{V}
\]

---

Unit:

Farad (F)

---

# 13. Parallel Plate Capacitor

Capacitance:

\[
C=
\frac{\epsilon_0A}{d}
\]

Where:

- A = Area of plates
- d = Distance between plates

---

# With Dielectric

\[
C=
\frac{K\epsilon_0A}{d}
\]

Where:

K = Dielectric constant

---

# 14. Combination of Capacitors

---

# Parallel Combination

Same potential difference.

Total capacitance:

\[
C=C_1+C_2+C_3
\]

---

# Series Combination

Same charge.

Formula:

\[
\frac{1}{C}
=

\frac1{C_1} +
\frac1{C_2} +
\frac1{C_3}
\]

---

# 15. Energy Stored in Capacitor

Formula:

\[
U=\frac12CV^2
\]

---

Other forms:

\[
U=\frac{Q^2}{2C}
\]

\[
U=\frac12QV
\]

---

# Energy Density

\[
u=\frac12\epsilon_0E^2
\]

---

# 16. Dielectrics

## Definition

Insulating materials placed between capacitor plates.

Examples:

- Glass
- Mica
- Plastic

---

# Polarisation

Alignment of electric dipoles inside dielectric.

---

# Effect of Dielectric

Capacitance increases:

\[
C'=KC
\]

# Board Important Questions

## 1. State Coulomb's law.

**Answer:**

The electrostatic force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of charges and inversely proportional to square of distance between them.

---

## 2. State Gauss's law.

**Answer:**

The total electric flux through a closed surface is equal to charge enclosed divided by permittivity of free space.

---

## 3. Why is electric field inside a conductor zero?

**Answer:**

If electric field existed, free electrons would move, violating electrostatic equilibrium.

---

## 4. Define capacitance.

**Answer:**

Capacitance is the ratio of charge stored to potential difference.

\[
C=\frac{Q}{V}
\]

# Chapter Summary

- **Electrostatics** deals with charges at rest and their interactions.
- Coulomb's law explains force between charges.
- Electric field describes the region of influence of charges.
- Gauss's law simplifies electric field calculations for symmetric charge distributions.
- Electric potential represents electrical energy per unit charge.
- Capacitors store electrical energy and their capacitance depends on geometry and dielectric materials.
