# Introduction

Many chemical reactions do not proceed to completion but attain a state where the **forward and backward reactions occur at equal rates**. This state is called **chemical equilibrium**. Chemical equilibrium is dynamic in nature and is governed by the **Law of Mass Action**, **Equilibrium Constants**, **Le Chatelier's Principle**, and concepts of **ionic equilibrium** such as pH, buffer solutions, and solubility product.

---

# 1. Chemical Equilibrium

## Definition

Chemical equilibrium is the **dynamic state** in which the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the backward reaction.

---

# Characteristics

- Dynamic in nature.
- Forward rate = Backward rate.
- Concentrations remain constant.
- Occurs in a closed system.
- Can be attained from either direction.

---

# Dynamic Equilibrium

```
A + B

⇌

C + D

Forward Rate

=

Backward Rate
```

---

# Difference Between Physical and Chemical Equilibrium

| Physical Equilibrium | Chemical Equilibrium    |
| -------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Physical change      | Chemical change         |
| Example: Ice ⇌ Water | N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃         |
| No new substance     | New substances involved |

---

# 2. Law of Mass Action

## Statement

At constant temperature, the rate of a chemical reaction is proportional to the product of the active masses (concentrations) of the reactants.

---

For

```
aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD
```

---

# Equilibrium Constant (Kc)

```
Kc

=

[C]^c[D]^d

──────────────

[A]^a[B]^b
```

---

# Characteristics of Kc

- Constant at fixed temperature.
- Independent of catalyst.
- Depends only on temperature.

---

# Meaning of Kc

| Value of Kc | Interpretation                      |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Kc >> 1     | Products favored                    |
| Kc << 1     | Reactants favored                   |
| Kc ≈ 1      | Both present in appreciable amounts |

---

# 3. Equilibrium Constant in Terms of Pressure (Kp)

For gaseous reactions,

```
Kp

=

(Pc)^c(Pd)^d

────────────────

(Pa)^a(Pb)^b
```

---

# Relation Between Kp and Kc

```
Kp

=

Kc(RT)^Δn
```

Where

```
Δn

=

Moles of gaseous products

−

Moles of gaseous reactants
```

---

# Difference Between Kc and Kp

| Kc                         | Kp                  |
| -------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Concentration based        | Pressure based      |
| mol L⁻¹                    | atm/bar             |
| Used for solutions & gases | Used only for gases |

---

# 4. Reaction Quotient (Qc)

## Definition

The ratio of product and reactant concentrations at any stage of the reaction.

---

# Interpretation

| Condition | Result              |
| --------- | ------------------- |
| Qc < Kc   | Forward reaction    |
| Qc > Kc   | Backward reaction   |
| Qc = Kc   | Equilibrium reached |

---

# 5. Le Chatelier's Principle

## Statement

When a system at equilibrium is disturbed by changing concentration, pressure, or temperature, it shifts in the direction that opposes the disturbance.

---

# Effect of Concentration

```
Increase Reactant

↓

Equilibrium shifts Right

↓

More Product
```

---

```
Increase Product

↓

Equilibrium shifts Left

↓

More Reactant
```

---

# Effect of Pressure

Applicable only for gaseous reactions.

Increase Pressure

↓

Shift toward fewer moles.

Decrease Pressure

↓

Shift toward greater moles.

---

# Effect of Temperature

### Exothermic Reaction

```
Heat + Reactants ⇌ Products
```

Increase temperature

↓

Shifts backward.

---

### Endothermic Reaction

Increase temperature

↓

Shifts forward.

---

# Effect of Catalyst

- No change in equilibrium position.
- Equilibrium reached faster.

---

# Effect of Inert Gas

### Constant Volume

No effect.

---

### Constant Pressure

May shift toward the side with more gaseous moles.

---

# Summary Table

| Change                             | Direction of Shift |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Increase reactant                  | Forward            |
| Increase product                   | Backward           |
| Increase pressure                  | Fewer gas moles    |
| Increase temperature (Exothermic)  | Backward           |
| Increase temperature (Endothermic) | Forward            |
| Catalyst                           | No shift           |

---

# 6. Ionic Equilibrium

## Definition

Equilibrium established between ions in solution.

---

# Strong Electrolytes

Completely ionized.

Examples

- HCl
- NaOH
- KNO₃

---

# Weak Electrolytes

Partially ionized.

Examples

- CH₃COOH
- NH₄OH

---

# Difference Between Strong and Weak Electrolytes

| Strong              | Weak               |
| ------------------- | ------------------ |
| Complete ionization | Partial ionization |
| High conductivity   | Low conductivity   |

---

# 7. Acid Dissociation Constant (Ka)

For

```
HA ⇌ H⁺ + A⁻
```

```
Ka

=

[H⁺][A⁻]

──────────

[HA]
```

---

# Base Dissociation Constant (Kb)

For

```
BOH ⇌ B⁺ + OH⁻
```

```
Kb

=

[B⁺][OH⁻]

────────────

[BOH]
```

---

# Relation Between Ka and Kb

```
Ka × Kb

=

Kw
```

---

# 8. Ostwald's Dilution Law

Applicable to

```
Weak Electrolytes
```

---

## Statement

The degree of ionization increases with dilution.

---

# Formula

```
Ka

=

Cα²

────────

1−α
```

Where

- C = Concentration
- α = Degree of ionization

---

# Limitations

- Valid only for weak electrolytes.
- Not applicable to strong electrolytes.

---

# 9. Ionic Product of Water

Water undergoes self-ionization.

```
H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻
```

---

# Ionic Product

```
Kw

=

[H⁺][OH⁻]

=

1 × 10⁻¹⁴

(at 25°C)
```

---

# Neutral Solution

```
[H⁺]

=

[OH⁻]

=

1 × 10⁻⁷ M
```

---

# 10. pH Scale

## Definition

Negative logarithm of hydrogen ion concentration.

---

# Formula

```
pH

=

−log[H⁺]
```

---

# pOH

```
pOH

=

−log[OH⁻]
```

---

# Relation

```
pH + pOH = 14
```

---

# Nature of Solution

| pH  | Nature  |
| --- | ------- |
| < 7 | Acidic  |
| = 7 | Neutral |
| > 7 | Basic   |

---

# 11. Buffer Solutions

## Definition

Solutions that resist changes in pH on addition of small amounts of acid or base.

---

# Types

### Acidic Buffer

Weak acid + Salt

Example

```
CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa
```

---

### Basic Buffer

Weak base + Salt

Example

```
NH₄OH + NH₄Cl
```

---

# Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation

### Acidic Buffer

```
pH

=

pKa

+

log

Salt

──────

Acid
```

---

### Basic Buffer

```
pOH

=

pKb

+

log

Salt

──────

Base
```

---

# Applications of Buffers

- Blood pH regulation.
- Pharmaceuticals.
- Biological systems.
- Industrial processes.

---

# Difference Between Acidic and Basic Buffers

| Acidic Buffer    | Basic Buffer     |
| ---------------- | ---------------- |
| pH < 7           | pH > 7           |
| Weak acid + Salt | Weak base + Salt |

---

# 12. Solubility Product (Ksp)

## Definition

The product of ionic concentrations of a sparingly soluble salt in a saturated solution.

---

For

```
AB ⇌ A⁺ + B⁻
```

```
Ksp

=

[A⁺][B⁻]
```

---

# Common Ion Effect

## Definition

Suppression of ionization of an electrolyte by adding another electrolyte containing a common ion.

---

# Applications

- Qualitative analysis.
- Selective precipitation.
- Purification of salts.

---

# Difference Between Kc and Ksp

| Kc                  | Ksp                     |
| ------------------- | ----------------------- |
| General equilibrium | Solubility equilibrium  |
| All reactions       | Sparingly soluble salts |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Calculate the pH of a solution having [H⁺] = 1 × 10⁻³ M.

### Solution

```
pH

= −log(10⁻³)

= 3
```

### Answer

**pH = 3**

---

## Example 2

### Question

What happens to equilibrium if pressure is increased for a gaseous reaction?

### Solution

According to Le Chatelier's principle, equilibrium shifts toward the side having fewer gaseous moles.

### Answer

**It shifts toward the side with fewer moles of gas.**

---

## Example 3

### Question

State the value of the ionic product of water at 25°C.

### Solution

```
Kw

=

1 × 10⁻¹⁴
```

### Answer

**1 × 10⁻¹⁴**

---

## Example 4

### Question

Differentiate between strong and weak electrolytes.

### Solution

Strong electrolytes ionize completely, whereas weak electrolytes ionize only partially.

### Answer

**Strong → Complete ionization; Weak → Partial ionization.**

---

## Example 5

### Question

What is the effect of a catalyst on chemical equilibrium?

### Solution

A catalyst increases the rates of both forward and backward reactions equally without changing the equilibrium position.

### Answer

**It does not change the equilibrium position but helps attain equilibrium faster.**

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Kc** with **Qc**.
- Forgetting that **Kc changes only with temperature**.
- Applying pressure effects to reactions without gaseous components.
- Mixing up **Ka** and **Kb**.
- Using natural logarithm instead of common logarithm for pH calculations.
- Assuming a catalyst changes the equilibrium constant.

---

# Exam Tips

- Memorize the expressions for **Kc, Kp, Ka, Kb, Kw, and Ksp**.
- Learn all applications of **Le Chatelier's Principle** with examples.
- Practice pH and buffer calculations regularly.
- Remember that **Kw = 1 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25°C**.
- Revise the conditions under which **Ostwald's Dilution Law** is applicable.

# Chapter Summary

- **Chemical equilibrium** is a dynamic state where the forward and backward reaction rates become equal, resulting in constant concentrations of reactants and products.
- The **Law of Mass Action** leads to the definition of equilibrium constants **Kc** and **Kp**, which describe the extent of chemical reactions.
- **Le Chatelier's Principle** predicts how equilibrium shifts in response to changes in concentration, pressure, temperature, or the addition of catalysts and inert gases.
- **Ionic equilibrium** deals with the ionization of acids, bases, and salts, introducing important concepts such as **Ka, Kb, Kw, pH, pOH, and buffer solutions**.
- The **solubility product (Ksp)** explains the behavior of sparingly soluble salts and the **common ion effect**.
- These concepts are fundamental for understanding **acid-base chemistry, industrial chemical processes, analytical chemistry, biological systems, and environmental applications**.
