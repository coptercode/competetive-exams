# Introduction

**Haloalkanes and haloarenes** are organic compounds containing one or more halogen atoms attached to carbon.

Halogens:

```
F, Cl, Br, I
```

# 2. Haloalkanes

## Definition

Compounds in which halogen atom is attached to an sp³ hybridised carbon atom of an alkyl group.

General formula:

\[
R-X
\]

Where:

- R = Alkyl group
- X = Halogen

---

# Examples

## Methyl chloride

\[
CH_3Cl
\]

## Ethyl bromide

\[
C_2H_5Br
\]

---

# 3. Haloarenes

## Definition

Compounds in which halogen atom is directly attached to an aromatic ring.

General formula:

\[
Ar-X
\]

---

# Examples

## Chlorobenzene

\[
C_6H_5Cl
\]

---

Halogen attached to primary carbon.

Example:

\[

---

## Secondary Haloalkane (2°)

Halogen attached to secondary carbon.

Example:

\[

## Tertiary Haloalkane (3°)

Halogen attached to tertiary carbon.

Example:

\[
(CH_3)_3CCl
\]

# 7. Nature of C-X Bond

The carbon-halogen bond is polar.

Reason:

Halogens are more electronegative than carbon.

---

Polarity:

\[
C^{\delta+}-X^{\delta-}
\]

---

# Bond Strength

Decreases down the group:

\[
C-F>C-Cl>C-Br>C-I
\]

---

# Reactivity Order

\[
RI>RBr>RCl>RF
\]

Reason:

C-I bond is weakest.

# 8. Preparation of Haloalkanes

---

# 1. From Alcohols

Alcohols react with halogen acids.

General reaction:

\[
ROH+HX\rightarrow RX+H_2O
\]

---

Example:

\[
C_2H_5OH+HCl
\rightarrow
C_2H_5Cl+H_2O
\]

---

# Reagents Used

- HCl + ZnCl₂
- HBr
- SOCl₂

---

# Thionyl Chloride Method

\[
ROH+SOCl_2
\rightarrow
RCl+SO_2+HCl
\]

Advantages:

- Pure alkyl chloride formed.
- By-products are gases.

---

# 2. From Alkanes

By free radical halogenation.

Example:

\[
CH_4+Cl_2
\overset{hv}{\rightarrow}
CH_3Cl+HCl
\]

---

# 3. From Alkenes

Addition of hydrogen halides.

Example:

\[
CH_2=CH_2+HBr
\rightarrow
CH_3CH_2Br
\]

# 13. SN2 Mechanism

## Definition

Bimolecular nucleophilic substitution.

---

Rate:

\[
Rate=k[RX][Nu^-]
\]

---

Features:

- One-step reaction
- Backside attack
- Inversion of configuration

---

Example:

\[
CH_3Br+OH^-
\rightarrow CH_3OH+Br^-
\]

---

# SN2 Reactivity

\[
CH_3X>1^\circ>2^\circ>3^\circ
\]

---

# 14. SN1 Mechanism

## Definition

---

Rate:

---

## Definition

Removal of HX to form alkene.

---

Example:

\[

CH_2=CH_2+HBr
\]

---

# Saytzeff Rule

Major product is the more substituted alkene.

---

# 17. Wurtz Reaction

## Definition

Coupling of alkyl halides using sodium metal.

---

Reaction:

\[
2RX+2Na
\rightarrow
R-R+2NaX
\]

---

Example:

\[
2CH_3Cl+2Na
\rightarrow
C_2H_6+2NaCl
\]

---

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. Why are haloarenes less reactive than haloalkanes?

**Answer:**

Because the C-X bond in haloarenes has partial double bond character due to resonance, making it stronger.

---

## 2. Explain SN1 reaction.

**Answer:**

SN1 reaction occurs in two steps involving carbocation formation followed by nucleophile attack. Its rate depends only on haloalkane concentration.

---

## 3. Explain SN2 reaction.

**Answer:**

SN2 reaction is a one-step bimolecular substitution involving backside attack of nucleophile with inversion of configuration.

---

## 4. State Wurtz reaction.

**Answer:**

Alkyl halides react with sodium metal in dry ether to form higher alkanes.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing SN1 and SN2 mechanisms.
- Forgetting reactivity order of alkyl halides.
- Mixing haloalkanes and haloarenes.
- Writing chlorobenzene as highly reactive.
- Forgetting environmental effects of CFCs and DDT.

# Chapter Summary

- **Haloalkanes and Haloarenes** describe organic compounds containing halogen atoms.
- Haloalkanes undergo nucleophilic substitution reactions through SN1 and SN2 mechanisms.
