# Introduction

A **force** is a push or pull acting on an object that can change its state of rest or motion, direction, or shape. Forces are responsible for various physical phenomena such as rotation, balancing, circular motion, and the stability of objects. This chapter covers the concepts of **Turning Effect of Force (Torque), Principle of Moments, Centre of Gravity, Uniform Circular Motion, and Centrifugal Force**.

---

# 1. Force

## Definition

A **Force** is a push or pull that changes or tends to change the state of rest, motion, or shape of a body.

---

## SI Unit

```
Newton (N)
```

---

## Effects of Force

- Changes the speed of a body.
- Changes the direction of motion.
- Changes the shape or size.
- Produces rotation.
- Brings a moving body to rest.

---

# 2. Turning Effect of Force (Moment of Force / Torque)

## Definition

The **Moment of Force (Torque)** is the turning effect produced by a force about a fixed point or axis.

---

## Formula

```
Torque (τ) = Force × Perpendicular Distance

τ = F × d
```

Where,

- τ = Torque (N·m)
- F = Force (N)
- d = Perpendicular distance from pivot (m)

---

## SI Unit

```
Newton metre (N·m)
```

---

## Direction of Torque

### Clockwise Moment

Produces clockwise rotation.

```
↻
```

---

### Anticlockwise Moment

Produces anticlockwise rotation.

```
↺
```

---

## Factors Affecting Torque

- Magnitude of force.
- Perpendicular distance from pivot.
- Direction of applied force.

---

## Applications

- Opening a door.
- Using a spanner.
- Steering wheel.
- Seesaw.

---

# 3. Principle of Moments

## Statement

When a body is in equilibrium,

```
Sum of Clockwise Moments

=

Sum of Anticlockwise Moments
```

---

## Mathematical Form

```
CW Moment = ACW Moment
```

or

```
F₁ × d₁ = F₂ × d₂
```

---

## Conditions for Equilibrium

- Net force = 0
- Net moment = 0

---

## Applications

- Beam balance.
- Seesaw.
- Crowbar.
- Lever systems.

---

# 4. Centre of Gravity (CG)

## Definition

The **Centre of Gravity** is the point through which the entire weight of a body appears to act.

---

## Properties

- Weight acts vertically downward through CG.
- A body balances at its CG.
- Depends on shape and mass distribution.

---

## CG of Regular Bodies

| Object      | Centre of Gravity         |
| ----------- | ------------------------- |
| Uniform Rod | Midpoint                  |
| Square      | Intersection of diagonals |
| Rectangle   | Intersection of diagonals |
| Circle      | Centre                    |
| Sphere      | Centre                    |

---

## Shifting of Centre of Gravity

The CG shifts when:

- Shape changes.
- Mass distribution changes.
- Part of the object is removed.
- External load is added.

---

## Stability of Bodies

### Stable Equilibrium

CG rises slightly and returns to its original position.

Example:

```
Cone resting on its base.
```

---

### Unstable Equilibrium

CG lowers after disturbance.

Example:

```
Cone resting on its tip.
```

---

### Neutral Equilibrium

CG remains at the same height.

Example:

```
Sphere on a horizontal surface.
```

---

# 5. Uniform Circular Motion

## Definition

Motion of an object in a circular path with **constant speed**.

---

## Characteristics

- Speed remains constant.
- Velocity continuously changes.
- Acceleration acts towards the centre.
- Requires centripetal force.

---

## Centripetal Force

### Definition

The force acting towards the centre of a circular path.

---

## Formula

```
        mv²
Fc = --------
         r
```

Where,

- Fc = Centripetal force
- m = Mass
- v = Velocity
- r = Radius

---

## Centripetal Acceleration

```
        v²
a = --------
        r
```

---

## Examples

- Satellite motion.
- Earth revolving around the Sun.
- Stone tied to a string.
- Cars moving around curved roads.

---

# 6. Centrifugal Force

## Definition

A **Centrifugal Force** is the apparent (pseudo) force experienced by an object in a rotating frame, acting away from the centre of rotation.

---

## Characteristics

- Acts outward.
- Not a real force.
- Observed only in rotating reference frames.
- Equal in magnitude to centripetal force but opposite in direction.

---

## Applications

- Centrifuge machines.
- Spin dryers.
- Washing machines.
- Banking of roads.
- Merry-go-round.

---

# Difference Between Centripetal and Centrifugal Force

| Centripetal Force            | Centrifugal Force          |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Real force                   | Pseudo force               |
| Acts towards centre          | Acts away from centre      |
| Required for circular motion | Apparent in rotating frame |
| Provided by external agent   | Experienced by observer    |

---

# Difference Between Stable and Unstable Equilibrium

| Stable Equilibrium           | Unstable Equilibrium              |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Returns to original position | Moves away from original position |
| CG rises on disturbance      | CG lowers on disturbance          |
| More stable                  | Less stable                       |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A force of **20 N** acts at a perpendicular distance of **0.5 m** from the pivot. Calculate the torque.

### Solution

Given:

```
F = 20 N

d = 0.5 m
```

Using

```
τ = F × d
```

```
τ = 20 × 0.5

= 10 N·m
```

### Answer

```
Torque = 10 N·m
```

---

## Example 2

### Question

State the Principle of Moments.

### Solution

For a body in equilibrium, clockwise and anticlockwise moments balance each other.

### Answer

```
Clockwise Moment = Anticlockwise Moment
```

---

## Example 3

### Question

Calculate the centripetal force acting on a **2 kg** body moving at **6 m/s** in a circle of radius **3 m**.

### Solution

```
m = 2 kg

v = 6 m/s

r = 3 m
```

```
Fc = mv²/r

= 2 × 36 / 3

= 24 N
```

### Answer

```
24 N
```

---

## Example 4

### Question

Where is the centre of gravity of a uniform circular disc?

### Solution

For a uniform circular disc, the weight is evenly distributed.

### Answer

```
At the centre of the disc.
```

---

## Example 5

### Question

Name one device that works on centrifugal force.

### Solution

Centrifugal force is used to separate substances of different densities.

### Answer

```
Centrifuge Machine
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **force** with **torque**.
- Forgetting that **distance must be perpendicular** in torque calculations.
- Mixing up **clockwise** and **anticlockwise** moments.
- Confusing **centripetal** and **centrifugal** forces.
- Assuming velocity remains constant in direction during circular motion.
- Forgetting that **centrifugal force is a pseudo force**.
- Ignoring SI units in numerical problems.

# Chapter Summary

- A **force** is a push or pull that can change the motion, direction, or shape of an object.
- The **turning effect of a force**, called **torque (moment of force)**, depends on the applied force and its perpendicular distance from the pivot, and is given by **τ = F × d**.
- The **Principle of Moments** states that for a body in equilibrium, the total clockwise moments equal the total anticlockwise moments.
- The **Centre of Gravity (CG)** is the point through which the entire weight of a body acts and determines the stability of objects.
- In **uniform circular motion**, an object moves with constant speed but continuously changing direction due to **centripetal force**, which acts towards the centre of the circle.
- **Centrifugal force** is an apparent outward force observed in rotating reference frames and explains the operation of devices such as centrifuges and spin dryers.
- These concepts form the foundation for understanding mechanical equilibrium, rotational motion, and many real-life engineering and scientific applications.
