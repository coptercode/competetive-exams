# Introduction

Food consumed by humans contains complex organic molecules that cannot be directly utilized by the body. These molecules are broken down into simpler, absorbable forms through the process of **digestion**. The digested nutrients are then absorbed into the blood or lymph and transported to various tissues for energy production, growth, and repair.

The human digestive system consists of:

- Alimentary Canal
- Digestive Glands

---

# 1. Human Digestive System

## Definition

The digestive system is the organ system responsible for **ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation, and egestion** of food.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Where does the digestion of proteins begin?

### Solution

Protein digestion begins in the **stomach** by the action of pepsin.

### Answer

Stomach.

---

## Example 2

### Question

Which organ produces bile?

### Solution

The liver secretes bile, which is stored in the gall bladder.

### Answer

Liver.

---

## Example 3

### Question

Name the enzyme that converts starch into maltose.

### Solution

Salivary amylase (ptyalin) initiates starch digestion in the mouth.

### Answer

Salivary amylase.

---

## Example 4

### Question

Where are fats mainly absorbed?

### Solution

Fatty acids and glycerol enter the **lacteals** present inside the intestinal villi.

### Answer

Lacteals.

---

## Example 5

### Question

State the calorific value of fats.

### Solution

Fats yield the highest amount of energy among major nutrients.

9 kcal per gram.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **digestion** with **absorption**.
- Assuming bile contains digestive enzymes.
- Mixing up pepsin, trypsin, and lipase.
- Forgetting that fats are absorbed into lacteals, not directly into blood.
- Confusing gastrin with secretin and CCK.
- Mixing up bolus and chyme.

# Chapter Summary

- The **human digestive system** consists of the alimentary canal and associated digestive glands that work together to digest and absorb nutrients.
- Digestion begins in the **mouth**, continues in the **stomach**, and is completed in the **small intestine** through the coordinated action of digestive enzymes and bile.
- The **liver** produces bile for fat emulsification, while the **pancreas** secretes enzymes that digest carbohydrates, proteins, fats, and nucleic acids.
- The **small intestine**, with its numerous villi and microvilli, is the principal site of nutrient absorption, transferring sugars and amino acids into blood and fats into lymphatic lacteals.
- Hormones such as **gastrin, secretin, and cholecystokinin (CCK)** regulate digestive secretions and ensure efficient digestion.
- Proper digestion and absorption are essential for maintaining **nutrition, energy balance, growth, tissue repair, and overall health**, making this system fundamental to human survival.
