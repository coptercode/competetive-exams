# Introduction

**Coordination compounds** are compounds in which a central metal atom or ion is bonded to surrounding molecules or ions called ligands.

---

# Example

\[
[Cu(NH_3)_4]SO_4
\]

Contains:

- Central metal ion → Cu²⁺
- Ligand → NH₃
- Coordination entity → [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺

---

# 1. Important Terms

## Coordination Entity

The charged or neutral species containing central metal and ligands.

Example:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}
\]

---

# Central Metal Atom/Ion

## Definition

The metal atom or ion to which ligands are attached.

Examples:

- Fe
- Co
- Cu
- Ni

---

# Ligands

## Definition

Ions or molecules that donate electron pairs to the central metal atom.

They act as:

```
Lewis Bases
```

---

# Examples

| Ligand   | Formula | Type    |
| -------- | ------- | ------- |
| Ammonia  | NH₃     | Neutral |
| Water    | H₂O     | Neutral |
| Chloride | Cl⁻     | Anionic |
| Cyanide  | CN⁻     | Anionic |

---

```
     ▼              ▼              ▼

```

---

# Monodentate Ligands

Donate one electron pair.

Examples:

- NH₃
- H₂O
- Cl⁻

---

# Bidentate Ligands

Donate two electron pairs.

Examples:

## Ethylenediamine (en)

\[
NH_2-CH_2-CH_2-NH_2
\]

---

## Oxalate ion

\[
C_2O_4^{2-}
\]

---

# Polydentate Ligands

Contain multiple donor atoms.

Example:

## EDTA

Hexadentate ligand.

---

# 3. Coordination Number

## Definition

Number of ligand donor atoms directly attached to central metal ion.

---

Example:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}
\]

Coordination number:

```
6
```

---

# 4. Werner’s Theory

Proposed by:

## Alfred Werner (1893)

---

# Main Postulates

## 1. Primary Valency

Corresponds to oxidation state.

- Ionisable
- Satisfied by negative ions

---

## 2. Secondary Valency

Corresponds to coordination number.

- Non-ionisable
- Satisfied by ligands

---

# Example:

\[
CoCl_3.6NH_3
\]

Structure:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_6]Cl_3
\]

---

# 5. Double Salts vs Coordination Compounds

| Double Salt                | Coordination Compound   |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Completely dissociates     | Retains complex ion     |
| Shows all ions in solution | Shows complex behaviour |

---

Example:

Mohr's salt:

\[
FeSO_4.(NH_4)_2SO_4.6H_2O
\]

# 7. Oxidation State Calculation

Example:

\[
[Fe(CN)_6]^{4-}
\]

Let Fe oxidation state = x

CN charge = -1

\[
x+6(-1)=-4
\]

\[
x=+2
\]

Oxidation state:

\[
Fe^{2+}
\]

# 8. Isomerism in Coordination Compounds

Coordination compounds show:

```
Isomerism

      │

 ┌────┴────┐

 ▼         ▼

Structural Stereoisomerism
```

---

# 9. Structural Isomerism

Different bonding arrangement.

Types:

1. Ionisation isomerism
2. Solvate isomerism
3. Linkage isomerism
4. Coordination isomerism

---

# Ionisation Isomerism

Exchange of ions inside and outside coordination sphere.

Example:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_5Br]SO_4
\]

and

\[
[Co(NH_3)_5SO_4]Br
\]

---

# Linkage Isomerism

Occurs when ambidentate ligands attach through different atoms.

Example:

NO₂⁻

Nitro:

\[
M-NO_2
\]

Nitrito:

\[
M-ONO
\]

---

# 10. Stereoisomerism

Same bonds but different arrangement.

Types:

```
Geometrical Isomerism

Optical Isomerism
```

---

# Geometrical Isomerism

Different spatial arrangement.

Example:

\[
[Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2]
\]

Shows:

- Cis form
- Trans form

---

# Optical Isomerism

Compounds exist as mirror images.

Example:

\[
[Co(en)_3]^{3+}
\]

---

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. What are ligands?

**Answer:**

Ligands are ions or molecules that donate electron pairs to the central metal ion in a coordination compound.

---

## 2. State Werner's theory.

**Answer:**

Werner proposed that metals have primary valency (oxidation state) and secondary valency (coordination number).

---

## 3. Explain coordination number.

**Answer:**

Coordination number is the number of ligand donor atoms directly attached to the central metal ion.

---

## 4. What is chelation?

**Answer:**

Formation of ring structures when polydentate ligands attach to metal ions is called chelation.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing ligand charge and oxidation state.
- Writing wrong ligand names.
- Mixing coordination number with oxidation number.
- Forgetting cis-trans arrangement.
- Confusing strong and weak field ligands.

- **Coordination Compounds** explain the chemistry of metal complexes formed by central metal ions and ligands.
