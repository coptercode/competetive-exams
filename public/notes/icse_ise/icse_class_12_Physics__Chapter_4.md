# Introduction

**Magnetism and Matter** deals with the magnetic properties of materials and the origin of magnetism.

Every magnetic material contains tiny magnetic dipoles due to:

- Orbital motion of electrons
- Spin motion of electrons

---

# 1. Magnetic Dipole

## Definition

A system consisting of two equal and opposite magnetic poles separated by a small distance is called a magnetic dipole.

---

Example:

```
        N -------- S
```

---

# Magnetic Dipole Moment

## Definition

The strength of a magnetic dipole is represented by its magnetic dipole moment.

---

Formula:

\[
M=m(2l)
\]

Where:

- m = Pole strength
- 2l = Distance between poles

---

For current loop:

\[
M=NIA
\]

Where:

- N = Number of turns
- I = Current
- A = Area of loop

---

# Unit

\[
Am^2
\]

---

# Direction of Magnetic Moment

Direction:

```
South pole → North pole
```

---

# 2. Magnetic Field Due to a Bar Magnet

A bar magnet behaves like a magnetic dipole.

---

# Magnetic Field on Axial Line

\[
B=
\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}
\frac{2M}{r^3}
\]

---

# Magnetic Field on Equatorial Line

\[
B=
\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}
\frac{M}{r^3}
\]

Direction:

Opposite to magnetic moment.

---

# 3. Torque on a Magnetic Dipole

When a magnetic dipole is placed in a magnetic field, it experiences torque.

---

Formula:

\[
\tau=MB\sin\theta
\]

---

Where:

- M = Magnetic moment
- B = Magnetic field

---

# Maximum Torque

When:

\[
\theta=90^\circ
\]

\[
\tau_{max}=MB
\]

---

# 4. Potential Energy of Magnetic Dipole

Formula:

\[
U=-MB\cos\theta
\]

---

Cases:

## Stable Equilibrium

\[
\theta=0^\circ
\]

\[
U=-MB
\]

---

## Unstable Equilibrium

\[
\theta=180^\circ
\]

\[
U=+MB
\]

---

# 5. Magnetisation

## Definition

Magnetic moment developed per unit volume of a material is called magnetisation.

---

Formula:

\[
M=\frac{Magnetic\ moment}{Volume}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
Am^{-1}
\]

---

# 6. Magnetic Intensity (H)

## Definition

The magnetic field produced by external sources is called magnetic intensity.

---

Formula:

\[
H=\frac{B}{\mu_0}-M
\]

---

Unit:

\[
Am^{-1}
\]

---

# 7. Relation Between B, H and M

\[
B=\mu_0(H+M)
\]

---

Where:

- B = Magnetic induction
- H = Magnetic intensity
- M = Magnetisation

---

# 8. Magnetic Susceptibility

## Definition

The ratio of magnetisation to magnetic intensity is called magnetic susceptibility.

---

Formula:

\[
\chi_m=\frac{M}{H}
\]

---

Properties:

- Indicates magnetic behaviour of material.
- Determines how easily material gets magnetised.

---

# 9. Magnetic Permeability

## Definition

The ability of a material to allow magnetic field lines through it.

---

Formula:

\[
\mu=\frac{B}{H}
\]

---

Relative permeability:

\[
\mu_r=\frac{\mu}{\mu_0}
\]

---

# 10. Classification of Magnetic Materials

Magnetic materials are classified into:

```
Magnetic Materials

        │

 ┌──────┼────────┐

 ▼      ▼        ▼

Diamagnetic Paramagnetic Ferromagnetic
```

---

# 11. Diamagnetic Materials

## Definition

Materials weakly repelled by magnetic fields.

---

Examples:

- Bismuth
- Copper
- Water
- Silver

---

Properties:

- No permanent magnetic moment
- Susceptibility is negative

\[
\chi_m<0
\]

---

Effect:

Move from strong field to weak field region.

---

# 12. Paramagnetic Materials

## Definition

Materials weakly attracted by magnetic fields.

---

Examples:

- Aluminium
- Oxygen
- Platinum

---

Properties:

- Have unpaired electrons
- Small positive susceptibility

\[
\chi_m>0
\]

---

# Curie's Law

For paramagnetic materials:

\[
\chi_m=\frac{C}{T}
\]

---

Where:

- C = Curie constant
- T = Temperature

---

# 13. Ferromagnetic Materials

## Definition

Materials strongly attracted by magnetic fields and can retain magnetism.

---

Examples:

- Iron
- Cobalt
- Nickel

---

Properties:

- Large positive susceptibility
- Strong magnetisation
- Formation of domains

---

# Magnetic Domains

Ferromagnetic materials contain small regions called domains.

Each domain behaves like a tiny magnet.

---

# 14. Comparison of Magnetic Materials

| Property       | Diamagnetic | Paramagnetic     | Ferromagnetic      |
| -------------- | ----------- | ---------------- | ------------------ |
| Attraction     | Repelled    | Weakly attracted | Strongly attracted |
| Susceptibility | Negative    | Small positive   | Large positive     |
| Example        | Copper      | Aluminium        | Iron               |
| Magnetisation  | Opposite    | Same direction   | Strong             |

---

# 15. Effect of Temperature

---

# Curie Temperature

## Definition

Temperature above which ferromagnetic materials become paramagnetic.

---

Example:

Iron loses ferromagnetism above Curie temperature.

---

The Earth's magnetic field is similar to that of a bar magnet.

# 17. Magnetic Declination

## Definition

The angle between geographic meridian and magnetic meridian.

---

Symbol:

\[
D
\]

---

# 18. Angle of Dip (Inclination)

## Definition

The angle made by Earth's magnetic field with horizontal direction.

---

Symbol:

\[
I
\]

---

At magnetic equator:

\[
I=0^\circ
\]

---

At magnetic poles:

\[
I=90^\circ
\]

---

# 19. Horizontal Component of Earth's Field

Formula:

\[
B_H=B\cos I
\]

---

Vertical component:

\[
B_V=B\sin I
\]

---

# 20. Hysteresis

## Definition

The lag of magnetisation behind the applied magnetic field is called hysteresis.

---

Graph:

```
        B

        |
        |      /
        |     /____
        |____/    \____ H
```

---

# 21. Hysteresis Loop

Important properties:

---

# Retentivity

## Definition

Ability of a material to retain magnetism after removing external field.

---

# Coercivity

## Definition

Reverse magnetic field required to remove residual magnetism.

---

# 22. Soft Iron and Steel

| Soft Iron              | Steel                     |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Low coercivity         | High coercivity           |
| Easily magnetised      | Difficult to magnetise    |
| Used in electromagnets | Used in permanent magnets |
| Narrow hysteresis loop | Wide hysteresis loop      |

---

# 23. Energy Loss Due to Hysteresis

Energy loss is proportional to area of hysteresis loop.

---

Applications:

- Transformer cores
- Electrical machines

---

# 24. Permanent Magnets

Materials used:

- Steel
- Alnico

Properties:

- High retentivity
- High coercivity

---

# 25. Electromagnets

Materials used:

- Soft iron

Properties:

- Low coercivity
- Easily magnetised

# Board Important Questions

## 1. Define magnetic susceptibility.

**Answer:**

Magnetic susceptibility is the ratio of magnetisation to magnetic intensity.

\[
\chi_m=\frac MH
\]

---

## 2. Explain types of magnetic materials.

**Answer:**

Magnetic materials are classified as diamagnetic, paramagnetic, and ferromagnetic based on their response to external magnetic fields.

---

## 3. What is hysteresis?

**Answer:**

The lag of magnetisation behind the applied magnetic field is called hysteresis.

---

## 4. Differentiate soft iron and steel.

| Soft Iron      | Steel             |
| -------------- | ----------------- |
| Low coercivity | High coercivity   |
| Electromagnets | Permanent magnets |

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing magnetic moment direction.
- Mixing magnetic intensity and magnetic field.
- Forgetting Curie's law.
- Confusing retentivity and coercivity.
- Mixing Earth's declination and dip.
