# Introduction

The human body requires a continuous transport system to deliver **oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and other essential substances** to cells while removing metabolic wastes. This function is performed by **blood, lymph, and the circulatory system**.

The circulatory system consists of:

- Blood
- Blood vessels
- Heart
- Lymphatic system

---

# 1. Body Fluids

## Definition

Body fluids are liquids that transport nutrients, gases, hormones, enzymes, and waste products throughout the body.

The two major body fluids are:

- Blood
- Lymph

---

# 3. Erythrocytes (RBC)

## Characteristics

- Biconcave
- No nucleus (in mammals)
- Contain haemoglobin
- Life span: 120 days
- Produced in red bone marrow

---

## Functions

- Oxygen transport
- Carbon dioxide transport
- Acid-base balance

---

# 5. Platelets (Thrombocytes)

## Characteristics

- Cell fragments
- Produced in bone marrow
- Life span: 7–10 days

---

## Functions

- Blood clotting
- Vessel repair

---

| ------------------- | ----------------- | -------------- |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Which blood group is called the universal donor?

### Solution

Individuals with **O negative** blood lack A, B, and Rh antigens, making their blood compatible with all ABO groups.

### Answer

O Negative.

---

## Example 2

### Question

Name the natural pacemaker of the heart.

### Solution

The **SA node** initiates electrical impulses that regulate heartbeat.

### Answer

Sinoatrial (SA) Node.

---

## Example 3

### Question

What is the normal blood pressure of a healthy adult?

### Solution

The average blood pressure is **120/80 mm Hg**.

### Answer

120/80 mm Hg.

---

## Example 4

### Question

Which chamber of the heart has the thickest wall?

### Solution

The **left ventricle** pumps blood to the entire body and therefore has the thickest muscular wall.

### Answer

Left Ventricle.

---

## Example 5

### Question

What is the function of platelets?

### Solution

Platelets initiate blood clotting and help prevent excessive blood loss.

### Answer

Blood clotting.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing plasma with serum.
- Mixing up arteries and veins.
- Assuming veins always carry deoxygenated blood (pulmonary vein is an exception).
- Confusing systolic and diastolic pressure.
- Mixing up P wave, QRS complex, and T wave in ECG.
- Forgetting that the SA node is the natural pacemaker.

# Chapter Summary

- **Blood** is a specialized connective tissue composed of **plasma** and **formed elements (RBCs, WBCs, and platelets)**, each performing essential transport, defense, and clotting functions.
- The **human heart** is a four-chambered muscular organ that pumps blood through a **double circulatory system**, ensuring complete separation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood.
- The **cardiac cycle** coordinates atrial and ventricular contractions, while the **SA node**, AV node, Bundle of His, and Purkinje fibres regulate the heartbeat.
- The **ECG** records the heart's electrical activity, with the **P wave, QRS complex, and T wave** representing different phases of cardiac excitation.
- **Blood pressure**, blood groups, clotting mechanisms, and lymph circulation are vital for maintaining efficient transport, immunity, and homeostasis.
- Understanding body fluids and circulation is fundamental for diagnosing and managing **cardiovascular diseases**, supporting clinical medicine, transfusion therapy, and overall human health.
