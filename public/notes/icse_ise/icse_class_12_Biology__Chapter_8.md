# Introduction

**Health** is a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease.

Human health is affected by:

- Genetic factors
- Environmental conditions
- Lifestyle
- Pathogens
- Immune system

## Definition

# 6. Innate Immunity

## Definition

Non-specific defense mechanism present from birth.

---

# Components

## 1. Physical Barriers

Examples:

- Skin
- Mucous membrane

---

## 2. Physiological Barriers

Examples:

- Acid in stomach
- Lysozyme in tears

---

## 3. Cellular Barriers

Examples:

- White blood cells
- Natural killer cells

---

## 4. Cytokine Barriers

Example:

Interferons

---

# 7. Acquired Immunity

## Definition

Specific immunity developed after exposure to pathogens.

---

# Features

- Specific
- Memory-based
- Long-lasting

---

# Types

## Humoral Immunity

Mediated by:

```
B-lymphocytes
```

Produces:

```
Antibodies
```

---

## Cell-Mediated Immunity

Mediated by:

```
T-lymphocytes
```

---

# 8. Antibodies

## Definition

Proteins produced by B-cells against antigens.

---

# Structure

Antibody:

```
       Y-shaped molecule

          Antigen binding sites
                    │
                    ▼
              Heavy chains
              Light chains
```

---

# Types of Antibodies

| Type | Function            |
| ---- | ------------------- |
| IgA  | Mucosal immunity    |
| IgG  | Main blood antibody |
| IgM  | First response      |
| IgE  | Allergy response    |
| IgD  | B-cell activation   |

---

# 9. Vaccination

## Definition

Introduction of weakened or inactive pathogens to stimulate immunity.

---

# Principle

Vaccines create:

```
Memory Cells
```

which provide protection.

---

# Examples

- BCG vaccine
- Polio vaccine
- Hepatitis vaccine

---

# Primary and Secondary Immune Response

## Primary Response

First exposure.

Slow and weak.

---

## Secondary Response

Second exposure.

Fast and strong due to memory cells.

---

# 12. Cancer

Uncontrolled growth and division of abnormal cells.

---

# Types

## Benign Tumour

- Localized
- Non-invasive

---

## Malignant Tumour

- Spreads to other tissues

Called:

```
Metastasis
```

---

# Causes of Cancer

- Genetic mutations
- Radiation
- Chemicals
- Viruses

---

# Cancer Detection

Methods:

- Biopsy
- Imaging techniques
- Blood tests

---

# Cancer Treatment

## Surgery

Removal of tumour.

---

## Radiotherapy

Uses radiation.

---

## Chemotherapy

Uses anti-cancer drugs.

---

## Immunotherapy

Uses immune system against cancer.

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. Differentiate innate and acquired immunity.

| Innate Immunity    | Acquired Immunity       |
| ------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Present from birth | Develops after exposure |
| Non-specific       | Specific                |
| No memory          | Has memory              |

---

## 2. Name the causative organism of malaria.

**Answer:**

Plasmodium

---

## 3. What are antibodies?

**Answer:**

Antibodies are immunoglobulin proteins produced by B-lymphocytes against antigens.

---

## 4. Explain AIDS transmission.

**Answer:**

AIDS spreads through infected blood, sexual contact, infected needles, and mother-to-child transmission.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing antigen and antibody.
- Writing HIV as AIDS causative disease.
- Confusing innate and acquired immunity.
- Forgetting malaria vector.
- Mixing benign and malignant tumours.

# Chapter Summary

- **Human Health and Diseases** explains how diseases affect the human body and how defence mechanisms protect us.
- Infectious diseases are caused by pathogens such as bacteria, viruses, protozoans, fungi, and worms.
- Immunity includes innate and acquired defence systems.
- Vaccination strengthens immune memory and prevents diseases.
- Cancer results from uncontrolled cell division and requires early diagnosis.
- Awareness and healthy lifestyle choices help prevent diseases and substance abuse.
