# Introduction

**Electromagnetism** is the branch of physics that studies the relationship between electricity and magnetism. When an electric current flows through a conductor, it produces a magnetic field. This principle is used in **electromagnets, electric bells, relays, electric motors, generators, transformers, and various electrical devices**. This chapter covers **magnetic fields, electromagnets, electromagnetic induction, electric motor, electric generator, and domestic applications**.

---

# 1. Magnetism

## Definition

**Magnetism** is the property by which certain materials attract iron, nickel, cobalt, and their alloys.

---

## Properties of Magnets

- A magnet has two poles:
  - North Pole (N)
  - South Pole (S)
- Like poles repel.
- Unlike poles attract.
- Magnetic poles always exist in pairs.

---

# 2. Magnetic Field

## Definition

A **magnetic field** is the region around a magnet or a current-carrying conductor where a magnetic force can be experienced.

---

## Magnetic Field Lines

### Characteristics

- Originate from the North Pole.
- Terminate at the South Pole.
- Never intersect each other.
- Closer lines indicate a stronger magnetic field.

---

## Representation

```
N  →→→→→→→→→→  S
```

---

# 3. Magnetic Field Around a Current-Carrying Conductor

When electric current flows through a straight conductor, concentric circular magnetic field lines are produced around it.

---

## Right-Hand Thumb Rule

### Statement

If the right-hand thumb points in the direction of the current, the curled fingers indicate the direction of the magnetic field.

---

## Diagram

```
        ↑ Current

        │

     ⟳ ⟳ ⟳

(Magnetic Field)
```

# 4. Electromagnet

## Definition

An **electromagnet** is a temporary magnet produced by passing electric current through a coil wound around a soft iron core.

---

## Construction

- Soft iron core
- Insulated copper wire
- Battery
- Switch

---

## Working

When current flows through the coil:

```
Electric Current

↓

Magnetic Field

↓

Soft Iron Becomes Magnetized
```

When the current is switched off, the magnetism disappears.

---

## Factors Affecting Strength

- Number of turns in the coil.
- Magnitude of current.
- Nature of the core.
- Length of the coil.

---

## Applications

- Electric bells.
- Relays.
- Cranes for lifting scrap iron.
- Magnetic locks.
- Loudspeakers.

---

# Difference Between Permanent Magnet and Electromagnet

| Permanent Magnet       | Electromagnet       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------- |
| Permanent magnetism    | Temporary magnetism |
| Cannot be switched off | Can be switched off |
| Made of hard steel     | Made of soft iron   |
| Fixed strength         | Adjustable strength |

---

# 5. Electromagnetic Induction

## Definition

**Electromagnetic Induction** is the phenomenon of producing an induced current in a conductor when the magnetic field linked with it changes.

---

## Discoverer

```
Michael Faraday
```

---

## Methods of Producing Induced Current

- Moving a magnet towards a coil.
- Moving a coil in a magnetic field.
- Rotating the coil.
- Changing the magnetic field.

---

## Factors Affecting Induced EMF

- Strength of magnetic field.
- Speed of motion.
- Number of turns in the coil.

---

# 6. Electric Bell

## Principle

Works on the principle of an **electromagnet**.

---

## Main Parts

- Electromagnet
- Soft iron armature
- Hammer
- Gong
- Contact screw
- Spring
- Battery

---

## Working

```
Switch ON

↓

Current flows

↓

Electromagnet attracts armature

↓

Hammer strikes gong

↓

Circuit breaks

↓

Armature returns

↓

Cycle repeats
```

---

## Applications

- Doorbells.
- Alarm systems.
- School bells.

---

# 7. Electric Motor

## Definition

An **electric motor** converts **electrical energy into mechanical energy**.

---

## Principle

A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force.

---

## Main Parts

- Armature (Coil)
- Split-ring commutator
- Carbon brushes
- Magnet
- DC supply

---

## Fleming's Left-Hand Rule

### Statement

If the thumb, forefinger, and middle finger of the left hand are held mutually perpendicular:

- Forefinger → Magnetic field
- Middle finger → Current
- Thumb → Force (Motion)

---

## Applications

- Electric fans.
- Mixers.
- Washing machines.
- Water pumps.
- Electric vehicles.

---

# 8. Electric Generator (Dynamo)

## Definition

An **electric generator** converts **mechanical energy into electrical energy**.

---

## Principle

Works on **electromagnetic induction**.

---

## Main Parts

- Armature
- Slip rings
- Carbon brushes
- Magnet
- Shaft

---

## Fleming's Right-Hand Rule

### Statement

If the thumb, forefinger, and middle finger of the right hand are mutually perpendicular:

- Thumb → Motion
- Forefinger → Magnetic field
- Middle finger → Induced current

---

## Applications

- Hydroelectric power stations.
- Wind turbines.
- Thermal power plants.
- Diesel generators.

---

# Difference Between Motor and Generator

| Electric Motor                | Electric Generator             |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Electrical → Mechanical       | Mechanical → Electrical        |
| Uses electrical energy        | Produces electrical energy     |
| Uses Fleming's Left-Hand Rule | Uses Fleming's Right-Hand Rule |
| Split-ring commutator         | Slip rings                     |

---

# Difference Between Electromagnet and Permanent Magnet

| Electromagnet          | Permanent Magnet    |
| ---------------------- | ------------------- |
| Temporary              | Permanent           |
| Requires current       | No current required |
| Soft iron core         | Hard steel          |
| Strength can be varied | Fixed strength      |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Define an electromagnet.

### Solution

An electromagnet becomes magnetic only when current flows.

### Answer

An electromagnet is a temporary magnet produced by passing electric current through a coil wound around a soft iron core.

---

## Example 2

### Question

State the Right-Hand Thumb Rule.

### Solution

The rule gives the direction of the magnetic field around a current-carrying conductor.

### Answer

If the right-hand thumb points in the direction of the current, the curled fingers indicate the direction of the magnetic field.

---

## Example 3

### Question

Which rule is used to determine the direction of motion in an electric motor?

### Solution

The motor works on the force experienced by a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field.

### Answer

**Fleming's Left-Hand Rule.**

---

## Example 4

### Question

What is electromagnetic induction?

### Solution

Changing the magnetic field linked with a conductor induces current.

### Answer

Electromagnetic induction is the production of an induced current in a conductor due to a change in the magnetic field linked with it.

---

## Example 5

### Question

Differentiate between an electric motor and an electric generator.

### Solution

A motor uses electrical energy to produce motion, whereas a generator uses motion to produce electricity.

### Answer

- **Electric Motor:** Converts electrical energy into mechanical energy.
- **Electric Generator:** Converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **electromagnets** with **permanent magnets**.
- Mixing up **Fleming's Left-Hand Rule** and **Fleming's Right-Hand Rule**.
- Assuming an electromagnet remains magnetic after the current is switched off.
- Confusing the functions of an **electric motor** and an **electric generator**.
- Forgetting that **soft iron** is used in electromagnets due to its temporary magnetism.
- Drawing incorrect directions for magnetic field lines.

# Chapter Summary

- **Electromagnetism** explains the relationship between electric current and magnetic fields.
- A **current-carrying conductor** produces a magnetic field, whose direction is determined using the **Right-Hand Thumb Rule**.
- An **electromagnet** is a temporary magnet formed by passing current through a coil wound around a **soft iron core**, and its strength depends on factors such as the current and number of turns.
- **Electromagnetic induction**, discovered by **Michael Faraday**, is the process of generating an induced current by changing the magnetic field linked with a conductor.
- An **electric motor** converts **electrical energy into mechanical energy** using **Fleming's Left-Hand Rule**, while an **electric generator** converts **mechanical energy into electrical energy** based on **Fleming's Right-Hand Rule** and the principle of electromagnetic induction.
- Electromagnetic principles are widely used in **electric bells, motors, generators, transformers, cranes, loudspeakers, medical equipment, and power generation systems**, making them fundamental to modern electrical engineering.
