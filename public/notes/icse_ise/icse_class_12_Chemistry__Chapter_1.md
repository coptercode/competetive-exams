# Introduction

A **solution** is a homogeneous mixture of two or more components.

A solution consists of:

```
Solution

   │

 ┌─┴─────────┐

 ▼           ▼

Solvent    Solute
```

---

# 1. Components of Solution

## Solvent

The component present in larger quantity that dissolves another substance.

Example:

In salt water:

```
Water → Solvent
```

---

## Solute

The component present in smaller quantity that gets dissolved.

Example:

```
Salt → Solute
```

---

# 2. Types of Solutions

Based on physical state:

| Solute | Solvent | Example          |
| ------ | ------- | ---------------- |
| Gas    | Gas     | Air              |
| Gas    | Liquid  | CO₂ in water     |
| Liquid | Liquid  | Alcohol in water |
| Solid  | Liquid  | Salt in water    |
| Solid  | Solid   | Alloys           |

---

# 3. Concentration of Solutions

Concentration represents the amount of solute present in a given amount of solution.

---

# 1. Mass Percentage

## Definition

Mass of solute present in 100 g of solution.

Formula:

\[
Mass\% =
\frac{Mass\ of\ solute}{Mass\ of\ solution}
\times100
\]

---

Example:

10 g salt + 90 g water

Mass of solution:

= 100 g

Mass percentage:

\[
=\frac{10}{100}\times100
\]

= 10%

---

# 2. Volume Percentage

## Definition

Volume of solute present in 100 mL solution.

Formula:

\[
Volume\%=
\frac{Volume\ of\ solute}{Volume\ of\ solution}
\times100
\]

---

Example:

20 mL alcohol in 100 mL solution:

Volume %

= 20%

---

# 3. Mass by Volume Percentage

Formula:

\[
Mass/Volume\%=
\frac{Mass\ of\ solute}{Volume\ of\ solution}
\times100
\]

---

Example:

5 g glucose in 100 mL solution

= 5% (w/v)

---

# 4. Parts Per Million (ppm)

Used for very dilute solutions.

Formula:

\[
ppm=
\frac{Number\ of\ parts\ of\ component}
{Total\ parts\ of\ solution}
\times10^6
\]

---

Applications:

- Pollution analysis
- Water quality testing

---

# 5. Mole Fraction

## Definition

Ratio of number of moles of a component to total moles of all components.

Formula:

\[
X_A=
\frac{n_A}{n_A+n_B}
\]

---

For binary solution:

\[
X_A+X_B=1
\]

---

# 6. Molarity (M)

## Definition

Number of moles of solute present in one litre of solution.

Formula:

\[
M=
\frac{Number\ of\ moles\ of\ solute}
{Volume\ of\ solution\ in\ litre}
\]

---

Unit:

```
mol L⁻¹
```

---

# 7. Molality (m)

## Definition

Number of moles of solute present in one kilogram of solvent.

Formula:

\[
m=
\frac{Moles\ of\ solute}
{Mass\ of\ solvent\ in\ kg}
\]

---

Unit:

```
mol kg⁻¹
```

---

# Difference Between Molarity and Molality

| Molarity                    | Molality                 |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Based on volume of solution | Based on mass of solvent |
| Temperature dependent       | Temperature independent  |
| Unit: mol/L                 | Unit: mol/kg             |

---

# 4. Solubility

## Definition

Maximum amount of solute that dissolves in a given amount of solvent at a specific temperature.

---

# Factors Affecting Solubility

## 1. Nature of Solute and Solvent

"Like dissolves like"

Polar solutes dissolve in polar solvents.

---

## 2. Temperature

For solids:

Solubility usually increases with temperature.

---

For gases:

Solubility decreases with temperature.

---

## 3. Pressure

Mainly affects gases.

Higher pressure:

→ Higher gas solubility

---

# 5. Henry's Law

## Statement

At constant temperature, solubility of a gas in liquid is directly proportional to partial pressure of gas.

---

Formula:

\[
p=K_Hx
\]

Where:

- p = Partial pressure of gas
- KH = Henry's constant
- x = Mole fraction of dissolved gas

---

# Applications

## 1. Carbonated Drinks

CO₂ is dissolved under high pressure.

---

## 2. Scuba Diving

Controls nitrogen dissolution in blood.

---

## 3. High Altitude Problems

Reduced oxygen solubility causes altitude sickness.

---

# 6. Vapour Pressure of Liquid Solutions

The pressure exerted by vapour molecules above a liquid surface is called vapour pressure.

---

# Raoult's Law

## Statement

The partial vapour pressure of any volatile component is directly proportional to its mole fraction in solution.

---

Formula:

\[
P_A=P_A^0X_A
\]

Where:

- PA = Partial vapour pressure
- PA° = Vapour pressure of pure component
- XA = Mole fraction

---

For binary solution:

\[
P_{total}=P_A+P_B
\]

---

# 7. Ideal Solutions

A solution that obeys Raoult's law at all concentrations.

---

# Characteristics

- ΔHmix = 0
- ΔVmix = 0
- Similar intermolecular forces

---

Examples:

- Benzene + Toluene

---

# 8. Non-Ideal Solutions

Solutions that do not obey Raoult's law.

---

Types:

```
Non-Ideal Solution

        │

 ┌──────┴──────┐

 ▼             ▼

Positive     Negative
Deviation    Deviation
```

---

# Positive Deviation

Vapour pressure is higher than expected.

Cause:

Weak attraction between molecules.

Example:

Ethanol + Acetone

---

# Negative Deviation

Vapour pressure is lower than expected.

Cause:

Strong intermolecular attraction.

Example:

Chloroform + Acetone

---

# 9. Azeotropes

## Definition

Mixtures that boil at constant temperature with same composition in liquid and vapour phases.

---

# Types

## 1. Minimum Boiling Azeotrope

Shows positive deviation.

Example:

Ethanol-water mixture

---

## 2. Maximum Boiling Azeotrope

Shows negative deviation.

Example:

Nitric acid-water mixture

---

# 10. Colligative Properties

## Definition

Properties that depend only on number of solute particles, not their nature.

---

# Four Colligative Properties

```
Colligative Properties

        │

 ┌──────┼──────┐

 ▼      ▼      ▼

Relative   Elevation   Depression

Vapour     of Boiling  of Freezing

Pressure   Point       Point

        │

        ▼

 Osmotic Pressure
```

---

# 1. Relative Lowering of Vapour Pressure

Formula:

\[
\frac{P^0-P}{P^0}=X_B
\]

---

# 2. Elevation in Boiling Point

Formula:

\[
\Delta T_b=K_bm
\]

Where:

- Kb = Molal elevation constant
- m = Molality

---

# 3. Depression in Freezing Point

Formula:

\[
\Delta T_f=K_fm
\]

Where:

- Kf = Molal depression constant

---

# 4. Osmotic Pressure

## Definition

Pressure required to stop osmosis.

---

Formula:

\[
\pi=CRT
\]

Where:

- C = Molar concentration
- R = Gas constant
- T = Temperature

---

# 11. Osmosis

## Definition

Movement of solvent molecules through a semipermeable membrane from dilute solution to concentrated solution.

---

# Types

## Reverse Osmosis

Pressure applied greater than osmotic pressure.

Applications:

- Water purification
- Desalination

---

# 12. Van't Hoff Factor (i)

## Definition

Correction factor used for abnormal colligative properties.

---

Formula:

\[
i=
\frac{Observed\ colligative\ property}
{Calculated\ colligative\ property}
\]

---

# For Dissociation

\[
i>1
\]

Example:

NaCl → Na⁺ + Cl⁻

---

# For Association

\[
i<1
\]

Example:

Acetic acid dimer formation

---

# Modified Equations

## Elevation in boiling point:

\[
\Delta T_b=iK_bm
\]

---

## Depression in freezing point:

\[
\Delta T_f=iK_fm
\]

---

## Osmotic pressure:

\[
\pi=iCRT
\]

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. State Henry's law.

**Answer:**

At constant temperature, the solubility of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of the gas.

\[
p=K_Hx
\]

---

## 2. Differentiate ideal and non-ideal solutions.

| Ideal Solution     | Non-Ideal Solution         |
| ------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Obeys Raoult's law | Deviates from Raoult's law |
| ΔHmix = 0          | ΔHmix ≠ 0                  |

---

## 3. What are colligative properties?

**Answer:**

Properties depending only on the number of solute particles present in solution are called colligative properties.

---

## 4. Why is molality preferred over molarity?

**Answer:**

Molality does not change with temperature because it depends on mass, while molarity depends on volume.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing molarity and molality.
- Forgetting mole fraction sum equals 1.
- Mixing positive and negative deviation.
- Using wrong units in calculations.
- Forgetting Van't Hoff factor for association/dissociation.
