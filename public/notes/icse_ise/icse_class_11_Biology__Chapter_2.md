# Introduction

The enormous diversity of living organisms makes their study difficult without proper classification. **Biological Classification** is the scientific arrangement of organisms into groups based on similarities and differences in their structure, nutrition, reproduction, and evolutionary relationships.

One of the most widely accepted systems is **R.H. Whittaker's Five-Kingdom Classification (1969)**, which classifies all living organisms into **Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia**.

This chapter also introduces **Viruses, Viroids, Prions, and Lichens**, which do not fit perfectly into the five-kingdom system.

---

# 1. Need for Biological Classification

Classification helps to

- Identify organisms correctly.
- Study biodiversity systematically.
- Understand evolutionary relationships.
- Avoid confusion due to local names.
- Predict characteristics of unknown organisms.

---

# 2. Basis of Classification

Whittaker classified organisms using the following criteria:

- Cell structure
- Cell organization
- Mode of nutrition
- Reproduction
- Evolutionary relationships (Phylogeny)

---

# Comparison of Five Kingdoms

| Kingdom  | Cell Type   | Cell Number          | Cell Wall                             | Nutrition          |
| -------- | ----------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Monera   | Prokaryotic | Unicellular          | Present (Peptidoglycan in eubacteria) | Auto/Heterotrophic |
| Protista | Eukaryotic  | Mostly Unicellular   | Present or Absent                     | Auto/Heterotrophic |
| Fungi    | Eukaryotic  | Mostly Multicellular | Chitin                                | Heterotrophic      |
| Plantae  | Eukaryotic  | Multicellular        | Cellulose                             | Autotrophic        |

# 4. Kingdom Monera

## General Characteristics

- Smallest organisms.
- Prokaryotic.
- Unicellular.
- No true nucleus.
- No membrane-bound organelles.
- Reproduce by binary fission.
- Cell wall generally made of peptidoglycan (except Mycoplasma).

---

## Types of Monera

### Archaebacteria

Primitive bacteria found in extreme environments.

---

### Characteristics

- Cell wall lacks peptidoglycan.
- Unique membrane lipids.
- Can survive extreme conditions.

---

### Types

#### Methanogens

Produce methane gas.

Example

_Methanobacterium_

---

#### Halophiles

Live in highly salty environments.

---

#### Thermoacidophiles

Live in hot acidic springs.

---

## Eubacteria (True Bacteria)

### Characteristics

- Most common bacteria.
- Cell wall contains peptidoglycan.
- May possess flagella.
- Reproduce by binary fission.

---

### Types Based on Nutrition

#### Autotrophic

Produce their own food.

- Photosynthetic bacteria
- Chemosynthetic bacteria

---

#### Heterotrophic

Depend on organic food.

Examples

- Lactobacillus
- Escherichia coli

---

## Cyanobacteria (Blue-Green Algae)

Characteristics

- Photosynthetic.
- Contain chlorophyll-a.
- Fix atmospheric nitrogen.

Example

- Nostoc
- Anabaena

---

## Mycoplasma

Smallest living cells.

Characteristics

- No cell wall.
- Pleomorphic (variable shape).
- Resistant to many antibiotics.

---

# 5. Kingdom Protista

## General Characteristics

- Eukaryotic.
- Mostly unicellular.
- Aquatic habitat.
- Locomotion by cilia, flagella, or pseudopodia.
- Both autotrophic and heterotrophic nutrition.

---

## Groups of Protists

### Chrysophytes

Examples

- Diatoms
- Desmids

Characteristics

- Cell wall made of silica.
- Major producers in aquatic ecosystems.
- Diatomaceous earth formed from dead diatoms.

---

### Dinoflagellates

Characteristics

- Two flagella.
- Cellulose plates.
- Marine organisms.
- Some produce toxins.

Example

_Gonyaulax_

(Red tides)

---

### Euglenoids

Characteristics

- No cell wall.
- Flexible protein-rich pellicle.
- Photosynthetic in sunlight.
- Heterotrophic in darkness.

Example

_Euglena_

---

### Slime Moulds

Characteristics

- Saprophytic.
- Form plasmodium.
- Produce spores during unfavorable conditions.

---

### Protozoans

Animal-like protists.

#### Amoeboid Protozoans

Move using pseudopodia.

Example

_Amoeba_

---

#### Flagellated Protozoans

Move using flagella.

Example

_Trypanosoma_

---

#### Ciliated Protozoans

Move using cilia.

Example

_Paramecium_

---

#### Sporozoans

Non-motile parasites.

Example

_Plasmodium_

---

- Mostly multicellular.

- Heterotrophic.

# 7. Kingdom Plantae

## General Characteristics

- Multicellular.
- Cellulose cell wall.
- Chlorophyll present.
- Photosynthetic.
- Food stored as starch.

Examples

- Mango
- Rose
- Fern
- Pine

---

# 8. Kingdom Animalia

## General Characteristics

- Multicellular.
- Cell wall absent.
- Heterotrophic.
- Well-developed nervous system.
- Active locomotion in most members.

Examples

- Human
- Dog
- Fish
- Earthworm

# 9. Viruses

## Discovery

**Dmitri Ivanovsky (1892)**

---

## Definition

Viruses are **acellular infectious particles** that can reproduce only inside living host cells.

---

## Structure

```
Virus
   │
 ┌─┴─────────┐
 ▼           ▼
DNA/RNA   Protein Coat
           (Capsid)
```

---

## Characteristics

- Living inside host.
- Non-living outside host.
- Obligate parasites.
- Cause many diseases.

---

## Examples

| Virus                | Disease        |
| -------------------- | -------------- |
| HIV                  | AIDS           |
| Influenza Virus      | Flu            |
| Coronavirus          | COVID-19       |
| Tobacco Mosaic Virus | Mosaic Disease |

---

# 10. Viroids

Discovered by

**T. O. Diener**

---

## Characteristics

- Smaller than viruses.
- Only RNA.
- No protein coat.
- Infect plants.

Example

Potato Spindle Tuber Disease

---

# 11. Prions

Discovered by

**Stanley Prusiner**

---

## Characteristics

- Proteinaceous infectious particles.
- No DNA.
- No RNA.
- Cause neurological disorders.

Example

Mad Cow Disease

---

## Definition

Lichens are a **symbiotic association** between

```
Alga

```

---

↓

---

Fungus

# Difference Between Archaebacteria and Eubacteria

| Archaebacteria                | Eubacteria                       |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Extreme habitats              | Normal habitats                  |
| Cell wall lacks peptidoglycan | Cell wall contains peptidoglycan |
| Ancient organisms             | Modern bacteria                  |

---

# Difference Between Virus, Viroid and Prion

| Virus                     | Viroid              | Prion           |
| ------------------------- | ------------------- | --------------- |
| DNA or RNA + Protein      | RNA only            | Protein only    |
| Infect plants and animals | Infect plants       | Infect animals  |
| Protein coat present      | Protein coat absent | No nucleic acid |

---

# Difference Between Fungi and Plantae

| Fungi            | Plants              |
| ---------------- | ------------------- |
| Chitin cell wall | Cellulose cell wall |
| Heterotrophic    | Autotrophic         |
| Glycogen storage | Starch storage      |
| No chlorophyll   | Chlorophyll present |

### Question

Why are lichens called symbiotic organisms?

### Solution

A lichen consists of an **alga** (which prepares food) and a **fungus** (which provides water, minerals, and protection), both living together for mutual benefit.

### Answer

They are a symbiotic association of an alga and a fungus.

### Question

Differentiate between viruses and viroids.

### Solution

Viruses contain nucleic acid (DNA or RNA) enclosed in a protein coat, whereas viroids consist only of naked RNA without a protein coat.

### Answer

Viruses = DNA/RNA + protein coat; Viroids = RNA only, no protein coat.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Archaebacteria** with **Eubacteria**.
- Writing fungi as autotrophic organisms.
- Assuming viruses are true living cells.
- Mixing up viroids and prions.
- Forgetting that fungi have **chitin** cell walls, not cellulose.
- Confusing cyanobacteria with true algae.

# Chapter Summary

- **Biological Classification** organizes the vast diversity of living organisms into meaningful groups based on cellular organization, nutrition, reproduction, and evolutionary relationships.
- **R. H. Whittaker's Five-Kingdom Classification** divides organisms into **Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia**, providing a systematic framework for studying life.
- **Kingdom Monera** includes prokaryotes such as archaebacteria, eubacteria, cyanobacteria, and mycoplasma, while **Protista** comprises diverse unicellular eukaryotes like diatoms, dinoflagellates, euglenoids, slime moulds, and protozoans.
- **Fungi** are heterotrophic organisms with chitinous cell walls and reproduce through various spores, whereas **Plantae** and **Animalia** include multicellular autotrophic and heterotrophic organisms respectively.
- **Viruses, viroids, prions, and lichens** represent unique biological entities that highlight the diversity and complexity of life beyond the traditional kingdoms.
- Understanding biological classification is fundamental for studying **biodiversity, evolution, ecology, medicine, agriculture, biotechnology, and conservation biology**.
