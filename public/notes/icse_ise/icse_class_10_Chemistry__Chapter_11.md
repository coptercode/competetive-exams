# Introduction

**Organic Chemistry** is the branch of chemistry that deals with the study of **carbon compounds**, except a few simple compounds such as carbonates, bicarbonates, carbides, and oxides of carbon. Carbon is unique because of its ability to form long chains and rings through **catenation** and to form stable covalent bonds. Organic compounds are widely used in fuels, medicines, plastics, food, textiles, cosmetics, and many industrial products.

---

# 1. Organic Chemistry

## Definition

**Organic Chemistry** is the branch of chemistry that studies compounds containing carbon and hydrogen, along with elements such as oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur, and halogens.

---

## Characteristics of Organic Compounds

- Mostly covalent compounds.
- Low melting and boiling points.
- Poor conductors of electricity.
- Combustible in nature.
- Complex molecular structures.

---

# 2. Unique Nature of Carbon

## (i) Tetravalency

Carbon has **four valence electrons** and forms **four covalent bonds**.

Example:

```
CH₄ (Methane)
```

---

## (ii) Catenation

Carbon atoms join together to form **long chains, branched chains, and rings**.

Example:

```
C–C–C–C–C
```

---

# 3. Hydrocarbons

## Definition

**Hydrocarbons** are compounds containing **only carbon and hydrogen**.

---

## Classification

# 4. Alkanes

## Definition

**Alkanes** are saturated hydrocarbons containing **only single covalent bonds**.

---

## General Formula

```
CnH₂n+₂
```

---

## Examples

| Compound | Formula |
| -------- | ------- |
| Methane  | CH₄     |
| Ethane   | C₂H₆    |
| Propane  | C₃H₈    |
| Butane   | C₄H₁₀   |

---

## Properties

- Saturated compounds.
- Undergo substitution reactions.
- Burn with a clean blue flame.

---

# 5. Alkenes

## Definition

**Alkenes** are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing **one double bond**.

---

## General Formula

```
CnH₂n
```

---

## Examples

| Compound | Formula |
| -------- | ------- |
| Ethene   | C₂H₄    |
| Propene  | C₃H₆    |

---

## Properties

- Unsaturated compounds.
- Undergo addition reactions.
- Decolourize bromine water.

---

# 6. Alkynes

## Definition

**Alkynes** are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing **one triple bond**.

---

## General Formula

```
CnH₂n−₂
```

---

## Examples

| Compound | Formula |
| -------- | ------- |
| Ethyne   | C₂H₂    |
| Propyne  | C₃H₄    |

---

# 7. Homologous Series

## Definition

A **Homologous Series** is a group of organic compounds having:

- Same functional group.
- Same general formula.
- Similar chemical properties.
- Successive members differ by **–CH₂–**.

---

## Example

| Compound | Formula |
| -------- | ------- |
| Methane  | CH₄     |
| Ethane   | C₂H₆    |
| Propane  | C₃H₈    |
| Butane   | C₄H₁₀   |

---

# Characteristics of Homologous Series

- Same functional group.
- Similar chemical properties.
- Gradual change in physical properties.
- Difference of **14 u (CH₂)** between successive members.

---

# 8. Functional Group

## Definition

A **Functional Group** is an atom or group of atoms responsible for the characteristic chemical properties of an organic compound.

---

## Common Functional Groups

| Functional Group | Name            | Example       |
| ---------------- | --------------- | ------------- |
| –OH              | Alcohol         | Ethanol       |
| –COOH            | Carboxylic Acid | Ethanoic Acid |
| –CHO             | Aldehyde        | Ethanal       |
| –CO–             | Ketone          | Propanone     |
| –X               | Halo Compound   | Chloroethane  |

---

# 9. IUPAC Nomenclature

## Rules

1. Count the longest carbon chain.
2. Identify the functional group.
3. Number the carbon atoms.
4. Name substituents.
5. Write the complete IUPAC name.

---

## Examples

| Common Name | IUPAC Name    |
| ----------- | ------------- |
| CH₄         | Methane       |
| C₂H₆        | Ethane        |
| C₂H₄        | Ethene        |
| C₂H₂        | Ethyne        |
| CH₃OH       | Methanol      |
| CH₃COOH     | Ethanoic Acid |

---

# 10. Isomerism

## Definition

**Isomerism** is the phenomenon in which compounds have the **same molecular formula** but **different structural arrangements**.

---

## Example

```
C₄H₁₀

↓

n-Butane

Isobutane
```

# 11. Ethanol

## Formula

```
C₂H₅OH
```

---

## Preparation

Fermentation of sugar.

---

## Properties

- Colourless liquid.
- Miscible with water.
- Neutral.
- Good solvent.

---

## Uses

- Alcoholic beverages.
- Medicines.
- Solvent.
- Fuel (bioethanol).
- Sanitizers.

---

# 12. Ethanoic Acid

## Formula

```
CH₃COOH
```

---

## Common Name

Acetic Acid

---

## Properties

- Colourless liquid.
- Sour smell.
- Weak acid.
- Turns blue litmus red.

---

## Uses

- Vinegar.
- Food preservation.
- Textile industry.
- Medicines.

# Difference Between Saturated and Unsaturated Hydrocarbons

| Saturated                        | Unsaturated               |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Single bonds only                | Double or triple bonds    |
| Alkanes                          | Alkenes/Alkynes           |
| Less reactive                    | More reactive             |
| Do not decolourize bromine water | Decolourize bromine water |

# Difference Between Ethanol and Ethanoic Acid

| Ethanol         | Ethanoic Acid   |
| --------------- | --------------- |
| Alcohol         | Carboxylic acid |
| –OH group       | –COOH group     |
| Neutral         | Weak acid       |
| Used as solvent | Used in vinegar |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **alkanes, alkenes, and alkynes**.
- Forgetting the **general formulae** of hydrocarbons.
- Mixing up **ethanol** and **ethanoic acid**.
- Confusing **functional groups** with molecular formulae.
- Forgetting that **alkenes decolourize bromine water**.
- Assuming all carbon compounds are organic (carbonates and oxides are exceptions).
- Writing incorrect IUPAC names.

# Chapter Summary

- **Organic Chemistry** is the study of carbon compounds and their properties, with carbon's **tetravalency** and **catenation** being responsible for the vast number of organic compounds.
- **Hydrocarbons** are classified into **alkanes (saturated), alkenes (double bond), and alkynes (triple bond)**, each having its own general formula and characteristic reactions.
- A **homologous series** consists of compounds with the same functional group and similar chemical properties, differing by a **–CH₂–** unit.
- **Functional groups** such as **–OH** and **–COOH** determine the chemical behavior of organic compounds.
- **IUPAC nomenclature** provides a systematic method for naming organic compounds, while **isomerism** explains compounds with the same molecular formula but different structures.
- **Ethanol** is an important alcohol used as a solvent, fuel, and disinfectant, whereas **ethanoic acid** is widely used in vinegar, food preservation, and chemical industries.
- Organic compounds are indispensable in **medicine, fuels, plastics, textiles, cosmetics, agriculture, and pharmaceuticals**, making organic chemistry one of the most significant branches of chemistry.
