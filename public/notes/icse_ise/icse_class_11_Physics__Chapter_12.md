# Introduction

**Oscillation** is the repeated back-and-forth motion of a body about its mean position. Oscillatory motion is common in nature and technology, such as **pendulums, springs, vibrations of molecules, musical instruments, and clocks**.

This chapter explains **periodic motion, simple harmonic motion (SHM), velocity, acceleration, energy, spring systems, and pendulum motion**.

---

# 1. Periodic Motion

## Definition

A motion that repeats itself after equal intervals of time is called periodic motion.

---

# Examples

- Motion of a pendulum.
- Rotation of Earth.
- Vibrations of tuning fork.
- Motion of planets.

---

# Time Period (T)

The time taken to complete one oscillation.

---

## Formula

```
T = Total Time / Number of Oscillations
```

---

# Frequency (f)

The number of oscillations completed per second.

---

## Formula

```
f = 1/T
```

---

# SI Unit

```
Hertz (Hz)
```

---

# Angular Frequency

```
ω = 2πf
```

or

```
ω = 2π/T
```

---

# Difference Between Period and Frequency

| Time Period              | Frequency               |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Time for one oscillation | Oscillations per second |
| Unit: second             | Unit: Hertz             |

---

# 2. Oscillatory Motion

## Definition

Motion in which a body moves repeatedly to and fro about an equilibrium position.

---

# Characteristics

- Has a mean position.
- Has restoring force.
- Repeats after equal time intervals.

---

# Examples

- Spring-mass system.
- Simple pendulum.
- Vibrating string.

---

# 3. Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

## Definition

A type of oscillatory motion in which acceleration is directly proportional to displacement and always directed towards the mean position.

---

# Mathematical Condition

```
a ∝ -x
```

or

```
a = -ω²x
```

---

# Meaning of Negative Sign

Acceleration is always opposite to displacement.

---

# Examples

- Mass attached to spring.
- Small oscillations of pendulum.
- Vibrations of atoms.

---

# Difference Between Periodic Motion and SHM

| Periodic Motion          | SHM                                       |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| Repeats after equal time | Acceleration proportional to displacement |
| General motion           | Special type of oscillation               |

---

# 4. Displacement in SHM

The displacement of a particle from mean position is:

```
x = A sin(ωt + φ)
```

Where:

- A = Amplitude
- ω = Angular frequency
- φ = Phase constant

---

# Amplitude (A)

Maximum displacement from mean position.

---

# SI Unit

```
metre (m)
```

---

# Phase

The state of motion of an oscillating particle.

---

# 5. Velocity in SHM

Velocity is maximum at mean position.

---

# Formula

```
v = ω√(A² − x²)
```

---

# Maximum Velocity

At mean position:

```
vmax = ωA
```

---

# Graph

```
Velocity

  |
  |     /\
  |    /  \
  |___/____\____ Position
```

---

# 6. Acceleration in SHM

Acceleration is maximum at extreme positions.

---

# Formula

```
a = -ω²x
```

---

# Maximum Acceleration

```
amax = ω²A
```

---

# At Mean Position

```
x = 0

a = 0
```

---

# At Extreme Position

```
x = A

a = Maximum
```

---

# 7. Energy in SHM

Total mechanical energy remains constant.

---

# Total Energy

```
E = ½mω²A²
```

---

# Kinetic Energy

```
KE = ½mω²(A²-x²)
```

---

# Potential Energy

```
PE = ½mω²x²
```

---

# Energy Variation

At Mean Position:

```
KE = Maximum

PE = Zero
```

---

At Extreme Position:

```
KE = Zero

PE = Maximum
```

---

# Energy Graph

```
Energy

PE       KE
 /\      \/
/  \    /  \
--------------
Position
```

---

# 8. Spring Mass System

## Definition

A mass attached to a spring performs SHM when displaced from equilibrium.

---

# Hooke's Law

```
F = -kx
```

---

# Time Period

```
T = 2π√(m/k)
```

Where:

- m = Mass
- k = Spring constant

---

# Factors Affecting Time Period

Increases with:

- Increase in mass.

Decreases with:

- Increase in spring constant.

---

# 9. Simple Pendulum

## Definition

A small bob suspended from a fixed point by a light string that performs oscillatory motion.

---

# Conditions for SHM

- Small angular displacement.
- Light and inextensible string.
- Negligible air resistance.

---

# Time Period Formula

```
T = 2π√(l/g)
```

Where:

- l = Length of pendulum
- g = Acceleration due to gravity

---

# Dependence

```
T ∝ √l
```

```
T ∝ 1/√g
```

---

# Factors Not Affecting Time Period

- Mass of bob.
- Amplitude (for small oscillations).

---

# 10. Phase Difference

Phase difference describes the difference in phase between two oscillating particles.

---

# Same Phase

Phase difference:

```
0°
```

---

# Opposite Phase

Phase difference:

```
180°
```

---

# Difference Between Two SHM Particles

| Phase Difference | Condition                  |
| ---------------- | -------------------------- |
| 0                | Same position and velocity |
| π                | Opposite motion            |

---

# 11. Damped Oscillation

## Definition

Oscillation in which amplitude decreases gradually due to resistance.

---

# Causes

- Friction.
- Air resistance.
- Energy loss.

---

# Examples

- Shock absorbers.
- Door closers.

---

# 12. Forced Oscillations

## Definition

Oscillations produced by an external periodic force.

---

# Example

- Vibrating machine parts.
- Musical instruments.

---

# 13. Resonance

## Definition

The phenomenon in which the amplitude becomes maximum when the frequency of external force equals the natural frequency of the system.

---

# Condition

```
External Frequency

=

Natural Frequency
```

---

# Applications

Positive:

- Radio tuning.
- Musical instruments.
- Quartz clocks.

Negative:

- Bridge vibrations.
- Building damage.

---

# 14. Natural Frequency

The frequency at which a system oscillates freely.

---

# Formula

```
f = 1/T
```

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

A particle completes 20 oscillations in 10 seconds. Find frequency.

### Solution

```
f = Number of Oscillations / Time

= 20/10

= 2 Hz
```

### Answer

**2 Hz**

---

## Example 2

### Question

A spring with mass 2 kg and spring constant 50 N/m. Find time period.

### Solution

```
T = 2π√(m/k)

= 2π√(2/50)

≈ 1.26 s
```

### Answer

**1.26 s**

---

## Example 3

### Question

A pendulum has length 1 m. Find its time period.

(g = 9.8 m/s²)

### Solution

```
T = 2π√(l/g)

= 2π√(1/9.8)

≈ 2.0 s
```

### Answer

**2 s**

---

## Example 4

### Question

A particle performs SHM with amplitude 0.1 m and angular frequency 5 rad/s. Find maximum velocity.

### Solution

```
vmax = ωA

= 5 × 0.1

= 0.5 m/s
```

### Answer

**0.5 m/s**

---

## Example 5

### Question

Find maximum acceleration for amplitude 0.2 m and angular frequency 10 rad/s.

### Solution

```
amax = ω²A

= 100 × 0.2

= 20 m/s²
```

### Answer

**20 m/s²**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing periodic motion with SHM.
- Forgetting the negative sign in acceleration equation.
- Using pendulum formula for large amplitudes.
- Confusing frequency and angular frequency.
- Assuming velocity is maximum at extreme positions.
- Ignoring damping effects in real systems.

# Chapter Summary

- **Oscillation** describes repeated motion about an equilibrium position, while **simple harmonic motion** is a special type where acceleration is proportional to displacement and directed towards the mean position.
- SHM is characterized by relationships between **displacement, velocity, acceleration, and energy**, with energy continuously transforming between kinetic and potential forms.
- A **spring-mass system** and **simple pendulum** are important examples of SHM with well-defined time period equations.
- Real oscillations may experience damping due to friction and resistance, while forced oscillations can lead to **resonance** when frequencies match.
- Oscillation principles are essential in **engineering, electronics, mechanical systems, musical instruments, clocks, earthquake studies, and modern technology**.
