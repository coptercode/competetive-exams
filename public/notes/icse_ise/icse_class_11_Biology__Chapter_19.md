# Introduction

During metabolism, cells produce various **waste products** such as carbon dioxide, ammonia, urea, uric acid, excess salts, and water. These wastes are toxic if accumulated and must be eliminated from the body. The **human excretory system** maintains internal homeostasis by removing metabolic wastes and regulating water and electrolyte balance.

The human excretory system consists of:

- Kidneys
- Ureters
- Urinary bladder
- Urethra

---

# 1. Excretion

## Definition

**Excretion** is the process of removing metabolic waste products and excess substances from the body.

---

# Importance

- Removes toxic wastes.
- Maintains water balance.
- Regulates electrolyte concentration.
- Maintains blood pH.
- Maintains osmotic balance.

---

## A. Ammonotelism

### Definition

Excretion of nitrogen mainly as **ammonia**.

### Characteristics

- Highly toxic
- Requires large amount of water

### Examples

- Bony fishes
- Aquatic invertebrates
- Tadpoles

---

## B. Ureotelism

### Definition

Excretion of nitrogen mainly as **urea**.

### Characteristics

- Less toxic
- Moderate water requirement

### Examples

- Humans
- Mammals
- Adult amphibians

---

## C. Uricotelism

### Definition

Excretion of nitrogen mainly as **uric acid**.

### Characteristics

- Least toxic
- Conserves water

### Examples

- Birds
- Reptiles
- Insects

---

# Difference Between Ammonotelism, Ureotelism and Uricotelism

| Ammonotelism            | Ureotelism       | Uricotelism       |
| ----------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------- |
| Ammonia                 | Urea             | Uric Acid         |
| Highly toxic            | Moderately toxic | Least toxic       |
| Large water requirement | Moderate water   | Very little water |
| Fishes                  | Mammals          | Birds             |

# A. Ultrafiltration

## Definition

High-pressure filtration of blood in the **glomerulus**.

---

## Site

```
Glomerulus

↓

Bowman's Capsule
```

---

## Filtrate Contains

- Water
- Glucose
- Amino acids
- Salts
- Urea

---

# B. Selective Reabsorption

## Definition

Useful substances are reabsorbed into blood.

---

## Site

```

```

---

## Reabsorbed

- Glucose
- Amino acids
- Water
- Sodium ions

---

# C. Tubular Secretion

## Definition

Transfer of wastes from blood into nephron.

---

## Secreted Substances

- Hydrogen ions
- Potassium ions
- Ammonia
- Drugs

---

# Difference Between Ultrafiltration and Selective Reabsorption

| Ultrafiltration      | Selective Reabsorption |
| -------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Blood → Filtrate     | Filtrate → Blood       |
| Occurs in glomerulus | Mainly in PCT          |
| Pressure-driven      | Active and passive     |

# 8. Concentration of Urine

## Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH)

Secreted by

```
Posterior Pituitary
```

---

## Functions

- Increases water reabsorption.
- Produces concentrated urine.

---

# Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS)

## Functions

- Increases blood pressure.
- Promotes sodium reabsorption.
- Conserves water.

---

# Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF)

## Functions

- Lowers blood pressure.
- Increases sodium excretion.
- Reduces water retention.

---

# Difference Between ADH and Aldosterone

| ADH                 | Aldosterone            |
| ------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Water reabsorption  | Sodium reabsorption    |
| Posterior pituitary | Adrenal cortex         |
| Concentrates urine  | Increases blood volume |

---

# Difference Between ADH and ANF

| ADH                | ANF                 |
| ------------------ | ------------------- |
| Conserves water    | Promotes water loss |
| Increases BP       | Decreases BP        |
| Concentrated urine | Dilute urine        |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

What is the structural and functional unit of the kidney?

### Solution

Each kidney contains about one million **nephrons**, which perform filtration and urine formation.

### Answer

Nephron.

---

## Example 2

### Question

Where does ultrafiltration occur?

### Solution

Blood is filtered under high pressure in the **glomerulus** within Bowman's capsule.

### Answer

Glomerulus.

---

## Example 3

### Question

Which hormone increases water reabsorption in the kidney?

### Solution

**Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH)** increases the permeability of collecting ducts, promoting water reabsorption.

### Answer

ADH.

---

## Example 4

### Question

Which animals are ureotelic?

### Solution

Humans and most mammals excrete nitrogen mainly in the form of urea.

### Answer

Humans and other mammals.

---

## Example 5

### Question

What is haemodialysis?

### Solution

Haemodialysis is the artificial purification of blood using a dialysis machine when kidneys cannot function properly.

### Answer

Artificial purification of blood in kidney failure.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Bowman's capsule** with the **glomerulus**.
- Mixing up **ultrafiltration**, **reabsorption**, and **tubular secretion**.
- Assuming urine normally contains glucose or proteins.
- Confusing the functions of **ADH**, **aldosterone**, and **ANF**.
- Mixing up **ammonotelic**, **ureotelic**, and **uricotelic** animals.
- Assuming dialysis permanently cures kidney failure.

# Chapter Summary

- **Excretion** is the process of eliminating metabolic wastes while maintaining water, electrolyte, and acid-base balance.
- The **kidneys**, composed of millions of **nephrons**, are the primary organs responsible for urine formation through **ultrafiltration, selective reabsorption, and tubular secretion**.
- Different organisms eliminate nitrogenous wastes as **ammonia, urea, or uric acid**, depending on their habitat and water availability.
- The **counter-current mechanism** and hormones such as **ADH, RAAS, and ANF** regulate urine concentration, blood volume, and blood pressure.
- Disorders such as **uremia, kidney stones, glomerulonephritis, and renal failure** impair kidney function and may require **haemodialysis** or kidney transplantation.
- Understanding the excretory system is essential for **human physiology, nephrology, clinical medicine, and maintenance of homeostasis**.
