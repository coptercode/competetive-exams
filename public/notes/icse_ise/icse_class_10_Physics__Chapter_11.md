# Introduction

**Calorimetry** is the branch of physics that deals with the measurement of heat gained or lost by a body during physical or chemical processes. It is based on the **Principle of Heat Exchange**, which states that in an isolated system, the heat lost by the hotter body is equal to the heat gained by the colder body until thermal equilibrium is reached. This chapter covers **heat, temperature, specific heat capacity, heat capacity, latent heat, calorimeter, and methods of heat transfer**.

---

# 1. Heat

## Definition

**Heat** is a form of energy that flows from a body at a higher temperature to a body at a lower temperature.

---

## Characteristics

- Heat is a form of energy.
- It always flows from a hotter body to a colder body.
- It continues to flow until thermal equilibrium is reached.
- SI Unit: **Joule (J)**

---

# 2. Temperature

## Definition

**Temperature** is the degree of hotness or coldness of a body.

---

## Measuring Instrument

```
Thermometer
```

---

## SI Unit

```
Kelvin (K)
```

Common Unit:

```
Degree Celsius (°C)
```

| Heat                    | Temperature                    |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Form of energy          | Measure of hotness or coldness |

| Depends on mass         | Independent of mass            |

# 3. Specific Heat Capacity

## Definition

**Specific Heat Capacity (c)** is the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of **1 kg** of a substance by **1°C (or 1 K)**.

---

## Formula

```
          Heat
c = ----------------
      Mass × Rise in Temperature

c = Q / mΔT
```

Where,

- **Q** = Heat supplied (J)
- **m** = Mass (kg)
- **ΔT** = Change in temperature (°C or K)

---

## SI Unit

```
J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹
```

---

# 4. Heat Capacity

## Definition

**Heat Capacity (C)** is the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of an entire body by **1°C or 1 K**.

---

## Formula

```
C = mc
```

---

## SI Unit

```
J K⁻¹
```

---

# Difference Between Specific Heat Capacity and Heat Capacity

| Specific Heat Capacity | Heat Capacity        |
| ---------------------- | -------------------- |
| Heat for 1 kg          | Heat for entire body |
| Property of material   | Property of object   |
| Unit: J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹       | Unit: J K⁻¹          |

---

# 5. Principle of Heat Exchange

## Statement

In an isolated system,

```
Heat Lost

=

Heat Gained
```

---

## Formula

```
m₁c₁(T₁ − T)

=

m₂c₂(T − T₂)
```

Where,

- **T₁** = Initial temperature of hot body
- **T₂** = Initial temperature of cold body
- **T** = Final equilibrium temperature

---

# 6. Calorimeter

## Definition

A **Calorimeter** is an instrument used to measure the quantity of heat exchanged.

---

## Construction

- Copper vessel
- Wooden outer jacket
- Stirrer
- Thermometer
- Lid

---

## Functions

- Prevents heat loss.
- Measures heat exchange accurately.
- Used to determine specific heat capacity and latent heat.

---

# 7. Latent Heat

## Definition

**Latent Heat** is the heat absorbed or released during a change of state without any change in temperature.

---

## Types

### (i) Latent Heat of Fusion

Heat required to convert **1 kg of solid into liquid** at its melting point without change in temperature.

---

### Formula

```
Q = mLf
```

Where,

- **Lf** = Specific Latent Heat of Fusion

---

### (ii) Latent Heat of Vaporization

Heat required to convert **1 kg of liquid into vapour** at its boiling point without change in temperature.

---

### Formula

```
Q = mLv
```

Where,

- **Lv** = Specific Latent Heat of Vaporization

---

# Difference Between Fusion and Vaporization

| Fusion                  | Vaporization            |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Solid → Liquid          | Liquid → Gas            |
| Occurs at melting point | Occurs at boiling point |
| Uses Lf                 | Uses Lv                 |

# 10. Methods of Heat Transfer

---

## (i) Conduction

### Definition

Transfer of heat through a material without actual movement of its particles.

---

### Occurs Mainly In

```
Solids
```

---

### Examples

- Metal spoon in hot tea.
- Iron rod heated at one end.

---

## (ii) Convection

### Definition

Transfer of heat by the actual movement of particles.

---

### Occurs In

```
Liquids and Gases
```

---

### Examples

- Boiling water.
- Sea breeze.
- Land breeze.

---

## (iii) Radiation

### Definition

Transfer of heat without the need for a material medium.

---

### Characteristics

- Travels through vacuum.
- Electromagnetic waves.
- Fastest method of heat transfer.

---

### Examples

- Heat from the Sun.
- Room heaters.
- Campfire.

---

# Difference Between Conduction, Convection and Radiation

| Conduction               | Convection            | Radiation          |
| ------------------------ | --------------------- | ------------------ |
| No movement of particles | Movement of particles | No medium required |
| Solids                   | Liquids & Gases       | Vacuum or medium   |
| Slow                     | Moderate              | Fastest            |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Calculate the heat required to raise the temperature of **2 kg** of water from **20°C to 30°C**.

Given:

```
c = 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹
```

### Solution

```
Q = mcΔT

= 2 × 4200 × (30 − 20)

= 84,000 J
```

### Answer

```
84,000 J
```

---

## Example 2

### Question

Define specific heat capacity.

### Solution

It refers to the heat required for unit mass.

### Answer

The amount of heat required to raise the temperature of **1 kg** of a substance by **1°C (or 1 K)**.

---

## Example 3

### Question

A body has a mass of **4 kg** and a specific heat capacity of **500 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹**. Find its heat capacity.

### Solution

```
C = mc

= 4 × 500

= 2000 J K⁻¹
```

### Answer

```
2000 J K⁻¹
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **heat** with **temperature**.
- Forgetting the formula **Q = mcΔT**.
- Mixing up **specific heat capacity** and **heat capacity**.
- Using the wrong unit for specific heat capacity.
- Forgetting that **latent heat** changes the state, not the temperature.
- Assuming **radiation** requires a medium (it does not).
- Ignoring the **Principle of Heat Exchange** in numerical problems.

- Heat Exchange → **Heat Lost = Heat Gained**.

# Chapter Summary

- **Calorimetry** is the study of heat transfer and measurement based on the **Principle of Heat Exchange**, where heat lost equals heat gained in an isolated system.
- **Heat** is a form of energy transferred due to a temperature difference, while **temperature** measures the degree of hotness or coldness of a body.
- The quantity of heat involved in temperature changes is calculated using **Q = mcΔT**, where **specific heat capacity** is a characteristic property of a material and **heat capacity** depends on both mass and material.
- **Latent heat** is involved during changes of state, such as **fusion** and **vaporization**, without any change in temperature.
- Heat is transferred by **conduction**, **convection**, and **radiation**, each operating under different conditions and widely applied in daily life and engineering.
- Calorimetry principles are essential in **cooking, refrigeration, industrial processes, thermal power generation, climate control, and scientific experiments**.
