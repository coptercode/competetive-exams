# Introduction

The **Laws of Motion**, formulated by Sir Isaac Newton, explain how objects move when forces act upon them. These laws describe the relationship between force, mass, acceleration, and momentum. They form the foundation of classical mechanics and are widely used in engineering, transportation, sports, and everyday life.

---

# 1. Types of Forces

A **force** is a push or pull that changes or tends to change the state of rest or motion of an object.

## SI Unit

```
newton (N)
```

## Characteristics of Force

- It is a vector quantity.
- It has both magnitude and direction.
- It can change the speed, direction, or shape of an object.

---

## Contact Forces

These forces act only when two objects are in physical contact.

### Examples

- Frictional Force
- Muscular Force
- Normal Reaction Force
- Tension Force
- Spring Force

### Applications

- Walking
- Writing
- Lifting objects
- Pulling a rope

---

## Non-Contact Forces

These forces act without physical contact.

### Examples

- Gravitational Force
- Magnetic Force
- Electrostatic Force

### Applications

- Earth attracting objects
- Magnets attracting iron
- Charged objects attracting paper bits

---

# 2. Newton's First Law of Motion

## Statement

> **An object remains at rest or continues to move with uniform velocity in a straight line unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force.**

This law is also called the **Law of Inertia**.

---

## Inertia

Inertia is the property of a body that resists any change in its state of rest or motion.

### SI Unit

No SI unit (it is a property of matter).

---

## Types of Inertia

### 1. Inertia of Rest

A body at rest tends to remain at rest.

**Example**

- Passengers fall backward when a bus starts suddenly.
- Dust comes out when a carpet is beaten.

---

### 2. Inertia of Motion

A moving body tends to continue moving.

**Example**

- Passengers move forward when a moving bus stops suddenly.
- A cyclist continues moving even after stopping pedalling.

---

### 3. Inertia of Direction

A moving body tends to continue moving in the same direction.

**Example**

- A stone tied to a string moves in a circular path.
- Passengers are pushed sideways when a vehicle turns sharply.

---

# 3. Linear Momentum

Momentum is the quantity of motion possessed by an object.

## Formula

```
Momentum (p)

= Mass × Velocity

p = mv
```

where

- p = Momentum
- m = Mass
- v = Velocity

---

## SI Unit

```
kg·m/s
```

---

## Characteristics

- Momentum is a vector quantity.
- Greater mass means greater momentum.
- Greater velocity means greater momentum.

---

## Change in Momentum

```
Change in Momentum

= Final Momentum − Initial Momentum
```

---

# 4. Newton's Second Law of Motion

## Statement

> **The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied force and occurs in the direction of the force.**

---

## Mathematical Derivation

Momentum

```
p = mv
```

Force is proportional to the rate of change of momentum.

```
F ∝ Δp/Δt
```

Since

```
Δp = m(v − u)
```

Therefore,

```
F = ma
```

where

- F = Force
- m = Mass
- a = Acceleration

---

## SI Unit of Force

```
newton (N)
```

### Definition of One Newton

One Newton is the force required to produce an acceleration of **1 m/s²** in a body of mass **1 kg**.

```
1 N = 1 kg·m/s²
```

---

## Gravitational Units

### Kilogram-force (kgf)

```
1 kgf = 9.8 N
```

### Gram-force (gf)

```
1 gf = 0.0098 N
```

---

## Applications

- Rocket propulsion
- Pushing shopping carts
- Kicking a football
- Vehicle acceleration

---

# 5. Newton's Third Law of Motion

## Statement

> **For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.**

---

## Characteristics

- Action and reaction always occur together.
- They act on different bodies.
- They are equal in magnitude.
- They act in opposite directions.

---

## Examples

### Walking

- Foot pushes the ground backward.
- Ground pushes the person forward.

---

### Swimming

- Swimmer pushes water backward.
- Water pushes swimmer forward.

---

### Gun Recoil

- Bullet moves forward.
- Gun moves backward.

---

### Rocket Launch

- Exhaust gases move downward.
- Rocket moves upward.

---

### Bird Flying

- Wings push air downward.
- Air pushes bird upward.

---

# Balanced and Unbalanced Forces

## Balanced Forces

Balanced forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

### Result

- No change in motion.
- Object remains at rest or moves with constant velocity.

---

## Unbalanced Forces

Unbalanced forces are unequal.

### Result

- Object accelerates.
- Motion changes.

---

# Mass and Weight

## Mass

Mass is the quantity of matter contained in a body.

### SI Unit

```
kilogram (kg)
```

Characteristics

- Constant everywhere.
- Scalar quantity.

---

## Weight

Weight is the gravitational force acting on a body.

### Formula

```
Weight

W = mg
```

where

- W = Weight
- m = Mass
- g = Acceleration due to gravity

---

### SI Unit

```
newton (N)
```

Characteristics

- Depends on gravity.
- Vector quantity.

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Find the momentum of a body of mass **5 kg** moving with a velocity of **4 m/s**.

### Solution

```
p = mv

= 5 × 4

= 20 kg·m/s
```

### Answer

```
20 kg·m/s
```

---

## Example 2

### Question

A force acts on a **6 kg** object producing an acceleration of **3 m/s²**.

Find the force.

### Solution

```
F = ma

= 6 × 3

= 18 N
```

### Answer

```
18 N
```

---

## Example 3

### Question

Calculate the weight of a **10 kg** object.

Take

```
g = 9.8 m/s²
```

### Solution

```
W = mg

= 10 × 9.8

= 98 N
```

### Answer

```
98 N
```

---

## Example 4

### Question

A car of mass **800 kg** accelerates at **2 m/s²**.

Find the force acting on it.

### Solution

```
F = ma

= 800 × 2

= 1600 N
```

### Answer

```
1600 N
```

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **mass** with **weight**.
- Assuming action and reaction act on the same object.
- Using weight instead of mass in **F = ma**.
- Forgetting that momentum is a vector quantity.
- Ignoring units while solving numerical problems.
- Using **kgf** and **N** interchangeably without conversion.
- Mixing balanced and unbalanced forces.
- Forgetting that inertia depends only on mass.

# Chapter Summary

- A **force** is a push or pull that changes or tends to change the state of motion of a body.
- Forces are classified into **contact** and **non-contact** forces.
- **Newton's First Law** explains the concept of inertia.
- **Inertia** is the tendency of an object to resist changes in its state of motion.
- **Momentum** is the product of mass and velocity.
- **Newton's Second Law** gives the relationship **F = ma**.
- One **Newton** is the force required to accelerate a **1 kg** mass by **1 m/s²**.
- **Newton's Third Law** states that every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
- **Balanced forces** do not change motion, whereas **unbalanced forces** produce acceleration.
- **Mass** remains constant, while **weight** depends on gravitational acceleration.
- Newton's Laws of Motion explain the movement of vehicles, rockets, sports equipment, and many real-life phenomena.
