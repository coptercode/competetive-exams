# Introduction

Chemistry has its own universal language consisting of **symbols, formulae, equations, and chemical nomenclature**. This language enables scientists worldwide to communicate chemical reactions and compositions clearly and accurately. In this chapter, we learn about valency, radicals, writing chemical formulae, balancing chemical equations, atomic mass, molecular mass, and percentage composition.

---

# 1. Valency and Radicals

## Valency

**Valency** is the combining capacity of an atom or radical. It indicates the number of electrons an atom loses, gains, or shares during chemical bonding.

### Examples of Valency

| Element   | Symbol | Valency |
| --------- | ------ | ------- |
| Hydrogen  | H      | 1       |
| Oxygen    | O      | 2       |
| Nitrogen  | N      | 3       |
| Carbon    | C      | 4       |
| Sodium    | Na     | 1       |
| Magnesium | Mg     | 2       |
| Aluminium | Al     | 3       |
| Chlorine  | Cl     | 1       |

---

## Radicals

A **radical** is an atom or a group of atoms that behaves as a single unit and carries a positive or negative charge.

### Types of Radicals

### Basic (Positive) Radicals

These are positively charged ions (cations).

Examples:

| Radical   | Formula | Valency |
| --------- | ------- | ------- |
| Sodium    | Na⁺     | 1       |
| Potassium | K⁺      | 1       |
| Calcium   | Ca²⁺    | 2       |
| Magnesium | Mg²⁺    | 2       |
| Aluminium | Al³⁺    | 3       |
| Ammonium  | NH₄⁺    | 1       |

---

### Acid (Negative) Radicals

These are negatively charged ions (anions).

Examples:

| Radical   | Formula | Valency |
| --------- | ------- | ------- |
| Chloride  | Cl⁻     | 1       |
| Hydroxide | OH⁻     | 1       |
| Nitrate   | NO₃⁻    | 1       |
| Sulphate  | SO₄²⁻   | 2       |
| Carbonate | CO₃²⁻   | 2       |
| Phosphate | PO₄³⁻   | 3       |

---

# 2. Formula Construction

A **chemical formula** represents the composition of a compound using symbols and subscripts.

---

## Cross-Over Method

The valencies of the combining ions are crossed over to become subscripts.

### Rules

- Write the positive ion first.
- Cross the valencies.
- Reduce to the simplest ratio if possible.
- Use brackets for polyatomic ions when more than one is required.

---

## Examples

### Sodium Chloride

```
Na⁺ + Cl⁻

Formula = NaCl
```

---

### Magnesium Chloride

```
Mg²⁺ + Cl⁻

Formula = MgCl₂
```

---

### Aluminium Oxide

```
Al³⁺ + O²⁻

Formula = Al₂O₃
```

---

### Calcium Hydroxide

```
Ca²⁺ + OH⁻

Formula = Ca(OH)₂
```

---

### Ammonium Sulphate

```
NH₄⁺ + SO₄²⁻

Formula = (NH₄)₂SO₄
```

# 3. Chemical Equations

A **chemical equation** is the symbolic representation of a chemical reaction.

---

## Parts of a Chemical Equation

### Reactants

Substances that take part in a chemical reaction.

### Products

Substances formed after the reaction.

---

### Example

```
2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
```

Reactants:

- Hydrogen
- Oxygen

Product:

- Water

---

## Balancing Chemical Equations

According to the **Law of Conservation of Mass**, the number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides of the equation.

---

### Hit-and-Trial Method

Steps:

1. Write the unbalanced equation.
2. Count the atoms of each element.
3. Add coefficients to balance atoms.
4. Verify the equation.

---

### Example

Unbalanced:

```
H₂ + O₂ → H₂O
```

Balanced:

```
2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
```

# 4. Relative Atomic Mass and Molecular Mass

## Relative Atomic Mass (Aᵣ)

The relative atomic mass is the average mass of an atom compared with **1/12th of the mass of a Carbon-12 atom**.

It has **no unit**.

---

### Examples

| Element   | Atomic Mass |
| --------- | ----------: |
| Hydrogen  |           1 |
| Carbon    |          12 |
| Nitrogen  |          14 |
| Oxygen    |          16 |
| Sodium    |          23 |
| Magnesium |          24 |
| Chlorine  |        35.5 |
| Calcium   |          40 |

---

## Relative Molecular Mass (Mᵣ)

Relative molecular mass is the sum of the relative atomic masses of all atoms present in a molecule.

---

### Example 1

Water

```
H₂O

= (2 × 1) + 16

= 18
```

---

### Example 2

Carbon Dioxide

```
CO₂

= 12 + (2 × 16)

= 44
```

---

### Example 3

Sulphuric Acid

```
H₂SO₄

= (2 × 1) + 32 + (4 × 16)

= 98
```

---

# 5. Percentage Composition

The percentage composition of an element in a compound is the percentage by mass of that element present in the compound.

---

## Formula

```
Percentage of an Element

= (Mass of Element in Compound / Molecular Mass of Compound) × 100
```

---

## Example

Find the percentage of hydrogen in water.

```
Molecular Mass of H₂O

= 18

Mass of Hydrogen

= 2
```

```
Percentage

= (2/18) × 100

= 11.11%
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **valency** with **charge**.
- Forgetting to reduce subscripts to the simplest ratio.
- Omitting brackets for polyatomic ions.
- Writing incorrect chemical symbols.
- Forgetting to balance chemical equations.
- Using atomic mass instead of molecular mass in calculations.
- Incorrectly calculating percentage composition.
- Ignoring the Law of Conservation of Mass while balancing equations.

# Chapter Summary

- **Valency** is the combining capacity of an atom or radical.
- **Radicals** are positively or negatively charged ions that act as single units in chemical reactions.
- Chemical formulae are written using the **cross-over method**, ensuring electrical neutrality.
- A **chemical equation** represents a chemical reaction symbolically and must obey the **Law of Conservation of Mass**.
- **Relative Atomic Mass (Aᵣ)** is measured relative to **Carbon-12**, while **Relative Molecular Mass (Mᵣ)** is the sum of the atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule.
- **Percentage composition** determines the mass percentage of each element present in a compound.
- Understanding the language of chemistry is essential for writing formulas, balancing equations, and performing quantitative chemical calculations.
