# Introduction

The **cell cycle** is the sequence of events through which a cell grows, duplicates its genetic material, and divides to produce new daughter cells. Cell division is essential for **growth, repair, reproduction, development, and maintenance** of living organisms.

There are two major types of cell division:

- **Mitosis** (Equational Division)
- **Meiosis** (Reduction Division)

Proper regulation of the cell cycle ensures genetic stability and normal functioning of organisms.

---

# 1. Cell Cycle

## Definition

The **cell cycle** is the ordered sequence of events that occurs from the formation of a cell until it divides into two daughter cells.

---

## Importance

- Growth of organisms
- Replacement of worn-out cells
- Tissue repair
- Asexual reproduction
- Formation of gametes (through meiosis)
- Maintenance of chromosome number

---

# 2. Interphase

## Definition

Interphase is the longest phase of the cell cycle during which the cell grows and prepares for division.

It occupies about **90–95%** of the total cell cycle.

---

# Stages of Interphase

```
Interphase
     │
 ┌───┼─────────┐
 ▼   ▼         ▼
G₁   S        G₂
```

---

## A. G₁ Phase (Gap 1)

### Characteristics

- Cell grows in size.
- RNA and proteins are synthesized.
- Organelles increase in number.
- Cell performs normal metabolic activities.

---

## B. S Phase (Synthesis Phase)

### Characteristics

- DNA replication occurs.
- Chromosomes duplicate.
- Histone proteins are synthesized.
- DNA content doubles.

---

## C. G₂ Phase (Gap 2)

### Characteristics

- Final growth phase.
- Protein synthesis continues.
- Cell prepares for mitosis.
- Spindle proteins are produced.

---

# G₀ Phase

Some cells exit the cell cycle and enter a resting stage called **G₀ phase**.

Examples

- Nerve cells
- Mature muscle cells

---

# 3. M Phase (Mitotic Phase)

Consists of

```
Mitosis

+

Cytokinesis
```

---

# 4. Mitosis

## Definition

Mitosis is an **equational cell division** in which one parent cell produces **two genetically identical daughter cells**, each having the same chromosome number as the parent.

---

## Importance

- Growth
- Repair
- Regeneration
- Asexual reproduction
- Maintenance of chromosome number

---

# Stages of Mitosis

```
Mitosis
   │
 ┌─┼───────────────┐
 ▼ ▼ ▼ ▼
Prophase
Metaphase
Anaphase
Telophase
```

---

# A. Prophase

## Characteristics

- Chromatin condenses into chromosomes.
- Each chromosome consists of two sister chromatids.
- Nuclear membrane disappears.
- Nucleolus disappears.
- Spindle fibres begin to form.

---

# B. Metaphase

## Characteristics

- Chromosomes align at the equatorial plate.
- Spindle fibres attach to centromeres through kinetochores.

---

# C. Anaphase

## Characteristics

- Centromeres split.
- Sister chromatids separate.
- Chromatids move toward opposite poles.

---

# D. Telophase

## Characteristics

- Chromosomes reach opposite poles.
- Nuclear membrane reappears.
- Nucleolus reappears.
- Chromosomes uncoil into chromatin.

---

# Cytokinesis

## Definition

Division of cytoplasm resulting in the formation of two daughter cells.

---

## In Animal Cells

Occurs by

```
Cleavage Furrow
```

---

## In Plant Cells

Occurs by

```
Cell Plate Formation
```

---

# Significance of Mitosis

- Maintains chromosome number.
- Produces identical cells.
- Helps healing of wounds.
- Essential for embryonic development.
- Enables asexual reproduction.

---

# 5. Meiosis

## Definition

Meiosis is a **reduction division** in which one diploid parent cell produces **four haploid daughter cells**.

Occurs in reproductive organs during gamete formation.

---

## Importance

- Formation of gametes.
- Maintains chromosome number across generations.
- Produces genetic variation.
- Basis of evolution.

---

# Types of Meiotic Division

```
Meiosis
     │
 ┌───┴──────────┐
 ▼              ▼
Meiosis I   Meiosis II
Reduction   Equational
Division    Division
```

---

# 6. Meiosis I

## Definition

Reductional division where homologous chromosomes separate.

---

# Stages of Meiosis I

```
Prophase I
    │
┌───┼────────────────────────┐
▼   ▼    ▼      ▼          ▼
Leptotene
Zygotene
Pachytene
Diplotene
Diakinesis
```

---

## A. Leptotene

- Chromosomes become visible.
- Chromatin condenses.

---

## B. Zygotene

- Homologous chromosomes pair.
- Pairing is called

```
Synapsis
```

---

## C. Pachytene

- Crossing over occurs.
- Exchange of genetic material takes place.
- Enzyme involved:

```
Recombinase
```

---

## D. Diplotene

- Homologous chromosomes begin to separate.
- Chiasmata become visible.

---

## E. Diakinesis

- Chiasmata move toward chromosome ends (Terminalization).
- Nuclear membrane disappears.
- Spindle formation begins.

---

# Metaphase I

- Homologous chromosome pairs align at the equator.

---

# Anaphase I

- Homologous chromosomes separate.
- Centromeres do **not** divide.

---

# Telophase I

- Chromosomes reach poles.
- Two haploid cells are formed.

---

# Interkinesis

Short resting stage between Meiosis I and Meiosis II.

No DNA replication occurs.

---

# 7. Meiosis II

Similar to mitosis.

---

# Stages

```
Prophase II

↓

Metaphase II

↓

Anaphase II

↓

Telophase II
```

---

## Anaphase II

- Centromeres divide.
- Sister chromatids separate.

---

## End Result

```
One Diploid Cell

↓

Four Haploid Cells
```

---

# 8. Crossing Over

## Definition

Exchange of genetic material between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes during **Pachytene**.

---

## Importance

- Genetic variation.
- New gene combinations.
- Evolution.
- Diversity among offspring.

---

# 9. Cell Cycle Checkpoints

Cell cycle checkpoints ensure that the cell proceeds to the next phase only when conditions are favorable.

---

## Types

### G₁ Checkpoint

Checks

- Cell size
- Nutrients
- DNA damage

---

### G₂ Checkpoint

Checks

- DNA replication
- DNA damage

---

### M Checkpoint

Checks

- Proper spindle attachment
- Chromosome alignment

# Difference Between Mitosis and Meiosis

| Mitosis                      | Meiosis                  |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| One division                 | Two divisions            |
| Two daughter cells           | Four daughter cells      |
| Chromosome number maintained | Chromosome number halved |
| No crossing over             | Crossing over occurs     |
| Somatic cells                | Germ cells               |
| Growth and repair            | Gamete formation         |

---

# Difference Between Animal and Plant Cytokinesis

| Animal Cell              | Plant Cell           |
| ------------------------ | -------------------- |
| Cleavage furrow          | Cell plate           |
| Centripetal              | Centrifugal          |
| Cell membrane constricts | New cell wall formed |

---

# Difference Between Meiosis I and Meiosis II

| Meiosis I                       | Meiosis II                  |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Reduction division              | Equational division         |
| Homologous chromosomes separate | Sister chromatids separate  |
| Crossing over occurs            | No crossing over            |
| Chromosome number reduced       | Chromosome number unchanged |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

In which phase does DNA replication occur?

### Solution

DNA replication takes place during the **S phase** of interphase.

### Answer

S phase.

---

## Example 2

### Question

During which stage of meiosis does crossing over occur?

### Solution

Crossing over occurs during the **Pachytene stage of Prophase I**.

### Answer

Pachytene of Prophase I.

---

## Example 3

### Question

Why is mitosis called equational division?

### Solution

Because the chromosome number remains unchanged in the daughter cells.

### Answer

Chromosome number is maintained.

---

## Example 4

### Question

How many daughter cells are produced after meiosis?

### Solution

One diploid cell undergoes two successive divisions to form **four haploid daughter cells**.

### Answer

Four haploid cells.

---

## Example 5

### Question

Differentiate between cleavage furrow and cell plate.

### Solution

Cleavage furrow forms during cytokinesis in animal cells, while cell plate formation occurs in plant cells due to the presence of a rigid cell wall.

### Answer

Animal cells – cleavage furrow; Plant cells – cell plate.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **mitosis** with **meiosis**.
- Forgetting that DNA replication occurs only during the **S phase**.
- Mixing up **Anaphase I** and **Anaphase II**.
- Assuming crossing over occurs in mitosis.
- Confusing **synapsis** with **crossing over**.
- Forgetting that centromeres divide only during **Anaphase of mitosis** and **Anaphase II**, not in **Anaphase I**.

# Chapter Summary

- The **cell cycle** consists of **Interphase** and the **M phase**, ensuring orderly growth, DNA replication, and cell division.
- **Interphase** includes **G₁, S, and G₂ phases**, during which the cell grows, duplicates its DNA, and prepares for division.
- **Mitosis** is an equational division producing two genetically identical diploid daughter cells, essential for growth, repair, and asexual reproduction.
- **Meiosis** is a reduction division involving two successive divisions that produce four genetically distinct haploid gametes, maintaining chromosome number across generations.
- **Crossing over** during **Pachytene of Prophase I** generates genetic variation, which is fundamental to evolution and biodiversity.
- Accurate regulation of the cell cycle through checkpoints ensures genomic stability, making cell division central to development, heredity, medicine, biotechnology, and agriculture.
