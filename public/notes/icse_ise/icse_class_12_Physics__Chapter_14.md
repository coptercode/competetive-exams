# Introduction

**Radioactivity** is the spontaneous disintegration of unstable atomic nuclei with the emission of radiations.

Discovered by:

```
Henri Becquerel (1896)
```

---

# 1. Radioactive Substances

Certain nuclei are unstable due to an imbalance between:

- Protons
- Neutrons

They become stable by emitting radiations.

---

# 2. Types of Radioactive Radiation

Radioactive decay produces:

```
Radioactive Radiations

        │

 ┌──────┼──────┐

 ▼      ▼      ▼

Alpha   Beta   Gamma
(α)     (β)    (γ)
```

# 3. Alpha Radiation (α)

## Nature

Helium nucleus:

\[
^4_2He
\]

Contains:

- 2 protons
- 2 neutrons

---

Properties:

- Positive charge
- Heavy particles
- Low penetrating power
- High ionising power

---

Range:

```
Few centimetres in air
```

---

# Alpha Decay

Example:

\[
^{238}_{92}U
\rightarrow
^{234}_{90}Th +
^{4}_{2}He
\]

---

# 4. Beta Radiation (β)

## Nature

High-speed electrons.

Symbol:

\[
\beta^-
\]

---

Properties:

- Negative charge
- Small mass
- Medium penetrating power

---

# Beta Decay

A neutron changes into proton:

\[
n \rightarrow p+e^-+\bar\nu
\]

---

Example:

\[
^{14}_6C
\rightarrow
^{14}_7N +
e^-
\]

---

# 5. Gamma Radiation (γ)

## Nature

Electromagnetic radiation.

---

Properties:

- No charge
- No mass
- Highest penetrating power
- Lowest ionising power

---

# Gamma Decay

Occurs when nucleus loses excess energy.

---

# 6. Radioactive Decay Law

## Statement

The rate of radioactive decay is directly proportional to the number of undecayed nuclei present.

---

Formula:

\[
-\frac{dN}{dt}\propto N
\]

---

Therefore:

\[
N=N_0e^{-\lambda t}
\]

---

Where:

- N = Number of nuclei after time t
- N₀ = Initial number of nuclei
- λ = Decay constant

---

# 7. Decay Constant

## Definition

The probability of decay of a nucleus per unit time is called decay constant.

Symbol:

\[
\lambda
\]

---

Unit:

\[
s^{-1}
\]

---

# 8. Activity of Radioactive Substance

## Definition

The rate of radioactive decay is called activity.

---

Formula:

\[
A=\lambda N
\]

---

Unit:

```
Becquerel (Bq)
```

---

# 9. Half-Life

## Definition

The time required for half of the radioactive nuclei to decay is called half-life.

---

Symbol:

\[
T_{1/2}
\]

---

Formula:

\[
T_{1/2}
=

\frac{0.693}{\lambda}
\]

---

After one half-life:

\[
N=\frac{N_0}{2}
\]

---

After two half-lives:

\[
N=\frac{N_0}{4}
\]

---

# 10. Mean Life

## Definition

The average lifetime of radioactive nuclei is called mean life.

---

Formula:

\[
\tau=\frac1\lambda
\]

---

Relation:

\[
\tau=1.44T_{1/2}
\]

---

---

\[
^{14}_7N+\alpha

^{17}_8O+p

# 13. Nuclear Reaction

## Definition

A reaction involving changes in atomic nuclei is called nuclear reaction.

---

General form:

\[
A+B\rightarrow C+D+Energy
\]

---

# 14. Conservation Laws in Nuclear Reactions

Nuclear reactions obey conservation of:

1. Mass-energy
2. Charge
3. Momentum
4. Angular momentum

# 15. Nuclear Fission

## Definition

The splitting of a heavy nucleus into two lighter nuclei with release of energy.

---

Example:

\[
^{235}U+n
\rightarrow
^{141}Ba +
^{92}Kr +
3n +
Energy
\]

---

# Features

- Requires neutron bombardment
- Produces large energy
- Releases more neutrons

---

---

Types:

## Controlled Chain Reaction

Used in:

```
Nuclear reactors
```

---

## Uncontrolled Chain Reaction

Used in:

```

```

---

# 17. Nuclear Reactor

## Definition

A device that produces controlled nuclear energy through fission.

---

# Main Components

---

# 18. Nuclear Fusion

## Definition

The combination of two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus with release of energy.

---

Example:

Hydrogen nuclei combine to form helium.

---

Reaction:

\[
^2H+^3H
\rightarrow
^4He+n+Energy
\]

---

# Conditions Required

Fusion requires:

- Very high temperature
- High pressure

---

Occurs in:

```
Sun and stars
```

---

# 19. Difference Between Fission and Fusion

| Nuclear Fission         | Nuclear Fusion            |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Heavy nucleus splits    | Light nuclei combine      |
| Requires neutron        | Requires high temperature |
| Used in reactors        | Occurs in stars           |
| Chain reaction possible | Difficult to control      |

# Board Important Questions

## 1. Define radioactivity.

**Answer:**

Radioactivity is the spontaneous disintegration of unstable nuclei with emission of radiations.

---

## 2. State radioactive decay law.

**Answer:**

The rate of decay of a radioactive substance is directly proportional to the number of undecayed nuclei.

\[
N=N_0e^{-\lambda t}
\]

---

## 3. Define half-life.

**Answer:**

The time required for half of the radioactive nuclei to decay is called half-life.

\[
T_{1/2}=\frac{0.693}{\lambda}
\]

---

## 4. Explain nuclear fission.

**Answer:**

Nuclear fission is the splitting of a heavy nucleus into lighter nuclei with release of energy.

---

## 5. Explain nuclear fusion.

**Answer:**

Nuclear fusion is the combination of light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus with release of energy.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing alpha, beta, and gamma properties.
- Forgetting half-life relation.
- Mixing fission and fusion.
- Confusing activity and decay constant.
- Forgetting fusion requires extremely high temperature.

# Chapter Summary

- **Radioactivity** is the spontaneous decay of unstable nuclei.
- Alpha, beta, and gamma radiations have different properties and penetrating abilities.
- Radioactive decay follows an exponential law.
- Half-life and decay constant describe radioactive stability.
- Nuclear fission releases energy by splitting heavy nuclei.
- Nuclear fusion releases energy by combining light nuclei and powers stars.
- Nuclear energy has important applications in electricity generation, medicine, and research.
