# Introduction

The atom is the basic unit of matter. Earlier, Dalton considered the atom to be indivisible, but later experiments by **J.J. Thomson, Ernest Rutherford, James Chadwick, and Niels Bohr** revealed that atoms consist of smaller particles called **electrons, protons, and neutrons**.

The study of atomic structure explains the arrangement of electrons around the nucleus, atomic spectra, quantum numbers, and electronic configuration.

# 2. Atomic Models

## Thomson's Atomic Model

### Also Known As

```
Plum Pudding Model
```

---

### Assumptions

- Atom is a positively charged sphere.
- Electrons are embedded inside it.

---

### Limitations

- Could not explain alpha-particle scattering.
- Could not explain nucleus.

---

# Rutherford's Atomic Model

## Alpha Particle Scattering Experiment

### Observations

- Most α-particles passed straight.
- Few were deflected.
- Very few rebounded.

---

### Conclusions

- Atom is mostly empty space.
- Positive charge concentrated in nucleus.
- Electrons revolve around nucleus.

---

### Limitations

- Could not explain atomic stability.
- Could not explain line spectra.

---

# Bohr's Atomic Model

## Postulates

- Electrons move in fixed circular orbits.
- Electrons do not lose energy in stationary orbits.
- Energy is emitted or absorbed only during transition.

# 6. Electronic Configuration

Arrangement of electrons in orbitals.

---

# Aufbau Principle

Electrons occupy lower-energy orbitals first.

---

# (n + l) Rule

Lower value of

```
n + l
```

fills first.

If equal,

Lower n fills first.

---

# Pauli Exclusion Principle

No two electrons in an atom can have the same four quantum numbers.

Maximum

```
2 electrons/orbital
```

with opposite spins.

---

# Hund's Rule

Electrons occupy degenerate orbitals singly before pairing.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **orbit** with **orbital**.
- Assuming electrons move in fixed circular paths in the quantum mechanical model.
- Forgetting the **2n²** rule for maximum electrons in a shell.
- Mixing up the four quantum numbers and their significance.
- Incorrectly applying Hund's rule by pairing electrons too early.
- Confusing Bohr's model with the quantum mechanical model.

# Chapter Summary

- The atom is composed of **electrons, protons, and neutrons**, discovered through landmark experiments by Thomson, Goldstein, Rutherford, Chadwick, and others.
- **Bohr's atomic model** successfully explained the stability of hydrogen atoms and their line spectrum by introducing quantized energy levels.
- The **dual nature of matter**, proposed by **de Broglie**, and **Heisenberg's uncertainty principle** laid the foundation for the **quantum mechanical model** of the atom.
- In the quantum model, electrons occupy **orbitals**, which are probability regions characterized by **quantum numbers**.
- The arrangement of electrons follows the **Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion principle, and Hund's rule**, giving rise to the electronic configuration of atoms.
- Understanding atomic structure is fundamental to explaining **chemical bonding, periodic properties, spectroscopy, and the behavior of elements**.
