# Introduction

**Hydrogen Chloride (HCl)** is a colourless gas with a pungent smell. When dissolved in water, it forms **Hydrochloric Acid**, a strong acid widely used in laboratories and industries. Hydrogen chloride is highly soluble in water, which is demonstrated by the famous **Fountain Experiment**. It is also used in the preparation of chloride salts, cleaning metals, and manufacturing various chemicals.

---

# 1. Hydrogen Chloride

## Definition

**Hydrogen Chloride (HCl)** is a covalent compound composed of hydrogen and chlorine.

---

## Molecular Formula

```
HCl
```

---

## Nature

- Covalent compound.
- Colourless gas.
- Pungent smell.
- Highly soluble in water.

---

# 2. Laboratory Preparation of Hydrogen Chloride

## Principle

Hydrogen chloride gas is prepared by heating **sodium chloride** with **concentrated sulfuric acid** below **200°C**.

---

## Chemical Equation

```
NaCl + H₂SO₄

↓

NaHSO₄ + HCl↑
```

---

## Apparatus Required

- Round-bottom flask.
- Thistle funnel.
- Delivery tube.
- Gas jar.

---

## Collection of Gas

Hydrogen chloride is collected by **upward displacement of air** because:

- It is denser than air.
- Highly soluble in water (cannot be collected over water).

---

# 3. Industrial Preparation

Hydrogen chloride is produced as a by-product during several industrial processes, including the manufacture of chlorinated organic compounds.

---

# 4. Physical Properties

- Colourless gas.
- Pungent, choking smell.
- Denser than air.
- Highly soluble in water.
- Does not burn.
- Turns moist blue litmus red (after dissolving in water).

---

# 5. Chemical Properties

---

## (i) Formation of Hydrochloric Acid

```
HCl + H₂O

↓

H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻
```

---

## (ii) Reaction with Ammonia

```
NH₃ + HCl

↓

NH₄Cl
```

### Observation

Dense white fumes of **ammonium chloride** are formed.

---

## (iii) Reaction with Metals

Example

```
Zn + 2HCl

↓

ZnCl₂ + H₂↑
```

Hydrogen gas is evolved.

---

## (iv) Reaction with Metal Carbonates

```
CaCO₃ + 2HCl

↓

CaCl₂ + CO₂↑ + H₂O
```

---

# 6. Fountain Experiment

## Purpose

To demonstrate the **high solubility of hydrogen chloride gas in water**.

---

## Principle

When water enters a flask filled with HCl gas:

- The gas dissolves rapidly.
- Pressure inside the flask decreases.
- Water rushes in as a fountain.

---

## Observation

A coloured fountain is produced if coloured water is used.

---

## Conclusion

Hydrogen chloride is **extremely soluble in water**.

---

# 7. Hydrochloric Acid

## Definition

Hydrochloric Acid is the aqueous solution of hydrogen chloride gas.

---

## Nature

- Strong acid.
- Completely ionizes in water.
- Good conductor of electricity.

---

# 8. Aqua Regia

## Definition

**Aqua Regia** is a mixture of concentrated hydrochloric acid and concentrated nitric acid.

---

## Ratio

```
3 : 1

(HCl : HNO₃)
```

---

## Uses

- Dissolves gold.
- Dissolves platinum.
- Cleaning laboratory apparatus.

---

# 9. Test for Chloride Ions

## Reagent

Silver Nitrate Solution (AgNO₃)

---

## Reaction

```
NaCl + AgNO₃

↓

AgCl↓ + NaNO₃
```

---

## Observation

Curdy white precipitate of **silver chloride (AgCl)**.

---

## Confirmation Test

```
AgCl + Excess NH₄OH

↓

Soluble Complex
```

The precipitate dissolves in excess ammonium hydroxide.

---

# 10. Uses of Hydrochloric Acid

- Manufacture of chloride salts.
- Laboratory reagent.
- Preparation of dyes.
- Leather industry.
- Food processing.
- Pharmaceutical industry.

# Difference Between Hydrogen Chloride Gas and Hydrochloric Acid

| Hydrogen Chloride (HCl)      | Hydrochloric Acid (HCl(aq)) |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Covalent gas                 | Aqueous solution            |
| Does not ionize              | Ionizes completely          |
| Does not conduct electricity | Conducts electricity        |
| Dry gas                      | Acidic solution             |

---

# Difference Between HCl and Aqua Regia

| Hydrochloric Acid      | Aqua Regia                  |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------- |
| HCl dissolved in water | Mixture of HCl and HNO₃     |
| Strong acid            | Powerful oxidizing mixture  |
| Cannot dissolve gold   | Dissolves gold and platinum |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **hydrogen chloride gas** with **hydrochloric acid**.
- Forgetting that dry HCl gas **does not conduct electricity**.
- Assuming HCl can be collected over water (it cannot because it is highly soluble).
- Mixing up the ratio of **aqua regia** (3:1, HCl:HNO₃).
- Forgetting that **AgCl dissolves in excess ammonium hydroxide**.
- Confusing the fountain experiment with gas pressure experiments.
- Assuming HCl is prepared above 200°C in the laboratory.

# Chapter Summary

- **Hydrogen chloride (HCl)** is a colourless, pungent-smelling covalent gas that is highly soluble in water, forming **hydrochloric acid**, a strong acid.
- In the laboratory, HCl gas is prepared by heating **sodium chloride** with **concentrated sulfuric acid** below **200°C** and is collected by **upward displacement of air**.
- The **Fountain Experiment** demonstrates the exceptional solubility of hydrogen chloride gas in water.
- Hydrochloric acid reacts with **metals, metal carbonates, and ammonia**, producing hydrogen gas, carbon dioxide, or ammonium chloride, depending on the reactant.
- **Aqua regia**, a **3:1 mixture of concentrated hydrochloric acid and nitric acid**, is capable of dissolving noble metals such as **gold and platinum**.
- **Chloride ions** are identified by adding **silver nitrate solution**, which forms a **curdy white precipitate of silver chloride** that dissolves in excess ammonium hydroxide.
- Hydrogen chloride and hydrochloric acid have wide applications in **laboratories, industries, pharmaceuticals, food processing, and metal cleaning**.
