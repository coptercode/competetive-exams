# Introduction

A **triangle** is a three-sided polygon and one of the most important figures in geometry. Two triangles are said to be **congruent** if they are exactly equal in shape and size. Congruency helps in proving many geometric properties and theorems. This chapter covers the criteria for congruency, properties of isosceles triangles, the Midpoint Theorem, the Intercept Theorem, and inequalities related to triangles.

---

# 1. Congruency of Triangles

## Congruent Triangles

Two triangles are **congruent** if all their corresponding sides and corresponding angles are equal.

### Symbol

```

ΔABC ≅ ΔPQR

```

---

## Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles (CPCT)

If two triangles are congruent, then their corresponding sides and corresponding angles are equal.

### Example

If

```

ΔABC ≅ ΔPQR

```

Then

```

AB = PQ

BC = QR

AC = PR

∠A = ∠P

∠B = ∠Q

∠C = ∠R

```

---

# 2. Criteria for Congruency

---

## SSS (Side-Side-Side)

If the three sides of one triangle are equal to the three corresponding sides of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.

### Condition

```

AB = PQ

BC = QR

AC = PR

```

Therefore,

```

ΔABC ≅ ΔPQR

```

---

## SAS (Side-Angle-Side)

If two sides and the included angle of one triangle are equal to the corresponding parts of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.

### Condition

```

AB = PQ

∠B = ∠Q

BC = QR

```

---

## ASA (Angle-Side-Angle)

If two angles and the included side are equal, then the triangles are congruent.

---

## AAS (Angle-Angle-Side)

If two angles and one corresponding side are equal, then the triangles are congruent.

---

## RHS (Right Angle-Hypotenuse-Side)

Applicable only for right-angled triangles.

If

- One right angle
- Hypotenuse
- One corresponding side

are equal, then the triangles are congruent.

---

# 3. Isosceles Triangle Theorems

## Isosceles Triangle

A triangle having two equal sides.

```

AB = AC

```

Then

```

ΔABC

```

is an isosceles triangle.

---

## Theorem 1

### Angles Opposite Equal Sides are Equal

If

```

AB = AC

```

Then

```

∠B = ∠C

```

---

## Converse

If

```

∠B = ∠C

```

Then

```

AB = AC

```

---

## Equilateral Triangle

All sides are equal.

```

AB = BC = AC

```

Each angle is

```

60°

```

---

# 4. Midpoint Theorem

## Statement

The line joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is:

- Parallel to the third side.
- Equal to half of the third side.

---

### Diagram

```

      A
     /\
    /  \

D----E
/ \
B--------C

```

Where

```

D and E

are midpoints.

```

Then

```

DE || BC

DE = ½ BC

```

---

## Converse of Midpoint Theorem

A line passing through the midpoint of one side of a triangle and parallel to another side bisects the third side.

---

## Applications

- Construction problems
- Parallel lines
- Length calculations
- Coordinate geometry

---

# 5. Intercept Theorem (Basic Proportionality Theorem)

## Statement

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle and intersects the other two sides, then it divides those sides proportionally.

---

### Diagram

```

      A
     /\
    /  \

D----E
/ \
B--------C

```

If

```

DE || BC

```

Then

```

AD/DB

=

AE/EC

```

---

## Converse

If

```

AD/DB

=

AE/EC

```

Then

```

DE || BC

```

---

## Applications

- Similar figures
- Ratio problems
- Construction
- Coordinate geometry

---

# 6. Triangle Inequalities

## Triangle Inequality Theorem

The sum of any two sides of a triangle is greater than the third side.

```

AB + BC > AC

BC + AC > AB

AB + AC > BC

```

---

## Angle-Side Relationship

The greater side lies opposite the greater angle.

Example

If

```

AB > AC

```

Then

```

∠C > ∠B

```

---

## Converse

The greater angle lies opposite the greater side.

---

## Exterior Angle Property

An exterior angle of a triangle is greater than either of its opposite interior angles.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **congruent** triangles with **similar** triangles.
- Applying the RHS criterion to non-right triangles.
- Forgetting that the included angle is required for SAS congruency.
- Misidentifying corresponding sides and angles.
- Using the Midpoint Theorem without confirming the points are actual midpoints.
- Reversing the ratios in the Intercept Theorem.
- Ignoring the Triangle Inequality Theorem before determining whether three lengths can form a triangle.
- Assuming equal angles imply congruent triangles without sufficient information.

# Chapter Summary

- **Congruent triangles** have exactly the same shape and size.
- The five criteria for proving congruency are **SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, and RHS**.
- By **CPCT**, corresponding sides and angles of congruent triangles are equal.
- In an **isosceles triangle**, angles opposite equal sides are equal, and the converse is also true.
- The **Midpoint Theorem** states that the line joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and half its length.
- The **Intercept (Basic Proportionality) Theorem** establishes proportional division of sides by a line parallel to one side of a triangle.
- The **Triangle Inequality Theorem** ensures that the sum of any two sides is always greater than the third side.
- These concepts provide the foundation for proving geometric results and are essential for higher studies in geometry.
