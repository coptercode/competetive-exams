# Introduction

**Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants prepare their own food using **carbon dioxide**, **water**, and **sunlight** in the presence of **chlorophyll**. During this process, light energy is converted into chemical energy stored in the form of **glucose**, and **oxygen** is released as a by-product. Photosynthesis is the primary source of food and oxygen for nearly all living organisms on Earth.

---

# 1. Photosynthesis

## Definition

**Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants synthesize glucose from carbon dioxide and water using sunlight in the presence of chlorophyll.

---

## Chemical Equation

```
        Sunlight
6CO₂ + 6H₂O  ─────────►  C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
        Chlorophyll
```

---

# 2. Requirements for Photosynthesis

The essential requirements are:

- Sunlight
- Chlorophyll
- Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
- Water (H₂O)
- Suitable temperature

---

# 3. Chloroplast

## Definition

A **Chloroplast** is the green plastid present in plant cells where photosynthesis takes place.

---

## Structure of Chloroplast

A chloroplast consists of:

- Outer membrane
- Inner membrane
- Stroma
- Grana
- Thylakoids
- Chlorophyll pigments

---

## Grana

Stacks of thylakoids containing chlorophyll where the **light-dependent reactions** occur.

---

## Stroma

The fluid-filled matrix surrounding the grana where the **Calvin Cycle (dark reaction)** takes place.

---

# 4. Chlorophyll

## Definition

**Chlorophyll** is the green pigment that absorbs light energy required for photosynthesis.

---

## Functions

- Absorbs sunlight.
- Traps light energy.
- Converts light energy into chemical energy.

---

# 5. Phases of Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis occurs in **two stages**.

---

## (i) Light-Dependent Reaction

### Site

Grana (Thylakoid membranes)

---

### Requirements

- Sunlight
- Chlorophyll
- Water

---

### Events

- Light energy is absorbed.
- Water undergoes **photolysis**.
- Oxygen is released.
- ATP is produced.
- NADPH is formed.

---

## Photolysis

### Definition

**Photolysis** is the splitting of water molecules into hydrogen ions, electrons, and oxygen in the presence of light.

### Equation

```
2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂
```

---

# 6. Dark Reaction (Calvin Cycle)

## Site

Stroma

---

## Characteristics

- Does not require direct sunlight.
- Uses ATP and NADPH produced during the light reaction.
- Carbon dioxide is converted into glucose.

---

## Importance

- Food synthesis.
- Storage of chemical energy.

---

# 7. Glucose

## Fate of Glucose

Glucose produced during photosynthesis is used for:

- Respiration
- Growth
- Formation of starch
- Cellulose synthesis
- Protein synthesis (with minerals)
- Fat synthesis

---

# 8. Factors Affecting Photosynthesis

## Light Intensity

- Increases the rate up to an optimum level.
- Very low light decreases the rate.

---

## Carbon Dioxide Concentration

- Increased CO₂ increases the rate up to a limit.

---

## Temperature

- Optimum temperature promotes enzyme activity.
- Very high or very low temperatures reduce the rate.

---

## Water

- Essential raw material.
- Water deficiency reduces photosynthesis.

---

## Chlorophyll

- Healthy green leaves photosynthesize efficiently.
- Chlorophyll deficiency decreases the rate.

---

# 9. Limiting Factors

## Definition

A **Limiting Factor** is the factor present in the least amount that limits the rate of photosynthesis.

---

### Examples

- Light intensity
- Carbon dioxide concentration
- Temperature
- Water availability

---

# 10. Experiments to Demonstrate Photosynthesis

## (i) Requirement of Light

### Aim

To show that sunlight is necessary for photosynthesis.

### Observation

Only the exposed part of the leaf turns blue-black with iodine.

### Conclusion

Light is essential for photosynthesis.

---

## (ii) Requirement of Chlorophyll

### Example

Variegated leaf experiment.

### Observation

Only the green portions turn blue-black.

### Conclusion

Chlorophyll is necessary for photosynthesis.

---

## (iii) Requirement of Carbon Dioxide

### Experiment

Plant kept in a bell jar with potassium hydroxide (KOH).

### Observation

The leaf does not form starch.

### Conclusion

Carbon dioxide is necessary for photosynthesis.

---

## (iv) Test for Starch

### Procedure

- Boil the leaf in water.
- Boil in alcohol to remove chlorophyll.
- Wash in warm water.
- Add iodine solution.

### Observation

Blue-black colour indicates starch.

---

# 11. Importance of Photosynthesis

- Produces food.
- Releases oxygen.
- Maintains atmospheric oxygen and carbon dioxide balance.
- Stores solar energy as chemical energy.
- Supports food chains.
- Provides raw materials for living organisms.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Photosynthesis** with **Respiration**.
- Forgetting that chlorophyll is located in the **chloroplast**.
- Mixing up the **light reaction** and **dark reaction**.
- Assuming the dark reaction occurs only at night.
- Forgetting that oxygen is released during **photolysis**.
- Ignoring the role of **ATP** and **NADPH**.
- Confusing **stroma** with **grana**.
- Forgetting the steps of the starch test.

# Chapter Summary

- **Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants manufacture glucose using **carbon dioxide**, **water**, **sunlight**, and **chlorophyll**, releasing **oxygen** as a by-product.
- The process takes place in the **chloroplast**, where the **grana** carry out the **light-dependent reactions** and the **stroma** carries out the **Calvin Cycle (dark reaction)**.
- During the **light reaction**, chlorophyll absorbs light energy, water undergoes **photolysis**, oxygen is released, and **ATP** and **NADPH** are produced.
- During the **dark reaction**, carbon dioxide is fixed into glucose using the ATP and NADPH generated in the light reaction.
- The rate of photosynthesis depends on **light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, temperature, water availability, and chlorophyll content**, with the least available factor acting as the **limiting factor**.
- Simple laboratory experiments demonstrate that **light, chlorophyll, and carbon dioxide** are all essential for photosynthesis.
- Photosynthesis is the foundation of nearly all ecosystems, maintaining atmospheric oxygen levels, supporting food chains, and converting solar energy into chemical energy.
