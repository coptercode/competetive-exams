# Introduction

**Chemical kinetics** is the branch of chemistry that deals with:

- Rate of chemical reactions
- Factors affecting reaction rate
- Mechanism of reactions

---

# 1. Rate of Reaction

## Definition

The change in concentration of reactants or products per unit time is called the rate of reaction.

---

For a reaction:

\[
A \rightarrow B
\]

Rate:

\[
Rate=-\frac{\Delta[A]}{\Delta t}
\]

or

\[
Rate=\frac{\Delta[B]}{\Delta t}
\]

---

# 2. Average Rate of Reaction

## Definition

Rate measured over a particular time interval.

Formula:

\[
Average\ Rate=
-\frac{Change\ in\ concentration}{Time}
\]

---

Example:

If concentration decreases from 1M to 0.5M in 10 seconds:

\[
Rate=\frac{1-0.5}{10}
\]

\[
=0.05M/s
\]

---

# 3. Instantaneous Rate

## Definition

Rate of reaction at a specific moment of time.

---

It is obtained by:

```
Slope of concentration-time graph
```

# 1. Effect of Concentration

According to collision theory:

Higher concentration:

→ More collisions

→ Faster reaction

---

# 2. Effect of Temperature

Increase in temperature:

- Increases kinetic energy
- Increases effective collisions

Therefore:

Reaction rate increases.

---

# 3. Effect of Catalyst

Catalyst increases reaction rate by lowering activation energy.

---

# 4. Effect of Surface Area

Powdered substances react faster than large pieces.

Reason:

Greater surface area provides more collision sites.

---

# 5. Rate Law

## Definition

The relationship between reaction rate and reactant concentration is called rate law.

---

For reaction:

\[
aA+bB\rightarrow Products
\]

Rate law:

\[
Rate=k[A]^m[B]^n
\]

---

Where:

- k = Rate constant
- m,n = Order with respect to reactants

---

# 6. Rate Constant (k)

## Definition

Rate constant is the proportionality constant in rate law.

---

It depends on:

- Temperature
- Nature of reaction
- Catalyst

---

# 7. Order of Reaction

## Definition

---

For:

\[
Rate=k[A]^m[B]^n
\]

Order:

\[
=m+n
\]

# 8. Zero Order Reaction

## Definition

Rate is independent of reactant concentration.

---

Rate law:

\[
Rate=k[A]^0
\]

Therefore:

\[
Rate=k
\]

---

# Integrated Rate Equation

\[
[A]_t=[A]_0-kt
\]

---

Where:

- [A]₀ = Initial concentration
- [A]t = Concentration after time t

---

# Half-Life of Zero Order Reaction

\[
t_{1/2}
=

\frac{[A]_0}{2k}
\]

---

# 9. First Order Reaction

## Definition

Rate depends on concentration of one reactant.

---

Rate law:

\[
Rate=k[A]
\]

---

# Integrated Rate Equation

\[
k=
\frac{2.303}{t}
\log
\frac{[A]_0}{[A]_t}
\]

---

# Half-Life

\[
t_{1/2}
=

\frac{0.693}{k}
\]

---

# Important Point

Half-life is independent of initial concentration.

---

# 10. Second Order Reaction

## Definition

Rate depends on square of concentration or two reactants.

---

Rate law:

\[
Rate=k[A]^2
\]

---

# Integrated Rate Equation

\[
\frac{1}{[A]_t}
=

\frac{1}{[A]_0}+kt
\]

---

# Half-Life

\[
t_{1/2}
=

\frac{1}{k[A]_0}
\]

---

# 11. Molecularity of Reaction

## Definition

Number of reacting species participating in an elementary reaction step.

---

# Types

## Unimolecular

One molecule involved.

Example:

\[
A\rightarrow Products
\]

---

## Bimolecular

Two molecules involved.

Example:

\[
A+B\rightarrow Products
\]

---

## Termolecular

Three molecules involved.

---

# Difference Between Order and Molecularity

| Order                       | Molecularity               |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Experimental quantity       | Theoretical quantity       |
| Can be zero or fractional   | Always whole number        |
| Applies to overall reaction | Applies to elementary step |

---

# 13. Arrhenius Equation

## Definition

Shows relationship between rate constant and temperature.

---

Formula:

\[
k=Ae^{-E_a/RT}
\]

---

Where:

- A = Frequency factor
- Ea = Activation energy
- R = Gas constant
- T = Temperature

---

# Log Form

\[
\log k=
\log A-\frac{E_a}{2.303RT}
\]

---

# 14. Effect of Catalyst

Catalyst:

- Lowers activation energy
- Provides alternate pathway
- Increases reaction rate

---

Energy diagram:

```

Higher Energy Barrier


With catalyst

Lower Energy Barrier
```

---

# 15. Temperature Dependence of Rate

Arrhenius equation:

\[
\log\frac{k_2}{k_1}
=

\frac{E_a}{2.303R}
\left(
\frac{T_2-T_1}{T_1T_2}
\right)
\]

---

# 16. Pseudo First Order Reaction

## Definition

A reaction that appears first order due to excess concentration of one reactant.

---

Example:

Hydrolysis of ester:

\[
CH_3COOC_2H_5+H_2O
\rightarrow
CH_3COOH+C_2H_5OH
\]

---

Water is taken in excess.

**Answer:**

## 3. What is activation energy?

**Answer:**

The minimum energy required for reactant molecules to undergo effective collision and form products.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing order with molecularity.
- Forgetting negative sign for reactant concentration.
- Using wrong half-life formula.
- Ignoring units of rate constant.
- Mixing activation energy and enthalpy.

- **Chemical Kinetics** explains the speed and mechanism of chemical reactions.
- Reaction rate depends on concentration, temperature, catalyst, and surface area.
- Rate laws determine reaction order experimentally.
