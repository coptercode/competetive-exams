# Introduction

Plants require **water** and **mineral salts** for growth, photosynthesis, respiration, and other metabolic activities. These substances are mainly absorbed by the **roots**, especially through the **root hairs**. Water enters the root cells by **osmosis**, while mineral ions are absorbed by both **diffusion** and **active transport**. The absorbed water and minerals are then transported to different parts of the plant through the **xylem**.

# 2. Root System

## Definition

The **Root System** is the underground part of a plant responsible for anchorage and absorption of water and minerals.

---

## Functions

- Absorbs water.
- Absorbs mineral salts.
- Anchors the plant.
- Stores food (in some plants).
- Conducts water to the stem.

---

# 3. Root Hair

## Definition

A **Root Hair** is a long, thin, tubular extension of an epidermal cell of the root.

---

## Structure

- Thin cell wall
- Cell membrane
- Large vacuole
- Dense cytoplasm
- Nucleus

---

## Adaptations of Root Hairs

- Large surface area for absorption.
- Thin and permeable cell wall.
- Large vacuole containing concentrated cell sap.
- Close contact with soil particles.
- Numerous root hairs increase absorption efficiency.

---

# 4. Imbibition

## Definition

**Imbibition** is the absorption of water by solid colloidal substances without forming a solution.

---

## Examples

- Swelling of dry seeds.
- Absorption of water by dry wood.
- Germination of seeds.

---

## Importance

- Helps seed germination.
- Softens seed coat.
- Initiates metabolic activities.

---

# 5. Diffusion

## Definition

**Diffusion** is the movement of molecules from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.

---

## Characteristics

- Passive process.
- No energy required.
- Continues until equilibrium is reached.

---

## Examples

- Spread of perfume smell.
- Exchange of gases in leaves.
- Movement of mineral ions (in some cases).

---

# 6. Osmosis

## Definition

**Osmosis** is the movement of water molecules through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water concentration to a region of lower water concentration.

---

## Conditions Required

- Selectively permeable membrane.
- Difference in water concentration.
- Two solutions of different concentrations.

---

## Types of Solutions

### Hypotonic Solution

Contains more water and less solute.

Water enters the cell.

---

### Hypertonic Solution

Contains less water and more solute.

Water leaves the cell.

---

### Isotonic Solution

Equal concentration inside and outside the cell.

No net movement of water.

---

# 7. Turgidity

## Definition

A plant cell becomes **Turgid** when water enters by osmosis, causing the cell to swell.

---

## Characteristics

- Vacuole enlarges.
- Cell becomes firm.
- Turgor pressure develops.
- Plant remains erect.

---

## Importance

- Maintains rigidity.
- Supports leaves and stems.
- Helps in opening stomata.

---

# 8. Plasmolysis

## Definition

**Plasmolysis** is the shrinking of the cytoplasm away from the cell wall due to loss of water by osmosis.

---

## Causes

- Hypertonic solution.
- Loss of water from the cell.

---

## Effects

- Cell becomes flaccid.
- Plant wilts.
- Growth is reduced.

---

# 9. Active Transport

## Definition

**Active Transport** is the movement of substances from a region of lower concentration to a region of higher concentration using cellular energy (ATP).

---

## Characteristics

- Requires ATP.
- Moves against the concentration gradient.
- Carried out by carrier proteins.

---

## Importance

- Absorption of mineral ions.
- Essential nutrient uptake.
- Maintains ion balance.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Diffusion** with **Osmosis**.
- Assuming active transport does not require energy.
- Forgetting that osmosis involves only **water molecules**.
- Mixing up **Turgidity** and **Plasmolysis**.
- Assuming mineral salts are absorbed only by diffusion.
- Forgetting that **root hairs** are the main absorbing structures.
- Confusing hypotonic and hypertonic solutions.
- Ignoring the role of ATP in active transport.

# Chapter Summary

- **Plant Physiology** deals with the study of the functions and life processes of plants.
- **Root hairs** are specialized epidermal cell extensions that efficiently absorb water and mineral salts due to their large surface area, thin walls, and concentrated cell sap.
- **Imbibition** is the absorption of water by dry colloidal substances and plays an important role in seed germination.
- **Diffusion** is the passive movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, while **Osmosis** specifically involves the movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane.
- A plant cell becomes **turgid** when water enters by osmosis, whereas **plasmolysis** occurs when water leaves the cell in a hypertonic solution.
- **Active Transport** uses ATP to move mineral ions against the concentration gradient into root cells.
- Water absorbed by the roots moves through the **xylem** to the stem and leaves, supplying the plant with essential water and dissolved minerals.
- Efficient absorption by roots is essential for plant growth, photosynthesis, respiration, and overall survival.
