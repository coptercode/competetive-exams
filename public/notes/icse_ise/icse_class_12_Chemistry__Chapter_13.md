# Introduction

**Carbohydrates** are organic compounds containing:

- Carbon
- Hydrogen
- Oxygen

General formula:

\[
C_x(H_2O)_y
\]

They are also called:

```
Saccharides
```

---

# Definition

Carbohydrates are **polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones** or compounds that produce them on hydrolysis.

---

Examples:

- Glucose
- Fructose
- Sucrose
- Starch
- Cellulose

---

# 2. Monosaccharides

## Definition

Carbohydrates that cannot be hydrolysed into simpler sugars.

---

Examples:

- Glucose
- Fructose
- Ribose

---

General formula:

\[
C_nH_{2n}O_n
\]

---

# Types of Monosaccharides

Based on functional group:

```
Monosaccharides

       │

 ┌─────┴─────┐

 ▼           ▼

Aldoses    Ketoses
```

---

# Aldoses

Contain aldehyde group:

\[
-CHO
\]

Example:

Glucose

---

# Ketoses

Contain ketone group:

\[

> C=O
> \]

Example:

Fructose

---

# 4. Oligosaccharides

## Definition

Carbohydrates that produce 2–10 monosaccharide molecules on hydrolysis.

---

Examples:

- Sucrose
- Maltose
- Lactose

---

# 5. Polysaccharides

## Definition

Complex carbohydrates made up of many monosaccharide units.

---

Examples:

- Starch
- Cellulose
- Glycogen

---

# 6. Glucose

## Definition

Glucose is an aldohexose sugar.

Molecular formula:

\[
C_6H_{12}O_6
\]

---

Common name:

```
Blood sugar
```

---

# 7. Structure of Glucose

Open chain structure:

Contains:

- One aldehyde group
- Five hydroxyl groups

---

Structure:

\[
CHO-(CHOH)_4-CH_2OH
\]

---

# 8. Cyclic Structure of Glucose

Glucose exists mainly in cyclic form.

It forms:

```
α-glucose

and

β-glucose
```

---

# 9. Mutarotation

## Definition

Change in optical rotation due to interconversion of α and β forms of glucose.

---

Process:

```
α-D-Glucose

      ⇌

Open Chain Form

      ⇌

β-D-Glucose
```

---

# 10. Fructose

## Definition

Fructose is a ketohexose sugar.

Formula:

\[
C_6H_{12}O_6
\]

---

Functional group:

\[

> C=O
> \]

---

Common name:

```
Fruit sugar
```

# 11. Difference Between Glucose and Fructose

| Glucose             | Fructose              |
| ------------------- | --------------------- |
| Aldohexose          | Ketohexose            |
| Contains -CHO group | Contains ketone group |

# 12. Disaccharides

Disaccharides are formed by joining two monosaccharides.

---

# Glycosidic Bond

The bond connecting two sugar molecules is called:

```

```

# 13. Sucrose

## Definition

Sucrose is a disaccharide formed from glucose and fructose.

---

Reaction:

\[
Glucose+Fructose
\rightarrow
Sucrose+H_2O
\]

---

Sources:

- Sugar cane
- Sugar beet

---

# Important Point

Sucrose is:

```
Non-reducing sugar
```

# 16. Reducing and Non-Reducing Sugars

---

# Reducing Sugars

Contain free aldehyde or ketone group.

Examples:

- Glucose
- Fructose
- Maltose
- Lactose

# Non-Reducing Sugar

No free carbonyl group.

Example:


# Exam Important Questions

## 1. Define carbohydrates.

**Answer:**

Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones or compounds that produce them on hydrolysis.

---

## 2. What is mutarotation?

**Answer:**

The change in optical rotation due to conversion between α and β forms of glucose is called mutarotation.

---

## 3. Why is sucrose a non-reducing sugar?

**Answer:**

Because both anomeric carbon atoms are involved in glycosidic bond formation and no free carbonyl group is available.

---

## 4. Differentiate starch and cellulose.

| Starch               | Cellulose               |
| -------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Storage carbohydrate | Structural carbohydrate |
| α-glucose units      | β-glucose units         |

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing glucose and fructose structures.
- Forgetting sucrose is non-reducing.
- Mixing starch and cellulose functions.
- Confusing aldose and ketose sugars.
- Forgetting glycosidic linkage.
