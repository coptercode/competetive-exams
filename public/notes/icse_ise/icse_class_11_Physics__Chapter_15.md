# Introduction

This chapter focuses on the advanced applications of gravitational principles in **planetary motion, satellites, orbital mechanics, escape velocity, and space technology**.

Gravity controls the motion of planets, moons, artificial satellites, and galaxies. Understanding gravitational interactions helps in designing **space missions, communication satellites, GPS systems, and astronomical observations**.

# 1. Artificial Satellites

## Definition

An **artificial satellite** is a man-made object launched into space that revolves around a planet or celestial body in a fixed orbit.

---

# Examples

- Communication satellites
- Weather satellites
- Navigation satellites
- Remote sensing satellites

---

# 2. Orbital Motion of Satellites

A satellite remains in orbit because the gravitational force provides the necessary centripetal force.

---

# Gravitational Force

```
F = GMm/r²
```

---

# Centripetal Force

```
F = mv²/r
```

---

Equating:

```
GMm/r² = mv²/r
```

Therefore,

```
v = √GM/r
```

---

# Orbital Velocity

## Definition

The velocity required for a satellite to remain in a stable circular orbit.

---

# Formula

```
Vo = √GM/r
```

Where:

- G = Gravitational constant
- M = Mass of Earth
- r = Orbital radius

---

# Near Earth's Surface

```
Vo = √gR
```

Value:

```
≈ 7.9 km/s
```

---

# 3. Time Period of Satellite

## Definition

The time taken by a satellite to complete one revolution around Earth.

---

# Formula

```
T = 2π√(r³/GM)
```

---

# Relation

```
T² ∝ r³
```

---

# Kepler's Third Law

The square of orbital period is directly proportional to the cube of orbital radius.

---

# 4. Escape Velocity

## Definition

The minimum velocity required by an object to escape from a planet's gravitational field.

---

# Formula

```
Ve = √2GM/R
```

or

```
Ve = √2gR
```

---

# Earth Escape Velocity

```
11.2 km/s
```

---

# Relation with Orbital Velocity

```
Ve = √2Vo
```

---

# Difference Between Orbital and Escape Velocity

| Orbital Velocity         | Escape Velocity            |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Keeps satellite in orbit | Allows object to escape    |
| 7.9 km/s                 | 11.2 km/s                  |
| Circular motion          | Leaves gravitational field |

---

# 5. Geostationary Satellites

## Definition

A satellite that appears stationary relative to Earth because its orbital period equals Earth's rotation period.

---

# Conditions

1. Orbit must be circular.
2. Orbit must be in equatorial plane.
3. Satellite moves from west to east.
4. Time period = 24 hours.

---

# Height Above Earth

Approximately:

```
36000 km
```

---

# Applications

- Television broadcasting.
- Communication.
- Internet services.
- Weather monitoring.

---

# 6. Polar Satellites

## Definition

Satellites that move in an orbit passing over Earth's poles.

---

# Characteristics

- Low altitude orbit.
- Period around 90 minutes.
- Cover entire Earth.

---

# Applications

- Weather forecasting.
- Remote sensing.
- Mapping.
- Environmental monitoring.

---

# 7. Weightlessness

## Definition

The condition in which apparent weight of a body becomes zero.

---

# Cause

Objects in orbit experience continuous free fall.

---

# Examples

- Astronauts inside spacecraft.
- Floating objects in satellites.

---

# Difference Between Mass and Weight in Space

| Mass             | Weight                         |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Remains constant | Becomes zero in weightlessness |
| Scalar quantity  | Force                          |

---

# 8. Gravitational Potential Energy of Satellite

Energy possessed due to position in gravitational field.

---

# Formula

```
U = -GMm/r
```

---

# Total Energy of Satellite

Total energy:

```
E = KE + PE
```

---

# Formula

```
E = -GMm/2r
```

---

# Important Point

Total energy of a bound satellite is always negative.

---

# 9. Geosynchronous Satellites

## Definition

A satellite whose orbital period is equal to Earth's rotational period.

---

# Difference

| Geostationary    | Geosynchronous     |
| ---------------- | ------------------ |
| Appears fixed    | May appear moving  |
| Equatorial orbit | Any inclined orbit |

# 10. Rocket Propulsion

## Principle

Based on conservation of linear momentum.

---

# Working

Hot gases are expelled downward.

The rocket moves upward due to reaction force.

---

# Thrust

```
F = rate of change of momentum
```

---

# Applications

- Space missions.
- Satellite launching.
- Exploration missions.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

### Question

Find orbital velocity of a satellite near Earth's surface.

Given:

```
g = 9.8 m/s²
R = 6.4×10⁶ m
```

### Solution

```
Vo = √gR

= √(9.8×6.4×10⁶)

≈ 7.9×10³ m/s
```

### Answer

**7.9 km/s**

---

## Example 2

### Question

Find escape velocity from Earth.

### Solution

```
Ve = √2gR

= √(2×9.8×6.4×10⁶)

≈ 11200 m/s
```

### Answer

**11.2 km/s**

---

## Example 3

### Question

A satellite has mass 1000 kg and orbital radius r. Write its total energy.

### Solution

```
E = -GMm/2r
```

### Answer

**Negative total energy indicates bound orbit**

---

## Example 4

### Question

Why do astronauts feel weightless?

### Solution

Because they and their spacecraft are in continuous free fall around Earth.

---

## Example 5

### Question

Why are geostationary satellites useful?

### Solution

They remain fixed relative to Earth and provide continuous communication.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing orbital velocity with escape velocity.
- Forgetting radius includes Earth's radius plus satellite height.
- Using Celsius instead of Kelvin in temperature-based problems.
- Assuming satellites are outside Earth's gravity.
- Confusing geostationary and geosynchronous satellites.

# Chapter Summary

- Artificial satellites move around Earth because gravity provides the required centripetal force.
- **Orbital velocity** allows satellites to remain in orbit, while **escape velocity** allows objects to leave a planet's gravitational field.
- **Geostationary satellites** are essential for communication, whereas **polar satellites** are used for weather monitoring and Earth observation.
- Satellite motion follows **Kepler's laws of planetary motion**.
- Concepts of gravitational energy, weightlessness, and rocket propulsion form the foundation of modern **space technology, navigation systems, communication networks, and astronomical research**.
