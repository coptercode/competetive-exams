# Introduction

**Hydrocarbons** are the simplest organic compounds containing only **carbon** and **hydrogen** atoms. They are the basic building blocks of organic chemistry and occur naturally in **petroleum, natural gas, and coal**.

Hydrocarbons are classified into **aliphatic** and **aromatic** compounds. Aliphatic hydrocarbons include **alkanes, alkenes, and alkynes**, while aromatic hydrocarbons contain one or more **benzene rings**.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing the general formulas of **alkanes, alkenes, and alkynes**.
- Assuming benzene undergoes addition reactions under ordinary conditions.
- Forgetting that **terminal alkynes** show weak acidic nature.
- Mixing up **hydrogenation** and **halogenation** reactions.
- Incorrectly writing the structure of benzene.
- Confusing bromine water test with combustion reactions.

# Chapter Summary

- **Hydrocarbons** are organic compounds composed exclusively of **carbon and hydrogen** and are classified into **aliphatic** and **aromatic** hydrocarbons.
- **Alkanes** are saturated hydrocarbons that mainly undergo **substitution reactions**, whereas **alkenes** and **alkynes** are unsaturated hydrocarbons that readily undergo **addition reactions**.
- **Benzene** is the simplest aromatic hydrocarbon, possessing exceptional stability due to **resonance** and obeying **Hückel's rule**. It predominantly undergoes **electrophilic substitution reactions** such as nitration, halogenation, sulphonation, and Friedel–Crafts reactions.
- Hydrocarbons are major constituents of **petroleum** and **natural gas**, making them essential sources of fuels and raw materials for the chemical industry.
- The study of hydrocarbons forms the foundation for understanding advanced organic chemistry, polymers, pharmaceuticals, petrochemicals, and industrial synthesis.
