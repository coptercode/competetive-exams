# Introduction

**Practical Chemistry** involves performing laboratory experiments to understand chemical principles through observation and analysis. It includes the **identification of gases, tests for cations and anions, action of heat on compounds, preparation of salts, use of indicators, and laboratory safety**. Practical work helps students develop observation, analytical thinking, and experimental skills.

---

# 1. Laboratory Safety Rules

## General Safety Precautions

- Wear a laboratory coat and safety goggles.
- Handle acids and alkalis carefully.
- Never taste chemicals.
- Do not smell chemicals directly; use the **wafting** method.
- Always add **acid to water**, never water to acid.
- Keep the work area clean.
- Wash hands after experiments.

# 3. Identification of Gases

## (i) Hydrogen (H₂)

### Test

Bring a burning splint near the gas.

### Observation

```
Burns with a 'pop' sound.
```

---

## (ii) Oxygen (O₂)

### Test

Insert a glowing splint.

### Observation

```
Glowing splint relights.
```

---

## (iii) Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)

### Test

Bubble the gas through lime water.

### Observation

```
Lime water turns milky.
```

### Reaction

```
Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂

↓

CaCO₃ + H₂O
```

---

## (iv) Ammonia (NH₃)

### Tests

- Pungent smell.
- Turns moist red litmus blue.
- Forms dense white fumes with HCl.

---

## (v) Chlorine (Cl₂)

### Test

Expose moist blue litmus paper.

### Observation

```
Turns red and then bleaches.
```

---

## (vi) Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂)

### Test

Pass through acidified potassium dichromate solution.

### Observation

```
Orange solution turns green.
```

Using **NaOH** and **NH₄OH**

| Cation | NaOH                        | NH₄OH                        |
| ------ | --------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Cu²⁺   | Light blue ppt              | Deep blue solution in excess |
| Fe²⁺   | Dirty green ppt             | Dirty green ppt              |
| Fe³⁺   | Reddish-brown ppt           | Reddish-brown ppt            |
| Zn²⁺   | White ppt soluble in excess | White ppt soluble in excess  |
| Al³⁺   | White ppt soluble in excess | White ppt insoluble          |
| Pb²⁺   | White ppt soluble in excess | White ppt insoluble          |

# 7. Preparation of Salts

## (i) By Neutralization

```
HCl + NaOH

↓

NaCl + H₂O
```

---

## (ii) By Precipitation

```
AgNO₃ + NaCl

↓

AgCl↓ + NaNO₃
```

---

## (iii) By Action of Acid on Metal

```
Zn + H₂SO₄

↓

ZnSO₄ + H₂↑
```

---

## (iv) By Action of Acid on Carbonate

```
CaCO₃ + 2HCl

↓

CaCl₂ + CO₂ + H₂O
```

# 8. Crystallization

## Definition

**Crystallization** is the process of obtaining pure crystals of a soluble substance from its saturated solution.

---

## Steps

1. Prepare a saturated solution.
2. Filter impurities.
3. Heat gently.
4. Allow to cool.
5. Collect crystals.
6. Dry the crystals.

---

# 9. Filtration

## Definition

**Filtration** is the process of separating an insoluble solid from a liquid using filter paper.

---

# 10. Distillation

## Definition

**Distillation** is used to separate a liquid from a solution based on differences in boiling points.

---

# Difference Between Filtration and Crystallization

| Filtration                 | Crystallization              |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Separates insoluble solids | Obtains pure soluble solids  |
| Uses filter paper          | Uses evaporation and cooling |
| Physical separation        | Purification technique       |

# Solved Examples

```

## Example 2

Which reagent is used to test chloride ions?

### Solution

Silver nitrate forms an insoluble white precipitate.

### Answer

```
Silver nitrate (AgNO₃)
```

```

What is crystallization?

### Solution

It is used to obtain pure crystals from a saturated solution.

### Answer

The process of obtaining pure crystals of a soluble substance from its saturated solution.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing the tests for **oxygen** and **hydrogen**.
- Mixing up **chloride** and **sulfate** tests.
- Forgetting that **CO₂ turns lime water milky**.
- Confusing **filtration** with **crystallization**.
- Ignoring laboratory safety precautions.
- Misidentifying precipitate colours in cation analysis.
- Using incorrect reagents for anion tests.

# Chapter Summary

- **Practical Chemistry** develops experimental skills through laboratory techniques, observations, and chemical analysis.
- Common laboratory activities include the **identification of gases, cations, and anions**, as well as **salt preparation, crystallization, filtration, and distillation**.
- Characteristic tests such as the **pop test for hydrogen**, **lime water test for carbon dioxide**, and **silver nitrate test for chloride ions** are essential for qualitative analysis.
- Reagents like **sodium hydroxide** and **ammonium hydroxide** help identify metal ions based on the **colour and solubility of precipitates**.
- Following **laboratory safety rules** is essential for preventing accidents and ensuring accurate experimental results.
- Practical chemistry forms the foundation for scientific investigation and has important applications in **research, medicine, environmental science, industry, and education**.
