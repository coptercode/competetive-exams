# Introduction

**Electromagnetic Induction** is the phenomenon of production of an induced emf and current in a conductor due to a change in magnetic flux linked with it.

Discovered by:

```
Michael Faraday (1831)
```

---

# 1. Magnetic Flux

## Definition

The amount of magnetic field passing through a surface is called magnetic flux.

---

Formula:

\[
\Phi_B = \vec{B}\cdot\vec{A}
\]

For angle θ:

\[
\Phi_B=BA\cos\theta
\]

---

Where:

- B = Magnetic field
- A = Area of surface
- θ = Angle between B and normal to surface

---

# Unit

\[
Weber(Wb)
\]

---

# 2. Faraday's Laws of Electromagnetic Induction

---

# First Law

## Statement

Whenever the magnetic flux linked with a circuit changes, an induced emf is produced in the circuit.

---

# Second Law

## Statement

The magnitude of induced emf is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux.

---

Formula:

\[
e\propto\frac{d\Phi}{dt}
\]

For N turns:

\[
e=-N\frac{d\Phi}{dt}
\]

---

# 3. Lenz's Law

## Statement

The induced current always flows in such a direction that it opposes the cause producing it.

---

Mathematical representation:

\[
e=-\frac{d\Phi}{dt}
\]

The negative sign represents Lenz's law.

---

# 4. Direction of Induced Current

Determined by:

```
Lenz's Law
```

and

```
Fleming's Right Hand Rule
```

---

# Fleming's Right Hand Rule

Hold the thumb, forefinger, and middle finger mutually perpendicular.

- Forefinger → Magnetic field
- Thumb → Motion of conductor
- Middle finger → Induced current

---

# 5. Motional EMF

## Definition

The emf produced when a conductor moves in a magnetic field is called motional emf.

---

Formula:

\[
e=Blv
\]

---

Where:

- B = Magnetic field
- l = Length of conductor
- v = Velocity

---

# 6. Motional EMF in Moving Rod

For a rod moving perpendicular to magnetic field:

\[
e=Blv
\]

---

Current produced:

\[
I=\frac{e}{R}
\]

---

# 7. Energy Consideration in Electromagnetic Induction

When a conductor moves in a magnetic field:

Mechanical energy converts into electrical energy.

---

Principle:

```
Conservation of Energy
```

---

# 8. Eddy Currents

## Definition

Circular currents induced in bulk conductors due to changing magnetic flux are called eddy currents.

---

# Applications

1. Magnetic braking
2. Induction furnace
3. Speedometers
4. Damping in galvanometers

---

# Disadvantages

- Cause energy loss
- Produce heating

---

# Reducing Eddy Current Loss

Using:

```
Laminated iron cores
```

---

# 9. Self Induction

## Definition

The phenomenon in which a changing current in a coil induces emf in the same coil is called self induction.

---

# Self Inductance

Symbol:

\[
L
\]

---

Formula:

\[
e=-L\frac{dI}{dt}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
Henry(H)
\]

---

# 10. Inductance of a Solenoid

Formula:

\[
L=\frac{\mu_0N^2A}{l}
\]

---

Where:

- N = Number of turns
- A = Area
- l = Length of solenoid

---

# 11. Mutual Induction

## Definition

The phenomenon in which change of current in one coil induces emf in another nearby coil is called mutual induction.

---

Formula:

\[
e=-M\frac{dI}{dt}
\]

---

Where:

M = Mutual inductance

---

# 12. Mutual Inductance

## Definition

Mutual inductance is the property by which one coil induces emf in another coil.

---

Unit:

\[
Henry(H)
\]

---

# 13. Energy Stored in an Inductor

An inductor stores energy in its magnetic field.

---

Formula:

\[
U=\frac12LI^2
\]

---

Where:

- L = Inductance
- I = Current

---

# 14. AC Generator

## Definition

A device that converts mechanical energy into electrical energy using electromagnetic induction.

---

# Principle

Based on:

```
Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction
```

---

# Construction

Main parts:

1. Armature coil
2. Magnetic field
3. Slip rings
4. Brushes

---

# Working

When coil rotates in magnetic field:

- Magnetic flux changes
- Induced emf is produced

---

# Generated EMF

\[
e=E_0\sin\omega t
\]

---

# 15. Transformer

## Definition

A device used to increase or decrease AC voltage.

---

# Principle

Based on:

```
Mutual induction
```

---

# Construction

Parts:

1. Primary coil
2. Secondary coil
3. Laminated iron core

---

# 16. Transformer Equation

\[
\frac{V_s}{V_p}
=

\frac{N_s}{N_p}
\]

---

Where:

- Vs = Secondary voltage
- Vp = Primary voltage
- Ns = Secondary turns
- Np = Primary turns

---

# 17. Types of Transformers

---

# Step-Up Transformer

Increases voltage.

Condition:

\[
N_s>N_p
\]

---

# Step-Down Transformer

Decreases voltage.

Condition:

\[
N_s<N_p
\]

---

# 18. Transformer Efficiency

Formula:

\[
\eta=
\frac{Output\ Power}{Input\ Power}
\times100
\]

---

For ideal transformer:

\[
\eta=100\%
\]

---

# Board Important Questions

## 1. State Faraday's laws of electromagnetic induction.

**Answer:**

Whenever magnetic flux linked with a circuit changes, an induced emf is produced. The magnitude of induced emf is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux.

---

## 2. Explain Lenz's law.

**Answer:**

The induced current always opposes the change in magnetic flux that produces it.

---

## 3. Derive motional emf.

\[
e=Blv
\]

Important derivation for exams.

---

## 4. Explain the working of a transformer.

**Answer:**

A transformer works on mutual induction. An alternating current in the primary coil produces changing magnetic flux, inducing emf in the secondary coil.

---

## 5. Why are transformer cores laminated?

**Answer:**

To reduce eddy current losses and improve efficiency.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting negative sign in Faraday's law.
- Confusing self induction and mutual induction.
- Mixing step-up and step-down transformer conditions.
- Forgetting transformer works only with AC.
- Confusing magnetic flux and magnetic field.
