# 1. Amino Acids

## Definition

Amino acids are organic compounds containing:

- Amino group (-NH₂)
- Carboxyl group (-COOH)

General formula:

\[
NH_2-CHR-COOH
\]

---

# Structure of Amino Acid

```
        H
        |
NH₂ — C — COOH
        |
        R
```

Where:

R = Side chain

---

# 2. Classification of Amino Acids

Based on number of amino and carboxyl groups:

---

## 1. Neutral Amino Acids

Contain:

- One amino group
- One carboxyl group

Example:

Glycine

\[
NH_2CH_2COOH
\]

---

## 2. Acidic Amino Acids

Contain extra carboxyl group.

Example:

Glutamic acid

---

## 3. Basic Amino Acids

Contain extra amino group.

Example:

Lysine

---

# 3. Essential and Non-Essential Amino Acids

---

# Essential Amino Acids

Cannot be synthesized by the human body.

Examples:

- Valine
- Leucine
- Lysine
- Phenylalanine

---

# Non-Essential Amino Acids

Synthesized by the body.

Example:

- Glycine
- Alanine

---

# 4. Zwitter Ion

## Definition

Amino acids exist as dipolar ions containing both positive and negative charges.

Structure:

\[
^{+}NH_3-CHR-COO^-
\]

---

Properties:

- Electrically neutral
- High melting point
- Water soluble

---

# 5. Isoelectric Point

## Definition

The pH at which an amino acid exists as a zwitter ion and has no net charge.

---

At isoelectric point:

```
Positive charge = Negative charge
```

---

# 6. Peptide Bond

## Definition

The bond formed between two amino acids by condensation reaction is called a peptide bond.

---

Bond:

\[
-CO-NH-
\]

---

Reaction:

\[
Amino\ acid + Amino\ acid
\rightarrow
Peptide + H_2O
\]

---

# Polypeptide

# 8. Proteins

## Definition

Proteins are high molecular weight polymers of α-amino acids linked by peptide bonds.

---

General structure:

\[
(-NH-CHR-CO-)_{n}
\]

---

# 9. Classification of Proteins

```
               Proteins

                   │

        ┌──────────┴──────────┐

        ▼                     ▼

Fibrous Proteins       Globular Proteins
```

---

# Fibrous Proteins

## Characteristics:

- Long chain structure
- Insoluble in water
- Structural function

Examples:

- Keratin
- Collagen

---

# Globular Proteins

## Characteristics:

- Folded structure
- Soluble in water
- Functional role

Examples:

- Insulin
- Haemoglobin

---

# 10. Structure of Proteins

Protein structure exists at four levels.

```
Protein Structure

        │

 ┌──────┼──────┐

 ▼      ▼      ▼

Primary Secondary Tertiary

              │

              ▼

          Quaternary
```

---

# 1. Primary Structure

## Definition

Sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide chain.

---

Example:

\[
Ala-Gly-Val-Leu
\]

---

# 2. Secondary Structure

Arrangement of polypeptide chain due to hydrogen bonding.

Types:

## α-Helix

Spiral structure.

---

## β-Pleated Sheet

Folded sheet structure.

---

# 3. Tertiary Structure

Three-dimensional folding of a protein chain.

Maintained by:

- Hydrogen bonds
- Disulfide bonds
- Ionic interactions

---

# 4. Quaternary Structure

Arrangement of multiple polypeptide chains.

Example:

Haemoglobin

---

# 11. Denaturation of Proteins

## Definition

Loss of biological activity due to destruction of secondary and tertiary structures is called denaturation.

---

Causes:

- Heat
- pH change
- Chemicals

---

Example:

Boiling of egg white.

---

# 15. Nucleoside

## Definition

A nucleoside contains:

- Sugar
- Nitrogen base

but no phosphate group.

---

Difference:

| Nucleoside   | Nucleotide               |
| ------------ | ------------------------ |
| Sugar + Base | Sugar + Base + Phosphate |
| No phosphate | Contains phosphate       |

---

# 17. DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)

## Definition

DNA stores genetic information in living organisms.

---

Sugar:

```
Deoxyribose
```

Bases:

- Adenine
- Guanine
- Cytosine
- Thymine

---

# Watson-Crick Model of DNA

DNA has:

```
Double Helix Structure
```

---

# Base Pairing Rules

A pairs with T:

\[
A=T
\]

G pairs with C:

\[
G\equiv C
\]

---

# Hydrogen Bonds

A-T:

```
2 Hydrogen bonds
```

G-C:

```
3 Hydrogen bonds
```

# 19. Difference Between DNA and RNA

| DNA                        | RNA                     |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Deoxyribose sugar          | Ribose sugar            |
| Double stranded            | Mostly single stranded  |
| Contains thymine           | Contains uracil         |
| Stores genetic information | Helps protein synthesis |

# Exam Important Questions

## 1. What are amino acids?

**Answer:**

Amino acids are organic compounds containing amino (-NH₂) and carboxyl (-COOH) groups that act as building blocks of proteins.

---

## 2. Explain peptide bond.

**Answer:**

The bond formed between two amino acids by elimination of water is called a peptide bond.

\[
-CO-NH-
\]

---

## 3. What is protein denaturation?

**Answer:**

Loss of biological activity due to destruction of secondary and tertiary structures of proteins is called denaturation.

---

## 4. Differentiate DNA and RNA.

| DNA                        | RNA                     |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Double helix               | Single strand           |
| Thymine present            | Uracil present          |
| Stores genetic information | Helps protein synthesis |

---

## 5. What is a nucleotide?

**Answer:**

A nucleotide is the basic unit of nucleic acids consisting of sugar, nitrogen base, and phosphate group.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing nucleoside and nucleotide.
- Forgetting DNA contains thymine and RNA contains uracil.
- Mixing protein structure levels.
- Forgetting peptide bond formula.
- Confusing essential and non-essential amino acids.

# Chapter Summary

- **Proteins and Nucleic Acids** are essential biomolecules responsible for structure, function, and heredity.
- Proteins are polymers of amino acids connected by peptide bonds.
- Protein activity depends on primary, secondary, tertiary, and quaternary structures.
- Nucleic acids DNA and RNA store and transfer genetic information.
- DNA follows complementary base pairing, while RNA plays major roles in protein synthesis.
