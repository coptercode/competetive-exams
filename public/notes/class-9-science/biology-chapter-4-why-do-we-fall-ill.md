# Introduction

Health is a state of physical, mental and social well-being. Diseases occur when normal body functions are disturbed.

This chapter covers:

- Health and disease
- Causes of diseases
- Infectious and non-infectious diseases
- Disease-causing organisms
- Prevention and immunity

---

# PART A: HEALTH AND DISEASE

---

# 1. Health

## Definition

Health is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease.

---

A healthy person has:

- Proper body functions
- Good mental condition
- Ability to perform daily activities

---

# 2. Disease

## Definition

A condition in which normal functioning of the body is affected is called disease.

---

Examples:

- Fever
- Diabetes
- Tuberculosis

---

# 3. Symptoms

## Definition

The signs that indicate the presence of a disease are called symptoms.

---

Examples:

- Fever
- Headache
- Pain
- Weakness

---

# 4. Causes of Diseases

Diseases may occur due to:

1. Infectious agents
2. Genetic factors
3. Poor nutrition
4. Environmental conditions
5. Lifestyle factors

---

# PART B: TYPES OF DISEASES

---

# 5. Infectious Diseases

## Definition

Diseases caused by microorganisms that can spread from one person to another are called infectious diseases.

---

Caused by:

- Bacteria
- Viruses
- Fungi
- Protozoa
- Worms

---

Examples:

- Tuberculosis
- Malaria
- Common cold

---

# 6. Non-Infectious Diseases

## Definition

Diseases that do not spread from one person to another are called non-infectious diseases.

---

Examples:

- Diabetes
- Cancer
- Heart disease

---

Causes:

- Genetic factors
- Lifestyle
- Nutritional deficiency

---

# Difference Between Infectious and Non-Infectious Diseases

| Infectious Diseases | Non-Infectious Diseases |
| ------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Caused by pathogens | Not caused by pathogens |
| Can spread          | Cannot spread           |
| Example: Malaria    | Example: Diabetes       |

---

---

## Definition

Disease-causing microorganisms are called pathogens.

---

Types:

1. Bacteria
2. Viruses
3. Protozoa
4. Fungi
5. Worms

---

# 8. Bacterial Diseases

# PART D: MODES OF TRANSMISSION

---

# 12. Spread of Infectious Diseases

Diseases spread through:

---

## 1. Air

Example:

- Common cold
- Tuberculosis

---

## 2. Water

Example:

- Cholera

---

## 3. Food

Example:

- Food poisoning

---

## 4. Direct Contact

Example:

- Skin infections

---

## 5. Vectors

Organisms that transmit diseases are called vectors.

---

Example:

Mosquito:

\[
\boxed{\text{Malaria carrier}}
\]

---

# PART E: PREVENTION OF DISEASES

---

# 13. General Methods of Prevention

---

## 1. Personal Hygiene

Includes:

- Regular bathing
- Clean food habits
- Hand washing

---

## 2. Safe Drinking Water

Prevents water-borne diseases.

---

## 3. Proper Sanitation

Helps control spread of infections.

---

## 4. Balanced Diet

Provides nutrients and strengthens immunity.

---

## 5. Vaccination

Provides protection against specific diseases.

---

# PART F: IMMUNITY

---

# 14. Immunity

## Definition

The ability of the body to resist diseases is called immunity.

---

# 15. Types of Immunity

---

## 1. Innate Immunity

Natural defence present from birth.

---

Examples:

- Skin
- White blood cells

---

## 2. Acquired Immunity

Develops after exposure to pathogens or vaccination.

---

# 16. Vaccination

## Definition

The process of introducing weakened or inactive microorganisms to develop immunity is called vaccination.

---

Examples:

- Polio vaccine
- BCG vaccine

---

# PART G: TREATMENT OF DISEASES

---

# 17. Principles of Treatment

Treatment includes:

1. Killing pathogens.
2. Reducing symptoms.
3. Improving body resistance.

---

# 18. Antibiotics

## Definition

Medicines that kill or stop the growth of bacteria are called antibiotics.

---

Example:

- Penicillin

---

Note:

Antibiotics do not work against viruses.

---

# PART H: IMPORTANT CONCEPTS

---

# 19. Causes of Poor Health

- Lack of nutrition
- Poor sanitation
- Stress
- Lack of exercise
- Pollution

---

# 20. Disease Prevention Levels

## Primary Prevention

Prevent disease before it occurs.

Examples:

- Vaccination
- Hygiene

---

## Secondary Prevention

Early detection and treatment.

---

## Tertiary Prevention

Reducing complications of disease.

---

# Important Points

- Health includes physical, mental and social well-being.
- Diseases can be infectious or non-infectious.
- Pathogens cause infectious diseases.
- Vaccination improves immunity.
- Hygiene and sanitation prevent diseases.
- Antibiotics work against bacteria, not viruses.

---

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing symptoms and causes.
- Thinking all diseases spread from person to person.
- Believing antibiotics cure viral diseases.
- Forgetting vaccination provides immunity.
- Mixing vectors and pathogens.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Diseases affect normal body functions.
- Infectious diseases are caused by pathogens and can spread.
- Non-infectious diseases do not spread.
- Immunity protects the body from infections.
- Prevention through hygiene, nutrition and vaccination helps maintain health.

\[
\boxed{
\text{A healthy lifestyle and prevention are the best ways to avoid diseases.}
}
