# Introduction

All matter is made up of tiny particles called atoms and molecules. This chapter explains the basic laws of chemical combinations and calculations involving atoms and molecules.

This chapter covers:

- Atomic theory
- Atoms and molecules
- Atomic mass
- Molecular mass
- Mole concept
- Chemical formula writing

---

# PART A: ATOMIC THEORY

---

# 1. Atom

## Definition

An atom is the smallest particle of an element that takes part in chemical reactions.

---

Examples:

- Hydrogen atom (H)
- Oxygen atom (O)
- Carbon atom (C)

---

# 2. Molecule

## Definition

A molecule is the smallest particle of a substance that can exist independently and shows all the properties of that substance.

---

Examples:

\[
H_2,\ O_2,\ H_2O
\]

---

# 3. Element

## Definition

A pure substance made up of only one type of atom is called an element.

---

Examples:

- Iron
- Gold
- Oxygen

---

# 4. Compound

## Definition

A substance formed by the chemical combination of two or more elements in a fixed ratio is called a compound.

---

Examples:

Water:

\[
H_2O
\]

Carbon dioxide:

\[
CO_2
\]

---

# PART B: DALTON'S ATOMIC THEORY

---

# 5. Dalton's Atomic Theory

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{John Dalton}}
\]

---

Main Postulates:

1. Matter is made up of tiny particles called atoms.
2. Atoms cannot be divided during chemical reactions.
3. Atoms of the same element have similar properties.
4. Atoms combine in whole number ratios to form compounds.
5. Chemical reactions involve rearrangement of atoms.

---

# PART C: LAWS OF CHEMICAL COMBINATION

---

# 6. Law of Conservation of Mass

## Statement

Mass can neither be created nor destroyed during a chemical reaction.

---

\[
\boxed{
\text{Mass of reactants = Mass of products}
}
\]

---

# 7. Law of Constant Proportions

## Statement

A pure compound always contains the same elements combined in the same proportion by mass.

---

Example:

Water always contains hydrogen and oxygen in a fixed ratio.

---

# PART D: ATOMIC MASS

---

# 8. Atomic Mass

## Definition

The mass of an atom compared with 1/12th mass of carbon-12 atom is called atomic mass.

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{\text{Atomic mass unit (u)}}
\]

---

# 9. Atomic Mass Unit

## Definition

One atomic mass unit is defined as 1/12th of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{u}
\]

---

# 10. Common Atomic Masses

| Element  | Symbol | Atomic Mass |
| -------- | ------ | ----------- |
| Hydrogen | H      | 1 u         |
| Carbon   | C      | 12 u        |
| Nitrogen | N      | 14 u        |
| Oxygen   | O      | 16 u        |
| Sodium   | Na     | 23 u        |
| Chlorine | Cl     | 35.5 u      |

---

# PART E: MOLECULAR MASS

---

# 11. Molecular Mass

## Definition

The sum of atomic masses of all atoms present in one molecule is called molecular mass.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Molecular\ mass=
\text{Sum of atomic masses}
}
\]

---

# Example

Find molecular mass of water:

\[
H_2O
\]

Atomic masses:

H = 1 u

O = 16 u

Calculation:

\[
=2(1)+16
\]

\[
\boxed{18u}
\]

---

# 12. Formula Mass

## Definition

The sum of atomic masses of all atoms in an ionic compound is called formula mass.

---

Example:

NaCl:

\[
23+35.5
\]

\[
\boxed{58.5u}
\]

---

# PART F: MOLE CONCEPT

---

# 13. Mole

## Definition

A mole is the amount of substance containing:

\[
\boxed{6.022\times10^{23}}
\]

particles.

---

This number is called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Avogadro number}}
\]

---

# 14. Avogadro Number

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{N_A}
\]

Value:

\[
\boxed{
6.022\times10^{23}
}
\]

---

One mole contains:

- Atoms
- Molecules
- Ions

---

# 15. Molar Mass

## Definition

The mass of one mole of a substance is called molar mass.

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{g/mol}
\]

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Number\ of\ moles=
\frac{Given\ mass}{Molar\ mass}
}
\]

---

# Example

Find moles in 18 g of water.

Molar mass of water:

\[
18g/mol
\]

\[
n=\frac{18}{18}
\]

\[
\boxed{1\ mole}
\]

---

# PART G: WRITING CHEMICAL FORMULAS

---

# 16. Chemical Formula

## Definition

A chemical formula represents the composition of a compound using symbols and numbers.

---

Example:

Water:

\[
\boxed{H_2O}
\]

---

# 17. Valency

## Definition

The combining capacity of an element is called valency.

---

Examples:

| Element  | Valency |
| -------- | ------- |
| Hydrogen | 1       |
| Oxygen   | 2       |
| Nitrogen | 3       |
| Carbon   | 4       |
| Sodium   | 1       |

---

# 18. Rules for Writing Formula

Steps:

1. Write symbols of elements.
2. Write their valencies.
3. Cross the valencies.
4. Simplify if needed.

---

Example:

Calcium chloride:

Calcium:

\[
Ca^{2+}
\]

Chlorine:

\[
Cl^-
\]

Formula:

\[
\boxed{CaCl_2}
\]

---

# Important Formula Sheet

\[
\boxed{
n=\frac{Mass}{Molar\ Mass}
}
\]

---

\[
\boxed{
Number\ of\ particles=
n\times6.022\times10^{23}
}
\]

---

\[
\boxed{
\text{Sum of atomic masses}
}
\]

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Find molecular mass of carbon dioxide.

Formula:

\[
CO_2
\]

Atomic masses:

C = 12

O = 16

Calculation:

\[
12+2(16)
\]

\[
\boxed{44u}
\]

---

## Example 2

Find number of moles in 40 g of oxygen.

Molar mass of oxygen:

\[
32g/mol
\]

\[
n=\frac{40}{32}
\]

\[
\boxed{1.25\ mol}
\]

---

## Example 3

Write formula of aluminium oxide.

Aluminium valency:

\[
3
\]

Oxygen valency:

\[
2
\]

Formula:

\[
\boxed{Al_2O_3}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing atomic mass and molecular mass.
- Forgetting Avogadro number.
- Using incorrect valencies.
- Mixing mole and molar mass.
- Writing chemical formulas without balancing valencies.

- Chemical compounds have fixed composition.

- Chemical formulas show the composition of compounds.
