# Introduction

Everything around us is made up of matter. Matter has mass and occupies space.

This chapter covers:

- Nature of matter
- States of matter
- Physical and chemical properties
- Changes in matter
- Mixtures and their types

---

# PART A: MATTER

---

# 1. Matter

## Definition

Anything that has mass and occupies space is called matter.

---

Examples:

- Air
- Water
- Metals
- Plants
- Animals

---

# 2. Characteristics of Matter

Matter:

1. Has mass
2. Occupies space
3. Is made up of particles
4. Has inter-particle spaces
5. Has attraction between particles

---

# PART B: PARTICLE NATURE OF MATTER

---

# 3. Particles of Matter

Matter is made up of tiny particles called atoms and molecules.

---

Properties of particles:

### 1. Particles are very small

They cannot be seen with naked eyes.

---

### 2. Particles have spaces between them

Example:

Sugar dissolves in water because water particles have spaces.

---

### 3. Particles are continuously moving

This movement increases with temperature.

---

### 4. Particles attract each other

This force is called intermolecular force.

---

# PART C: STATES OF MATTER

---

# 4. States of Matter

Matter exists mainly in three states:

1. Solid
2. Liquid
3. Gas

---

# 5. Solid State

## Definition

A state of matter having definite shape and volume is called solid.

---

Examples:

- Iron
- Wood
- Ice

---

Properties:

- Strong intermolecular force
- Fixed shape
- Fixed volume
- Very low compressibility

---

# 6. Liquid State

## Definition

A state of matter having definite volume but no fixed shape is called liquid.

---

Examples:

- Water
- Oil
- Milk

---

Properties:

- Takes shape of container
- Fixed volume
- Less intermolecular force than solids
- Slightly compressible

---

# 7. Gaseous State

## Definition

A state of matter having neither fixed shape nor fixed volume is called gas.

---

Examples:

- Oxygen
- Carbon dioxide
- Nitrogen

---

Properties:

- No fixed shape
- No fixed volume
- Highly compressible
- Weak intermolecular force

---

# PART D: CHANGE OF STATE

---

# 8. Melting

## Definition

The change of solid into liquid on heating is called melting.

---

Example:

\[
Ice \rightarrow Water
\]

---

# 9. Freezing

## Definition

The change of liquid into solid on cooling is called freezing.

---

Example:

\[
Water \rightarrow Ice
\]

---

# 10. Vaporisation

## Definition

The change of liquid into gas is called vaporisation.

---

Example:

\[
Water \rightarrow Steam
\]

---

# 11. Condensation

## Definition

The change of gas into liquid is called condensation.

---

Example:

\[
Steam \rightarrow Water
\]

---

# 12. Sublimation

## Definition

The direct conversion of solid into gas without becoming liquid is called sublimation.

---

Examples:

- Camphor
- Naphthalene

---

# PART E: PHYSICAL PROPERTIES

---

# 13. Physical Property

## Definition

A property that can be observed or measured without changing the chemical nature of a substance is called physical property.

---

Examples:

- Colour
- Shape
- Density
- Melting point
- Boiling point

---

# 14. Physical Change

## Definition

A change in which no new substance is formed is called physical change.

---

Examples:

- Melting of ice
- Cutting paper
- Dissolving sugar in water

---

Characteristics:

- Usually reversible
- No new substance formed

---

# PART F: CHEMICAL PROPERTIES

---

# 15. Chemical Property

## Definition

A property that describes the ability of a substance to undergo chemical change is called chemical property.

---

Examples:

- Flammability
- Reactivity
- Rusting

---

# 16. Chemical Change

## Definition

A change in which a new substance with different properties is formed is called chemical change.

---

Examples:

- Burning of wood
- Rusting of iron
- Cooking food

---

Characteristics:

- New substance formed
- Usually irreversible
- Energy changes occur

---

# PART G: MIXTURES

---

# 17. Mixture

## Definition

A combination of two or more substances physically mixed together is called a mixture.

---

Examples:

- Air
- Salt water
- Soil

---

Properties:

- Components retain their properties
- Can be separated physically
- No fixed composition

---

# 18. Types of Mixtures

Mixtures are classified into:

1. Homogeneous mixtures
2. Heterogeneous mixtures

---

# 19. Homogeneous Mixture

## Definition

A mixture having uniform composition throughout is called a homogeneous mixture.

---

Examples:

- Salt solution
- Sugar solution
- Air

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Solution}}
\]

---

# 20. Heterogeneous Mixture

## Definition

A mixture having non-uniform composition is called a heterogeneous mixture.

---

Examples:

- Sand and water
- Oil and water

---

# PART H: SEPARATION OF MIXTURES

---

# 21. Methods of Separation

---

## Filtration

Used to separate:

- Insoluble solid from liquid

Example:

Sand and water

---

## Evaporation

Used to obtain dissolved solids from solutions.

Example:

Salt from seawater

---

## Distillation

Used to separate liquids based on boiling points.

---

## Chromatography

Used to separate coloured substances.

---

# Important Points

- Matter has mass and occupies space.
- Matter exists as solids, liquids and gases.
- Physical changes do not form new substances.
- Chemical changes form new substances.
- Mixtures can be separated by physical methods.
- Homogeneous mixtures have uniform composition.

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Classify water as solid, liquid or gas.

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Liquid}}
\]

---

## Example 2

Is melting of ice a physical or chemical change?

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Physical change}}
\]

because no new substance is formed.

---

## Example 3

Name the method used to separate salt from seawater.

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Evaporation}}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing physical and chemical changes.
- Thinking gases have fixed volume.
- Forgetting mixtures retain properties of components.
- Mixing homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures.
- Forgetting matter occupies space.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Matter is anything having mass and occupying space.
- Matter exists in three main states.
- Changes in state occur due to temperature changes.
- Physical changes do not create new substances.
- Chemical changes produce new substances.
- Mixtures contain two or more substances and can be separated physically.

\[
