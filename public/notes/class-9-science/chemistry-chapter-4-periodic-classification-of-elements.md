# Introduction

Periodic classification is the arrangement of elements in a systematic order based on their properties.

This chapter covers:

- Need for classification of elements
- Mendeleev periodic table
- Modern periodic law
- Groups and periods
- Periodic trends

---

# PART A: CLASSIFICATION OF ELEMENTS

---

# 1. Need for Classification

## Definition

Classification of elements means arranging elements into groups based on similarities in their properties.

---

Advantages:

- Easy study of elements.
- Helps predict properties.
- Shows relationships between elements.

---

# PART B: EARLY ATTEMPTS OF CLASSIFICATION

---

# 2. Dobereiner's Triads

## Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Johann Wolfgang Dobereiner}}
\]

---

## Definition

Groups of three elements having similar properties were called Dobereiner's triads.

---

Example:

Lithium (Li)

Sodium (Na)

Potassium (K)

---

Rule:

The atomic mass of the middle element was approximately the average of the other two.

---

Limitations:

- Only a few elements could be arranged.
- Not applicable to all elements.

---

# 3. Newlands' Law of Octaves

## Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{John Newlands}}
\]

---

## Statement

When elements are arranged in increasing order of atomic masses, every eighth element has properties similar to the first element.

---

Example:

Like musical notes repeating after every eighth note.

---

Limitations:

- Worked only for lighter elements.
- Could not accommodate newly discovered elements.

---

# PART C: MENDELEEV PERIODIC TABLE

---

# 4. Mendeleev's Periodic Law

## Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Dmitri Mendeleev}}
\]

---

## Statement

The properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic masses.

---

# 5. Features of Mendeleev's Table

- Elements arranged according to increasing atomic mass.
- Similar elements placed in same groups.
- Gaps left for undiscovered elements.
- Predicted properties of unknown elements.

---

# 6. Achievements of Mendeleev Table

---

## 1. Prediction of New Elements

Mendeleev predicted elements that were discovered later.

Example:

Eka-aluminium → Gallium

---

## 2. Correction of Atomic Masses

Incorrect atomic masses were corrected.

---

# 7. Limitations of Mendeleev Table

- Position of hydrogen was uncertain.
- Isotopes could not be placed properly.
- Some elements had increasing atomic mass order problems.

---

# PART D: MODERN PERIODIC TABLE

---

# 8. Modern Periodic Law

## Statement

The physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.

---

Modern basis:

\[
\boxed{\text{Atomic number}}
\]

---

# 9. Structure of Modern Periodic Table

The modern periodic table contains:

- 7 periods
- 18 groups

---

# PART E: PERIODS

---

# 10. Periods

## Definition

Horizontal rows in the periodic table are called periods.

---

There are:

\[
\boxed{7\ periods}
\]

---

# 11. Characteristics of Periods

- Elements in a period have the same number of electron shells.
- Atomic number increases from left to right.

---

# PART F: GROUPS

---

# 12. Groups

## Definition

Vertical columns in the periodic table are called groups.

---

There are:

\[
\boxed{18\ groups}
\]

---

# 13. Characteristics of Groups

Elements in the same group have:

- Similar chemical properties.
- Same number of valence electrons.

---

# Important Groups

---

## Group 1: Alkali Metals

Examples:

- Lithium
- Sodium
- Potassium

Properties:

- Highly reactive metals.
- Valency = 1

---

## Group 17: Halogens

Examples:

- Fluorine
- Chlorine
- Bromine

Properties:

- Reactive non-metals.
- Valency = 1

---

## Group 18: Noble Gases

Examples:

- Helium
- Neon
- Argon

Properties:

- Chemically inactive.
- Complete outer shell.

---

# PART G: PERIODIC TRENDS

---

# 14. Atomic Radius

## Definition

The distance from the nucleus to the outermost electron shell is called atomic radius.

---

Trend:

Across a period:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases}}
\]

Down a group:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increases}}
\]

---

# 15. Valency

## Definition

The combining capacity of an element is called valency.

---

Trend:

- Same group elements have same valency.
- Changes across periods.

---

# 16. Metallic Character

## Definition

The tendency of an element to lose electrons and form positive ions is called metallic character.

---

Trend:

Across a period:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases}}
\]

Down a group:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increases}}
\]

---

# 17. Non-Metallic Character

The tendency to gain electrons is called non-metallic character.

---

Trend:

Across a period:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increases}}
\]

Down a group:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases}}
\]

---

# PART H: CLASSIFICATION OF ELEMENTS

---

# 18. Metals

Properties:

- Good conductors of heat and electricity.
- Malleable.
- Ductile.
- Usually solids.

Examples:

- Iron
- Copper
- Aluminium

---

# 19. Non-Metals

Properties:

- Poor conductors.
- Brittle.
- Usually gases or solids.

Examples:

- Oxygen
- Sulphur
- Carbon

---

# 20. Metalloids

## Definition

Elements having properties of both metals and non-metals are called metalloids.

---

Examples:

- Silicon
- Boron

---

# Important Points

- Mendeleev arranged elements based on atomic mass.
- Modern periodic table is based on atomic number.
- There are 7 periods and 18 groups.
- Elements in the same group have similar properties.
- Periodic trends help predict element behaviour.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing atomic mass with atomic number.
- Forgetting periods are horizontal rows.
- Forgetting groups are vertical columns.
- Mixing metals and non-metals.
- Thinking all elements in the same period have similar properties.
