# Introduction

Chemical bonding is the force of attraction that holds atoms together to form molecules and compounds.

This chapter covers:

- Need for chemical bonding
- Ionic bonds
- Covalent bonds
- Lewis structures
- Properties of ionic and covalent compounds

---

# PART A: CHEMICAL BONDING

---

# 1. Chemical Bond

## Definition

The attractive force that holds atoms together in a molecule or compound is called a chemical bond.

---

Atoms form bonds to achieve:

\[
\boxed{\text{Stable electronic configuration}}
\]

similar to noble gases.

---

# 2. Octet Rule

## Definition

Atoms combine with other atoms to achieve eight electrons in their outermost shell.

---

Example:

Oxygen:

Electronic configuration:

\[
2,6
\]

Needs:

\[
2\ electrons
\]

to complete octet.

---

# 3. Duplet Rule

Atoms having only the first shell become stable by having two electrons.

---

Example:

Hydrogen:

\[
1
\]

Needs:

\[
1\ electron
\]

to achieve duplet.

---

# PART B: TYPES OF CHEMICAL BONDS

---

Chemical bonds are mainly:

1. Ionic bond
2. Covalent bond

---

# PART C: IONIC BOND

---

# 4. Ionic Bond

## Definition

A chemical bond formed by the transfer of electrons from one atom to another is called an ionic bond.

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Electrovalent bond}}
\]

---

# 5. Formation of Ionic Bond

Example:

Formation of sodium chloride:

\[
Na + Cl \rightarrow NaCl
\]

---

Sodium:

Electronic configuration:

\[
2,8,1
\]

Loses one electron:

\[
Na^+
\]

---

Chlorine:

Electronic configuration:

\[
2,8,7
\]

Gains one electron:

\[
Cl^-
\]

---

The attraction between:

\[
Na^+ \text{ and } Cl^-
\]

forms ionic bond.

---

# 6. Properties of Ionic Compounds

---

## 1. Physical State

Usually solids.

---

## 2. Melting and Boiling Point

High due to strong attraction between ions.

---

## 3. Solubility

Generally soluble in water.

---

## 4. Electrical Conductivity

Conduct electricity in:

- Molten state
- Aqueous solution

---

Examples:

- NaCl
- MgCl₂
- CaO

---

# PART D: COVALENT BOND

---

# 7. Covalent Bond

## Definition

A chemical bond formed by sharing of electrons between atoms is called covalent bond.

---

Examples:

- Hydrogen molecule (H₂)
- Oxygen molecule (O₂)
- Methane (CH₄)

---

# 8. Types of Covalent Bonds

---

## Single Covalent Bond

Sharing of one pair of electrons.

Example:

\[
H-H
\]

---

## Double Covalent Bond

Sharing of two pairs of electrons.

Example:

\[
O=O
\]

---

## Triple Covalent Bond

Sharing of three pairs of electrons.

Example:

\[
N\equiv N
\]

---

# PART E: LEWIS STRUCTURE

---

# 9. Lewis Dot Structure

## Definition

A diagram showing valence electrons around atoms is called Lewis structure.

---

Example:

Hydrogen molecule:

\[
H:H
\]

---

Oxygen:

\[
O::O
\]

---

# 10. Steps to Draw Lewis Structure

1. Find valence electrons.
2. Place atoms.
3. Share electrons to complete octet.
4. Show shared pairs as bonds.

---

# PART F: DIFFERENCE BETWEEN IONIC AND COVALENT BONDS

| Ionic Bond                      | Covalent Bond             |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Transfer of electrons           | Sharing of electrons      |
| Forms ions                      | Forms molecules           |
| Usually metals + non-metals     | Usually non-metals        |
| High melting point              | Lower melting point       |
| Conduct electricity in solution | Generally poor conductors |

---

# PART G: VALENCY AND BOND FORMATION

---

# 11. Valency

## Definition

The combining capacity of an atom is called valency.

---

Examples:

| Element  | Electronic Configuration | Valency |
| -------- | ------------------------ | ------- |
| Hydrogen | 1                        | 1       |
| Oxygen   | 2,6                      | 2       |
| Nitrogen | 2,5                      | 3       |
| Carbon   | 2,4                      | 4       |
| Sodium   | 2,8,1                    | 1       |

---

# 12. Formation of Molecules

---

## Hydrogen Molecule

Each hydrogen atom needs one electron.

\[
H+H \rightarrow H_2
\]

A single covalent bond is formed.

---

## Oxygen Molecule

Each oxygen atom shares two electrons.

\[
O=O
\]

A double bond is formed.

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

What type of bond is present in NaCl?

Na transfers electron to Cl.

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ionic bond}}
\]

---

## Example 2

What type of bond is present in H₂?

Hydrogen atoms share electrons.

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Covalent bond}}
\]

---

## Example 3

Find valency of oxygen.

Electronic configuration:

\[
2,6
\]

Electrons needed:

\[
8-6=2
\]

Valency:

\[
\boxed{2}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing ionic and covalent bonds.
- Forgetting ionic bonds involve electron transfer.
- Forgetting covalent bonds involve sharing.
- Counting total electrons instead of valence electrons.
- Drawing incorrect Lewis structures.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Chemical bonds hold atoms together.
- Atoms form bonds to achieve stability.
- Ionic bonds are formed by electron transfer.
- Covalent bonds are formed by electron sharing.
- Lewis structures represent valence electrons.
- Bond type determines properties of compounds.
