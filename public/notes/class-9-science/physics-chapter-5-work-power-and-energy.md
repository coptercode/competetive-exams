# Introduction

Work, power and energy are important concepts used to describe the ability of objects to perform activities.

This chapter covers:

- Work done by a force
- Energy
- Kinetic energy
- Potential energy
- Conservation of energy
- Power

---

# PART A: WORK

---

# 1. Work

## Definition

Work is said to be done when a force acting on an object produces displacement in the direction of the force.

---

Conditions for work:

1. A force must act on an object.
2. The object must undergo displacement.
3. Displacement should have a component in the direction of force.

---

# 2. Formula for Work

\[
\boxed{
W=F\times s
}
\]

---

Where:

W = Work done

F = Force

s = Displacement

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{Joule(J)}
\]

---

# 3. One Joule

## Definition

One joule is the amount of work done when a force of 1 N moves an object through 1 m in the direction of force.

---

\[
\boxed{
1J=1Nm
}
\]

---

# 4. Types of Work

---

## Positive Work

When force and displacement are in the same direction.

Example:

Pulling a cart.

---

## Negative Work

When force and displacement are in opposite directions.

Example:

Friction acting on a moving object.

---

## Zero Work

When displacement is zero or perpendicular to force.

Examples:

- Holding a bag without moving
- Carrying a load horizontally

---

# PART B: ENERGY

---

# 5. Energy

## Definition

The capacity of an object to do work is called energy.

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{Joule(J)}
\]

---

Forms of energy:

- Mechanical energy
- Heat energy
- Electrical energy
- Chemical energy
- Nuclear energy

---

# PART C: KINETIC ENERGY

---

# 6. Kinetic Energy

## Definition

The energy possessed by an object due to its motion is called kinetic energy.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
KE=\frac12 mv^2
}
\]

---

Where:

m = mass

v = velocity

---

Factors affecting kinetic energy:

- Increases with mass
- Increases with square of velocity

---

# Derivation of Kinetic Energy

Work done:

\[
W=Fs
\]

From Newton's second law:

\[
F=ma
\]

Therefore:

\[
W=mas
\]

Using equation:

\[
v^2-u^2=2as
\]

For initial velocity:

\[
u=0
\]

\[
v^2=2as
\]

\[
s=\frac{v^2}{2a}
\]

Substituting:

\[
KE=\frac12mv^2
\]

---

# PART D: POTENTIAL ENERGY

---

# 7. Potential Energy

## Definition

The energy possessed by an object due to its position or configuration is called potential energy.

---

Example:

- Water stored in a dam
- A stretched rubber band

---

# 8. Gravitational Potential Energy

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
PE=mgh
}
\]

---

Where:

m = mass

g = acceleration due to gravity

h = height

---

# Example

Find potential energy of a 5 kg object raised to 10 m.

Given:

\[
m=5kg
\]

\[
h=10m
\]

\[
g=9.8m/s^2
\]

\[
PE=mgh
\]

\[
=5\times9.8\times10
\]

\[
\boxed{490J}
\]

---

# PART E: MECHANICAL ENERGY

---

# 9. Mechanical Energy

## Definition

The sum of kinetic energy and potential energy is called mechanical energy.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Mechanical\ Energy=KE+PE
}
\]

---

# PART F: LAW OF CONSERVATION OF ENERGY

---

# 10. Conservation of Energy

## Statement

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It can only be converted from one form to another.

---

Examples:

### Falling Object

At height:

\[
PE \rightarrow Maximum
\]

During fall:

\[
PE \rightarrow KE
\]

At ground:

\[
KE \rightarrow Maximum
\]

---

# PART G: POWER

---

# 11. Power

## Definition

The rate of doing work is called power.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
P=\frac{W}{t}
}
\]

---

Where:

P = Power

W = Work

t = Time

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{Watt(W)}
\]

---

# 12. One Watt

## Definition

Power is one watt when one joule of work is done in one second.

---

\[
\boxed{
1W=1J/s
}
\]

---

# 13. Commercial Unit of Energy

Electrical energy is measured in:

\[
\boxed{\text{kWh (kilowatt hour)}}
\]

---

Conversion:

\[
\boxed{
1kWh=3.6\times10^6J
}
\]

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

A force of 20 N moves an object through 5 m. Find work done.

\[
W=Fs
\]

\[
=20\times5
\]

\[
\boxed{100J}
\]

---

## Example 2

Find kinetic energy of a 4 kg object moving at 3 m/s.

\[
KE=\frac12mv^2
\]

\[
=\frac12(4)(3^2)
\]

\[
=2\times9
\]

\[
\boxed{18J}
\]

---

## Example 3

A machine does 500 J work in 10 seconds. Find power.

\[
P=\frac{W}{t}
\]

\[
=\frac{500}{10}
\]

\[
\boxed{50W}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing work and energy.
- Forgetting displacement is necessary for work.
- Using mass instead of weight in potential energy.
- Forgetting velocity is squared in kinetic energy.
- Mixing power and energy units.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Work is done when force causes displacement.
- Energy is the capacity to do work.
- Kinetic energy depends on motion.
- Potential energy depends on position.
- Energy is conserved and changes from one form to another.
- Power measures the rate of doing work.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Energy is the ability to perform work and power measures how fast it is done.}
}
\]
