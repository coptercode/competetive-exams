# Introduction

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. All living organisms are made up of cells.

This chapter covers:

- Discovery of cell
- Cell theory
- Types of cells
- Plant and animal cells
- Cell organelles and their functions

---

# PART A: DISCOVERY OF CELL

---

# 1. Cell

## Definition

A cell is the smallest structural and functional unit of a living organism.

---

Examples:

- Amoeba → single cell
- Human body → many cells

---

# 2. Discovery of Cell

## Discovered by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Robert Hooke}}
\]

Year:

\[
\boxed{1665}
\]

---

He observed thin slices of cork using a microscope and named the small compartments as "cells".

---

# 3. Cell Theory

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Schleiden and Schwann}}
\]

---

# Statements of Cell Theory

1. All living organisms are made up of cells.
2. Cell is the basic unit of life.
3. New cells arise from existing cells.

---

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Rudolf Virchow}}
\]

---

# PART B: TYPES OF CELLS

---

# 4. Based on Number of Cells

Organisms are classified into:

1. Unicellular organisms
2. Multicellular organisms

---

# 5. Unicellular Organisms

## Definition

Organisms made up of a single cell are called unicellular organisms.

---

Examples:

- Amoeba
- Bacteria
- Paramecium

---

Functions like nutrition, respiration and reproduction occur within one cell.

---

# 6. Multicellular Organisms

## Definition

Organisms made up of many cells are called multicellular organisms.

---

Examples:

- Humans
- Plants
- Animals

---

Different cells perform different functions.

---

# PART C: TYPES OF CELLS BASED ON STRUCTURE

---

# 7. Prokaryotic Cells

## Definition

Cells without a true nucleus are called prokaryotic cells.

---

Characteristics:

- No nuclear membrane
- No membrane-bound organelles
- Simple structure

---

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Bacteria}}
\]

---

# 8. Eukaryotic Cells

## Definition

Cells having a true nucleus enclosed by a nuclear membrane are called eukaryotic cells.

---

Characteristics:

- Well-defined nucleus
- Membrane-bound organelles present

---

Examples:

- Plant cells
- Animal cells

---

# PART D: PLANT CELL AND ANIMAL CELL

---

# 9. Plant Cell

## Characteristics

- Cell wall present
- Chloroplast present
- Large vacuole
- Fixed shape

---

Examples:

- Leaf cells
- Root cells

---

# 10. Animal Cell

## Characteristics

- Cell wall absent
- Chloroplast absent
- Small vacuoles
- Irregular shape

---

Examples:

- Muscle cells
- Nerve cells

---

# Difference Between Plant Cell and Animal Cell

| Plant Cell          | Animal Cell        |
| ------------------- | ------------------ |
| Cell wall present   | Cell wall absent   |
| Chloroplast present | Chloroplast absent |
| Large vacuole       | Small vacuoles     |
| Fixed shape         | Irregular shape    |

---

# PART E: CELL STRUCTURE

---

# 11. Cell Membrane

## Definition

The thin outer covering of a cell is called cell membrane.

---

Functions:

- Protects the cell
- Controls movement of substances in and out

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Plasma membrane}}
\]

---

# 12. Cell Wall

## Definition

A rigid outer covering present in plant cells is called cell wall.

---

Functions:

- Provides support
- Gives protection
- Maintains shape

---

Made up of:

\[
\boxed{\text{Cellulose}}
\]

---

# 13. Cytoplasm

## Definition

The jelly-like substance present between cell membrane and nucleus is called cytoplasm.

---

Functions:

- Contains organelles
- Site of many metabolic activities

---

# 14. Nucleus

## Definition

The control centre of the cell is called nucleus.

---

Functions:

- Controls cell activities
- Contains genetic material

---

Contains:

- DNA
- Chromosomes

---

# PART F: CELL ORGANELLES

---

# 15. Mitochondria

## Function

Produces energy for the cell.

---

Called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Powerhouse of the cell}}
\]

---

# 16. Ribosomes

## Function

Site of protein synthesis.

---

---

# 17. Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)

## Function

Helps in transport of materials inside the cell.

---

Types:

1. Rough ER
2. Smooth ER

---

# 18. Golgi Apparatus

## Function

- Packaging and secretion of materials.
- Formation of lysosomes.

---

# 19. Lysosomes

## Function

- Digest waste materials.
- Remove damaged cell parts.

---

Called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Suicide bags of the cell}}
\]

---

# 20. Vacuoles

## Function

- Store food
- Store water
- Store waste materials

---

Plant cells contain:

\[
\boxed{\text{Large central vacuole}}
\]

---

# 21. Chloroplast

## Function

Contains chlorophyll and performs photosynthesis.

---

Present in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Plant cells}}
\]

---

# PART G: CHROMOSOMES AND DNA

---

# 22. Chromosomes

## Definition

Thread-like structures present inside the nucleus that carry genetic information are called chromosomes.

---

Made of:

\[
\boxed{\text{DNA and proteins}}
\]

---

# 23. DNA

## Definition

DNA is the hereditary material that carries genetic information.

---

Function:

- Controls inheritance
- Transfers characteristics from parents to offspring

---

# Important Points

- Cell is the basic unit of life.
- Robert Hooke discovered cells.
- Plant cells have cell walls and chloroplasts.
- Nucleus controls cell activities.
- Mitochondria produce energy.
- DNA carries genetic information.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing cell wall and cell membrane.
- Forgetting mitochondria produces energy.
- Thinking all cells have chloroplasts.
- Confusing nucleus and cytoplasm functions.
- Forgetting bacteria are prokaryotic.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Cells are the basic units of living organisms.
- Cells may be unicellular or multicellular.
- Prokaryotic cells lack a true nucleus.
- Eukaryotic cells contain membrane-bound organelles.
- Plant and animal cells have different structures.
- Cell organelles perform specific functions.

\[
\boxed{
\text{The cell is the fundamental unit that makes life possible.}
}
