# Introduction

Sound is a form of energy that produces the sensation of hearing. It is produced by vibrating objects and travels through a medium as waves.

This chapter covers:

- Production of sound
- Propagation of sound waves
- Characteristics of sound
- Speed of sound
- Echo
- SONAR

---

# PART A: PRODUCTION OF SOUND

---

# 1. Sound

## Definition

Sound is a form of energy produced by vibrating objects that creates the sensation of hearing.

---

Examples:

- Vibrating guitar string
- Vibrating tuning fork
- Human vocal cords

---

# 2. Vibration

## Definition

The to-and-fro motion of an object about its mean position is called vibration.

---

Example:

A vibrating tuning fork produces sound.

---

# PART B: PROPAGATION OF SOUND

---

# 3. Sound Needs a Medium

Sound requires a material medium to travel.

---

Mediums:

- Solids
- Liquids
- Gases

---

Sound cannot travel through:

\[
\boxed{\text{Vacuum}}
\]

---

# 4. Sound Waves

## Definition

The waves produced by vibrating particles that transfer sound energy from one place to another are called sound waves.

---

Sound waves are:

\[
\boxed{\text{Mechanical waves}}
\]

---

# 5. Longitudinal Waves

## Definition

Waves in which particles of the medium vibrate parallel to the direction of wave propagation are called longitudinal waves.

---

Parts:

1. Compression
2. Rarefaction

---

# 6. Compression

## Definition

The region where particles are close together is called compression.

---

# 7. Rarefaction

## Definition

The region where particles are spread apart is called rarefaction.

---

# PART C: CHARACTERISTICS OF SOUND

---

# 8. Amplitude

## Definition

The maximum displacement of vibrating particles from their mean position is called amplitude.

---

Effect:

\[
\boxed{\text{Amplitude determines loudness}}
\]

---

Greater amplitude:

→ Louder sound

---

# 9. Frequency

## Definition

The number of vibrations produced in one second is called frequency.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Frequency=\frac{Number\ of\ vibrations}{Time}
}
\]

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{Hertz(Hz)}
\]

---

Effect:

\[
\boxed{\text{Frequency determines pitch}}
\]

---

# 10. Time Period

## Definition

The time taken to complete one vibration is called time period.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
T=\frac1f
}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{second(s)}
\]

---

# 11. Loudness

## Definition

The characteristic of sound that distinguishes loud and soft sounds is called loudness.

---

Depends on:

- Amplitude
- Distance from source

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{Decibel(dB)}
\]

---

# 12. Pitch

## Definition

The characteristic of sound that distinguishes high and low sounds is called pitch.

---

Depends on:

\[
\boxed{\text{Frequency}}
\]

---

Examples:

- Child's voice → high pitch
- Male voice → low pitch

---

# PART D: SPEED OF SOUND

---

# 13. Speed of Sound

## Definition

The distance travelled by sound per unit time is called speed of sound.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Speed=\frac{Distance}{Time}
}
\]

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{m/s}
\]

---

# 14. Speed of Sound in Different Media

Sound travels:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Solids > Liquids > Gases}
}
\]

---

Approximate speed in air:

\[
\boxed{340m/s}
\]

---

Factors affecting speed:

- Temperature
- Nature of medium

---

# PART E: REFLECTION OF SOUND

---

# 15. Reflection of Sound

## Definition

The bouncing back of sound waves when they strike a surface is called reflection of sound.

---

Examples:

- Echo
- Sound in halls

---

# 16. Laws of Reflection of Sound

1. Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection.

\[
\boxed{i=r}
\]

2. Incident sound, reflected sound and normal lie in the same plane.

---

# PART F: ECHO

---

# 17. Echo

## Definition

The repetition of sound due to reflection from a distant surface is called echo.

---

Examples:

- Sound heard near mountains
- Empty halls

---

# 18. Conditions for Hearing Echo

1. Time gap should be at least:

\[
\boxed{0.1s}
\]

2. Reflecting surface should be sufficiently far.

---

# 19. Uses of Echo

- Measuring distance
- Finding depth of oceans
- SONAR technology

---

# PART G: SONAR

---

# 20. SONAR

## Full Form:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Sound Navigation And Ranging}
}
\]

---

## Definition

SONAR is a device that uses ultrasonic waves to measure distances and locate underwater objects.

---

# 21. Principle of SONAR

SONAR works on:

\[
\boxed{\text{Reflection of ultrasonic waves}}
\]

---

# 22. Applications of SONAR

Used to find:

- Depth of oceans
- Submarines
- Sunken ships
- Underwater objects

---

# PART H: ULTRASONIC SOUND

---

# 23. Ultrasound

## Definition

Sound waves having frequency greater than:

\[
\boxed{20000Hz}
\]

are called ultrasonic waves.

---

Uses:

- Medical imaging
- Cleaning delicate objects
- SONAR

---

# Important Formula Sheet

## Frequency

\[
\boxed{
f=\frac{n}{t}
}
\]

---

## Time Period

\[
\boxed{
T=\frac1f
}
\]

---

## Speed of Sound

\[
\boxed{
v=\frac{d}{t}
}
\]

---

## Echo Distance

\[
\boxed{
Distance=\frac{Speed\times Time}{2}
}
\]

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

A sound wave travels 680 m in 2 seconds. Find speed.

\[
v=\frac{d}{t}
\]

\[
=\frac{680}{2}
\]

\[
\boxed{340m/s}
\]

---

## Example 2

A source produces 500 vibrations in 5 seconds. Find frequency.

\[
f=\frac{500}{5}
\]

\[
\boxed{100Hz}
\]

---

## Example 3

Find time period of a sound wave having frequency 50 Hz.

\[
T=\frac1f
\]

\[
=\frac1{50}
\]

\[
\boxed{0.02s}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Thinking sound can travel through vacuum.
- Confusing loudness with pitch.
- Forgetting frequency determines pitch.
- Forgetting amplitude determines loudness.
- Using echo formula without dividing by 2.
- Confusing ultrasonic waves with audible sound.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Sound is produced by vibrating objects.
- Sound requires a medium for propagation.
- Sound waves are longitudinal mechanical waves.
- Frequency determines pitch and amplitude determines loudness.
- Echo is produced by reflection of sound.
- SONAR uses ultrasonic waves to locate underwater objects.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Sound allows communication and helps us understand our surroundings.}
}
\]
