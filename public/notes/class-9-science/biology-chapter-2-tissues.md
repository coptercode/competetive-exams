# Introduction

A group of similar cells that perform a specific function is called a tissue.

This chapter covers:

- Plant tissues
- Meristematic tissues
- Permanent tissues
- Animal tissues
- Epithelial, connective, muscular and nervous tissues

---

# PART A: TISSUES

---

# 1. Tissue

## Definition

A group of cells having similar structure and performing a specific function is called a tissue.

---

Examples:

- Muscle tissue
- Nervous tissue
- Xylem tissue

---

# 2. Importance of Tissues

Tissues help organisms by:

- Increasing efficiency
- Performing specialised functions
- Dividing work among cells

---

# PART B: PLANT TISSUES

---

# 3. Types of Plant Tissues

Plant tissues are classified into:

1. Meristematic tissues
2. Permanent tissues

---

# PART C: MERISTEMATIC TISSUES

---

# 4. Meristematic Tissue

## Definition

A tissue containing actively dividing cells is called meristematic tissue.

---

Characteristics:

- Cells continuously divide.
- Cells have dense cytoplasm.
- Large nucleus present.
- Vacuoles are absent or very small.

---

# 5. Types of Meristematic Tissue

---

## 1. Apical Meristem

Location:

- Root tips
- Shoot tips

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increase in length of plant}}
\]

---

## 2. Lateral Meristem

Location:

- Sides of stems and roots

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increase in thickness}}
\]

---

## 3. Intercalary Meristem

Location:

- Base of leaves
- Internodes

Function:

- Growth of internodes.

---

# PART D: PERMANENT TISSUES

---

# 6. Permanent Tissue

## Definition

Tissues formed from meristematic cells that have stopped dividing are called permanent tissues.

---

Types:

1. Simple permanent tissues
2. Complex permanent tissues

---

# PART E: SIMPLE PERMANENT TISSUES

---

# 7. Parenchyma

## Characteristics:

- Living cells
- Thin cell walls
- Large spaces between cells

---

Functions:

- Storage of food
- Photosynthesis (when chlorophyll is present)

---

# 8. Collenchyma

## Characteristics:

- Living cells
- Thickened corners

---

Functions:

- Provides flexibility and support.

---

Example:

Leaf stalk

---

# 9. Sclerenchyma

## Characteristics:

- Dead cells
- Thick and hard cell walls

---

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Provides strength}}
\]

---

Example:

Coconut fibre

---

# PART F: COMPLEX PERMANENT TISSUES

---

# 10. Complex Tissue

A tissue made up of different types of cells working together is called complex tissue.

---

Types:

1. Xylem
2. Phloem

---

# 11. Xylem

## Function

Transports water and minerals from roots to other parts of the plant.

---

Components:

- Tracheids
- Vessels
- Xylem fibres
- Xylem parenchyma

---

# 12. Phloem

## Function

Transports prepared food from leaves to other parts of the plant.

---

Components:

- Sieve tubes
- Companion cells
- Phloem fibres
- Phloem parenchyma

---

# PART G: ANIMAL TISSUES

---

# 13. Types of Animal Tissues

Animal tissues are classified into:

1. Epithelial tissue
2. Connective tissue
3. Muscular tissue
4. Nervous tissue

---

# PART H: EPITHELIAL TISSUE

---

# 14. Epithelial Tissue

## Definition

The tissue that covers body surfaces and protects internal organs is called epithelial tissue.

---

Functions:

- Protection
- Absorption
- Secretion

---

# Types of Epithelial Tissue

---

## 1. Squamous Epithelium

Function:

- Allows diffusion

Example:

Blood vessels

---

## 2. Cuboidal Epithelium

Function:

- Secretion and absorption

Example:

Kidney tubules

---

## 3. Columnar Epithelium

Function:

- Absorption

Example:

Intestine

---

## 4. Ciliated Epithelium

Function:

- Movement of substances

Example:

Respiratory tract

---

# PART I: CONNECTIVE TISSUE

---

# 15. Connective Tissue

## Definition

A tissue that connects, supports and binds different parts of the body is called connective tissue.

---

Examples:

- Bone
- Blood
- Cartilage
- Tendons

---

# Types:

---

## 1. Blood

Function:

- Transport of oxygen and nutrients.

---

## 2. Bone

Function:

- Provides support and protection.

---

## 3. Cartilage

Function:

- Provides flexibility.

---

## 4. Tendon

Connects:

\[
\boxed{\text{Muscle to bone}}
\]

---

## 5. Ligament

Connects:

\[
\boxed{\text{Bone to bone}}
\]

---

# PART J: MUSCULAR TISSUE

---

# 16. Muscular Tissue

## Function

Responsible for movement of body parts.

---

Types:

1. Striated muscle
2. Smooth muscle
3. Cardiac muscle

---

# 17. Striated Muscle

Characteristics:

- Voluntary action
- Attached to bones

Example:

Arm muscles

---

# 18. Smooth Muscle

Characteristics:

- Involuntary action

Example:

Intestine

---

# 19. Cardiac Muscle

Characteristics:

- Found only in heart
- Involuntary
- Works continuously

---

# PART K: NERVOUS TISSUE

---

# 20. Nervous Tissue

## Definition

The tissue responsible for receiving and transmitting nerve impulses is called nervous tissue.

---

Location:

- Brain
- Spinal cord
- Nerves

---

# 21. Neuron

## Definition

The structural and functional unit of nervous tissue is called a neuron.

---

Parts:

1. Cell body
2. Dendrites
3. Axon

---

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Transmit nerve impulses}}
\]

---

\]

---

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Tendon}}
\]

---

## Example 3

Answer:

\[

\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing xylem and phloem.
- Mixing tendon and ligament.
- Forgetting cardiac muscles are involuntary.
- Confusing meristematic and permanent tissues.
- Forgetting neurons transmit impulses.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Tissues are groups of similar cells performing specific functions.
- Plant tissues are divided into meristematic and permanent tissues.
- Xylem transports water, while phloem transports food.
- Animal tissues include epithelial, connective, muscular and nervous tissues.
- Each tissue is specialised for a particular function.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Tissues provide organisation and efficiency in living organisms.}
}
\]
