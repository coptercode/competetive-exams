# Introduction

An atom is the smallest unit of an element that takes part in chemical reactions. The discovery of sub-atomic particles helped scientists understand the internal structure of atoms.

This chapter covers:

- Sub-atomic particles
- Atomic models
- Bohr model
- Electronic configuration
- Valency

# PART A: SUB-ATOMIC PARTICLES

---

# 1. Atom

## Definition

An atom is the smallest particle of an element that retains the chemical properties of that element.

---

Atoms consist of:

1. Electrons
2. Protons
3. Neutrons

# PART B: STRUCTURE OF ATOM

---

# 5. Nucleus

## Definition

The central region of an atom containing protons and neutrons is called the nucleus.

---

Contains:

- Protons
- Neutrons

---

Features:

- Very small
- Dense
- Positively charged

---

# 6. Atomic Number

## Definition

The number of protons present in the nucleus of an atom is called atomic number.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{Z}
\]

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Z=\text{Number of protons}
}
\]

For a neutral atom:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Number of protons}=\text{Number of electrons}
}
\]

---

Example:

Hydrogen:

\[
Z=1
\]

---

# 7. Mass Number

## Definition

The total number of protons and neutrons present in the nucleus is called mass number.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{A}
\]

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
A=Protons+Neutrons
}
\]

---

# 8. Relationship Between Atomic Number and Mass Number

\[
\boxed{
A=Z+N
}
\]

---

Where:

A = Mass number

Z = Atomic number

N = Number of neutrons

---

# PART C: ATOMIC MODELS

---

# 9. Thomson's Atomic Model

## Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{J.J. Thomson}}
\]

---

Main idea:

- Atom is a positively charged sphere.
- Electrons are embedded in it.

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Plum pudding model}}
\]

---

Limitations:

Could not explain the presence of nucleus.

---

# 10. Rutherford's Atomic Model

## Experiment

Alpha particles were bombarded on a thin gold foil.

---

Observations:

1. Most particles passed through.
2. Few particles were deflected.
3. Very few bounced back.

---

Conclusions:

- Atom has a small, dense nucleus.
- Most of the atom is empty space.
- Electrons revolve around nucleus.

---

Limitations:

Could not explain stability of atom.

---

# 11. Bohr's Atomic Model

## Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Niels Bohr}}
\]

---

Main points:

1. Electrons revolve around nucleus in fixed energy levels.
2. Electrons cannot exist between energy levels.
3. Each shell has fixed energy.

---

# PART D: ELECTRONIC CONFIGURATION

---

# 12. Electron Shells

Electrons are arranged in different shells around nucleus.

---

Shell names:

| Shell | Name         |
| ----- | ------------ |
| K     | First shell  |
| L     | Second shell |
| M     | Third shell  |
| N     | Fourth shell |

---

# 13. Maximum Number of Electrons

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
2n^2
}
\]

---

Where:

n = shell number

---

Examples:

K shell:

\[
2(1)^2=2
\]

Maximum electrons:

\[
\boxed{2}
\]

---

L shell:

\[
2(2)^2=8
\]

Maximum electrons:

\[
\boxed{8}
\]

---

# 14. Electronic Configuration

## Definition

The distribution of electrons in different shells of an atom is called electronic configuration.

---

Examples:

---

## Hydrogen

Atomic number:

\[
1
\]

Configuration:

\[
\boxed{1}
\]

---

## Oxygen

Atomic number:

\[
8
\]

\[
\boxed{2,6}
\]

---

## Sodium

\[

\]

\]

# PART E: VALENCY

---

# 15. Valency

## Definition

The combining capacity of an atom is called valency.

---

Determined by:

Number of electrons in outermost shell.

---

# 16. Valence Electrons

## Definition

Electrons present in the outermost shell are called valence electrons.

---

Examples:

Sodium:

Configuration:

\[
2,8,1
\]

Valence electrons:

\[
1
\]

Valency:

\[
\boxed{1}
\]

---

# 17. Rules for Finding Valency

If outer shell electrons are:

### 1, 2, 3, 4

Valency:

Same number

---

Example:

Carbon:

\[
2,4
\]

Valency:

\[
4
\]

---

### 5, 6, 7

Valency:

\[
8-\text{valence electrons}
\]

---

Example:

Oxygen:

\[
2,6
\]

Valency:

\[
8-6=2
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing atomic number and mass number.
- Forgetting neutrons have no charge.
- Mixing electrons and protons.
- Using wrong shell capacity.
- Forgetting valency depends on outermost electrons.

# Chapter Summary

- Atoms contain electrons, protons and neutrons.
- Nucleus contains protons and neutrons.
- Atomic number represents number of protons.
- Mass number is the sum of protons and neutrons.
- Bohr model explains electron arrangement in shells.
- Valency depends on valence electrons.
