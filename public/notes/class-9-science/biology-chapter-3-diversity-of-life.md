# Introduction

The Earth contains millions of living organisms with different shapes, sizes, structures and functions. The study of this variety of organisms is called biodiversity.

This chapter covers:

- Classification of organisms
- Need for classification
- Five kingdom classification
- Nomenclature
- Characteristics of major groups

---

# PART A: DIVERSITY OF LIFE

---

# 1. Biodiversity

## Definition

The variety of living organisms present on Earth is called biodiversity.

---

Examples:

- Plants
- Animals
- Microorganisms

---

# 2. Need for Classification

## Definition

Classification is the process of arranging organisms into groups based on similarities and differences.

---

Importance:

1. Makes study easier.
2. Helps identify organisms.
3. Shows relationships among organisms.
4. Helps understand evolution.

---

# PART B: CLASSIFICATION OF ORGANISMS

---

# 3. Early Classification

Earlier organisms were classified based on:

- Habitat
- Body structure
- Mode of nutrition

---

# 4. Two Kingdom Classification

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Carolus Linnaeus}}
\]

---

Kingdoms:

1. Plantae
2. Animalia

---

Limitations:

- Did not include microorganisms.
- Could not classify organisms with mixed characters.

---

# PART C: FIVE KINGDOM CLASSIFICATION

---

# 5. Five Kingdom Classification

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{R.H. Whittaker (1969)}}
\]

---

The five kingdoms are:

1. Monera
2. Protista
3. Fungi
4. Plantae
5. Animalia

---

# PART D: KINGDOM MONERA

---

# 6. Monera

## Characteristics

- Unicellular organisms.
- Prokaryotic cells.
- No true nucleus.
- No membrane-bound organelles.

---

Examples:

- Bacteria
- Cyanobacteria

---

# 7. Bacteria

Characteristics:

- Microscopic organisms.
- Found everywhere.
- Some are useful, some cause diseases.

---

Useful bacteria:

- Curd formation
- Nitrogen fixation

---

# PART E: KINGDOM PROTISTA

---

# 8. Protista

## Characteristics

- Mostly unicellular organisms.
- Eukaryotic cells.
- Mostly aquatic.

---

Examples:

- Amoeba
- Paramecium
- Euglena

---

# 9. Euglena

Special feature:

- Has both plant-like and animal-like characters.

---

Plant-like:

- Contains chlorophyll.

Animal-like:

- Shows movement.

---

# PART F: KINGDOM FUNGI

---

# 10. Fungi

## Characteristics

- Eukaryotic organisms.
- Mostly multicellular.
- Lack chlorophyll.
- Obtain food from dead organic matter.

---

Examples:

- Mushroom
- Yeast
- Bread mould

---

# 11. Features of Fungi

- Cell wall made of chitin.
- Reproduce by spores.
- Mostly decomposers.

---

# PART G: KINGDOM PLANTAE

---

# 12. Plantae

## Characteristics

- Multicellular organisms.
- Eukaryotic cells.
- Have chlorophyll.
- Prepare food by photosynthesis.

---

Examples:

- Plants
- Trees
- Algae

---

# 13. Classification of Plants

Plants are classified into:

1. Thallophyta
2. Bryophyta
3. Pteridophyta
4. Gymnosperms
5. Angiosperms

---

# 14. Angiosperms

## Characteristics:

- Flowering plants.
- Produce seeds enclosed inside fruits.

---

Examples:

- Mango
- Rose
- Wheat

---

# PART H: KINGDOM ANIMALIA

---

# 15. Animalia

## Characteristics:

- Multicellular organisms.
- Eukaryotic cells.
- Cannot prepare food.
- Show movement.

---

Examples:

- Humans
- Birds
- Fish

---

# 16. Classification of Animals

Animals are divided into:

1. Invertebrates
2. Vertebrates

---

# 17. Invertebrates

## Definition

Animals without a backbone are called invertebrates.

---

Examples:

- Earthworm
- Insects
- Snail

---

# 18. Vertebrates

## Definition

Animals having a backbone are called vertebrates.

---

Examples:

- Fish
- Birds
- Mammals

---

# PART I: BINOMIAL NOMENCLATURE

---

# 19. Nomenclature

## Definition

The system of naming organisms scientifically is called nomenclature.

---

Introduced by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Carolus Linnaeus}}
\]

---

# 20. Binomial Nomenclature

## Definition

A scientific naming system using two names is called binomial nomenclature.

---

Two parts:

1. Genus name
2. Species name

---

Example:

Human:

\[
\boxed{\textit{Homo sapiens}}
\]

---

Rules:

1. Genus starts with capital letter.
2. Species starts with small letter.
3. Names are written in italics.

---

# PART J: IMPORTANT DIFFERENCES

---

# Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cells

| Prokaryotic       | Eukaryotic                  |
| ----------------- | --------------------------- |
| No true nucleus   | True nucleus present        |
| Simple structure  | Complex structure           |
| Example: bacteria | Example: plants and animals |

---

# Plant Kingdom vs Animal Kingdom

| Plantae            | Animalia         |
| ------------------ | ---------------- |
| Have chlorophyll   | No chlorophyll   |
| Prepare food       | Depend on others |
| Usually stationary | Usually move     |

---

# Important Points

- Biodiversity refers to variety of life forms.
- Classification makes identification easier.
- Whittaker proposed five kingdom classification.
- Monera contains prokaryotes.
- Protista contains mostly unicellular eukaryotes.
- Fungi are decomposers.
- Plants perform photosynthesis.
- Animals are heterotrophic.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing Monera and Protista.
- Forgetting fungi lack chlorophyll.
- Mixing vertebrates and invertebrates.
- Writing scientific names incorrectly.
- Forgetting kingdom names and characteristics.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Life exists in many forms with great diversity.
- Classification groups organisms based on similarities.
- Five kingdom classification includes Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia.
- Binomial nomenclature gives scientific names to organisms.
- Classification helps understand relationships among living organisms.

\[
