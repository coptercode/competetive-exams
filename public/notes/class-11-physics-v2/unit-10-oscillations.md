# Introduction

Oscillation is the repeated back-and-forth motion of an object about a fixed equilibrium position.

Examples:

- Motion of a pendulum
- Vibrations of a spring
- Vibrations of molecules
- Musical instruments

This unit covers:

- Periodic motion
- Simple harmonic motion (SHM)
- Displacement, velocity and acceleration in SHM
- Energy in SHM
- Simple pendulum
- Damped oscillations
- Forced oscillations
- Resonance

---

# PART A: PERIODIC MOTION

---

# 1. Periodic Motion

## Definition

A motion that repeats itself after equal intervals of time is called periodic motion.

---

Examples:

- Earth revolving around the Sun
- Motion of a pendulum
- Rotation of clock hands

---

# 2. Oscillatory Motion

## Definition

The repeated to-and-fro motion of a particle about its mean position is called oscillatory motion.

---

Examples:

- Simple pendulum
- Spring-mass system

---

# 3. Important Terms

---

## Mean Position

The equilibrium position about which the particle oscillates.

---

## Amplitude (A)

The maximum displacement from the mean position.

Unit:

\[
\boxed{m}
\]

---

## Time Period (T)

The time taken to complete one oscillation.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
T=\frac{\text{Total time}}{\text{Number of oscillations}}
}
\]

Unit:

\[
\boxed{s}
\]

---

## Frequency (f)

Number of oscillations completed in one second.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
f=\frac1T
}
\]

Unit:

\[
\boxed{Hz}
\]

---

## Angular Frequency

\[
\boxed{
\omega=2\pi f
}
\]

or:

\[
\boxed{
\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}
}
\]

---

# PART B: SIMPLE HARMONIC MOTION (SHM)

---

# 4. Simple Harmonic Motion

## Definition

A motion in which acceleration is directly proportional to displacement and always directed towards the mean position is called simple harmonic motion.

---

Condition:

\[
\boxed{
a\propto -x
}
\]

---

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
a=-\omega^2x
}
\]

---

The negative sign indicates acceleration is towards the mean position.

---

# 5. Examples of SHM

- Oscillation of spring
- Small oscillations of pendulum
- Vibrations of tuning fork

---

# PART C: DISPLACEMENT EQUATION OF SHM

---

# 6. SHM Equation

The displacement of a particle in SHM is:

\[
\boxed{
x=A\sin(\omega t+\phi)
}
\]

or:

\[
\boxed{
x=A\cos(\omega t+\phi)
}
\]

---

where:

- A = amplitude
- ω = angular frequency
- φ = phase constant

---

# 7. Phase

Phase describes the state of oscillation at a particular time.

---

Phase:

\[
\boxed{
\omega t+\phi
}
\]

---

# PART D: VELOCITY IN SHM

---

# 8. Velocity Equation

Velocity:

\[
v=\frac{dx}{dt}
\]

For SHM:

\[
\boxed{
v=\omega\sqrt{A^2-x^2}
}
\]

---

Maximum velocity:

At mean position:

\[
x=0
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
v_{max}=A\omega
}
\]

---

# PART E: ACCELERATION IN SHM

---

# 9. Acceleration Equation

\[
\boxed{
a=-\omega^2x
}
\]

---

Maximum acceleration:

At extreme position:

\[
x=A
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
a_{max}=A\omega^2
}
\]

---

# PART F: ENERGY IN SHM

---

# 10. Total Energy

The total energy in SHM remains constant.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
E=\frac12m\omega^2A^2
}
\]

---

# 11. Kinetic Energy

\[
\boxed{
KE=
\frac12m\omega^2(A^2-x^2)
}
\]

---

Maximum kinetic energy:

At mean position:

\[
x=0
\]

\[
\boxed{
KE_{max}
=

\frac12m\omega^2A^2
}
\]

---

# 12. Potential Energy

\[
\boxed{
PE=
\frac12m\omega^2x^2
}
\]

---

Maximum potential energy:

At extreme position:

\[
x=A
\]

\[
\boxed{
PE_{max}
=

\frac12m\omega^2A^2
}
\]

---

# Energy Variation

At mean position:

\[
KE=max
\]

\[
PE=0
\]

---

At extreme position:

\[
KE=0
\]

\[
PE=max
\]

---

# PART G: SPRING OSCILLATOR

---

# 13. Mass-Spring System

A mass attached to a spring performs SHM.

---

Restoring force:

\[
\boxed{
F=-kx
}
\]

---

Using Newton's law:

\[
ma=-kx
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
a=-\frac{k}{m}x
}
\]

---

Comparing:

\[
a=-\omega^2x
\]

we get:

\[
\boxed{
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
}
\]

---

Time period:

\[
\boxed{
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac mk}
}
\]

---

# PART H: SIMPLE PENDULUM

---

# 14. Simple Pendulum

## Definition

A point mass suspended by a light, inextensible string that oscillates about a fixed point is called a simple pendulum.

---

# 15. Time Period of Simple Pendulum

For small oscillations:

\[
\boxed{
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac lg}
}
\]

---

where:

- l = length of pendulum
- g = acceleration due to gravity

---

# Factors Affecting Time Period

Depends on:

- Length of pendulum
- Acceleration due to gravity

Does not depend on:

- Mass of bob
- Amplitude (for small oscillations)

---

# PART I: DAMPED OSCILLATIONS

---

# 16. Damped Oscillation

## Definition

Oscillations in which amplitude decreases continuously due to resistive forces are called damped oscillations.

---

Causes:

- Air resistance
- Friction

---

Examples:

- Motion of a pendulum in air
- Shock absorbers

---

# Types of Damping

---

## Underdamping

Oscillations continue with decreasing amplitude.

---

## Critical Damping

Object returns to equilibrium quickly without oscillation.

---

## Overdamping

Object returns slowly without oscillation.

---

# PART J: FORCED OSCILLATIONS

---

# 17. Forced Oscillation

## Definition

Oscillation produced by an external periodic force is called forced oscillation.

---

Example:

- Vibrations of a machine

---

# 18. Resonance

## Definition

The phenomenon in which amplitude becomes maximum when frequency of external force equals natural frequency is called resonance.

---

Condition:

\[
\boxed{
f=f_0
}
\]

---

Applications:

Positive:

- Musical instruments
- Radio tuning

Negative:

- Bridge vibrations
- Building damage

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

A particle performs SHM with amplitude:

\[
A=0.1m
\]

and angular frequency:

\[
\omega=20rad/s
\]

Find maximum velocity.

Formula:

\[
v_{max}=A\omega
\]

\[
=0.1(20)
\]

\[
\boxed{2m/s}
\]

---

## Example 2

Find time period of a pendulum of length:

\[
1m
\]

Given:

\[
g=9.8m/s^2
\]

\[
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac1{9.8}}
\]

\[
\boxed{2.01s}
\]

---

## Example 3

A spring has:

\[
k=100N/m
\]

and mass:

\[
m=1kg
\]

Angular frequency:

\[
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
\]

\[
=\sqrt{100}
\]

\[
\boxed{10rad/s}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing periodic motion and SHM.
- Forgetting negative sign in SHM acceleration.
- Using degrees instead of radians.
- Assuming pendulum time period depends on mass.
- Confusing frequency and angular frequency.
- Forgetting energy remains constant in ideal SHM.
- Mixing damping and resonance concepts.

---

# Unit Summary

- Oscillation is repeated motion about equilibrium.
- SHM occurs when acceleration is proportional to displacement.
- Velocity is maximum at mean position.
- Acceleration is maximum at extreme position.
- Energy continuously changes between kinetic and potential forms.
- Pendulum and spring systems are important examples of SHM.
- Damping reduces amplitude.
- Resonance occurs when external frequency matches natural frequency.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Oscillations describe repeated motions around equilibrium positions.}
}
