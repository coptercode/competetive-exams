# Introduction

Electromagnetic waves are waves produced by **time-varying electric and magnetic fields**.

They are important because they allow:

- Wireless communication
- Radio broadcasting
- Television transmission
- Medical imaging
- Satellite communication

This unit covers:

- Displacement current
- Maxwell's equations
- Production of electromagnetic waves
- Properties of EM waves
- Electromagnetic spectrum

# PART A: DISPLACEMENT CURRENT

---

# 1. Need for Displacement Current

According to Ampere's law:

\[
\boxed{
\oint B\cdot dl=\mu_0I
}
\]

This law works for steady currents.

However, it fails in situations like a charging capacitor where current exists in wires but not between capacitor plates.

---

# 2. Displacement Current

## Definition

The current produced due to changing electric field is called displacement current.

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{I_d}
\]

---

# Formula

\[
\boxed{
I_d=\epsilon_0\frac{d\Phi_E}{dt}
}
\]

where:

- \(\epsilon_0\) = permittivity of free space
- \(\Phi_E\) = electric flux

---

# 3. Modified Ampere's Law

Maxwell modified Ampere's law as:

\[
\boxed{
\oint B\cdot dl
=

\mu_0(I_c+I_d)
}
\]

where:

- \(I_c\) = conduction current
- \(I_d\) = displacement current

---

# PART B: MAXWELL'S THEORY

---

# 4. Maxwell's Equations

Maxwell combined electricity and magnetism into a unified theory.

The four equations are:

---

# 1. Gauss's Law for Electricity

\[
\boxed{
\nabla\cdot E=
\frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}
}
\]

It describes electric charges producing electric fields.

---

# 2. Gauss's Law for Magnetism

\[
\boxed{
\nabla\cdot B=0
}
\]

It shows that magnetic monopoles do not exist.

---

# 3. Faraday's Law

\[
\boxed{
\nabla\times E
=

-\frac{\partial B}{\partial t}
}
\]

Changing magnetic field produces electric field.

---

# 4. Ampere-Maxwell Law

\[
\boxed{
\nabla\times B
=

\mu_0J+
\mu_0\epsilon_0
\frac{\partial E}{\partial t}
}
\]

Changing electric field produces magnetic field.

---

# PART C: ELECTROMAGNETIC WAVES

---

# 5. Production of Electromagnetic Waves

Electromagnetic waves are produced by:

- Accelerating charges
- Oscillating electric fields

A changing electric field produces a magnetic field and vice versa.

---

# 6. Nature of Electromagnetic Waves

Electromagnetic waves are:

- Transverse waves
- Non-mechanical waves
- Capable of travelling through vacuum

---

# 7. Properties of Electromagnetic Waves

---

## 1. They do not require a medium

They can travel through vacuum.

---

## 2. They travel with speed of light

\[
\boxed{
c=3\times10^8m/s
}
\]

---

## 3. Electric and magnetic fields are perpendicular

\[
\boxed{
E\perp B
}
\]

---

## 4. Both fields are perpendicular to direction of propagation

Therefore EM waves are:

\[
\boxed{\text{Transverse waves}}
\]

---

## 5. They carry energy and momentum

---

# 8. Speed of Electromagnetic Waves

From Maxwell's theory:

\[
\boxed{
c=
\frac1{\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}}
}
\]

where:

- \(\mu_0\) = permeability of free space
- \(\epsilon_0\) = permittivity of free space

---

# 9. Relation Between Electric and Magnetic Fields

For an electromagnetic wave:

\[
\boxed{
\frac EB=c
}
\]

or:

\[
\boxed{
E=cB
}
\]

---

\[

<
\text{Infrared}
<

<
\text{UV}

# PART E: ENERGY AND MOMENTUM OF EM WAVES

---

# 18. Energy Density

Energy stored in electric field:

\[
\boxed{
u_E=\frac12\epsilon_0E^2
}
\]

---

Energy stored in magnetic field:

\[
\boxed{
u_B=\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}
}
\]

---

Total energy density:

\[
\boxed{
u=u_E+u_B
}
\]

---

# 19. Radiation Pressure

Electromagnetic waves exert pressure when they strike a surface.

For complete absorption:

\[
\boxed{
P=\frac Ic
}
\]

For complete reflection:

\[
\boxed{
P=\frac{2I}{c}
}
\]

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

Find speed of electromagnetic waves in vacuum.

Given:

\[
\mu_0=4\pi\times10^{-7}
\]

\[
\epsilon_0=8.85\times10^{-12}
\]

Formula:

\[
c=\frac1{\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
c=3\times10^8m/s
}
\]

---

## Example 2

If electric field amplitude is:

\[
E=300V/m
\]

find magnetic field amplitude.

Using:

\[
E=cB
\]

\[
B=\frac Ec
\]

\[
=\frac{300}{3\times10^8}
\]

\[
\boxed{
B=10^{-6}T
}
\]

---

## Example 3

An electromagnetic wave has intensity:

\[
I=600W/m^2
\]

Find radiation pressure for absorption.

Formula:

\[
P=\frac Ic
\]

\[
=\frac{600}{3\times10^8}
\]

\[
\boxed{
P=2\times10^{-6}Pa
}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing displacement current with conduction current.
- Forgetting that EM waves can travel through vacuum.
- Mixing wavelength and frequency order in spectrum.
- Forgetting:

\[
E=cB
\]

- Confusing X-rays and gamma rays.
- Forgetting that EM waves are transverse.
- Using incorrect radiation pressure formula.

# Unit Summary

- Maxwell showed that changing electric and magnetic fields create electromagnetic waves.
- Displacement current completed Ampere's law.
- Electromagnetic waves do not require a material medium.
- They travel at the speed of light.
- The electromagnetic spectrum ranges from radio waves to gamma rays.
- Different regions have different frequencies, wavelengths, and applications.

\[
\boxed{
c=\frac1{\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}}
}
\]

is the fundamental equation describing electromagnetic wave propagation.
