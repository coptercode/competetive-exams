# Introduction

Ray optics deals with the study of light as a collection of rays travelling in straight lines.

It explains:

- Reflection of light
- Refraction of light
- Mirrors
- Lenses
- Prisms
- Optical instruments
- Total internal reflection

Applications:

- Cameras
- Microscopes
- Telescopes
- Optical fibres
- Spectacles

---

# PART A: REFLECTION OF LIGHT

---

# 1. Reflection

## Definition

The phenomenon in which light returns back into the same medium after striking a surface is called reflection.

---

# Laws of Reflection

## First Law

The incident ray, reflected ray and normal at the point of incidence lie in the same plane.

---

## Second Law

The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.

\[
\boxed{
i=r
}
\]

---

# 2. Spherical Mirrors

A spherical mirror is a reflecting surface forming part of a sphere.

Types:

1. Concave mirror
2. Convex mirror

---

# Important Terms

## Pole (P)

Centre point of mirror surface.

---

## Centre of Curvature (C)

Centre of the sphere from which mirror is formed.

---

## Radius of Curvature (R)

Distance between P and C.

\[
\boxed{
R=PC
}
\]

---

## Principal Axis

Line passing through P and C.

---

## Focus (F)

Point where parallel rays meet or appear to diverge.

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
f=\frac R2
}
\]

---

# 3. Mirror Formula

For spherical mirrors:

\[
\boxed{
\frac1f=
\frac1v+\frac1u
}
\]

where:

- f = focal length
- v = image distance
- u = object distance

---

# 4. Magnification of Mirror

\[
\boxed{
m=-\frac vu
}
\]

---

Interpretation:

\[
m>1
\]

Magnified image

\[
m<1
\]

Diminished image

---

# Sign Convention (Cartesian)

- All distances measured from pole.
- Direction of incident light is positive reference.
- Distances opposite to light direction are negative.

---

# PART B: REFRACTION OF LIGHT

---

# 5. Refraction

## Definition

The bending of light when it passes from one medium to another is called refraction.

Reason:

Change in speed of light.

---

# 6. Refractive Index

## Definition

Refractive index measures how much a medium slows down light.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\mu=\frac cv
}
\]

where:

- c = speed of light in vacuum
- v = speed in medium

---

# Relative Refractive Index

\[
\boxed{
\mu_{21}
=

\frac{\mu_2}{\mu_1}
}
\]

---

# 7. Snell's Law

## Statement

For refraction:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\sin i}{\sin r}=\mu
}
\]

---

# 8. Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

## Definition

The complete reflection of light back into a denser medium when it attempts to enter a rarer medium is called total internal reflection.

---

# Conditions for TIR

1. Light must travel from denser to rarer medium.

2. Angle of incidence must be greater than critical angle.

\[
\boxed{
i>C
}
\]

---

# Critical Angle

The angle of incidence in denser medium for which angle of refraction becomes 90°.

\[
\boxed{
\sin C=\frac1\mu
}
\]

---

# Applications of TIR

- Optical fibres
- Diamond brilliance
- Periscopes
- Binoculars

---

# PART C: LENSES

---

# 9. Lens

A transparent optical device bounded by two surfaces is called a lens.

Types:

1. Convex lens
2. Concave lens

---

# Convex Lens

- Converging lens
- Positive focal length

---

# Concave Lens

- Diverging lens
- Negative focal length

---

# 10. Lens Formula

\[
\boxed{
\frac1f=
\frac1v-\frac1u
}
\]

where:

- f = focal length
- v = image distance
- u = object distance

---

# 11. Magnification of Lens

\[
\boxed{
m=\frac vu
}
\]

---

# 12. Power of Lens

## Definition

The ability of a lens to converge or diverge light is called power.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
P=\frac1f
}
\]

where f is in metres.

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{\text{Dioptre (D)}}
\]

---

For lenses in contact:

\[
\boxed{
P=P_1+P_2
}
\]

---

# 13. Lens Maker's Formula

The relation between focal length and curvature of lens surfaces is:

\[
\boxed{
\frac1f
=

(\mu-1)
\left(
\frac1{R_1}
-

\frac1{R_2}
\right)
}
\]

where:

- μ = refractive index
- R₁, R₂ = radii of curvature

---

# PART D: PRISMS

---

# 14. Prism

A prism is a transparent refracting medium bounded by two plane surfaces inclined at an angle.

---

# 15. Angle of Prism

The angle between two refracting surfaces is called angle of prism.

Symbol:

\[
\boxed A
\]

---

# 16. Deviation of Light

When light passes through a prism, it bends from its original path.

Angle of deviation:

\[
\boxed{\delta}
\]

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
\delta=i_1+i_2-A
}
\]

---

# 17. Minimum Deviation

At minimum deviation:

\[
\boxed{
i_1=i_2
}
\]

and:

\[
\boxed{
r_1=r_2=\frac A2
}
\]

---

# Refractive Index of Prism

\[
\boxed{
\mu=
\frac{
\sin\frac{A+\delta_m}{2}
}
{
\sin\frac A2
}
}
\]

where:

- δₘ = minimum deviation

---

# PART E: OPTICAL FIBRE

---

# 18. Optical Fibre

A thin transparent fibre that transmits light using total internal reflection is called optical fibre.

---

# Structure

1. Core
2. Cladding
3. Protective coating

---

# Applications

- Communication
- Medical endoscopy
- Internet data transfer

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

A convex lens has focal length:

\[
20cm
\]

Find power.

Convert:

\[
f=0.2m
\]

Formula:

\[
P=\frac1f
\]

\[
P=\frac1{0.2}
\]

\[
\boxed{P=5D}
\]

---

## Example 2

Find critical angle for glass:

\[
\mu=1.5
\]

Formula:

\[
\sin C=\frac1\mu
\]

\[
\sin C=\frac1{1.5}
\]

\[
=0.667
\]

\[
\boxed{
C\approx41.8^\circ
}
\]

---

## Example 3

A mirror has:

\[
f=-10cm
\]

Find radius.

Formula:

\[
R=2f
\]

\[
R=2(-10)
\]

\[
\boxed{
R=-20cm
}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing reflection and refraction.
- Forgetting sign convention in mirror and lens problems.
- Using focal length in centimetres while calculating power.
- Forgetting TIR requires denser to rarer medium.
- Mixing mirror formula and lens formula.
- Using wrong prism minimum deviation formula.
- Forgetting that convex lens has positive power.

---

# Unit Summary

- Ray optics explains the behaviour of light using rays.
- Reflection follows:

\[
i=r
\]

- Refraction follows Snell's law.
- Total internal reflection enables optical fibre communication.
- Mirrors and lenses form images using reflection and refraction.
- Lens maker's formula connects lens shape with focal length.
- Prisms produce deviation and dispersion of light.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Light travels, reflects and refracts according to optical laws.}
}
