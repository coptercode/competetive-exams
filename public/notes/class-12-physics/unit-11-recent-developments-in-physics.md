# PART A: NANOSCIENCE

---

# 1. Nanoscience

## Definition

Nanoscience is the study of materials and structures having dimensions in the nanometre range.

---

# Nanometre

A nanometre is:

\[
\boxed{
1nm=10^{-9}m
}
\]

---

# Comparison

\[
1m=10^9nm
\]

A human hair is approximately:

\[
50,000-100,000nm
\]

wide.

---

# 2. Nanotechnology

## Definition

Nanotechnology is the design, production and application of materials and devices at the nanoscale level.

---

It involves controlling matter at:

\[
\boxed{
1-100nm
}
\]

scale.

---

# PART B: SPECIAL PROPERTIES OF NANOMATERIALS

---

# 3. Why Nanomaterials Are Special

Nanomaterials show different properties compared with bulk materials because of:

1. Large surface area
2. Quantum confinement
3. Size-dependent properties

---

# 4. High Surface Area to Volume Ratio

As particle size decreases:

- Surface area increases.
- Chemical activity increases.

---

Applications:

- Catalysts
- Sensors
- Drug delivery

---

# 5. Quantum Confinement

## Definition

The change in electronic properties of materials when their size becomes comparable to electron wavelength is called quantum confinement.

---

Effects:

- Change in colour
- Change in electrical properties
- Change in optical behaviour

---

# PART C: QUANTUM DOTS

---

# 6. Quantum Dots

## Definition

Quantum dots are semiconductor nanoparticles whose electronic properties depend on their size.

---

Size range:

\[
\boxed{
2-10nm
}
\]

---

Examples:

- Cadmium sulphide (CdS)
- Cadmium selenide (CdSe)

---

# 7. Properties of Quantum Dots

---

## 1. Size-dependent Colour

Smaller quantum dots:

\[
\rightarrow
\text{higher energy light}
\]

Larger quantum dots:

\[
\rightarrow
\text{lower energy light}
\]

---

## 2. High Brightness

They emit intense light.

---

## 3. Tunable Optical Properties

Their properties can be controlled by changing size.

---

# 8. Applications of Quantum Dots

---

## 1. Displays

Used in:

- Quantum dot LEDs
- Television displays

---

## 2. Medical Imaging

Used as fluorescent markers.

---

## 3. Solar Cells

Improve light absorption efficiency.

---

## 4. Quantum Computing

Used in quantum information systems.

---

# PART D: CARBON NANOMATERIALS

---

# 9. Carbon Nanotubes (CNT)

## Definition

Carbon nanotubes are cylindrical structures made of carbon atoms arranged in a graphene sheet.

---

Discovered by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Sumio Iijima (1991)}}
\]

---

# 10. Structure of Carbon Nanotube

A carbon nanotube consists of:

- Rolled graphene sheet
- Carbon atoms arranged in hexagonal pattern

---

# Types of Carbon Nanotubes

---

## 1. Single-Walled Carbon Nanotube (SWCNT)

Contains:

\[
\boxed{\text{One graphene layer}}
\]

---

## 2. Multi-Walled Carbon Nanotube (MWCNT)

Contains:

\[
\boxed{\text{Multiple graphene layers}}
\]

---

# 11. Properties of Carbon Nanotubes

---

## 1. High Strength

CNTs are stronger than steel by weight.

---

## 2. High Electrical Conductivity

They can behave as:

- Conductors
- Semiconductors

---

## 3. High Thermal Conductivity

They transfer heat efficiently.

---

## 4. Lightweight

Very low density.

---

# 12. Applications of Carbon Nanotubes

---

## Electronics

Used in:

- Nano circuits
- Transistors

---

## Materials Science

Used for:

- Strong composites
- Lightweight materials

---

## Medicine

Used in:

- Drug delivery
- Biosensors

---

## Energy Storage

Used in:

- Batteries
- Supercapacitors

# PART F: NANOMATERIAL PREPARATION

---

# 17. Top-Down Approach

## Definition

Large materials are reduced into nanosized particles.

---

Methods:

- Grinding
- Lithography
- Etching

---

# 18. Bottom-Up Approach

## Definition

Nanostructures are built from atoms and molecules.

---

Methods:

- Chemical synthesis
- Self-assembly

---

# Comparison

| Top-Down                   | Bottom-Up              |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Large material → nano size | Atoms → nano structure |
| Physical methods           | Chemical methods       |
| Example: Lithography       | Example: Self assembly |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

Convert:

\[
5nm
\]

into metres.

Given:

\[
1nm=10^{-9}m
\]

Therefore:

\[
5nm=5\times10^{-9}m
\]

\[
\boxed{
=5\times10^{-9}m
}
\]

---

## Example 2

Why do nanoparticles have higher chemical activity?

Because:

\[
\boxed{
\text{They have a larger surface area to volume ratio}
}
\]

---

## Example 3

A quantum dot changes colour when its size changes. Why?

Because:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Quantum confinement changes electronic energy levels}
}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing nanometre with micrometre.

Remember:

\[
1nm=10^{-9}m
\]

- Thinking nanomaterials have only smaller size, not different properties.
- Confusing quantum dots with ordinary semiconductor particles.
- Forgetting carbon nanotubes are made of carbon atoms.
- Mixing top-down and bottom-up approaches.
- Assuming all nanoparticles are naturally occurring.

- Quantum dots are size-dependent semiconductor nanoparticles.
- Carbon nanotubes have exceptional strength and electrical properties.
- Nanotechnology has applications in medicine, electronics, energy and environment.
