# PART A: ELECTROMAGNETIC INDUCTION

---

# 1. Electromagnetic Induction

## Definition

The phenomenon of producing an induced emf due to a change in magnetic flux linked with a circuit is called electromagnetic induction.

Discovered by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Michael Faraday}}
\]

---

# 2. Magnetic Flux

## Definition

Magnetic flux is the measure of magnetic field passing through a surface.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_B=BA\cos\theta
}
\]

where:

- B = magnetic field
- A = area
- θ = angle between B and area vector

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{\text{Weber (Wb)}}
\]

---

# 3. Faraday's Laws of Electromagnetic Induction

---

# First Law

## Statement

Whenever magnetic flux linked with a circuit changes, an induced emf is produced.

---

# Second Law

## Statement

The magnitude of induced emf is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux.

\[
\boxed{
E\propto-\frac{d\Phi}{dt}
}
\]

For N turns:

\[
\boxed{
E=-N\frac{d\Phi}{dt}
}
\]

---

# Negative Sign

The negative sign represents:

\[
\boxed{\text{Lenz's Law}}
\]

---

# 4. Lenz's Law

## Statement

The induced current flows in a direction that opposes the change producing it.

---

It is based on:

\[
\boxed{\text{Conservation of energy}}
\]

---

# Applications

- Electric generators
- Induction cookers
- Transformers

---

# PART B: SELF AND MUTUAL INDUCTION

---

# 5. Self Induction

## Definition

The phenomenon in which a changing current in a coil induces emf in the same coil is called self induction.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
E=-L\frac{dI}{dt}
}
\]

where:

- L = self inductance

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{\text{Henry (H)}}
\]

---

# 6. Self Inductance

## Definition

Self inductance is the property of a coil by which it opposes changes in current.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
L=\frac{N\Phi}{I}
}
\]

---

# Energy Stored in Inductor

\[
\boxed{
U=\frac12LI^2
}
\]

---

# 7. Mutual Induction

## Definition

The phenomenon where change of current in one coil induces emf in another coil is called mutual induction.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
E_2=-M\frac{dI_1}{dt}
}
\]

where:

- M = mutual inductance

---

# Mutual Inductance

\[
\boxed{
M=\frac{N_2\Phi_{21}}{I_1}
}
\]

---

# PART C: ALTERNATING CURRENT

---

# 8. Alternating Current (AC)

## Definition

A current whose magnitude and direction change periodically with time is called alternating current.

---

Equation:

\[
\boxed{
I=I_0\sin\omega t
}
\]

where:

- \(I_0\) = maximum current
- ω = angular frequency

---

# 9. AC Voltage

\[
\boxed{
V=V_0\sin\omega t
}
\]

---

# 10. Peak Value and RMS Value

---

# Peak Value

Maximum value of current:

\[
\boxed{I_0}
\]

---

# RMS Value

The effective value of AC is called RMS value.

\[
\boxed{
I_{rms}=\frac{I_0}{\sqrt2}
}
\]

---

Voltage:

\[
\boxed{
V_{rms}=\frac{V_0}{\sqrt2}
}
\]

---

# 11. Average Value of AC

For one complete cycle:

\[
\boxed{
I_{avg}=0
}
\]

---

# 12. Frequency and Time Period

Frequency:

\[
\boxed{
f=\frac1T
}
\]

Angular frequency:

\[
\boxed{
\omega=2\pi f
}
\]

---

# PART D: AC CIRCUITS

---

# 13. AC Through Resistor

For resistance R:

\[
V=IR
\]

---

Phase relation:

Voltage and current are in phase.

\[
\boxed{\phi=0}
\]

---

# 14. AC Through Inductor

Inductive reactance:

\[
\boxed{
X_L=\omega L
}
\]

---

Current lags voltage by:

\[
\boxed{90^\circ}
\]

---

# 15. AC Through Capacitor

Capacitive reactance:

\[
\boxed{
X_C=\frac1{\omega C}
}
\]

---

Current leads voltage by:

\[
\boxed{90^\circ}
\]

---

# 16. Series LCR Circuit

A circuit containing:

- Resistance (R)
- Inductance (L)
- Capacitance (C)

is called an LCR circuit.

---

# Impedance

\[
\boxed{
Z=\sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2}
}
\]

---

# Current

\[
\boxed{
I=\frac VZ
}
\]

---

# Phase Angle

\[
\boxed{
\tan\phi=
\frac{X_L-X_C}{R}
}
\]

---

# 17. Resonance

## Definition

The condition when:

\[
X_L=X_C
\]

is called resonance.

---

At resonance:

\[
\boxed{
Z=R
}
\]

Current is maximum:

\[
\boxed{
I_{max}=\frac VR
}
\]

---

# Resonant Frequency

\[
\boxed{
f_0=
\frac1{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}
}
\]

---

# Quality Factor

\[
\boxed{
Q=
\frac{\omega_0L}{R}
}
\]

---

# PART E: AC GENERATOR

---

# 18. AC Generator

## Principle

It works on:

\[
\boxed{\text{Electromagnetic induction}}
\]

---

# Construction

Main parts:

- Armature coil
- Magnetic field
- Slip rings
- Brushes

---

# Working

When the coil rotates in magnetic field:

- Magnetic flux changes.
- Induced emf is produced.

---

# Generated EMF

\[
\boxed{
E=E_0\sin\omega t
}
\]

---

# PART F: TRANSFORMER

---

# 19. Transformer

## Definition

A transformer is a device used to increase or decrease AC voltage.

---

Principle:

\[
\boxed{\text{Mutual induction}}
\]

---

# Types

## Step-Up Transformer

Increases voltage.

\[
\boxed{
N_s>N_p
}
\]

---

## Step-Down Transformer

Decreases voltage.

\[
\boxed{
N_s<N_p
}
\]

---

# 20. Transformer Equation

\[
\boxed{
\frac{V_s}{V_p}
=

\frac{N_s}{N_p}
}
\]

---

where:

- \(V_s\) = secondary voltage
- \(V_p\) = primary voltage
- \(N_s\) = secondary turns
- \(N_p\) = primary turns

---

# Current Relation

For ideal transformer:

\[
\boxed{
V_pI_p=V_sI_s
}
\]

---

# 21. Energy Losses in Transformer

---

## 1. Copper Loss

Due to resistance of coils.

\[
I^2R
\]

---

## 2. Eddy Current Loss

Due to induced currents in core.

Reduced by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Laminated core}}
\]

---

## 3. Hysteresis Loss

Due to repeated magnetisation.

Reduced by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Soft iron core}}
\]

---

## 4. Flux Leakage

Some magnetic flux does not link both coils.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting the negative sign in Faraday's law.
- Confusing self induction and mutual induction.
- Mixing peak value and RMS value.
- Using wrong reactance formula.
- Forgetting phase difference in AC circuits.
- Confusing step-up and step-down transformers.
- Ignoring transformer losses.
- Using DC formulas for AC circuits.

---

# Unit Summary

- Electromagnetic induction explains generation of electricity from magnetic fields.
- Faraday's law gives induced emf.
- Lenz's law determines direction of induced current.
- AC changes magnitude and direction periodically.
- LCR circuits show resonance behaviour.
- Transformers work on mutual induction and transfer AC power efficiently.

\boxed{
E=-N\frac{d\Phi}{dt}
}

is the fundamental equation of electromagnetic induction.
