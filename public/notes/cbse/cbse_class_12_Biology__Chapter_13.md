# Introduction

**Biodiversity** refers to the variety and variability of life forms present on Earth. It includes diversity among genes, species, and ecosystems.

The term biodiversity was popularized by:

```
Edward Wilson (1988)
```

---

# 1. Levels of Biodiversity

Biodiversity exists at three main levels:

```
Biodiversity

├── Genetic Diversity

├── Species Diversity

└── Ecosystem Diversity
```

---

# A. Genetic Diversity

## Definition

Variation in genes within a species.

---

## Examples

- Different varieties of rice.
- Different breeds of dogs.
- Genetic variation among humans.

---

## Importance

- Helps organisms adapt to environmental changes.
- Increases survival chances.

---

# B. Species Diversity

## Definition

Variety and richness of species in a region.

---

## Examples

- Number of plant and animal species in a forest.
- Species richness in tropical regions.

---

# C. Ecosystem Diversity

## Definition

Variety of ecosystems in a geographical area.

---

## Examples

- Forests
- Deserts
- Wetlands
- Grasslands
- Oceans

---

# Comparison of Biodiversity Levels

| Genetic Diversity        | Species Diversity  | Ecosystem Diversity   |
| ------------------------ | ------------------ | --------------------- |
| Variation within species | Variety of species | Variety of ecosystems |
| Genes involved           | Species involved   | Habitats involved     |

---

---

# 3. Latitudinal Gradient

## Observation

Tropical regions have greater biodiversity than temperate and polar regions.

---

## Reasons

### A. Tropical Regions Have:

- Stable climate.
- More sunlight.
- Longer evolutionary time.
- Higher productivity.

---

### B. Polar Regions Have:

- Extreme conditions.
- Lower productivity.
- Seasonal limitations.

---

# 4. Species-Area Relationship

Proposed by:

```
Alexander von Humboldt
```

---

## Statement

Within a region, species richness increases with increasing area.

---

## Equation

```
S = CAᶻ
```

Where:

- S = Species richness
- C = Constant
- A = Area
- Z = Slope of regression

---

## Log Form

```
log S = log C + Z log A
```

---

## Significance

Used to estimate biodiversity loss due to habitat destruction.

---

# 7. Loss of Biodiversity

## Current Situation

Human activities are causing rapid biodiversity loss.

---

# Causes of Biodiversity Loss

Known as:

```
The Evil Quartet
```

---

# 1. Habitat Loss and Fragmentation

## Definition

Destruction or division of natural habitats.

---

## Examples

- Deforestation
- Urbanization
- Agricultural expansion

---

## Effects

- Loss of species.
- Reduced population size.
- Extinction risk.

---

# 2. Over-Exploitation

## Definition

Excessive use of biological resources.

---

## Examples

- Overfishing
- Hunting
- Excessive logging

---

# 3. Alien Species Invasion

## Definition

Introduction of non-native species that negatively affect native species.

---

## Examples

### Water Hyacinth

Scientific name:

```
Eichhornia crassipes
```

Effects:

- Blocks waterways.
- Reduces oxygen levels.

---

### Lantana camara

Competes with native plants.

---

### African Catfish

Threatens native fish species.

---

# 4. Co-extinction

## Definition

Extinction of one species causing extinction of another dependent species.

---

## Examples

- Host-specific parasites.
- Pollinators and plants.

---

# 8. Extinction

## Definition

Permanent disappearance of a species from Earth.

---

## Causes

- Habitat destruction
- Climate change
- Pollution
- Over-exploitation
- Invasive species

---

# Background Extinction vs Mass Extinction

| Background Extinction | Mass Extinction              |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Slow natural process  | Rapid large-scale extinction |
| Few species affected  | Many species affected        |

---

# 9. Conservation of Biodiversity

## Definition

Protection and management of biodiversity to prevent extinction and maintain ecosystems.

---

# Two Main Strategies

```
Conservation

├── In-situ Conservation

└── Ex-situ Conservation
```

---

# 10. In-Situ Conservation

## Definition

Conservation of species in their natural habitats.

---

## Methods

---

# A. Biosphere Reserves

Large protected areas conserving:

- Wildlife
- Plants
- Traditional lifestyles

---

## Zones

### Core Zone

Strict protection.

---

### Buffer Zone

Research and limited activities.

---

### Transition Zone

Human activities allowed.

---

# B. National Parks

Protected areas for wildlife conservation.

Features:

- No human exploitation.
- Strict protection.

---

# C. Wildlife Sanctuaries

Areas where wildlife is protected.

Limited human activities may be allowed.

---

# D. Sacred Groves

Forest patches protected due to religious and cultural beliefs.

---

## Importance

- Preserve rare species.
- Protect genetic diversity.

---

# 11. Ex-Situ Conservation

## Definition

Conservation of species outside their natural habitats.

---

## Methods

---

# A. Zoological Parks

Protect and breed animals.

---

# B. Botanical Gardens

Maintain plant species.

---

# C. Seed Banks

Store seeds of endangered plants.

---

# D. Cryopreservation

Preservation of biological materials at very low temperatures.

Temperature:

```
-196°C
```

(using liquid nitrogen)

---

# E. Tissue Culture

Used for rapid multiplication of rare plants.

---

# Comparison: In-Situ vs Ex-Situ Conservation

| In-Situ                     | Ex-Situ                   |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Natural habitat             | Outside habitat           |
| Protects ecosystems         | Protects selected species |
| National parks, sanctuaries | Zoos, seed banks          |

---

# 12. Red Data Book

## Definition

A record containing information about threatened species.

Maintained by:

```
IUCN
```

(International Union for Conservation of Nature)

---

# 13. Biodiversity Hotspots

## Definition

Regions with:

- High species richness.
- High endemism.
- Severe threat of habitat loss.

---

## Criteria

A hotspot must have:

- At least 1500 endemic vascular plant species.
- Lost at least 70% of original habitat.

---

# 14. Sacred Groves

## Definition

Forest patches protected by local communities due to religious traditions.

---

## Importance

- Preserve rare plants.
- Maintain biodiversity.
- Protect gene pools.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

**Question:** What are the three levels of biodiversity?

**Solution:**

The three levels of biodiversity are:

1. Genetic diversity
2. Species diversity
3. Ecosystem diversity

---

### Example 2

**Question:** Explain the species-area relationship.

**Solution:**

According to Alexander von Humboldt, species richness increases with increasing area. The relationship is represented as:

```
S = CAᶻ
```

where S represents species richness and A represents area.

---

### Example 3

**Question:** What are the major causes of biodiversity loss?

**Solution:**

The major causes, known as the **Evil Quartet**, are:

1. Habitat loss and fragmentation
2. Over-exploitation
3. Alien species invasion
4. Co-extinction

---

### Example 4

**Question:** Differentiate between in-situ and ex-situ conservation.

**Solution:**

In-situ conservation protects species in their natural habitats, such as national parks and wildlife sanctuaries. Ex-situ conservation protects species outside their natural habitats, such as zoos, botanical gardens, and seed banks.

---

### Example 5

**Question:** Why are biodiversity hotspots important?

**Solution:**

Biodiversity hotspots contain high species richness and many endemic species but face severe habitat loss. Protecting them helps conserve a large number of threatened species.

---

### Example 6

**Question:** What is cryopreservation?

**Solution:**

Cryopreservation is the preservation of biological materials such as seeds, gametes, or tissues at extremely low temperatures, usually using liquid nitrogen at **−196°C**.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing biodiversity levels; genetic diversity occurs within species, while species diversity refers to variety of species.
- Forgetting that biodiversity is highest in tropical regions near the equator.
- Mixing up habitat loss and over-exploitation.
- Confusing in-situ and ex-situ conservation methods.
- Assuming all introduced species are harmful; only invasive alien species negatively affect native biodiversity.
- Forgetting the components of the Evil Quartet.
- Confusing biodiversity hotspots with areas having only high biodiversity; hotspots also require high endemism and severe habitat loss.
- Assuming conservation means only protecting animals; plants, microorganisms, ecosystems, and genetic resources are also conserved.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Biodiversity represents the variety of life at genetic, species, and ecosystem levels.
- Tropical regions show greater biodiversity due to favorable climate, productivity, and evolutionary history.
- The species-area relationship explains how species richness increases with area.
- Biodiversity provides ecological, economic, ethical, and aesthetic benefits.
- Major causes of biodiversity loss are habitat destruction, over-exploitation, invasive species, and co-extinction.
- Conservation strategies include **in-situ conservation** (national parks, sanctuaries, biosphere reserves) and **ex-situ conservation** (zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks, cryopreservation).
- Biodiversity hotspots and protected areas play an important role in preventing extinction.
- Sustainable use and global cooperation are essential for conserving Earth's biological diversity.
