# Introduction

**Electricity** is one of the most widely used forms of energy. It powers homes, industries, hospitals, communication systems, and transportation. Electric current flows through conductors due to the movement of electric charges. This chapter explains electric current, potential difference, Ohm's law, resistance, combination of resistors, heating effect of electric current, electric power, and electrical energy.

---

# 1. Electric Charge

**Electric charge** is the fundamental property of matter responsible for electrical phenomena.

### Types of Charges

- Positive charge
- Negative charge

### SI Unit

```
Coulomb (C)
```

---

# 2. Electric Current

**Electric current** is the rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor.

### Formula

```
I = Q/t
```

Where:

- **I** = Electric current
- **Q** = Electric charge
- **t** = Time

### SI Unit

```
Ampere (A)
```

### Definition of 1 Ampere

A current of **1 ampere** flows when **1 coulomb** of charge passes through a conductor in **1 second**.

---

# 3. Electric Circuit

An **electric circuit** is a closed conducting path through which electric current flows.

### Basic Components

- Cell or battery
- Connecting wires
- Switch (key)
- Bulb or resistor

---

### Simple Electric Circuit

```
 +| |−
 Battery
   │
 Switch
   │
 Bulb
   │
 ─────────
```

---

# 4. Electric Potential Difference

**Potential difference** is the work done in moving a unit positive charge from one point to another.

### Formula

```
V = W/Q
```

Where:

- **V** = Potential difference
- **W** = Work done
- **Q** = Charge

### SI Unit

```
Volt (V)
```

### Measuring Instrument

```
Voltmeter
```

A voltmeter is connected **in parallel** with the component.

---

# 5. Electric Current Measurement

### Measuring Instrument

```
Ammeter
```

An ammeter is connected **in series** with the circuit.

---

# 6. Resistance

**Resistance** is the property of a conductor that opposes the flow of electric current.

### Symbol

```
R
```

### SI Unit

```
Ohm (Ω)
```

---

## Factors Affecting Resistance

Resistance depends on:

- Length of the conductor.
- Area of cross-section.
- Nature of the material.
- Temperature.

### Formula

```
R = ρL/A
```

Where:

- **R** = Resistance
- **ρ (rho)** = Resistivity
- **L** = Length
- **A** = Area of cross-section

### SI Unit of Resistivity

```
Ohm metre (Ω m)
```

---

# 7. Ohm's Law

**Ohm's Law** states that, at constant temperature, the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it.

### Mathematical Form

```
V ∝ I
```

or

```
V = IR
```

Where:

- **V** = Potential difference
- **I** = Current
- **R** = Resistance

---

## V-I Graph

For an ohmic conductor, the graph between voltage and current is a straight line passing through the origin.

```
V
│
│
│      /
│     /
│    /
│___/________ I
```

---

# 8. Combination of Resistors

Resistors can be connected in:

- Series
- Parallel

---

## (A) Resistors in Series

### Circuit

```
Battery

──R₁──R₂──R₃──
```

### Characteristics

- Same current flows through each resistor.
- Potential difference is divided.

### Equivalent Resistance

```
R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃
```

---

## (B) Resistors in Parallel

### Circuit

```
      R₁
────/\/\/\────
│            │
│            │
────/\/\/\────
      R₂
│            │
────/\/\/\────
      R₃
```

### Characteristics

- Same potential difference across each resistor.
- Current divides among branches.

### Equivalent Resistance

```
1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃
```

---

## Difference Between Series and Parallel Combination

| Series                                     | Parallel                          |
| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| Same current flows                         | Same voltage across each resistor |
| Voltage is divided                         | Current is divided                |
| Equivalent resistance increases            | Equivalent resistance decreases   |
| Failure of one resistor breaks the circuit | Other branches continue to work   |

# 9. Heating Effect of Electric Current

When electric current passes through a resistor, electrical energy is converted into heat energy.

This is called the **heating effect of electric current**.

---

## Joule's Law of Heating

The heat produced is:

- Directly proportional to the square of current.
- Directly proportional to resistance.
- Directly proportional to time.

### Formula

```
H = I²Rt
```

Where:

- **H** = Heat produced
- **I** = Current
- **R** = Resistance
- **t** = Time

### SI Unit

```
Joule (J)
```

---

## Applications of Heating Effect

- Electric iron.
- Electric heater.
- Electric kettle.
- Toaster.
- Geyser.
- Electric fuse.

---

# 10. Electric Fuse

An **electric fuse** is a safety device that protects electrical circuits from excessive current.

### Working Principle

A fuse wire melts when excessive current flows through it due to the heating effect of electric current.

### Characteristics of Fuse Wire

- Low melting point.
- High resistance.

---

# 11. Electric Power

**Electric power** is the rate at which electrical energy is consumed or converted.

### Formula

```
P = W/t
```

Also,

```
P = VI
```

Using Ohm's law,

```
P = I²R
```

and

```
P = V²/R
```

### SI Unit

```
Watt (W)
```

### Larger Unit

```
Kilowatt (kW)

1 kW = 1000 W
```

---

# 12. Electrical Energy

Electrical energy is the total electrical work done or power consumed over time.

### Formula

```
E = Pt
```

### SI Unit

```
Joule (J)
```

---

## Commercial Unit of Electrical Energy

```
Kilowatt-hour (kWh)
```

Also called

```
One Unit
```

### Conversion

```
1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J
```

# 13. Household Electric Circuits

Domestic electrical wiring generally consists of:

- Live wire
- Neutral wire
- Earth wire

---

## Earth Wire

The earth wire:

- Prevents electric shock.
- Provides a low-resistance path for leakage current.
- Is connected to the metal body of electrical appliances.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

A charge of **20 C** flows through a conductor in **5 s**. Find the current.

**Solution**

Using,

```
I = Q/t
```

```
I = 20/5 = 4 A
```

**Answer:** **4 A**

---

### Example 2

A resistor of **10 Ω** carries a current of **2 A**. Find the potential difference.

**Solution**

Using Ohm's law,

```
V = IR
```

```
V = 2 × 10 = 20 V
```

**Answer:** **20 V**

---

### Example 3

Find the equivalent resistance of **3 Ω** and **5 Ω** connected in series.

**Solution**

```
R = 3 + 5 = 8 Ω
```

**Answer:** **8 Ω**

---

### Example 4

Calculate the power consumed by an appliance operating at **220 V** and drawing **2 A**.

**Solution**

Using,

```
P = VI
```

```
P = 220 × 2 = 440 W
```

**Answer:** **440 W**

---

### Example 5

Calculate the heat produced when **2 A** current flows through a **5 Ω** resistor for **10 s**.

**Solution**

Using,

```
H = I²Rt
```

```
H = (2)² × 5 × 10

= 4 × 5 × 10

= 200 J
```

**Answer:** **200 J**

---

### Example 6

Convert **2 kWh** into joules.

**Solution**

```
1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J
```

```
2 kWh

= 2 × 3.6 × 10⁶

= 7.2 × 10⁶ J
```

**Answer:** **7.2 × 10⁶ J**

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **current** with **potential difference**.
- Using the wrong unit for **resistance** or **power**.
- Forgetting to convert **kilowatts to watts** before calculations.
- Mixing up the formulas for **series** and **parallel** combinations of resistors.
- Forgetting that a **voltmeter is connected in parallel** and an **ammeter in series**.
- Using **centimetres** instead of **metres** while applying resistance formulas.
- Confusing **electrical energy** with **electric power**.
- Forgetting that **1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J**.

# Chapter Summary

- **Electric current** is the flow of electric charge and is measured in **amperes (A)**.
- **Potential difference** causes the flow of current and is measured in **volts (V)**.
- **Ohm's Law** states that, at constant temperature, the potential difference across a conductor is directly proportional to the current through it.
- The resistance of a conductor depends on its **length, cross-sectional area, material,** and **temperature**.
- In a **series combination**, the equivalent resistance increases, whereas in a **parallel combination**, it decreases.
- The **heating effect of electric current** is explained by **Joule's Law**, which forms the basis of electrical heating appliances.
- **Electric power** is the rate of consumption of electrical energy and is measured in **watts (W)**.
- The commercial unit of electrical energy is the **kilowatt-hour (kWh)**, where **1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J**.
- Household electrical safety is ensured using **fuses** and **earth wires**, which protect appliances and users from excessive current and electric shock.
