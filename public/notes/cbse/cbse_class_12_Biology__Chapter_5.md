# Introduction

The **molecular basis of inheritance** deals with the structure, function, replication, and expression of genetic material. DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) stores hereditary information and directs protein synthesis through transcription and translation. Advances in molecular biology have enabled technologies such as DNA fingerprinting, recombinant DNA technology, and genome sequencing.

---

# 1. The Genetic Material

## Characteristics of Genetic Material

An ideal genetic material should:

- Store genetic information.
- Replicate accurately.
- Express information as proteins.
- Undergo mutation to produce variation.
- Remain chemically stable.

---

## DNA vs RNA as Genetic Material

| DNA                                | RNA                              |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Deoxyribose sugar                  | Ribose sugar                     |
| Double-stranded (usually)          | Single-stranded (usually)        |
| More stable                        | Less stable                      |
| Genetic material in most organisms | Genetic material in some viruses |

# 3. Structure of DNA (Watson and Crick Model)

In **1953**, James Watson and Francis Crick proposed the **double helix model** of DNA.

---

## Features

- Two antiparallel polynucleotide strands.
- Right-handed double helix.
- Sugar-phosphate backbone on the outside.
- Nitrogenous bases inside.
- Complementary base pairing.

---

## Base Pairing

```
A = T

(2 Hydrogen Bonds)

G ≡ C

(3 Hydrogen Bonds)
```

---

## Chargaff's Rule

```
A = T

G = C

Purines = Pyrimidines
```

# 5. Search for Genetic Material

## Griffith's Experiment (1928)

Studied _Streptococcus pneumoniae_.

---

### Observation

Heat-killed virulent bacteria transformed non-virulent bacteria into virulent forms.

---

### Conclusion

Some "transforming principle" transferred hereditary information.

---

# 6. Avery, MacLeod and McCarty Experiment

They demonstrated that the transforming principle is **DNA**.

---

## Conclusion

DNA is the genetic material.

---

# 7. Hershey-Chase Experiment

Used:

- Radioactive phosphorus (**³²P**) to label DNA.
- Radioactive sulfur (**³⁵S**) to label proteins.

---

## Observation

Only DNA entered bacterial cells during bacteriophage infection.

---

## Conclusion

DNA is the genetic material.

# 8. DNA Replication

## Definition

DNA replication is the process by which DNA makes an identical copy of itself before cell division.

---

## Semi-Conservative Replication

Proposed by:

- Watson and Crick

Experimentally proved by:

- Meselson and Stahl (1958)

Each daughter DNA molecule contains:

- One parental strand.
- One newly synthesized strand.

---

## Steps

### 1. Initiation

- Replication begins at the **origin of replication (Ori)**.
- Helicase unwinds the DNA.

---

### 2. Elongation

- DNA polymerase synthesizes new DNA.
- Synthesis occurs only in the **5' → 3' direction**.

---

### 3. Termination

Two daughter DNA molecules are formed.

---

## Leading and Lagging Strands

| Leading Strand          | Lagging Strand             |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Continuous synthesis    | Discontinuous synthesis    |
| Toward replication fork | Away from replication fork |
| No Okazaki fragments    | Okazaki fragments present  |

---

## Enzymes Involved

| Enzyme         | Function                  |
| -------------- | ------------------------- |
| Helicase       | Unwinds DNA               |
| Primase        | Synthesizes RNA primer    |
| DNA Polymerase | Adds nucleotides          |
| DNA Ligase     | Joins Okazaki fragments   |
| Topoisomerase  | Relieves torsional strain |

# 9. Transcription

## Definition

Formation of RNA from a DNA template.

---

## Enzyme

RNA Polymerase

---

## Steps

### A. Initiation

RNA polymerase binds to the **promoter** region.

---

### B. Elongation

RNA nucleotides are added complementary to the DNA template.

---

### C. Termination

RNA polymerase reaches the **terminator** sequence and releases the RNA transcript.

---

## Types of RNA

| RNA  | Function              |
| ---- | --------------------- |
| mRNA | Carries genetic code  |
| tRNA | Transfers amino acids |
| rRNA | Forms ribosomes       |

- Read in a fixed direction (5' → 3')

# 12. Translation

## Definition

Protein synthesis from mRNA.

---

## Site

Ribosomes

---

## Steps

### 1. Activation of Amino Acids

Each amino acid binds to its specific tRNA.

---

### 2. Initiation

The small ribosomal subunit binds to mRNA, followed by the initiator tRNA at the AUG start codon.

---

### 3. Elongation

- tRNA molecules bring amino acids.
- Peptide bonds are formed.
- Ribosome moves along the mRNA.

---

### 4. Termination

When a stop codon is reached, the completed polypeptide is released.

---

## Codon–Anticodon Pairing

```
mRNA : AUG

tRNA : UAC
```

This complementary pairing ensures the correct amino acid is incorporated.

# 13. Regulation of Gene Expression – Lac Operon

Proposed by:

- Jacob and Monod

---

## Components

- Regulator gene (i)
- Promoter (P)
- Operator (O)
- Structural genes (Z, Y, A)

---

## In the Absence of Lactose

- Repressor protein binds to the operator.
- RNA polymerase cannot transcribe structural genes.

---

## In the Presence of Lactose

- Lactose (allolactose) acts as an inducer.
- Repressor becomes inactive.
- Structural genes are transcribed.
- Enzymes for lactose metabolism are produced.

---

## Simplified Diagram

```

↓

Operator

↓

Structural Genes

↓

Enzyme Production
```

# 15. DNA Fingerprinting

Developed by:

**Sir Alec Jeffreys (1985)**

---

## Principle

Based on variations in **Variable Number Tandem Repeats (VNTRs)** among individuals.

---

## Steps

1. Isolation of DNA.
2. Restriction enzyme digestion.
3. Gel electrophoresis.
4. Southern blotting.
5. Hybridization with labeled probes.
6. Autoradiography.
7. Comparison of banding patterns.

---

## Applications

- Forensic science
- Paternity testing
- Criminal investigations
- Identification of disaster victims
- Wildlife conservation
- Population genetics

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

**Question:** State Chargaff's rule.

**Solution:**

Chargaff's rule states that in double-stranded DNA:

- Adenine (A) equals Thymine (T)
- Guanine (G) equals Cytosine (C)

Thus:

```
A = T

G = C
```

---

### Example 2

**Question:** Why is DNA considered the genetic material in most organisms?

**Solution:**

DNA is chemically stable, replicates accurately, stores hereditary information, undergoes mutation to generate variation, and directs protein synthesis. Experiments by Avery–MacLeod–McCarty and Hershey–Chase confirmed that DNA is the genetic material.

---

### Example 3

**Question:** Differentiate between replication and transcription.

**Solution:**

| Replication                 | Transcription                 |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| DNA forms DNA               | DNA forms RNA                 |
| DNA polymerase acts         | RNA polymerase acts           |
| Occurs before cell division | Occurs during gene expression |

---

### Example 4

**Question:** Why is DNA replication called semi-conservative?

**Solution:**

Each daughter DNA molecule contains one parental (old) strand and one newly synthesized strand. This mode of replication was experimentally demonstrated by Meselson and Stahl.

---

### Example 5

**Question:** What is the role of the lac operon?

**Solution:**

The lac operon regulates the expression of genes involved in lactose metabolism. In the presence of lactose, the repressor is inactivated, allowing transcription of structural genes that produce enzymes required for lactose utilization.

---

### Example 6

**Question:** Mention two applications of DNA fingerprinting.

**Solution:**

DNA fingerprinting is used for:

- Forensic identification of criminals.
- Paternity and maternity testing.

It is also useful in identifying disaster victims, studying population genetics, and wildlife conservation.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing a **nucleotide** (base + sugar + phosphate) with a **nucleoside** (base + sugar only).
- Forgetting that **A pairs with T** through **two hydrogen bonds**, while **G pairs with C** through **three hydrogen bonds**.
- Mixing up **DNA replication** with **transcription**; replication copies DNA, whereas transcription synthesizes RNA.
- Assuming DNA polymerase synthesizes DNA in both directions; synthesis always occurs in the **5' → 3' direction**.
- Confusing **leading** and **lagging** strands and overlooking the role of **Okazaki fragments**.
- Forgetting the **start codon (AUG)** and the **stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA)**.
- Misunderstanding the **lac operon**; structural genes are expressed only when the repressor is inactivated by the inducer (allolactose).
- Assuming DNA fingerprinting analyzes entire genomes; it primarily compares highly variable repetitive DNA regions such as **VNTRs**.

# Chapter Summary

- **DNA** is the primary genetic material in most organisms and consists of nucleotides arranged in a double-helical structure.
- The **Watson and Crick model** explains complementary base pairing and the antiparallel arrangement of DNA strands.
- In eukaryotes, DNA is packaged around histone proteins to form **nucleosomes**, which organize into chromatin and chromosomes.
- Experiments by **Griffith**, **Avery–MacLeod–McCarty**, and **Hershey–Chase** established DNA as the genetic material.
- DNA undergoes **semi-conservative replication**, ensuring faithful transmission of genetic information during cell division.
- **Transcription** produces RNA from DNA, while **translation** synthesizes proteins according to the genetic code.
- The **genetic code** is triplet, nearly universal, degenerate, and read from a fixed start codon (**AUG**) to one of three stop codons.
- The **lac operon** demonstrates regulation of gene expression in prokaryotes through inducible control of lactose-metabolizing genes.
- The **Human Genome Project** expanded understanding of human genetics, and **DNA fingerprinting** provides powerful applications in forensics, medicine, and biological research.
