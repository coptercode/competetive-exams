# Introduction

**Electrochemistry** is the branch of chemistry that deals with the relationship between chemical reactions and electrical energy.

It involves:

- Production of electricity from chemical reactions.
- Use of electricity to drive chemical reactions.

---

# 1. Electrochemical Cells

## Definition

An electrochemical cell is a device where chemical energy is converted into electrical energy or electrical energy is converted into chemical energy.

---

# Types of Electrochemical Cells

```

       |
       |
```

# A. Galvanic Cell

## Definition

A cell in which a spontaneous chemical reaction produces electrical energy.

Example:

```
Daniell Cell
```

---

## Daniell Cell

Reaction:

\[
Zn + Cu^{2+}\rightarrow Zn^{2+}+Cu
\]

---

## Components

### Anode

- Zinc electrode
- Oxidation occurs

\[
Zn\rightarrow Zn^{2+}+2e^-
\]

---

### Cathode

- Copper electrode
- Reduction occurs

\[
Cu^{2+}+2e^-\rightarrow Cu
\]

---

# Cell Representation

\[
Zn|Zn^{2+}||Cu^{2+}|Cu
\]

---

# Salt Bridge

## Functions

1. Completes electrical circuit.
2. Maintains electrical neutrality.
3. Prevents direct mixing of solutions.
4. Minimizes liquid junction potential.

---

# 2. Electrode Potential

## Definition

The tendency of an electrode to lose or gain electrons when placed in a solution.

---

# Standard Electrode Potential (E°)

Measured under:

- 1 M concentration
- 1 atm pressure
- 298 K temperature

---

## Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)

Reference electrode:

\[
2H^+ +2e^- \rightarrow H_2
\]

Standard potential:

\[
E^\circ=0V
\]

---

# 3. Cell Potential

## Formula

\[
E^\circ_{cell}=E^\circ_{cathode}-E^\circ_{anode}
\]

---

## Interpretation

### Positive E°cell

Reaction is spontaneous.

### Negative E°cell

Reaction is non-spontaneous.

---

# 4. Gibbs Energy and Cell Potential

Relationship:

\[
\Delta G^\circ=-nFE^\circ_{cell}
\]

where:

- n = number of electrons
- F = Faraday constant

---

## Meaning

If:

\[
E^\circ_{cell}>0
\]

then:

\[
\Delta G^\circ<0
\]

Reaction is spontaneous.

---

# 5. Nernst Equation

## Definition

Equation used to calculate cell potential under non-standard conditions.

---

## General Equation

\[
E=E^\circ-\frac{0.0591}{n}\log Q
\]

(at 298 K)

---

where:

- E = cell potential
- E° = standard potential
- n = electrons transferred
- Q = reaction quotient

---

# For Daniell Cell

Reaction:

\[
Zn+Cu^{2+}\rightarrow Zn^{2+}+Cu
\]

\[
Q=\frac{[Zn^{2+}]}{[Cu^{2+}]}
\]

---

# 6. Electrolytic Conductance

## Definition

Ability of a solution to conduct electricity.

Depends on:

- Number of ions
- Charge of ions
- Mobility of ions

---

# Resistance

\[
R=\rho\frac{l}{A}
\]

where:

- ρ = resistivity
- l = length
- A = area

---

# Conductance

\[
G=\frac{1}{R}
\]

Unit:

\[
Siemens(S)
\]

---

# 7. Conductivity (Specific Conductance)

## Definition

Conductance of a solution placed between electrodes of unit length and unit area.

---

## Formula

\[
\kappa=G\frac{l}{A}
\]

---

## Unit

\[
S\,cm^{-1}
\]

---

# 8. Molar Conductivity

## Definition

Conductance of all ions produced by one mole of electrolyte placed between electrodes.

---

## Formula

\[
\Lambda_m=
\frac{\kappa\times1000}{C}
\]

where:

- C = concentration in mol/L

---

## Unit

\[
S\,cm^2 mol^{-1}
\]

---

# 9. Variation of Conductivity

## Strong Electrolytes

Examples:

- HCl
- NaCl

Conductivity increases sharply on dilution.

---

## Weak Electrolytes

Examples:

- CH₃COOH

Conductivity increases greatly on dilution due to increased ionization.

---

# 10. Kohlrausch's Law

## Statement

At infinite dilution, each ion contributes independently to the total molar conductivity.

---

## Formula

\[
\Lambda_m^0=
\lambda^0_+ + \lambda^0_-
\]

---

# Applications

## 1. Finding limiting molar conductivity.

## 2. Calculating degree of dissociation.

## 3. Finding dissociation constants of weak electrolytes.

# 11. Electrolysis

## Definition

The process of using electrical energy to drive a non-spontaneous chemical reaction.

---

# Electrolytic Cell

Components:

- Anode (+)
- Cathode (-)
- Electrolyte

---

# At Electrodes

## Cathode

Reduction occurs.

Example:

\[
Cu^{2+}+2e^-\rightarrow Cu
\]

---

## Anode

Oxidation occurs.

Example:

\[
2Cl^-\rightarrow Cl_2+2e^-
\]

---

# 12. Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis

---

# First Law

## Statement

The mass of substance deposited is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity passed.

\[
m\propto Q
\]

Since:

\[
Q=It
\]

Therefore:

\[
m=ZIt
\]

where:

- Z = electrochemical equivalent

---

# Second Law

## Statement

When the same quantity of electricity passes through different electrolytes, masses deposited are proportional to their equivalent masses.

---

## Formula

\[
m\propto \frac{M}{n}
\]

---

# Faraday Constant

One mole of electrons:

\[
F=96500C
\]

---

Cannot be recharged.

Example:

\]

---

# 14. Fuel Cells

Cells that convert fuel directly into electrical energy.

---

# Hydrogen-Oxygen Fuel Cell

Reaction:

\[
2H_2+O_2\rightarrow2H_2O
\]

---

- High efficiency.
- Environment friendly.
- Produces water as product.

---

# 15. Corrosion

## Definition

Slow deterioration of metals due to chemical or electrochemical reactions with the environment.

---

# Rusting of Iron

Requires:

- Oxygen
- Water

---

# Mechanism

## Anode:

\[
Fe\rightarrow Fe^{2+}+2e^-
\]

---

## Cathode:

\[
O_2+2H_2O+4e^-
\rightarrow4OH^-
\]

---

Formation:

\[
Fe(OH)_2
\]

which becomes:

\[
Fe_2O_3.xH_2O
\]

(rust)

---

# Prevention of Corrosion

Methods:

1. Painting
2. Oiling
3. Galvanization
4. Alloy formation
5. Cathodic protection

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** Calculate E°cell if:

\[
E^\circ_{Cu^{2+}/Cu}=+0.34V
\]

\[
E^\circ_{Zn^{2+}/Zn}=-0.76V
\]

### Solution

\[
E^\circ_{cell}
=

0.34-(-0.76)
\]

\[
\boxed{E^\circ_{cell}=1.10V}
\]

---

## Example 2

**Question:** Why does a salt bridge maintain electrical neutrality?

### Solution

During cell operation, ions accumulate in half-cells. Salt bridge supplies opposite ions, preventing charge buildup and maintaining electrical neutrality.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing anode and cathode signs in galvanic and electrolytic cells.
- Forgetting that oxidation always occurs at the anode and reduction at the cathode.
- Using Nernst equation without including the reaction quotient.
- Mixing conductivity with molar conductivity.
- Forgetting that molar conductivity increases on dilution.
- Assuming primary batteries can be recharged.
- Confusing fuel cells with ordinary batteries.
- Forgetting that corrosion is an electrochemical process involving oxidation and reduction.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Electrochemistry explains the conversion between chemical and electrical energy.
- Galvanic cells produce electricity through spontaneous reactions, while electrolytic cells use electricity to drive reactions.
- Cell potential determines the spontaneity of electrochemical reactions.
- The Nernst equation calculates electrode potential under non-standard conditions.
- Conductance, conductivity, and molar conductivity describe the ability of solutions to conduct electricity.
- Kohlrausch's law helps calculate limiting molar conductivity and properties of weak electrolytes.
- Faraday's laws explain the quantitative relationship between electricity and chemical changes.
- Batteries and fuel cells are practical applications of electrochemistry.
- Corrosion is an electrochemical process that can be prevented using protective methods.
