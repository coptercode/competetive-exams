# Introduction

**Morphology** is the branch of biology that deals with the **external structure and form** of organisms. In flowering plants (angiosperms), morphology involves the study of the **root, stem, leaves, flowers, fruits, and seeds**, along with their modifications and functions.

This chapter explains the morphology of vegetative and reproductive parts of flowering plants, different types of inflorescences, aestivation, placentation, and the characteristics of important plant families such as **Fabaceae** and **Solanaceae**.

---

# 1. Parts of a Flowering Plant

A flowering plant consists of two major systems:

### (A) Root System

- Develops from the **radicle**.
- Usually grows below the soil.
- Anchors the plant.
- Absorbs water and minerals.
- Stores food in some plants.

---

### (B) Shoot System

- Develops from the **plumule**.
- Consists of stem, branches, leaves, flowers, fruits, and seeds.
- Responsible for support, photosynthesis, transport, and reproduction.

# 3. Types of Root Systems

---

## (A) Tap Root System

Develops from the radicle.

Characteristics

- One main root.
- Lateral branches arise from the main root.
- Common in dicots.

Examples

- Mustard
- Gram
- Mango

---

## (B) Fibrous Root System

The primary root is replaced by many roots of similar size.

Characteristics

- No main root.
- Common in monocots.

Examples

- Wheat
- Rice
- Maize

---

## (C) Adventitious Root System

Roots arise from parts other than the radicle.

Examples

- Banyan
- Sugarcane
- Money plant

# 11. Inflorescence

Inflorescence is the arrangement of flowers on the floral axis.

---

## (A) Racemose Inflorescence

- Main axis continues to grow.
- Flowers develop in **acropetal succession** (older flowers at the base, younger at the top).

Examples

- Mustard
- Radish

---

## (B) Cymose Inflorescence

- Main axis terminates in a flower.
- Flowers develop in **basipetal succession** (older flowers at the top, younger at the base).

Examples

- Jasmine
- Ixora

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Differentiate between tap root and fibrous root systems.

**Solution**

- **Tap root:** One main root with lateral branches; common in dicots (e.g., mustard).
- **Fibrous root:** Many roots of similar size without a main root; common in monocots (e.g., wheat).

---

### Example 2

Name the stem modification found in potato and state its function.

**Solution**

The potato is a **stem tuber**, an underground stem modification that stores food and helps in vegetative propagation.

---

### Example 3

What is phyllotaxy? Name its three types.

**Solution**

Phyllotaxy is the arrangement of leaves on the stem.

Types:

- Alternate
- Opposite
- Whorled

---

### Example 4

State one difference between racemose and cymose inflorescences.

**Solution**

In racemose inflorescence, the main axis continues to grow and flowers develop in acropetal succession. In cymose inflorescence, the main axis terminates in a flower and flowers develop in basipetal succession.

---

### Example 5

What is placentation? Name any two types with examples.

**Solution**

Placentation is the arrangement of ovules inside the ovary.

- Marginal – Pea
- Axile – Tomato

---

### Example 6

Give one identifying feature each of Fabaceae and Solanaceae.

**Solution**

- **Fabaceae:** Papilionaceous flowers with marginal placentation and legume fruit.
- **Solanaceae:** Pentamerous flowers with axile placentation and berry or capsule fruit.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **root modifications** with **stem modifications**.
- Mixing up **reticulate** and **parallel venation**.
- Assuming all underground structures are roots; potato is a **stem tuber**, not a root.
- Confusing **racemose** and **cymose** inflorescences.
- Mixing up **aestivation** with **placentation**.
- Forgetting that **Fabaceae** has **marginal placentation**, while **Solanaceae** has **axile placentation**.
- Confusing **thorns** (modified stems) with **spines** (modified leaves).
- Writing incorrect floral formulas for important plant families.

# Chapter Summary

- Flowering plants consist of two major systems: the **root system** and the **shoot system**.
- Roots may be **tap**, **fibrous**, or **adventitious**, and show various modifications for storage, support, and respiration.
- Stems perform support, conduction, storage, and photosynthesis, and exhibit underground, subaerial, and aerial modifications.
- Leaves are the principal photosynthetic organs and differ in **venation**, **phyllotaxy**, and structural modifications.
- **Inflorescence** is the arrangement of flowers on the floral axis and is classified into **racemose** and **cymose** types.
- A typical flower consists of four floral whorls: **calyx**, **corolla**, **androecium**, and **gynoecium**.
- **Aestivation** describes the arrangement of sepals or petals in the floral bud, while **placentation** refers to the arrangement of ovules within the ovary.
- **Fabaceae** and **Solanaceae** are important angiosperm families with distinct floral characteristics used in plant identification.
- The study of plant morphology provides the basis for understanding plant diversity, classification, reproduction, and adaptation.
