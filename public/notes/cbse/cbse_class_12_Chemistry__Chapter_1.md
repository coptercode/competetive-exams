# Introduction

A **solution** is a homogeneous mixture of two or more components.

Examples:

- Salt solution
- Sugar solution
- Air
- Alloys

A solution consists of:

1. **Solvent** – Component present in larger amount.
2. **Solute** – Component present in smaller amount.

---

# 1. Types of Solutions

Solutions can exist in different physical states.

| Solute | Solvent | Example          |
| ------ | ------- | ---------------- |
| Gas    | Gas     | Air              |
| Gas    | Liquid  | CO₂ in water     |
| Liquid | Liquid  | Alcohol in water |
| Solid  | Liquid  | Salt in water    |
| Solid  | Solid   | Brass alloy      |

---

# 2. Concentration of Solutions

The amount of solute present in a given amount of solution is called concentration.

---

# A. Mass Percentage

## Formula

\[
\text{Mass \%} =
\frac{\text{Mass of solute}}{\text{Mass of solution}}
\times100
\]

where:

\[
\text{Mass of solution}
=

\text{Mass of solute}+\text{Mass of solvent}
\]

---

## Example

10 g salt is dissolved in 90 g water.

Mass %:

\[
=\frac{10}{10+90}\times100
\]

\[
=10\%
\]

---

# B. Volume Percentage

## Formula

\[
\text{Volume \%}
=

\frac{\text{Volume of solute}}
{\text{Volume of solution}}
\times100
\]

---

# C. Mass by Volume Percentage

Used mainly in medical solutions.

\[
\text{Mass/Volume \%}
=

\frac{\text{Mass of solute(g)}}
{\text{Volume of solution(mL)}}
\times100
\]

---

# D. Parts Per Million (ppm)

Used for very dilute solutions.

\[
ppm=
\frac{\text{Number of parts of solute}}
{\text{Number of parts of solution}}
\times10^6
\]

---

## Applications

- Pollution measurement
- Trace impurities in water

---

# E. Mole Fraction

## Definition

Ratio of moles of one component to total moles of all components.

For component A:

\[
X_A=\frac{n_A}{n_A+n_B}
\]

---

## Properties

\[
X_A+X_B=1
\]

---

# F. Molarity (M)

## Definition

Number of moles of solute dissolved per litre of solution.

---

## Formula

\[
M=
\frac{\text{Moles of solute}}
{\text{Volume of solution in litres}}
\]

---

## Unit

\[
mol\,L^{-1}
\]

---

## Example

0.5 mol NaCl in 2 L solution:

\[
M=\frac{0.5}{2}
\]

\[
=0.25M
\]

---

# G. Molality (m)

## Definition

Number of moles of solute present in 1 kg of solvent.

---

## Formula

\[
m=
\frac{\text{Moles of solute}}
{\text{Mass of solvent(kg)}}
\]

---

## Unit

\[
mol\,kg^{-1}
\]

---

# Molarity vs Molality

| Molarity                 | Molality                   |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Based on solution volume | Based on solvent mass      |
| Changes with temperature | Independent of temperature |
| Unit: mol/L              | Unit: mol/kg               |

---

# 3. Solubility

## Definition

Maximum amount of solute that dissolves in a given amount of solvent at a specific temperature.

---

# Factors Affecting Solubility

## A. Nature of Solute and Solvent

"Like dissolves like"

- Polar solutes dissolve in polar solvents.
- Non-polar solutes dissolve in non-polar solvents.

---

## B. Temperature

For solids:

- Solubility usually increases with temperature.

For gases:

- Solubility decreases with temperature.

---

## C. Pressure

Important mainly for gases.

---

# 4. Solubility of Gases in Liquids

## Henry's Law

Given by:

```
William Henry
```

---

## Statement

At constant temperature, solubility of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of the gas.

---

## Equation

\[
p=k_Hx
\]

where:

- p = partial pressure of gas
- x = mole fraction of dissolved gas
- kH = Henry's constant

---

# Applications of Henry's Law

## 1. Carbonated Drinks

CO₂ is dissolved under high pressure.

---

## 2. Scuba Diving

High pressure increases nitrogen solubility in blood.

Rapid release causes:

```
Decompression sickness
```

---

## 3. High Altitude

Low oxygen pressure causes reduced oxygen availability.

---

# 5. Vapour Pressure of Liquid Solutions

## Vapour Pressure

Pressure exerted by vapour in equilibrium with its liquid.

---

# Raoult's Law

For a volatile component:

\[
p_A=p_A^0X_A
\]

where:

- pA = partial vapour pressure
- pA⁰ = vapour pressure of pure component
- XA = mole fraction

---

# Binary Solution

For two volatile liquids:

\[
P=p_A+p_B
\]

---

# Ideal Solutions

A solution that obeys Raoult's law throughout concentration range.

---

## Characteristics

- Similar intermolecular forces.
- No heat absorption/release.
- No volume change.

Examples:

- Benzene + Toluene

---

# Non-Ideal Solutions

Do not obey Raoult's law.

---

# Positive Deviation

## Cause

A-B attraction is weaker.

Effects:

- Higher vapour pressure.
- Lower boiling point.

Example:

Ethanol + Acetone

---

# Negative Deviation

## Cause

A-B attraction is stronger.

Effects:

- Lower vapour pressure.
- Higher boiling point.

Example:

Chloroform + Acetone

---

# 6. Colligative Properties

## Definition

Properties that depend only on the number of solute particles and not their nature.

---

# Four Colligative Properties

1. Relative lowering of vapour pressure
2. Elevation of boiling point
3. Depression of freezing point
4. Osmotic pressure

---

# A. Relative Lowering of Vapour Pressure

For non-volatile solute:

\[
\frac{P^0-P}{P^0}=X_B
\]

where:

- P⁰ = vapour pressure of pure solvent
- P = vapour pressure of solution
- XB = mole fraction of solute

---

# B. Elevation in Boiling Point

Adding solute increases boiling point.

\[
\Delta T_b=T_b-T_b^0
\]

---

## Formula

\[
\Delta T_b=K_bm
\]

where:

- Kb = molal elevation constant
- m = molality

---

# C. Depression in Freezing Point

\[
\Delta T_f=T_f^0-T_f
\]

---

## Formula

\[
\Delta T_f=K_fm
\]

where:

- Kf = molal depression constant

---

# D. Osmotic Pressure

## Definition

Pressure required to stop osmosis.

---

## Formula

\[
\pi=CRT
\]

or

\[
\pi=\frac{nRT}{V}
\]

---

where:

- C = molar concentration
- R = gas constant
- T = temperature

---

# 7. Osmosis

## Definition

Movement of solvent molecules through a semipermeable membrane from dilute solution to concentrated solution.

---

## Diagram

```
Dilute Solution

      ↓
-------------
Membrane
-------------
      ↑

Concentrated Solution
```

---

# Types of Solutions

## Hypotonic

Lower solute concentration.

---

## Hypertonic

Higher solute concentration.

---

## Isotonic

Equal concentration.

---

# 8. Reverse Osmosis

## Definition

Process where solvent is forced from concentrated solution to pure solvent side by applying pressure greater than osmotic pressure.

---

## Application

- Water purification
- Desalination

---

# 9. Abnormal Molar Mass

Sometimes experimentally calculated molar mass differs from actual value due to:

- Association
- Dissociation

---

# van't Hoff Factor (i)

## Definition

Ratio of observed colligative property to calculated colligative property.

\[
i=
\frac{\text{Observed colligative property}}
{\text{Calculated colligative property}}
\]

---

# Effect of Dissociation

Number of particles increases.

\[
i>1
\]

Example:

\[
KCl\rightarrow K^+ + Cl^-
\]

---

# Effect of Association

Number of particles decreases.

\[
i<1
\]

Example:

Acetic acid dimerization:

\[
2CH_3COOH
\rightarrow
(CH_3COOH)_2
\]

---

# Modified Colligative Equations

## Elevation of boiling point

\[
\Delta T_b=iK_bm
\]

---

## Depression of freezing point

\[
\Delta T_f=iK_fm
\]

---

## Osmotic pressure

\[
\pi=iCRT
\]

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** Calculate molarity of a solution containing 2 mol solute in 500 mL solution.

### Solution

Volume:

\[
500mL=0.5L
\]

\[
M=\frac{2}{0.5}
\]

\[
\boxed{M=4M}
\]

---

## Example 2

**Question:** Find mole fraction of ethanol in a mixture containing 1 mole ethanol and 3 moles water.

### Solution

\[
X_{ethanol}
=

\frac{1}{1+3}
\]

\[
\boxed{X=0.25}
\]

---

## Example 3

**Question:** Why does adding salt increase boiling point of water?

### Solution

Salt is a non-volatile solute. It lowers vapour pressure of water. Therefore, higher temperature is required for vapour pressure to become equal to atmospheric pressure, increasing the boiling point.

---

## Example 4

**Question:** Why does KCl have van't Hoff factor greater than 1?

### Solution

KCl dissociates into two ions:

\[
KCl\rightarrow K^+ + Cl^-
\]

Number of particles increases, therefore:

\[
i>1
\]

---

## Example 5

**Question:** Explain reverse osmosis.

### Solution

In reverse osmosis, pressure greater than osmotic pressure is applied on the concentrated solution side, forcing solvent molecules through a semipermeable membrane. It is used for purification of water.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing molarity with molality; molarity depends on solution volume, while molality depends on solvent mass.
- Forgetting that molarity changes with temperature but molality does not.
- Using Raoult's law for non-ideal solutions without considering deviation.
- Assuming gases become more soluble at higher temperatures; gas solubility usually decreases with temperature.
- Forgetting that colligative properties depend on the number of particles, not their chemical identity.
- Ignoring van't Hoff factor in electrolyte solutions.
- Mixing up positive and negative deviations from Raoult's law.
- Forgetting units while calculating molarity and molality.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Solutions are homogeneous mixtures of solute and solvent.
- Concentration can be expressed using molarity, molality, mole fraction, mass percentage, and ppm.
- Gas solubility in liquids is governed by **Henry's Law**.
- **Raoult's Law** describes vapour pressure behaviour of ideal solutions.
- Non-ideal solutions show positive or negative deviations due to differences in intermolecular forces.
- Colligative properties depend on the number of solute particles and include boiling point elevation, freezing point depression, vapour pressure lowering, and osmotic pressure.
- Osmosis and reverse osmosis have important biological and industrial applications.
- Abnormal molar masses arise due to association or dissociation of solute particles and are explained using the **van't Hoff factor**.
- Understanding solutions is essential for chemistry, biology, medicine, and industrial applications.
