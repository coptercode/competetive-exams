# Introduction

Respiration is the process by which living organisms obtain energy from food. **Breathing** is the physical process of taking in oxygen-rich air and expelling carbon dioxide-rich air. Oxygen is essential for cellular respiration, while carbon dioxide produced during metabolism must be removed from the body.

The respiratory system consists of specialized organs that facilitate the exchange of gases between the atmosphere and body tissues.

---

# 1. Human Respiratory System

The respiratory system consists of:

```
Respiratory System
│
├── Conducting Part
└── Respiratory Part
```

---

## Conducting Part

Includes:

- External nostrils
- Nasal cavity
- Pharynx
- Larynx
- Trachea
- Bronchi
- Bronchioles

### Functions

- Conducts air.
- Filters dust particles.
- Warms inspired air.
- Moistens incoming air.

---

## Respiratory Part

Includes:

- Respiratory bronchioles
- Alveolar ducts
- Alveolar sacs
- Alveoli

### Function

- Exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide.

# 3. Larynx

The larynx is commonly called the **voice box**.

Functions

- Produces sound.
- Prevents food from entering the trachea with the help of the **epiglottis**.

---

# 4. Trachea

The trachea is supported by **C-shaped cartilaginous rings**.

Functions

- Maintains an open airway.
- Conducts air to the bronchi.

Its inner lining has **ciliated epithelium** and mucus-secreting cells that trap dust and microbes.

---

# 5. Lungs

Humans have a pair of lungs situated in the thoracic cavity.

### Right Lung

- Three lobes.

### Left Lung

- Two lobes.
- Smaller due to the presence of the heart.

Each lung is enclosed by a **double-layered pleural membrane** containing **pleural fluid**, which reduces friction during breathing.

---

# 6. Alveoli

Alveoli are tiny, thin-walled air sacs that serve as the functional units of the lungs.

### Characteristics

- Rich blood supply.
- Thin squamous epithelium.
- Large surface area (about **70–80 m²**).
- Moist lining.

---

## Importance

- Efficient gaseous exchange.
- Rapid diffusion of oxygen and carbon dioxide.

---

# 7. Mechanism of Breathing

Breathing consists of two phases:

```
Breathing
│
├── Inspiration
└── Expiration
```

---

# 8. Inspiration

Inspiration is the process of taking air into the lungs.

### Events

- Diaphragm contracts and flattens.
- External intercostal muscles contract.
- Ribs move upward and outward.
- Thoracic cavity volume increases.
- Intrapulmonary pressure decreases.
- Air enters the lungs.

---

# 9. Expiration

Expiration is the process of expelling air from the lungs.

### Events

- Diaphragm relaxes.
- External intercostal muscles relax.
- Ribs move downward and inward.
- Thoracic cavity volume decreases.
- Intrapulmonary pressure increases.
- Air is forced out.

---

# 10. Respiratory Volumes

---

## (A) Tidal Volume (TV)

Volume of air inspired or expired during normal breathing.

```
Approximately 500 mL
```

---

## (B) Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV)

Extra volume of air that can be inspired after a normal inspiration.

```
Approximately 2500–3000 mL
```

---

## (C) Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV)

Extra volume of air that can be expired after normal expiration.

```
Approximately 1000–1100 mL
```

---

## (D) Residual Volume (RV)

Volume of air remaining in the lungs after maximum expiration.

```
Approximately 1100–1200 mL
```

---

# 11. Respiratory Capacities

---

## Inspiratory Capacity (IC)

```
IC = TV + IRV
```

---

## Functional Residual Capacity (FRC)

```
FRC = ERV + RV
```

---

## Vital Capacity (VC)

Maximum volume of air that can be exhaled after a maximum inspiration.

```
VC = TV + IRV + ERV
```

---

## Total Lung Capacity (TLC)

```
TLC = VC + RV
```

---

# 12. Exchange of Gases

Gas exchange occurs by **simple diffusion**.

---

## External Respiration

Occurs between:

- Alveoli
- Blood capillaries

### Oxygen

Moves from alveoli to blood.

### Carbon Dioxide

Moves from blood to alveoli.

---

## Internal Respiration

Occurs between:

- Blood
- Body tissues

Oxygen diffuses into cells, while carbon dioxide diffuses into blood.

---

# 13. Transport of Oxygen

About **97%** of oxygen is transported by haemoglobin in the form of **oxyhaemoglobin**.

```
Hb + O₂ ⇌ HbO₂
```

About **3%** is transported dissolved in plasma.

---

# Oxyhaemoglobin Dissociation Curve

The oxygen-haemoglobin relationship is represented by a **sigmoid (S-shaped) curve**.

Factors promoting oxygen release:

- High CO₂ concentration
- High temperature
- High H⁺ concentration (low pH)
- Low oxygen concentration

This is known as the **Bohr Effect**.

---

# 14. Transport of Carbon Dioxide

Carbon dioxide is transported in three forms.

| Form                        | Percentage   |
| --------------------------- | ------------ |
| As bicarbonate ions (HCO₃⁻) | About 70%    |
| As carbaminohaemoglobin     | About 20–23% |
| Dissolved in plasma         | About 7%     |

---

# 15. Chloride Shift (Hamburger Phenomenon)

When bicarbonate ions leave red blood cells, chloride ions enter to maintain electrical neutrality.

This exchange is called the **chloride shift**.

---

# 16. Regulation of Respiration

Respiration is controlled by the **respiratory centres** in the medulla oblongata and pons.

### Medullary Respiratory Centre

- Regulates the normal rhythm of breathing.

### Pneumotaxic Centre (Pons)

- Regulates the duration of inspiration.

---

## Chemoreceptors

Located in:

- Carotid bodies
- Aortic bodies

They detect:

- Increased CO₂
- Increased H⁺ concentration
- Decreased O₂ (to a lesser extent)

An increase in carbon dioxide is the strongest stimulus for breathing.

---

# 17. Disorders of the Respiratory System

---

## Asthma

A chronic inflammatory disease causing narrowing of bronchi and bronchioles.

### Symptoms

- Wheezing
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness

---

## Emphysema

Destruction of alveolar walls leading to reduced respiratory surface area.

Major cause:

- Smoking

---

## Occupational Respiratory Disorders

Caused by prolonged inhalation of dust particles.

Examples

- Silicosis
- Asbestosis

Symptoms include:

- Lung fibrosis
- Reduced respiratory efficiency

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Why are alveoli considered the functional units of the lungs?

**Solution**

Alveoli provide a very large surface area with thin walls and an extensive capillary network, allowing rapid diffusion of oxygen into the blood and carbon dioxide out of the blood.

---

### Example 2

Differentiate between inspiration and expiration.

**Solution**

During inspiration, the diaphragm contracts, thoracic cavity volume increases, pressure decreases, and air enters the lungs. During expiration, the diaphragm relaxes, thoracic cavity volume decreases, pressure increases, and air leaves the lungs.

---

### Example 3

Why is haemoglobin important in oxygen transport?

**Solution**

Haemoglobin reversibly combines with oxygen to form oxyhaemoglobin, enabling about 97% of oxygen to be transported efficiently from the lungs to body tissues.

---

### Example 4

What is the Bohr Effect?

**Solution**

The Bohr Effect refers to the increased release of oxygen from oxyhaemoglobin when carbon dioxide concentration, hydrogen ion concentration, or temperature increases, or when oxygen concentration decreases.

---

### Example 5

State the role of bicarbonate ions in carbon dioxide transport.

**Solution**

About 70% of carbon dioxide is transported in the blood as bicarbonate ions (HCO₃⁻), making it the major form of carbon dioxide transport.

---

### Example 6

Why does smoking increase the risk of emphysema?

**Solution**

Smoking damages the alveolar walls, reducing the respiratory surface area available for gas exchange. This decreases the efficiency of oxygen uptake and carbon dioxide removal.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **breathing** with **cellular respiration**.
- Assuming the **left lung** has three lobes; it has **two**, while the **right lung** has **three**.
- Forgetting that **alveoli** are the primary sites of gaseous exchange.
- Mixing up **tidal volume** with **vital capacity**.
- Assuming oxygen is mainly transported dissolved in plasma; about **97%** is transported by **haemoglobin**.
- Forgetting that most carbon dioxide is transported as **bicarbonate ions**, not as carbaminohaemoglobin.
- Confusing **asthma** with **emphysema**.
- Assuming oxygen concentration is the main regulator of breathing; **increased carbon dioxide concentration** is the primary stimulus under normal conditions.

---

# Chapter Summary

- The **respiratory system** consists of conducting passages and respiratory structures that facilitate the exchange of gases between the atmosphere and the body.
- Air passes through the **nostrils, nasal cavity, pharynx, larynx, trachea, bronchi, bronchioles**, and finally reaches the **alveoli**, where gaseous exchange occurs.
- **Inspiration** and **expiration** are brought about by coordinated movements of the diaphragm and intercostal muscles, producing pressure changes that move air into and out of the lungs.
- Respiratory volumes and capacities, such as **tidal volume**, **vital capacity**, and **total lung capacity**, help assess lung function.
- Oxygen is transported mainly as **oxyhaemoglobin**, while carbon dioxide is transported predominantly as **bicarbonate ions**.
- Gas exchange occurs by **simple diffusion**, driven by differences in the partial pressures of oxygen and carbon dioxide.
- The **medulla oblongata** and **pons** regulate breathing, with carbon dioxide concentration acting as the principal stimulus.
- Disorders such as **asthma**, **emphysema**, and occupational lung diseases impair respiratory efficiency and reduce the body's ability to exchange gases effectively.
