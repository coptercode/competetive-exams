- Gene therapy

- Increasing crop productivity.
- Developing pest-resistant crops.
- Improving nutritional quality.
- Reducing dependence on chemical pesticides.

Organisms whose genetic material has been altered using genetic engineering techniques are called **genetically modified organisms**.

---

---

# 3. Bt Cotton

## Definition

Bt cotton is a genetically modified cotton variety containing genes from:

```
Bacillus thuringiensis
```

---

## Bt Gene

The bacterium produces insecticidal crystal proteins called:

```
Cry proteins
```

---

# Mechanism of Bt Toxin Action

1. Bt cotton produces inactive toxin crystals.
2. Insect larvae consume plant material.
3. Insect gut activates the toxin.
4. Toxin binds to intestinal cells.
5. Cells burst, killing the insect.

---

## Diagram

```
Bt Gene

↓

Cotton Plant

↓

Cry Protein

↓

Insect Larva Eats Plant

↓

Gut Damage

↓

Insect Death
```

# 4. RNA Interference (RNAi)

## Definition

RNA interference is a process where double-stranded RNA prevents expression of a specific gene.

---

## Mechanism

```
dsRNA

↓

Gene Silencing

↓

No Protein Production
```

---

## Application

Used to develop resistance against:

```
Meloidogyne incognita
```

(root-knot nematode)

in tobacco plants.

# 6. Recombinant Insulin Production

## Problem

Human insulin is required for diabetes treatment.

Previously:

- Insulin was obtained from animals.
- It sometimes caused allergic reactions.

---

## Recombinant Human Insulin

Produced using:

```
Escherichia coli
```

---

# Structure of Insulin

Human insulin consists of:

- A-chain
- B-chain

linked by disulfide bonds.

---

## Production Steps

```
Human Insulin Gene

↓

Inserted into E. coli Plasmid

↓

Bacterial Expression

↓

Insulin Chains Produced

↓

Purification

↓

Human Insulin
```

# 7. Gene Therapy

## Definition

Gene therapy involves introducing a normal functional gene into cells to correct a genetic disorder.

---

# ADA Deficiency

## Disease

Adenosine Deaminase (ADA) deficiency.

---

## Cause

Mutation in the ADA gene.

---

## Effect

Failure of immune system development.

---

# ADA Gene Therapy

Steps:

1. Lymphocytes are isolated from patient.
2. Functional ADA gene is introduced.
3. Modified cells are returned to patient.

---

## Diagram

```
Patient Cells

↓

Insert Normal ADA Gene

↓

Modified Cells

↓

Return to Patient

↓

Improved Immunity
```

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

**Question:** What is Bt cotton?

**Solution:**

Bt cotton is a genetically modified cotton variety containing genes from _Bacillus thuringiensis_. It produces Cry proteins that protect the plant from insect pests, especially cotton bollworms.

---

### Example 2

**Question:** Explain RNA interference.

**Solution:**

RNA interference is a gene-silencing mechanism in which double-stranded RNA prevents expression of a specific gene by blocking translation or destroying complementary mRNA. It is used to develop resistance against certain pests and pathogens.

---

### Example 3

**Question:** What is ADA deficiency?

**Solution:**

ADA deficiency is a genetic disorder caused by mutation in the adenosine deaminase gene. It results in immune system failure. Gene therapy introduces a functional ADA gene into patient cells to restore enzyme production.

---

### Example 4

**Question:** How is recombinant insulin produced?

**Solution:**

The human insulin gene is inserted into bacterial plasmids and introduced into _E. coli_. The bacteria produce insulin chains, which are purified and combined to form functional human insulin.

---

### Example 5

**Question:** What is ELISA used for?

**Solution:**

ELISA is used for detecting diseases based on antigen-antibody interactions. It is commonly used for diagnosis of HIV infection.

---

### Example 6

**Question:** What is biopiracy?

**Solution:**

Biopiracy refers to the unauthorized exploitation of biological resources and traditional knowledge without proper permission or benefit sharing with the original communities.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing Bt cotton with ordinary cotton; Bt cotton contains introduced **Cry genes** from _Bacillus thuringiensis_.
- Assuming Bt toxin directly kills all insects; it becomes active only after entering the insect gut.
- Confusing RNA interference with RNA synthesis; RNAi is a gene-silencing mechanism.
- Forgetting that gene therapy aims to correct genetic defects, not simply provide medicines.
- Mixing up PCR and ELISA; PCR detects nucleic acids, while ELISA is based on antigen-antibody interaction.
- Assuming transgenic animals are created only for food production; they are mainly used for research, disease studies, and product development.
- Confusing biopiracy with biotechnology research; biopiracy involves unauthorized exploitation of biological resources.
- Forgetting the importance of biosafety regulations in handling genetically modified organisms.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Biotechnology uses genetic engineering techniques to improve agriculture, medicine, and industrial processes.
- **Bt cotton** uses Cry proteins from _Bacillus thuringiensis_ to provide insect resistance.
- **RNA interference (RNAi)** silences specific genes and helps protect crops against pests.
- Recombinant DNA technology enables production of human insulin and other therapeutic proteins.
- **Gene therapy** provides a method to correct genetic disorders such as ADA deficiency.
- Molecular diagnostic tools like **PCR, ELISA, and DNA probes** allow early disease detection.
- Transgenic animals are valuable in studying gene function, producing biological products, and testing medicines.
- Biosafety, patents, and prevention of **biopiracy** are essential ethical considerations in biotechnology.
- Biotechnology continues to contribute significantly to healthcare, agriculture, and sustainable development.
