# Introduction

The **environment** includes all the living (**biotic**) and non-living (**abiotic**) components surrounding us. Living organisms interact continuously with one another and with their environment for survival. Human activities such as pollution, deforestation, excessive use of plastics, and improper waste disposal have disturbed ecological balance. This chapter explains ecosystems, food chains, food webs, biological magnification, ozone depletion, and methods of waste management for sustainable development.

---

# 1. Environment

The **environment** is the surroundings in which organisms live and interact.

---

## Components of the Environment

### (A) Biotic Components

These are the living components.

Examples:

- Plants
- Animals
- Humans
- Bacteria
- Fungi

---

### (B) Abiotic Components

These are the non-living components.

Examples:

- Air
- Water
- Soil
- Temperature
- Sunlight
- Minerals

---

# 2. Ecosystem

An **ecosystem** is a functional unit in which living organisms interact with one another and with their physical environment.

### Components of an Ecosystem

- Biotic components
- Abiotic components

---

## Types of Ecosystems

### Natural Ecosystems

Examples:

- Forest
- Pond
- River
- Desert
- Grassland

---

### Artificial Ecosystems

Examples:

- Aquarium
- Crop field
- Garden

---

# 3. Biotic Components of an Ecosystem

Living organisms are grouped into:

- Producers
- Consumers
- Decomposers

---

## (A) Producers

**Producers** are green plants and algae that prepare their own food through photosynthesis.

Examples:

- Grass
- Trees
- Algae

---

## (B) Consumers

Consumers depend directly or indirectly on producers for food.

### Types

#### Primary Consumers (Herbivores)

Eat plants.

Examples:

- Deer
- Rabbit
- Goat

---

#### Secondary Consumers

Eat herbivores.

Examples:

- Frog
- Snake
- Fox

---

#### Tertiary Consumers

Feed on secondary consumers.

Examples:

- Eagle
- Lion
- Tiger

---

## (C) Decomposers

**Decomposers** break down dead plants, animals, and organic wastes into simpler substances.

Examples:

- Bacteria
- Fungi

### Importance

- Recycle nutrients.
- Maintain soil fertility.
- Clean the environment.

---

# 4. Food Chain

A **food chain** is a linear sequence of organisms through which food and energy pass.

### Example

```
Grass

↓

Grasshopper

↓

Frog

↓

Snake

↓

Eagle
```

---

## Characteristics

- Begins with producers.
- Ends with top consumers.
- Shows one pathway of energy flow.

---

# 5. Trophic Levels

Each step in a food chain is called a **trophic level**.

| Trophic Level | Organism            |
| ------------- | ------------------- |
| First         | Producers           |
| Second        | Primary Consumers   |
| Third         | Secondary Consumers |
| Fourth        | Tertiary Consumers  |

---

# 6. Flow of Energy

Energy flows through an ecosystem in **one direction only**.

```
Sun

↓

Plants

↓

Herbivores

↓

Carnivores

↓

Decomposers
```

---

## Ten Percent Law

According to the **10% Law** proposed by **Raymond Lindeman**:

Only about **10%** of the energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next trophic level.

### Example

| Trophic Level  | Energy Available |
| -------------- | ---------------: |
| Plants         |          10000 J |
| Herbivores     |           1000 J |
| Carnivores     |            100 J |
| Top Carnivores |             10 J |

---

# 7. Food Web

A **food web** is a network of interconnected food chains.

### Example

```
          Grass
         /     \
      Rabbit   Deer
        |        |
      Fox      Tiger
        \      /
         Eagle
```

---

## Importance

- Increases ecosystem stability.
- Provides alternative food sources.
- Maintains ecological balance.

---

# 8. Biological Magnification (Biomagnification)

**Biological magnification** is the gradual increase in the concentration of non-biodegradable harmful substances at successive trophic levels of a food chain.

### Common Pollutants

- DDT
- Mercury
- Lead
- Pesticides

---

### Example

```
Water

↓

Plankton

↓

Small Fish

↓

Large Fish

↓

Human
```

The concentration of pollutants increases at each higher trophic level.

---

# 9. Biodegradable and Non-Biodegradable Substances

---

## Biodegradable Substances

These are broken down naturally by microorganisms.

### Examples

- Vegetable waste
- Paper
- Cotton
- Food waste
- Wood

---

## Non-Biodegradable Substances

These cannot be broken down easily by microorganisms.

### Examples

- Plastic
- Glass
- Polythene
- Aluminium cans
- Synthetic fibres

---

## Comparison

| Biodegradable        | Non-Biodegradable        |
| -------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Decompose naturally  | Do not decompose easily  |
| Environment-friendly | Cause pollution          |
| Recycled naturally   | Persist for long periods |

---

# 10. Ozone Layer

The **ozone layer** is a layer of ozone gas present in the **stratosphere**.

### Importance

- Absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV) rays from the Sun.
- Protects living organisms from skin cancer, cataracts, and DNA damage.

---

# 11. Ozone Depletion

**Ozone depletion** is the thinning of the ozone layer.

### Causes

- Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
- Halons
- Nitrogen oxides

---

## Harmful Effects

- Increased UV radiation.
- Skin cancer.
- Eye cataracts.
- Reduced crop productivity.
- Damage to marine organisms.

---

## Prevention

- Reduce the use of CFCs.
- Use eco-friendly refrigerants.
- Follow international environmental agreements.
- Promote sustainable technologies.

---

# 12. Waste Management

Proper waste management helps reduce pollution and conserve natural resources.

---

## The 3Rs Principle

### (A) Reduce

Reduce the generation of waste.

Examples:

- Avoid single-use plastics.
- Buy only what is needed.

---

### (B) Reuse

Use products again instead of discarding them.

Examples:

- Reuse bottles.
- Use cloth bags.
- Reuse containers.

---

### (C) Recycle

Convert waste materials into new products.

Examples:

- Paper recycling.
- Plastic recycling.
- Metal recycling.
- Glass recycling.

---

# 13. Role of Decomposers

Decomposers perform an essential ecological role.

### Functions

- Break down dead organisms.
- Return nutrients to the soil.
- Maintain nutrient cycles.
- Prevent accumulation of dead organic matter.

---

# 14. Sustainable Development

**Sustainable development** is development that meets present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

### Measures

- Afforestation.
- Conservation of water.
- Rainwater harvesting.
- Renewable energy.
- Proper waste disposal.
- Protection of biodiversity.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Who are the producers in an ecosystem?

**Solution**

Green plants and algae are producers because they prepare their own food by photosynthesis.

---

### Example 2

Define a food chain.

**Solution**

A food chain is a linear sequence of organisms through which food and energy pass from one trophic level to the next.

---

### Example 3

State the 10% Law.

**Solution**

Only about **10%** of the energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next trophic level, while the remaining energy is used for life processes or lost as heat.

---

### Example 4

Differentiate between biodegradable and non-biodegradable substances.

**Solution**

- **Biodegradable substances** are decomposed by microorganisms.
- **Non-biodegradable substances** persist in the environment because they do not decompose easily.

---

### Example 5

Why are decomposers important?

**Solution**

They break down dead organic matter, recycle nutrients, improve soil fertility, and help maintain ecological balance.

---

### Example 6

What causes ozone depletion?

**Solution**

The major cause of ozone depletion is the release of **chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)** and other ozone-depleting substances into the atmosphere.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **food chain** with **food web**.
- Mixing up **producers**, **consumers**, and **decomposers**.
- Assuming energy is recycled in an ecosystem (energy flows in one direction; nutrients are recycled).
- Confusing **biodegradable** and **non-biodegradable** wastes.
- Forgetting that only **10%** of energy is transferred to the next trophic level.
- Assuming the ozone layer protects against all types of radiation (it mainly absorbs harmful **ultraviolet (UV)** rays).
- Confusing **biological magnification** with decomposition.
- Assuming plastics are biodegradable.

# Chapter Summary

- The **environment** consists of **biotic** and **abiotic** components that interact within an **ecosystem**.
- Living organisms are classified into **producers, consumers,** and **decomposers**, each playing an essential role in maintaining ecological balance.
- A **food chain** represents a single pathway of energy transfer, while a **food web** shows multiple interconnected feeding relationships.
- According to the **10% Law**, only about **10%** of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next.
- **Biological magnification** leads to the accumulation of harmful non-biodegradable substances such as pesticides at higher trophic levels.
- **Biodegradable wastes** decompose naturally, whereas **non-biodegradable wastes** persist in the environment and contribute to pollution.
- The **ozone layer** protects life on Earth by absorbing harmful **ultraviolet (UV)** radiation, while **ozone depletion** caused by substances such as **CFCs** increases health and environmental risks.
- Effective **waste management**, following the **3Rs (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle)** and adopting **sustainable development** practices, helps conserve natural resources and protect the environment for future generations.
