# Introduction

A **circle** is one of the most important geometrical figures. In this chapter, we learn how to calculate the **circumference**, **area of a circle**, **length of an arc**, **area of a sector**, and **area of a segment**. These concepts are useful in engineering, architecture, construction, surveying, and many practical situations.

---

# 1. Circle

A **circle** is the set of all points in a plane that are at a fixed distance from a fixed point called the **centre**.

### Terms Related to a Circle

- **Centre** – Fixed point of the circle.
- **Radius (r)** – Distance from the centre to any point on the circle.
- **Diameter (d)** – Chord passing through the centre.

```
Diameter = 2 × Radius
```

---

# 2. Circumference of a Circle

The **circumference** is the boundary or perimeter of the circle.

### Formula

```
Circumference = 2πr
```

or

```
Circumference = πd
```

where

- **r** = Radius
- **d** = Diameter

---

### Example

Find the circumference of a circle whose radius is

```
7 cm
```

**Solution**

```
Circumference = 2πr
```

```
= 2 × 22/7 × 7
```

```
= 44 cm
```

---

# 3. Area of a Circle

The **area** is the region enclosed by the circle.

### Formula

```
Area = πr²
```

---

### Example

Find the area of a circle with radius

```
14 cm
```

**Solution**

```
Area = π × 14²
```

```
= 22/7 × 196
```

```
= 616 cm²
```

---

# 4. Arc of a Circle

An **arc** is a part of the circumference of a circle.

There are two types:

- Minor Arc
- Major Arc

```
     _______
   /         \
  |           |
   \_________/
```

The curved part between two points is called an arc.

---

# 5. Length of an Arc

If the angle subtended at the centre is

```
θ°
```

then

### Formula

```
Length of Arc

=

(θ/360) × 2πr
```

---

### Example

Find the length of the arc subtended by

```
90°
```

in a circle of radius

```
14 cm
```

**Solution**

```
= (90/360) × 2 × 22/7 × 14
```

```
= 22 cm
```

---

# 6. Sector of a Circle

A **sector** is the region enclosed by

- Two radii.
- One arc.

```
      /\
     /  \
    /    \
   (______)

```

---

# 7. Area of a Sector

If the angle at the centre is

```
θ°
```

then

### Formula

```
Area of Sector

=

(θ/360) × πr²
```

---

### Example

Find the area of a sector with

```
θ = 60°
```

and

```
r = 21 cm
```

**Solution**

```
= (60/360) × 22/7 × 21²
```

```
= 231 cm²
```

---

# 8. Segment of a Circle

A **segment** is the region enclosed by

- A chord.
- Its corresponding arc.

There are two types:

- Minor Segment
- Major Segment

---

# 9. Area of a Segment

The area of a segment is calculated as

### Formula

```
Area of Segment

=

Area of Sector

−

Area of Triangle
```

---

### Example

A sector has area

```
120 cm²
```

and the triangle inside it has area

```
40 cm²
```

Find the area of the segment.

**Solution**

```
Area of Segment

=

120 − 40
```

```
= 80 cm²
```

---

# 10. Combination of Plane Figures

Many practical problems involve figures made by combining circles with other shapes such as:

- Squares
- Rectangles
- Triangles
- Semicircles
- Quadrants

To solve such problems:

1. Divide the figure into known shapes.
2. Find the area of each part.
3. Add or subtract the areas as required.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **circumference** with **area**.
- Using the diameter instead of the radius without converting.
- Forgetting to convert the central angle into the fraction **θ/360** while finding the area of a sector or the length of an arc.
- Mixing up **sector** and **segment**.
- Forgetting to subtract the area of the triangle when calculating the area of a segment.
- Using an incorrect value of **π**.
- Making arithmetic mistakes while simplifying fractions.

# Chapter Summary

- The **circumference** of a circle is its boundary and is given by **2πr** or **πd**.
- The **area** enclosed by a circle is **πr²**.
- An **arc** is a part of the circumference, and its length is **(θ/360) × 2πr**.
- A **sector** is the region enclosed by two radii and the corresponding arc, and its area is **(θ/360) × πr²**.
- A **segment** is the region enclosed by a chord and its corresponding arc, and its area is obtained by subtracting the area of the triangle from the area of the sector.
- Many practical problems involve combinations of circles with other plane figures such as rectangles, squares, triangles, semicircles, and quadrants.
- These concepts are widely applied in engineering, architecture, construction, land measurement, and everyday geometry.
