# Introduction

Plants exhibit continuous growth throughout their life because they possess **meristematic tissues** that retain the ability to divide. Growth is influenced by both **internal factors** (genes and hormones) and **external factors** (light, water, temperature, nutrients, and oxygen).

Plant development is the sum of **growth**, **differentiation**, and **maturation**, regulated by plant growth regulators (PGRs) and environmental conditions.

---

# 1. Plant Growth

## Definition

Plant growth is a **permanent and irreversible increase** in size, volume, dry weight, and number of cells resulting from cell division and cell enlargement.

---

## Characteristics

- Permanent increase in size.
- Irreversible process.
- Requires energy.
- Influenced by internal and external factors.
- Occurs mainly in meristematic regions.

---

# 2. Meristems and Growth

Growth occurs due to the activity of meristematic tissues.

```
Meristems
│
├── Apical Meristem
├── Intercalary Meristem
└── Lateral Meristem
```

---

## Apical Meristem

Location:

- Root tips
- Shoot tips

Function:

- Increases plant length (primary growth).

---

## Intercalary Meristem

Location:

- Base of leaves
- Internodes (especially grasses)

Function:

- Rapid elongation of stems and leaves.

---

## Lateral Meristem

Location:

- Along stems and roots.

Function:

- Secondary growth.
- Increase in girth.

---

# 3. Phases of Growth

Growth occurs in three successive phases.

```
Cell Division
      │
Meristematic Phase
      │
Elongation Phase
      │
Maturation Phase
```

---

## (A) Meristematic Phase

Characteristics

- Rapid cell division.
- Thin cell walls.
- Dense cytoplasm.
- Large nucleus.
- Small vacuoles.

---

## (B) Elongation Phase

Characteristics

- Cell enlargement.
- Vacuole formation.
- Cell wall expansion.
- Rapid increase in length.

---

## (C) Maturation Phase

Characteristics

- Cells attain maximum size.
- Differentiation occurs.
- Specialized functions develop.

---

# 4. Growth Rate

Growth rate is the increase in growth per unit time.

---

## Types of Growth

### Arithmetic Growth

Only one daughter cell continues dividing.

```
Growth
│
│
│
│
└────────► Time
```

Characteristics

- Constant growth rate.
- Straight-line graph.

---

### Geometric Growth

Both daughter cells continue dividing.

```
Growth
│
│      *
│    *
│  *
│*
└────────► Time
```

Characteristics

- Exponential growth.
- J-shaped curve.

---

# 7. Differentiation

Differentiation is the process by which unspecialized cells become specialized in structure and function.

Examples

- Xylem cells
- Phloem cells
- Guard cells

---

# 8. Dedifferentiation

Dedifferentiation is the process by which mature, differentiated cells regain the ability to divide.

Example

- Formation of vascular cambium.
- Cork cambium.

---

# 9. Redifferentiation

Redifferentiation is the process in which dedifferentiated cells again become specialized.

Example

- Formation of secondary xylem and phloem.

---

# 10. Plant Growth Regulators (PGRs)

Plant growth regulators are organic compounds that regulate plant growth and development in very small quantities.

```
Plant Growth Regulators
│
├── Auxins
├── Gibberellins
├── Cytokinins
├── Abscisic Acid (ABA)
└── Ethylene
```

---

# 11. Auxins

First discovered by:

**F. W. Went**

Natural auxin:

```
IAA (Indole-3-acetic acid)
```

---

## Functions

- Cell elongation.
- Root initiation.
- Apical dominance.
- Parthenocarpy.
- Prevents premature leaf and fruit drop.
- Promotes vascular differentiation.

---

## Commercial Uses

- Weed control (2,4-D).
- Rooting of stem cuttings.
- Fruit development.

---

# 12. Gibberellins (GA)

Discovered from the fungus:

```
Gibberella fujikuroi
```

---

## Functions

- Stem elongation.
- Break seed dormancy.
- Promote bolting.
- Stimulate flowering in some plants.
- Increase fruit size.

---

# 13. Cytokinins

Natural cytokinin:

```
Zeatin
```

Discovered by:

**F. Skoog and C. O. Miller**

---

## Functions

- Cell division.
- Delay leaf senescence.
- Promote lateral bud growth.
- Nutrient mobilization.

---

# 14. Abscisic Acid (ABA)

Known as:

```
Stress Hormone
```

---

## Functions

- Stomatal closure.
- Seed dormancy.
- Bud dormancy.
- Leaf abscission.
- Inhibits growth.

---

# 15. Ethylene

A gaseous plant hormone.

---

## Functions

- Fruit ripening.
- Leaf and flower abscission.
- Breaking seed and bud dormancy.
- Promotes flowering in pineapple.
- Increases femaleness in cucurbits.

---

# 17. Photoperiodism

Photoperiodism is the response of plants to the relative length of day and night.

Discovered by:

**Garner and Allard**

---

## Types of Plants

### Short-Day Plants (SDP)

Flower when the day length is shorter than a critical duration.

Examples

- Rice
- Soybean
- Chrysanthemum

---

### Long-Day Plants (LDP)

Flower when day length exceeds a critical duration.

Examples

- Spinach
- Wheat
- Radish

---

### Day-Neutral Plants

Flower irrespective of day length.

Examples

- Tomato
- Cotton
- Sunflower

---

# 18. Phytochrome

Phytochrome is the photoreceptor pigment involved in photoperiodism.

It absorbs:

- Red light.
- Far-red light.

---

# 19. Vernalization

Vernalization is the induction of flowering by exposing plants to **low temperature**.

Proposed by:

**T. D. Lysenko**

---

## Examples

- Wheat
- Barley
- Cabbage

---

## Importance

- Early flowering.
- Uniform flowering.
- Better crop yield.

---

# 20. Seed Dormancy

Seed dormancy is the inability of a viable seed to germinate even under favourable conditions.

---

## Causes

- Hard seed coat.
- Immature embryo.
- Chemical inhibitors.
- Unfavourable environmental conditions.

---

## Methods of Breaking Dormancy

- Scarification.
- Stratification.
- Gibberellin treatment.
- Water soaking.

---

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Why is ABA called the stress hormone?

**Solution**

ABA helps plants withstand stress by inducing stomatal closure, reducing water loss through transpiration, and promoting seed and bud dormancy under unfavourable conditions.

---

### Example 2

State two functions of auxins.

**Solution**

Auxins promote cell elongation and maintain apical dominance. They also induce root formation and promote parthenocarpy in certain plants.

---

### Example 3

Differentiate between arithmetic and geometric growth.

**Solution**

In arithmetic growth, only one daughter cell continues to divide, producing a constant growth rate. In geometric growth, both daughter cells continue dividing, resulting in exponential growth.

---

### Example 4

What is photoperiodism?

**Solution**

Photoperiodism is the response of plants to the duration of light and darkness, especially in regulating flowering.

---

### Example 5

Define vernalization and mention one advantage.

**Solution**

Vernalization is the induction of flowering by exposing plants to low temperatures. It promotes early and uniform flowering, leading to improved crop yield.

---

### Example 6

What is the role of cytokinins in plants?

**Solution**

Cytokinins stimulate cell division, promote lateral bud growth, delay leaf senescence, and help in nutrient mobilization.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **growth** with **development**; development includes growth, differentiation, and maturation.
- Mixing up the phases of growth: **meristematic**, **elongation**, and **maturation**.
- Assuming **auxins** promote cell division; they mainly promote **cell elongation**, while **cytokinins** primarily stimulate cell division.
- Forgetting that **ABA** is mainly a growth inhibitor and stress hormone.
- Confusing **photoperiodism** (response to day length) with **vernalization** (response to low temperature).
- Assuming **ethylene** is a liquid hormone; it is a **gaseous** plant hormone.
- Mixing up **dedifferentiation** and **redifferentiation**.
- Forgetting that **phytochrome** is the photoreceptor involved in photoperiodism.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Plant growth is a permanent and irreversible increase in size and dry mass, brought about by the activity of **meristematic tissues**.
- Growth occurs in three phases: **meristematic**, **elongation**, and **maturation**, and may follow **arithmetic** or **geometric** patterns, producing a characteristic **sigmoid growth curve**.
- **Differentiation**, **dedifferentiation**, and **redifferentiation** are essential processes in plant development.
- Plant growth and development are regulated by five major **plant growth regulators (PGRs)**: **auxins**, **gibberellins**, **cytokinins**, **abscisic acid (ABA)**, and **ethylene**, each performing specific physiological functions.
- **Photoperiodism**, regulated by the pigment **phytochrome**, controls flowering based on day length, while **vernalization** promotes flowering through exposure to low temperatures.
- **Seed dormancy** prevents premature germination and can be broken by treatments such as scarification, stratification, and gibberellin application.
- Plant development is influenced by genetic makeup, hormones, and environmental factors, ensuring proper growth, reproduction, and adaptation.
