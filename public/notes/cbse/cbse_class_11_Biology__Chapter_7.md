# Introduction

Animals are multicellular organisms whose bodies are organized into tissues, organs, and organ systems. The study of the **structure and organization of animal tissues and organ systems** helps us understand how different parts of an animal work together to perform various life processes.

This chapter covers the four basic **animal tissues** and the detailed morphology and anatomy of the **cockroach**, which is used as the representative animal model in the current CBSE syllabus.

---

# 1. Animal Tissues

A **tissue** is a group of similar cells that perform a specific function.

Animal tissues are classified into four major types:

```
Animal Tissues
│
├── Epithelial Tissue
├── Connective Tissue
├── Muscular Tissue
└── Nervous Tissue
```

---

# 2. Epithelial Tissue

Epithelial tissue forms the **outer covering** of the body and lines internal organs and cavities.

## Characteristics

- Cells are closely packed.
- Very little intercellular material.
- Rest on a basement membrane.
- No blood vessels (avascular).
- Rapid regeneration.

---

## Functions

- Protection
- Absorption
- Secretion
- Excretion
- Diffusion
- Filtration

---

## Types of Epithelial Tissue

### (A) Simple Squamous Epithelium

- Single layer of flat cells.
- Allows diffusion.

**Location**

- Alveoli of lungs
- Blood capillaries

---

### (B) Cuboidal Epithelium

- Cube-shaped cells.

**Function**

- Secretion
- Absorption

**Location**

- Kidney tubules
- Glands

---

### (C) Columnar Epithelium

- Tall, pillar-like cells.

**Function**

- Absorption
- Secretion

**Location**

- Stomach
- Intestine

---

### (D) Ciliated Epithelium

Contains cilia that move substances.

**Location**

- Trachea
- Oviduct

---

### (E) Glandular Epithelium

Modified epithelial cells specialized for secretion.

Examples

- Sweat glands
- Salivary glands

---

# 3. Connective Tissue

Connective tissue supports and binds different organs together.

---

## Characteristics

- Cells widely spaced.
- Matrix abundant.
- Rich blood supply (except cartilage).

---

## Types

### (A) Loose Connective Tissue

#### Areolar Tissue

Functions

- Packing tissue
- Supports organs

#### Adipose Tissue

Functions

- Fat storage
- Heat insulation
- Protection

---

### (B) Dense Connective Tissue

#### Tendons

- Connect muscle to bone.
- Strong and inelastic.

#### Ligaments

- Connect bone to bone.
- Elastic and flexible.

---

### (C) Skeletal Connective Tissue

#### Cartilage

- Flexible.
- Chondrocytes present.
- No blood vessels.

Examples

- Nose
- Ear
- Trachea

---

#### Bone

- Hard tissue.
- Rich in calcium salts.
- Osteocytes present.

Functions

- Support
- Protection
- Blood cell formation

---

### (D) Fluid Connective Tissue

#### Blood

Functions

- Transport
- Immunity
- Clotting

#### Lymph

Functions

- Returns tissue fluid.
- Transport of fats.
- Immunity.

---

# 4. Muscular Tissue

Muscles bring about movement through contraction.

---

## Types

### (A) Skeletal Muscle

- Voluntary
- Striated
- Multinucleate

Location

- Attached to bones

---

### (B) Smooth Muscle

- Involuntary
- Non-striated
- Spindle-shaped

Location

- Intestine
- Blood vessels

---

### (C) Cardiac Muscle

- Involuntary
- Striated
- Branched fibers
- Intercalated discs present

Location

- Heart

# 9. Respiratory System

Respiration occurs through the **tracheal system**.

```
Spiracles
    │
 Trachea
    │
Tracheoles
    │
Body Cells
```

### Features

- Ten pairs of spiracles.
- Oxygen reaches tissues directly.
- Blood does not transport oxygen.

---

# 10. Excretory System

Excretion is carried out by **Malpighian tubules**.

### Functions

- Remove nitrogenous wastes.
- Maintain water balance.

Nitrogenous waste

```
Uric Acid
```

Hence, cockroach is **uricotelic**.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Why is epithelial tissue called a protective tissue?

**Solution**

Epithelial tissue forms the outer covering of the body and lines internal organs, protecting them from injury, infection, and dehydration.

---

### Example 2

Differentiate between tendons and ligaments.

**Solution**

- **Tendons** connect muscles to bones and are strong and inelastic.
- **Ligaments** connect bones to bones and are elastic, providing flexibility to joints.

---

### Example 3

Name the excretory organ of the cockroach.

**Solution**

The excretory organs of the cockroach are the **Malpighian tubules**, which remove nitrogenous wastes in the form of uric acid.

---

### Example 4

Why is the circulatory system of a cockroach called an open circulatory system?

**Solution**

In an open circulatory system, the blood (haemolymph) is not confined to blood vessels and directly bathes the body tissues.

---

### Example 5

How does respiration occur in a cockroach?

**Solution**

Air enters through **spiracles**, passes into the **tracheae** and **tracheoles**, and reaches body cells directly for gaseous exchange.

---

### Example 6

State one ecological importance of cockroaches.

**Solution**

Cockroaches act as **decomposers**, feeding on decaying organic matter and helping recycle nutrients in ecosystems.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **tendons** with **ligaments**.
- Assuming all epithelial tissues perform the same function; different types are specialized for protection, absorption, secretion, or movement.
- Mixing up **skeletal**, **smooth**, and **cardiac** muscles.
- Forgetting that the cockroach has an **open circulatory system**.
- Assuming oxygen is transported by blood in insects; in cockroaches, oxygen is delivered directly through the **tracheal system**.
- Confusing **Malpighian tubules** (excretory organs) with **tracheae** (respiratory organs).
- Forgetting that cockroaches are **uricotelic** and excrete **uric acid**.
- Confusing the **crop** (food storage) with the **gizzard** (food grinding).

# Chapter Summary

- Animal tissues are classified into **epithelial**, **connective**, **muscular**, and **nervous** tissues, each performing specialized functions in the body.
- **Epithelial tissue** provides protection and participates in absorption and secretion, while **connective tissue** supports and binds organs together.
- **Muscular tissue** enables movement, and **nervous tissue** coordinates body activities through the transmission of nerve impulses.
- The **cockroach (_Periplaneta americana_)** is a representative arthropod with a segmented body divided into **head, thorax,** and **abdomen**.
- Its digestive system consists of the **mouth, crop, gizzard, midgut, hindgut,** and **anus**, with digestion and absorption occurring mainly in the midgut.
- Cockroaches possess an **open circulatory system**, a **tracheal respiratory system**, **Malpighian tubules** for excretion, and a **double ventral nerve cord** for coordination.
- Separate male and female reproductive systems ensure sexual reproduction, with females laying eggs enclosed in an **ootheca**.
- Understanding the structural organization of animals provides a foundation for studying anatomy, physiology, and the functional integration of organ systems.
