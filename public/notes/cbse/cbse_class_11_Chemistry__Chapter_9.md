# Introduction

Hydrocarbons are the simplest organic compounds consisting only of **carbon** and **hydrogen** atoms. They form the basis of organic chemistry and are the principal constituents of **petroleum**, **natural gas**, and **coal**.

Hydrocarbons are broadly classified into:

- Aliphatic hydrocarbons
- Aromatic hydrocarbons

# 2. Alkanes

## Definition

Alkanes are **saturated hydrocarbons** containing only **single covalent bonds**.

General Formula


Examples

## Markovnikov's Rule

### Statement

During the addition of HX to an unsymmetrical alkene:

- Hydrogen attaches to the carbon atom having **more hydrogen atoms**.
- Halide attaches to the carbon atom having **fewer hydrogen atoms**.

---

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

State the general formula of alkanes.

**Solution**

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons.

General formula:

```
CnH₂n+₂
```

---

### Example 2

Which conformation of ethane is more stable?

**Solution**

The **staggered conformation** is more stable because the hydrogen atoms are farthest apart, resulting in minimum torsional strain and electron repulsion.

---

### Example 3

Predict the major product formed when propene reacts with HBr in the absence of peroxide.

**Solution**

According to **Markovnikov's rule**, hydrogen adds to the carbon atom already containing more hydrogen atoms.

Product:

```
CH₃CHBrCH₃

(2-Bromopropane)
```

---

### Example 4

What is formed when propene reacts with HBr in the presence of peroxide?

**Solution**

The reaction follows the **Anti-Markovnikov rule**.

Product:

```
CH₃CH₂CH₂Br

(1-Bromopropane)
```

---

### Example 5

Why are terminal alkynes acidic?

**Solution**

In terminal alkynes, the acidic hydrogen is attached to an **sp-hybridized carbon atom**, which has greater s-character and higher electronegativity. This stabilizes the conjugate base after the proton is removed, making terminal alkynes weakly acidic.

---

### Example 6

Why does benzene undergo substitution rather than addition reactions?

**Solution**

Addition reactions would destroy the delocalized π-electron cloud responsible for benzene's aromatic stability. Substitution reactions replace a hydrogen atom while preserving aromaticity, making them energetically more favorable.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **alkanes**, **alkenes**, and **alkynes** along with their general formulae.
- Mixing up **Markovnikov's rule** with the **Anti-Markovnikov rule**.
- Forgetting that the **peroxide effect occurs only with HBr** and not with HCl or HI.
- Assuming all alkynes are acidic; only **terminal alkynes** possess acidic hydrogen atoms.
- Confusing **staggered** and **eclipsed** conformations in Newman projections.
- Drawing benzene as a molecule with alternating single and double bonds instead of recognizing its **resonance hybrid**.
- Assuming benzene undergoes addition reactions under ordinary conditions; it predominantly undergoes **electrophilic substitution**.
- Forgetting that **ozonolysis** is used to determine the **position of the double bond** in alkenes.

# Chapter Summary

- **Hydrocarbons** are organic compounds composed only of carbon and hydrogen and are classified into **aliphatic** (alkanes, alkenes, alkynes) and **aromatic** hydrocarbons.
- **Alkanes** are saturated hydrocarbons that mainly undergo **free radical substitution reactions**, such as halogenation.
- The **staggered conformation** of ethane is more stable than the **eclipsed conformation** because it has lower torsional strain.
- **Alkenes** are unsaturated hydrocarbons that undergo **electrophilic addition reactions**, following **Markovnikov's rule** or the **Anti-Markovnikov rule** in the presence of peroxides.
- **Ozonolysis** is an important reaction used to identify the position of carbon-carbon double bonds.
- **Alkynes** contain carbon-carbon triple bonds, undergo addition reactions, and **terminal alkynes** exhibit weak acidic character due to the presence of an **sp-hybridized carbon atom**.
- **Benzene** is an aromatic compound with a planar hexagonal structure, delocalized π-electrons, and enhanced stability due to **resonance**.
- Aromatic hydrocarbons undergo **electrophilic substitution reactions**, including **nitration**, **halogenation**, **sulphonation**, and **Friedel-Crafts alkylation/acylation**, while retaining aromatic stability.
- The concepts learned in this chapter provide the foundation for understanding the chemistry of more complex organic compounds in higher classes.
