# Introduction

**Biomolecules** are organic molecules produced by living organisms that are essential for life processes.

Major biomolecules:

1. Carbohydrates
2. Proteins
3. Nucleic acids
4. Vitamins
5. Enzymes

---

# 1. Carbohydrates

## Definition

Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or substances that produce them on hydrolysis.

General formula:

\[
C_x(H_2O)_y
\]

---

# Classification of Carbohydrates

```
Carbohydrates

├── Monosaccharides

├── Oligosaccharides

└── Polysaccharides
```

---

# A. Monosaccharides

## Definition

Simplest carbohydrates that cannot be hydrolysed further.

Examples:

- Glucose
- Fructose
- Ribose

---

# Classification Based on Functional Group

## Aldoses

Contain aldehyde group:

\[
-CHO
\]

Example:

Glucose

---

## Ketoses

Contain ketone group:

\[

> C=O
> \]

Example:

Fructose

---

# Classification Based on Carbon Atoms

| Carbon atoms | Name    |
| ------------ | ------- |
| 3            | Triose  |
| 4            | Tetrose |
| 5            | Pentose |
| 6            | Hexose  |

Formula:

\[
C_6H_{12}O_6
\]

Also called:

```
Dextrose
```

---

# Open Chain Structure of Glucose

Contains:

- One aldehyde group
- Five hydroxyl groups

1. α-D-glucose
2. β-D-glucose

# 4. Disaccharides

## Definition

Carbohydrates formed by joining two monosaccharides.

General reaction:

\[
Monosaccharide+Monosaccharide
\rightarrow
Disaccharide+H_2O
\]

---

# A. Sucrose

Made of:

\[
Glucose+Fructose
\]

Bond:

\[
\alpha(1\rightarrow2)
\]

---

Properties:

- Non-reducing sugar.
- Common table sugar.

---

# B. Maltose

Made of:

\[
Glucose+Glucose
\]

Bond:

\[
\alpha(1\rightarrow4)
\]

---

Properties:

- Reducing sugar.

---

# C. Lactose

Made of:

\[
Galactose+Glucose
\]

Bond:

\[
\beta(1\rightarrow4)
\]

---

Properties:

- Milk sugar.
- Reducing sugar.

---

# Comparison of Disaccharides

| Sugar   | Components          | Reducing |
| ------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Sucrose | Glucose + Fructose  | No       |
| Maltose | Glucose + Glucose   | Yes      |
| Lactose | Galactose + Glucose | Yes      |

---

## Definition

Organic compounds containing:

- Amino group (-NH₂)
- Carboxyl group (-COOH)

---

# Classification

---

## A. Neutral Amino Acids

One amino group and one carboxyl group.

Example:

Glycine

---

## B. Acidic Amino Acids

More carboxyl groups.

Example:

Aspartic acid

---

## C. Basic Amino Acids

More amino groups.

Example:

Lysine

---

Cannot be synthesized by the body.

Examples:

- Valine
- Leucine
- Lysine

Must be obtained from diet.

---

# Non-Essential Amino Acids

Synthesized by the body.

Example:

Glycine

---

# 8. Peptide Bond

## Definition

Bond formed between amino acids by elimination of water.

Reaction:

\[
Amino\ acid+Amino\ acid
\rightarrow
Peptide+H_2O
\]

Bond:

\[
-CO-NH-
\]

---

# 9. Structure of Proteins

Protein structure occurs at four levels:

```
Primary

↓

Secondary

↓

Tertiary

↓

Quaternary
```

---

# A. Primary Structure

Sequence of amino acids in protein chain.

---

# B. Secondary Structure

Regular folding due to hydrogen bonding.

Types:

1. α-helix
2. β-pleated sheet

---

# C. Tertiary Structure

Three-dimensional folding of polypeptide chain.

---

# D. Quaternary Structure

Arrangement of multiple polypeptide chains.

Example:

Haemoglobin

---

# 10. Denaturation of Proteins

## Definition

Loss of biological activity due to disruption of secondary and tertiary structures.

---

Causes:

- Heat
- Change in pH
- Chemicals

---

Example:

Boiling of egg white.

# 11. Enzymes

## Definition

Enzymes are biological catalysts that increase the rate of biochemical reactions.

Most enzymes are:

```
Proteins
```

---

# Properties

- Highly specific.
- Work under mild conditions.
- Remain unchanged after reaction.

---

# Enzyme Action

## Lock and Key Model

Proposed by:

```
Emil Fischer
```

---

Concept:

Substrate fits into active site like a key into lock.

```
Enzyme + Substrate

↓

Enzyme-substrate complex

↓

Product + Enzyme
```

---

# Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity

## 1. Temperature

Optimum temperature for humans:

\[
37^\circ C
\]

---

## 2. pH

Each enzyme has optimum pH.

---

## 3. Substrate Concentration

Increasing substrate increases rate until saturation.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** What are carbohydrates?

### Solution

Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or compounds that produce them on hydrolysis.

---

## Example 2

**Question:** Why is sucrose a non-reducing sugar?

### Solution

Sucrose has both anomeric carbons involved in glycosidic bond formation, so it cannot open to form free aldehyde or ketone groups.

---

## Example 3

**Question:** What is denaturation of proteins?

### Solution

Denaturation is the loss of biological activity of proteins due to disruption of secondary and tertiary structures without breaking peptide bonds.

---

## Example 4

**Question:** Differentiate DNA and RNA.

### Solution

DNA contains deoxyribose sugar and thymine, while RNA contains ribose sugar and uracil.

---

## Example 5

**Question:** What is a peptide bond?

### Solution

A peptide bond is the amide linkage:

\[
-CO-NH-
\]

formed between two amino acids by removal of water.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing glucose and fructose; glucose is an aldose while fructose is a ketose.
- Forgetting that sucrose is non-reducing.
- Mixing up starch, glycogen, and cellulose.
- Forgetting that proteins are polymers of amino acids.
- Confusing peptide bonds with hydrogen bonds.
- Forgetting the difference between nucleoside and nucleotide.
- Mixing DNA bases with RNA bases.
- Forgetting vitamin deficiency diseases.
- Assuming denaturation breaks peptide bonds; it mainly affects higher structures.

# Chapter Summary

- Biomolecules are essential organic compounds involved in life processes.
- Carbohydrates include monosaccharides, disaccharides, and polysaccharides.
- Glucose and fructose are important monosaccharides.
- Proteins are polymers of amino acids linked by peptide bonds.
- Protein structure has four levels: primary, secondary, tertiary, and quaternary.
- Enzymes act as biological catalysts through specific active sites.
- Nucleic acids DNA and RNA store and transmit genetic information.
- Vitamins are essential micronutrients required for normal metabolism.
- Understanding biomolecules explains the chemical basis of life.
