# Introduction

The **cell** is the **basic structural and functional unit of life**. Every living organism, from the simplest bacterium to the most complex human being, is made up of one or more cells. Cells perform all vital life processes such as nutrition, respiration, excretion, growth, and reproduction.

This chapter discusses the **Cell Theory**, the differences between **prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells**, the structure and functions of various **cell organelles**, and the organization of the **nucleus and chromosomes**.

---

# 1. Discovery of the Cell

### Robert Hooke (1665)

- Observed thin slices of cork under a microscope.
- Coined the term **"Cell"**.

---

### Antonie van Leeuwenhoek

- First to observe **living cells**.

---

### Robert Brown

- Discovered the **nucleus**.

---

### Schleiden (1838)

- Proposed that all **plants are made of cells**.

---

### Schwann (1839)

- Proposed that **animals are made of cells**.

---

### Rudolf Virchow (1855)

- Proposed

```
Omnis cellula e cellula
```

Meaning:

**Every cell arises from a pre-existing cell.**

---

# 2. Cell Theory

The Cell Theory was proposed by **Matthias Schleiden** and **Theodor Schwann**, and later modified by **Rudolf Virchow**.

## Postulates

- All living organisms are composed of one or more cells.
- The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
- New cells arise only from pre-existing cells.

---

# 4. Prokaryotic Cells

Prokaryotic cells are simple cells lacking a true nucleus.

## Characteristics

- Small in size (1–10 μm).
- No nuclear membrane.
- DNA present in a nucleoid region.
- No membrane-bound organelles.
- 70S ribosomes.
- Cell wall usually present.

### Examples

- Bacteria
- Cyanobacteria
- Mycoplasma

---

# 5. Eukaryotic Cells

Eukaryotic cells possess a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

## Characteristics

- Large (10–100 μm).
- True nucleus.
- Membrane-bound organelles.
- 80S ribosomes.
- Linear chromosomes.

### Examples

- Plants
- Animals
- Fungi
- Protists

---

# Comparison of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

| Feature     | Prokaryotic      | Eukaryotic       |
| ----------- | ---------------- | ---------------- |
| Nucleus     | Absent           | Present          |
| Organelles  | Absent           | Present          |
| Ribosomes   | 70S              | 80S              |
| Chromosomes | Single, circular | Multiple, linear |
| Cell Size   | Small            | Large            |

# 7. Plasma Membrane (Cell Membrane)

The plasma membrane is a thin, flexible membrane surrounding the cell.

---

Proposed by

**Singer and Nicolson (1972)**

According to this model,

- Phospholipids form a bilayer.
- Proteins are embedded in the lipid bilayer.
- The membrane is dynamic and flexible.

# 8. Cell Wall

The cell wall is the outermost rigid covering in plant cells.

---

---

# 9. Endomembrane System

The endomembrane system consists of:

- Endoplasmic Reticulum
- Golgi Apparatus
- Lysosomes
- Vacuoles

---

# 10. Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)

A network of membrane-bound tubules.

---

## Types

### (A) Rough ER (RER)

- Ribosomes attached.
- Protein synthesis.

---

### (B) Smooth ER (SER)

- No ribosomes.
- Lipid synthesis.
- Detoxification.

---

# 11. Golgi Apparatus

Discovered by

**Camillo Golgi**

---

## Structure

Stacks of flattened membrane-bound sacs called **cisternae**.

---

## Functions

- Packaging.
- Modification.
- Secretion.
- Formation of lysosomes.
- Cell wall material formation in plants.

---

# 12. Lysosomes

Discovered by

**Christian de Duve**

Known as the **Suicidal Bags** of the cell.

---

## Characteristics

- Membrane-bound.
- Contain digestive enzymes.
- Acidic interior.

---

## Functions

- Intracellular digestion.
- Removal of worn-out organelles.
- Autolysis.

---

# 13. Vacuoles

Membrane-bound sacs filled with cell sap.

---

## Plant Cells

- Large central vacuole.
- Maintains turgor pressure.

---

## Animal Cells

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

State the three postulates of the Cell Theory.

**Solution**

- All living organisms are composed of one or more cells.
- The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
- All new cells arise from pre-existing cells.

---

### Example 2

Why are mitochondria called the powerhouse of the cell?

**Solution**

Mitochondria carry out cellular respiration and synthesize ATP, which provides energy for various cellular activities.

---

### Example 3

Differentiate between Rough ER and Smooth ER.

**Solution**

- **Rough ER** has ribosomes and is involved in protein synthesis.
- **Smooth ER** lacks ribosomes and functions in lipid synthesis and detoxification.

---

### Example 4

Name the plastid responsible for photosynthesis.

**Solution**

The **chloroplast** is the plastid responsible for photosynthesis because it contains chlorophyll pigments.

---

### Example 5

Classify chromosomes based on the position of the centromere.

**Solution**

- Metacentric
- Submetacentric
- Acrocentric
- Telocentric

---

### Example 6

State one major difference between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.

**Solution**

Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus and organelles, whereas eukaryotic cells possess a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing the **cell wall** with the **cell membrane**.
- Assuming all cells contain chloroplasts; chloroplasts are absent in animal cells.
- Mixing up the functions of **mitochondria** and **chloroplasts**.
- Forgetting that **70S ribosomes** occur in prokaryotes, mitochondria, and chloroplasts, while **80S ribosomes** are found in the eukaryotic cytoplasm.
- Confusing **Rough ER** with **Smooth ER**.
- Assuming lysosomes are present in equal abundance in plant cells; they are more prominent in animal cells.
- Mixing up the chromosome types based on centromere position.
- Forgetting that the **Fluid Mosaic Model** was proposed by **Singer and Nicolson**.

---

# Chapter Summary

- The **cell** is the basic structural and functional unit of life, and the **Cell Theory** states that all organisms are made of cells and new cells arise from pre-existing cells.
- Cells are classified into **prokaryotic** and **eukaryotic** types based on the presence or absence of a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
- The **plasma membrane**, explained by the **Fluid Mosaic Model**, is selectively permeable and regulates the movement of substances into and out of the cell.
- The **cell wall** provides rigidity, protection, and support in plant cells.
- The **endomembrane system** includes the **endoplasmic reticulum**, **Golgi apparatus**, **lysosomes**, and **vacuoles**, which together perform synthesis, packaging, transport, storage, and digestion.
- **Mitochondria** generate ATP through cellular respiration, while **chloroplasts** carry out photosynthesis.
- **Ribosomes** are the sites of protein synthesis, and the **nucleus** controls cellular activities by housing the genetic material.
- Chromosomes are classified as **metacentric**, **submetacentric**, **acrocentric**, or **telocentric** according to the position of the centromere.
- The study of cell structure provides the foundation for understanding physiology, genetics, molecular biology, and biotechnology.
