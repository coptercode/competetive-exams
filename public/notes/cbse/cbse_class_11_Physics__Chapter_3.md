# Introduction

Many real-life motions, such as the flight of a ball, the motion of a satellite, or the movement of a car around a curve, occur in **two dimensions**. Such motions require both **magnitude** and **direction** for complete description.

This chapter introduces **vectors**, their operations, and important applications in projectile motion and circular motion.

---

# 1. Scalars and Vectors

## Scalar Quantity

A **scalar** is a physical quantity that has **magnitude only**.

Examples

- Mass
- Time
- Distance
- Speed
- Energy
- Temperature
- Work

---

## Vector Quantity

A **vector** is a physical quantity that has both **magnitude and direction**.

Examples

- Displacement
- Velocity
- Acceleration
- Force
- Momentum

# Common Mistakes

- Treating **vectors** as ordinary algebraic quantities without considering direction.
- Confusing the **dot product** (scalar result) with the **cross product** (vector result).
- Interchanging **sinθ** and **cosθ** while resolving vectors into components.
- Forgetting that the **trajectory of a projectile is parabolic**, not circular.
- Assuming the **horizontal velocity of a projectile changes**; it remains constant when air resistance is neglected.
- Using **θ** instead of **2θ** in the formula for horizontal range.
- Assuming that an object in uniform circular motion has **zero acceleration** because its speed is constant; its **velocity changes continuously**, so it has **centripetal acceleration**.
- Confusing **centripetal force** with a new type of force; it is the **net inward force** provided by existing forces such as tension, friction, or gravity.

# Chapter Summary

- Physical quantities are classified as **scalars** and **vectors**, with vectors requiring both magnitude and direction.
- Vectors can be added, subtracted, resolved into rectangular components, and multiplied using **dot** and **cross products**.
- **Projectile motion** is a combination of uniform horizontal motion and uniformly accelerated vertical motion under gravity, producing a **parabolic trajectory**.
- The important projectile equations give the **time of flight**, **maximum height**, and **horizontal range**, with the range being maximum at **45°**.
- **Uniform circular motion** involves constant speed but continuously changing velocity due to a change in direction.
- Angular quantities such as **angular displacement**, **angular velocity**, and **angular acceleration** are related to their linear counterparts through the radius of the circular path.
- A body moving in a circle experiences **centripetal acceleration** and requires a **centripetal force** directed toward the centre of the circle.
- The concepts of vector analysis introduced in this chapter are essential for understanding force, momentum, rotational motion, electromagnetism, and many other topics in physics.
