# Introduction

A **wave** is a disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another without the transfer of matter. Waves are responsible for many natural phenomena such as sound, water ripples, earthquakes, and light.

The study of waves is important in acoustics, communication systems, musical instruments, medicine, and engineering.

---

# 1. Wave Motion

## Definition

Wave motion is the propagation of a disturbance through a medium or space, transferring energy without transferring matter.

---

## Characteristics

- Transfers energy.
- Does not transfer matter.
- Produced by vibrating particles.
- May require a material medium or may propagate through vacuum depending on the type of wave.

---

# 2. Types of Waves

---

## (A) Mechanical Waves

Require a material medium for propagation.

Examples

- Sound waves
- Water waves
- Seismic waves

---

## (B) Electromagnetic Waves

Do not require a material medium.

Examples

- Light
- Radio waves
- X-rays
- Microwaves

---

# 3. Mechanical Waves

Mechanical waves are classified into:

- Transverse waves
- Longitudinal waves

---

## (A) Transverse Waves

Particles vibrate **perpendicular** to the direction of wave propagation.

Examples

- Waves on a stretched string
- Electromagnetic waves (transverse in nature)

---

### Characteristics

- Have crests and troughs.
- Can be polarized.
- Usually propagate in solids and on liquid surfaces.

---

## (B) Longitudinal Waves

Particles vibrate **parallel** to the direction of wave propagation.

Examples

- Sound waves in air
- Compression waves in springs

---

### Characteristics

- Consist of compressions and rarefactions.
- Cannot be polarized.
- Propagate through solids, liquids, and gases.

---

# 4. Wave Terminology

---

## Amplitude (A)

Maximum displacement of a particle from its equilibrium position.

Unit

```
metre (m)
```

---

## Wavelength (λ)

Distance between two consecutive particles in the same phase.

Examples

- Crest to crest
- Compression to compression

Unit

```
metre (m)
```

---

## Time Period (T)

Time taken for one complete vibration.

Unit

```
second (s)
```

---

## Frequency (f)

Number of vibrations per second.

Formula

```
f = 1/T
```

Unit

```
hertz (Hz)
```

---

## Wave Speed (v)

Formula

```
v = fλ
```

Unit

```
m s⁻¹
```

---

## Angular Frequency

Formula

```
ω = 2πf
```

---

## Wave Number

Formula

```
k = 2π/λ
```

Unit

```
rad m⁻¹
```

---

# 5. Wave Equation

A progressive wave travelling along the positive x-axis is represented by

```
y = A sin(kx − ωt + φ)
```

or

```
y = A cos(kx − ωt + φ)
```

where

- **A** = Amplitude
- **k** = Wave number
- **ω** = Angular frequency
- **φ** = Initial phase

---

# 6. Progressive (Travelling) Waves

## Definition

A wave that continuously transfers energy from one point to another is called a progressive wave.

---

## Characteristics

- Energy is transmitted.
- Every particle oscillates with the same frequency.
- Amplitude remains constant in an ideal medium.
- No particle remains permanently at rest.

---

# 7. Stationary (Standing) Waves

## Definition

Standing waves are produced by the superposition of two progressive waves of the same frequency and amplitude travelling in opposite directions.

---

## Characteristics

- No net transfer of energy.
- Fixed nodes and antinodes are formed.
- All particles between two adjacent nodes oscillate in the same phase.

---

# 8. Nodes and Antinodes

---

## Node

A point where the displacement is always zero.

---

## Antinode

A point where the displacement is maximum.

---

## Distance Between

Adjacent nodes,

```
λ/2
```

Adjacent antinodes,

```
λ/2
```

Node to adjacent antinode,

```
λ/4
```

---

# 9. Principle of Superposition

## Statement

When two or more waves travel simultaneously through a medium, the resultant displacement at any point is the algebraic sum of the individual displacements.

---

# 10. Sound Waves

## Definition

Sound is a mechanical longitudinal wave produced by vibrating bodies.

---

## Characteristics

- Requires a medium.
- Cannot travel through vacuum.
- Travels as compressions and rarefactions.

---

# 11. Speed of Sound

General relation,

```
v = fλ
```

For gases,

```
        γP
v = √---------
         ρ
```

where

- **γ** = Ratio of specific heats
- **P** = Pressure
- **ρ** = Density

---

## Factors Affecting Speed

- Nature of medium
- Temperature
- Humidity
- Elasticity
- Density

# 13. Reflection of Sound

The laws of reflection of sound are the same as those for light.

---

## Applications

- Echo
- SONAR
- Sound boards

---

# 14. Echo

## Definition

Echo is the repetition of sound due to reflection.

---

## Condition

The reflected sound should be heard at least

```
0.1 s
```

after the original sound.

Minimum distance of reflecting surface,

```
≈ 17 m
```

(at room temperature)

---

# 15. Reverberation

Persistence of sound in an enclosed hall due to repeated reflections.

---

## Reduction Methods

- Curtains
- Carpets
- Acoustic tiles
- False ceilings

---

# 16. Doppler Effect

## Definition

The apparent change in frequency of a wave due to the relative motion between the source and the observer.

---

## Applications

- Radar speed detection
- Medical ultrasound
- Astronomy
- Weather forecasting

---

# 17. Beats

## Definition

Beats are periodic variations in loudness produced by the interference of two sound waves of slightly different frequencies.

---

## Beat Frequency

```
fb = |f₁ − f₂|
```

---

## Applications

- Tuning musical instruments
- Frequency measurement

---

# 18. Resonance

## Definition

Resonance occurs when the frequency of an external periodic force becomes equal to the natural frequency of a vibrating system.

---

## Effects

- Maximum amplitude
- Efficient energy transfer

---

## Applications

- Musical instruments
- Radio receivers
- Resonance tubes

---

# 19. Organ Pipes

---

## Open Organ Pipe

Both ends open.

Fundamental frequency,

```
f = v/2L
```

Allowed wavelengths,

```
λ = 2L, L, 2L/3 ...
```

---

## Closed Organ Pipe

One end closed.

Fundamental frequency,

```
f = v/4L
```

Allowed wavelengths,

```
λ = 4L, 4L/3, 4L/5 ...
```

Only odd harmonics are present.

---

# 20. Harmonics

---

## Overtones

---

## Relation

```

(for open pipes and strings)

```

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

A wave has a frequency of **50 Hz** and a wavelength of **4 m**. Calculate its speed.

**Solution**

Using

```
v = fλ
```

```
= 50 × 4

= 200 m s⁻¹
```

---

### Example 2

A particle performs **200 vibrations** in **20 s**. Calculate the frequency and time period.

**Solution**

Frequency,

```
f = 200/20

= 10 Hz
```

Time period,

```
T = 1/f

= 0.1 s
```

---

### Example 3

A sound wave travels with a speed of **340 m s⁻¹** and has a frequency of **680 Hz**. Find its wavelength.

**Solution**

Using

```
λ = v/f
```

```
= 340/680

= 0.5 m
```

---

### Example 4

Two tuning forks produce frequencies of **256 Hz** and **260 Hz**. Calculate the beat frequency.

**Solution**

```
fb

=

|260 − 256|

=

4 Hz
```

---

### Example 5

An open organ pipe has a length of **0.50 m**. Calculate its fundamental frequency.

Take

```
v = 340 m s⁻¹
```

**Solution**

```
f = v/2L
```

```
= 340/(2 × 0.50)

= 340 Hz
```

---

### Example 6

Explain why sound cannot travel through outer space.

**Solution**

Sound is a **mechanical longitudinal wave** and requires a **material medium** for propagation. Since outer space is nearly a vacuum and contains no medium, sound cannot travel through it.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **wave motion** with the movement of matter; waves transfer **energy**, not matter.
- Mixing up **transverse** and **longitudinal** waves.
- Using the incorrect relation between **frequency**, **wavelength**, and **wave speed**; always use **v = fλ**.
- Assuming the speed of sound is the same in all media; it depends on the **elasticity** and **density** of the medium.
- Confusing **echo** with **reverberation**.
- Forgetting that **stationary waves** do **not transfer net energy**.
- Mixing up **nodes** and **antinodes** in standing waves.
- Assuming all harmonics are present in a **closed organ pipe**; only **odd harmonics** are produced.

---

# Chapter Summary

- A **wave** is a disturbance that transfers energy without transporting matter.
- Waves are broadly classified into **mechanical** and **electromagnetic** waves; mechanical waves may be **transverse** or **longitudinal**.
- The important wave parameters are **amplitude**, **wavelength**, **frequency**, **time period**, **wave speed**, **angular frequency**, and **wave number**.
- **Progressive waves** carry energy from one point to another, whereas **stationary waves** are formed by superposition and contain fixed **nodes** and **antinodes**.
- **Sound** is a longitudinal mechanical wave that requires a material medium for propagation.
- The speed of sound depends on the properties of the medium, particularly its elasticity, density, temperature, and humidity.
- **Reflection of sound** gives rise to phenomena such as **echo** and **reverberation**, which have practical applications in acoustics and SONAR.
- The **Doppler effect** explains the apparent change in frequency due to the relative motion between the source and the observer.
- **Beats** result from the interference of two sound waves of slightly different frequencies and are useful in tuning musical instruments.
- **Resonance**, **organ pipes**, and **harmonics** are important concepts that explain the production and amplification of sound in musical instruments and many physical systems.
