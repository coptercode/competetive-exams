# Introduction

Organic compounds containing the carbonyl group are called carbonyl compounds.

Functional group:

\[

> C=O
> \]

Two important classes:

1. Aldehydes
2. Ketones

Carboxylic acids contain:

\[
-COOH
\]

group.

---

# 1. Aldehydes

## Definition

Compounds containing the functional group:

\[
-CHO
\]

\[
R-CHO
\]

Example:

\[
CH_3CHO
\]

(Ethanal)

---

\[

(Propanone)

# B. Rosenmund Reduction

## Definition

Reduction of acid chloride to aldehyde.

Reaction:

\[
RCOCl+H_2
\xrightarrow{Pd/BaSO_4}
RCHO+HCl
\]

---

# C. Stephen Reaction

Nitriles are reduced to aldehydes.

\[
RCN
\xrightarrow{SnCl_2/HCl}
RCHO
\]

---

# D. Etard Reaction

Oxidation of methyl group of aromatic compounds.

Example:

\[
C_6H_5CH_3
\xrightarrow{CrO_2Cl_2}
C_6H_5CHO
\]

---

- Hydrogen bonding
- Dimer formation

Solubility decreases with increasing carbon chain length.

# 10. Reactivity of Carbonyl Compounds

\[

\]

---

## Reason

Aldehydes are less sterically hindered than ketones.

# 14. Important Chemical Tests

---

# A. Tollens' Test

## Principle

Aldehydes reduce silver ions to metallic silver.

Reaction:

\[
Ag^+
\rightarrow
Ag
\]

Observation:

```
Silver mirror formation
```

---

# B. Fehling's Test

Aldehydes produce:

```
Brick-red Cu₂O precipitate
```

---

# C. Iodoform Test

Given by compounds containing:

\[
CH_3CO-
\]

group.

Observation:

```
Yellow precipitate of CHI₃
```

---

Examples:

- Acetaldehyde
- Methyl ketones

---

# 15. Aldol Condensation

## Definition

Aldehydes or ketones containing alpha hydrogen undergo condensation in presence of dilute base.

Example:

\[
2CH_3CHO
\rightarrow
CH_3CH(OH)CH_2CHO
\]

---

Product:

Aldol

---

# 16. Cannizzaro Reaction

## Definition

Aldehydes without alpha hydrogen undergo self oxidation-reduction.

Example:

\[
2HCHO+NaOH
\rightarrow
CH_3OH+HCOONa
\]

---

Products:

- Alcohol
- Carboxylic acid salt

---

# 17. Alpha Hydrogen

## Definition

Hydrogen attached to carbon adjacent to carbonyl carbon.

Position:

```
α-carbon
 |
C-C=O
```

---

Importance:

- Aldol condensation
- Enolate formation

---

# 18. Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) Reaction

## Definition

Alpha halogenation of carboxylic acids.

Reaction:

\[
RCH_2COOH
\xrightarrow{Br_2/P}
RCHBrCOOH
\]

---

# 19. Acidity of Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids are acidic because carboxylate ion is resonance stabilized.

---

Acidity order:

\[
RCOOH > ROH
\]

---

# Effect of Substituents

Electron withdrawing groups increase acidity.

Example:

\[
ClCH_2COOH > CH_3COOH
\]

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** Why are aldehydes more reactive than ketones?

### Solution

Aldehydes have less steric hindrance and only one alkyl group donating electrons. Ketones have two alkyl groups, reducing electrophilicity of carbonyl carbon.

---

## Example 2

**Question:** Give Tollens' test for aldehydes.

### Solution

Aldehydes reduce ammoniacal silver nitrate to metallic silver, producing a silver mirror.

---

## Example 3

**Question:** What is aldol condensation?

### Solution

Aldehydes or ketones containing alpha hydrogen react in presence of dilute base to form β-hydroxy aldehydes or ketones.

---

## Example 4

**Question:** Why are carboxylic acids acidic?

### Solution

Loss of H⁺ produces carboxylate ion, which is stabilized by resonance.

---

## Example 5

**Question:** What is iodoform test?

### Solution

Compounds containing methyl carbonyl group produce yellow precipitate of iodoform (CHI₃) on reaction with iodine and alkali.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing aldehydes and ketones.
- Forgetting that aldehydes give Tollens' and Fehling's tests.
- Mixing up Rosenmund, Stephen, and Etard reactions.
- Forgetting the role of alpha hydrogen in aldol condensation.
- Applying Cannizzaro reaction to aldehydes having alpha hydrogen.
- Confusing carbonyl carbon with alpha carbon.
- Forgetting that carboxylic acids form hydrogen-bonded dimers.
- Assuming ketones oxidise easily like aldehydes.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Aldehydes, ketones, and carboxylic acids contain carbonyl-related functional groups.
- Aldehydes are generally more reactive than ketones due to lower steric hindrance.
- Carbonyl compounds undergo nucleophilic addition reactions.
- Important tests include Tollens' test, Fehling's test, and iodoform test.
- Aldol condensation occurs due to alpha hydrogen, while Cannizzaro reaction occurs in aldehydes without alpha hydrogen.
- Carboxylic acids are acidic due to resonance stabilization of carboxylate ions.
- Important preparation methods include Rosenmund reduction, Stephen reaction, Etard reaction, and HVZ reaction.
- These compounds have major applications in industry, biology, and organic synthesis.
