# Introduction

Living organisms perform several essential activities to maintain life. These activities are called **life processes**. They include **nutrition, respiration, transportation,** and **excretion**. These processes provide energy, transport materials, remove wastes, and ensure the proper functioning of the body.

---

# 1. Life Processes

**Life processes** are the basic biological processes necessary for maintaining life.

### Major Life Processes

- Nutrition
- Respiration
- Transportation
- Excretion

---

# 2. Nutrition

**Nutrition** is the process by which organisms obtain food and utilize it for energy, growth, repair, and maintenance.

---

## Types of Nutrition

### (A) Autotrophic Nutrition

In **autotrophic nutrition**, organisms prepare their own food from simple inorganic substances.

Examples:

- Green plants
- Algae
- Some bacteria

---

### Photosynthesis

**Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants prepare food using sunlight, chlorophyll, carbon dioxide, and water.

### Chemical Equation

```
6CO₂ + 6H₂O

Sunlight
Chlorophyll

↓

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
```

### Requirements for Photosynthesis

- Sunlight
- Chlorophyll
- Carbon dioxide
- Water

### Importance

- Produces food.
- Releases oxygen.
- Maintains the balance of oxygen and carbon dioxide in the atmosphere.

---

### Structure of a Leaf (Simplified)

```
          Leaf

     _____________
    /             \
   | Chlorophyll   |
   |               |
   | Stomata       |
    \_____________/

CO₂ enters through stomata.
O₂ is released through stomata.
```

---

### (B) Heterotrophic Nutrition

Organisms that cannot prepare their own food depend on other organisms.

Examples:

- Humans
- Animals
- Fungi

---

## Types of Heterotrophic Nutrition

- Holozoic
- Saprophytic
- Parasitic

---

The **digestive system** converts complex food into simple absorbable substances.

### Main Organs

```
Mouth
  ↓
Oesophagus
  ↓
Stomach
  ↓
Small Intestine
  ↓
Large Intestine
  ↓

  ↓

---

## Digestion in Humans

### Mouth

- Food is chewed.
- Saliva contains **salivary amylase**, which digests starch into sugars.

---

### Oesophagus

- Food moves by **peristalsis**.

---

### Stomach

- Secretes **hydrochloric acid (HCl)**.
- Produces **pepsin** to digest proteins.
- Mucus protects the stomach lining.

---

### Small Intestine

- Receives bile from the liver.
- Receives pancreatic juice from the pancreas.
- Digestion is completed.
- Nutrients are absorbed through **villi**.

---

### Large Intestine

- Absorbs water.
- Forms faeces.

# 4. Respiration

**Respiration** is the process of breaking down food to release energy in the form of **ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate).**

---

## Types of Respiration

### (A) Aerobic Respiration

Occurs in the presence of oxygen.

### Equation

```
Glucose + Oxygen

↓

Carbon Dioxide + Water + Energy (ATP)
```

```
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

↓

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy
```

### Characteristics

- Requires oxygen.
- Produces more ATP.
- Occurs mainly in mitochondria.

---

### (B) Anaerobic Respiration

Occurs in the absence of oxygen.

#### In Yeast

```
Glucose

↓

Alcohol + CO₂ + Energy
```

#### In Human Muscles

```
Glucose

↓

Lactic Acid + Energy
```

Occurs during vigorous exercise.

---

## Difference Between Aerobic and Anaerobic Respiration

| Aerobic                       | Anaerobic                            |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Requires oxygen               | Does not require oxygen              |
| Produces more ATP             | Produces less ATP                    |
| End products: CO₂ and H₂O     | End products: Alcohol or lactic acid |
| Occurs mainly in mitochondria | Occurs in cytoplasm                  |

The transportation system consists of:

- Blood
- Heart
- Blood vessels
- Lymph

---

## Blood

Blood transports:

- Oxygen
- Carbon dioxide
- Nutrients
- Hormones
- Waste products

### Components of Blood

| Component                | Function                          |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| Plasma                   | Transports nutrients and hormones |
| Red Blood Cells (RBCs)   | Carry oxygen using haemoglobin    |
| White Blood Cells (WBCs) | Fight infections                  |
| Platelets                | Help in blood clotting            |

# 7. Human Heart

The **heart** is a muscular organ that pumps blood throughout the body.

### Structure

```
        Heart

   RA        LA
    |        |
   RV        LV

RA = Right Atrium
RV = Right Ventricle
LA = Left Atrium
LV = Left Ventricle
```

The heart has **four chambers**:

- Right Atrium
- Right Ventricle
- Left Atrium
- Left Ventricle

---

## Double Circulation

In humans, blood passes through the heart **twice** during one complete cycle.

### Pathway

```
Body
↓

Right Atrium
↓

Right Ventricle
↓

Lungs
↓

Left Atrium
↓

Left Ventricle
↓

Body
```

### Advantages

- Efficient oxygen supply.
- Separation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood.
- Supports high energy requirements.

---

# 8. Transportation in Plants

Plants transport:

- Water and minerals.
- Food.

---

## Xylem

Functions:

- Transports water.
- Transports minerals.
- Movement is mainly upward.

---

## Phloem

Functions:

- Transports prepared food.
- Movement occurs in both directions.

---

## Transpiration

**Transpiration** is the loss of water as water vapour through the stomata.

### Importance

- Helps in water transport.
- Cools the plant.
- Maintains water balance.

---

# 9. Excretion

**Excretion** is the removal of metabolic waste products from the body.

---

### Organs

```

   ↓

   ↓

---

## Structure of Kidney

The functional unit of the kidney is the **nephron**.

### Simplified Nephron

```
Blood
  ↓
Glomerulus
  ↓
Bowman's Capsule
  ↓
Tubule
  ↓
Collecting Duct
  ↓
Urine
```

---

## Formation of Urine

Urine formation takes place in three steps.

### (A) Ultrafiltration

- Occurs in the glomerulus.
- Water and small molecules are filtered.

---

### (B) Selective Reabsorption

- Useful substances such as glucose, amino acids, salts, and water are reabsorbed into the blood.

---

### (C) Tubular Secretion

- Additional waste substances are secreted into the tubule.

---

## Artificial Kidney (Dialysis)

**Dialysis** is used when kidneys fail to function properly.

It removes:

- Urea
- Excess salts
- Excess water

from the blood using a dialysis machine.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **nutrition** with **digestion**.
- Assuming photosynthesis occurs without sunlight.
- Mixing up **xylem** and **phloem** functions.
- Confusing **aerobic** and **anaerobic** respiration.
- Forgetting that **alveoli** are the site of gaseous exchange.
- Assuming the heart has only two chambers (the human heart has four).
- Confusing **ultrafiltration** with **selective reabsorption**.
- Thinking transpiration is the same as photosynthesis.

# Chapter Summary

- **Life processes** include **nutrition, respiration, transportation,** and **excretion**, all of which are essential for survival.
- **Autotrophs** prepare food by **photosynthesis**, while **heterotrophs** obtain food from other organisms.
- The **human digestive system** breaks down food into simple nutrients, with most digestion and absorption occurring in the **small intestine**.
- **Respiration** releases energy in the form of **ATP**, and may be **aerobic** or **anaerobic** depending on the availability of oxygen.
- The **human circulatory system** consists of the heart, blood, and blood vessels, and follows the principle of **double circulation**.
- In plants, **xylem** transports water and minerals, while **phloem** transports food. **Transpiration** helps in the upward movement of water.
- The **human excretory system** removes metabolic wastes through the kidneys, where **nephrons** filter blood and form urine.
- Proper functioning of these life processes ensures growth, repair, maintenance, and survival of living organisms.
