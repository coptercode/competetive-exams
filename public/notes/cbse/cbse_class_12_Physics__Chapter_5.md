# Introduction

Magnetism is a property of matter due to which materials experience attractive or repulsive forces in a magnetic field.

Every magnet has two poles:

- North pole (N)
- South pole (S)

---

# 1. Bar Magnet

A bar magnet is a magnetic material having two poles separated by a distance.

```
N -------- S
```

---

# Properties of Magnets

1. Like poles repel.
2. Unlike poles attract.
3. Magnetic poles always exist in pairs.
4. A magnet always produces a magnetic field.

---

# 2. Magnetic Field Lines

Magnetic field lines represent the magnetic field around a magnet.

---

# Properties

1. Outside a magnet:

\[
N \rightarrow S
\]

2. Inside a magnet:

\[
S \rightarrow N
\]

3. Magnetic field lines form closed loops.
4. They never intersect.

---

# 3. Magnetic Dipole

## Definition

A pair of equal and opposite magnetic poles separated by a small distance is called a magnetic dipole.

---

Examples:

- Bar magnet
- Current-carrying loop

---

# Magnetic Dipole Moment

## Definition

The strength of a magnetic dipole is called its magnetic moment.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
M=m(2l)
}
\]

where:

- m = pole strength
- 2l = magnetic length

---

## Unit

\[
\boxed{Am^2}
\]

---

# 4. Magnetic Field Due to a Bar Magnet

A bar magnet behaves like a magnetic dipole.

---

# A. Axial Position

Magnetic field at a point on the axis:

\[
\boxed{
B=
\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}
\frac{2M}{r^3}
}
\]

---

# B. Equatorial Position

Magnetic field:

\[
\boxed{
B=
\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}
\frac{M}{r^3}
}
\]

Direction is opposite to magnetic moment.

---

# 5. Torque on Magnetic Dipole

When a magnetic dipole is placed in a uniform magnetic field:

\[
\boxed{
\tau=MB\sin\theta
}
\]

where:

- M = magnetic moment
- B = magnetic field
- θ = angle between M and B

---

# Potential Energy of Magnetic Dipole

\[
\boxed{
U=-MB\cos\theta
}
\]

---

# Stable Equilibrium

When:

\[
\theta=0^\circ
\]

Dipole aligns with magnetic field.

---

# Unstable Equilibrium

When:

\[
\theta=180^\circ
\]

---

# 6. Earth's Magnetism

Earth behaves like a giant magnet.

The geographic north pole acts approximately as a magnetic south pole.

---

# Important Terms

---

# A. Magnetic Declination

## Definition

The angle between geographic meridian and magnetic meridian.

---

# B. Magnetic Inclination (Dip)

## Definition

The angle made by Earth's magnetic field with horizontal direction.

Symbol:

\[
\theta
\]

---

At magnetic equator:

\[
\theta=0^\circ
\]

---

At magnetic poles:

\[
\theta=90^\circ
\]

---

# C. Horizontal Component of Earth's Field

If Earth's magnetic field is B:

\[
\boxed{
B_H=B\cos\theta
}
\]

---

Vertical component:

\[
\boxed{
B_V=B\sin\theta
}
\]

---

# 7. Magnetisation

## Definition

Magnetic moment developed per unit volume of a material.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
M=\frac{\text{Magnetic moment}}{\text{Volume}}
}
\]

---

SI Unit:

\[
A/m
\]

---

# 8. Magnetic Intensity

Magnetic field strength produced by external sources.

Symbol:

\[
H
\]

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
B=\mu_0(H+M)
}
\]

---

where:

- B = magnetic induction
- H = magnetic intensity
- M = magnetisation

---

# 9. Magnetic Susceptibility

## Definition

Ratio of magnetisation to magnetic intensity.

\[
\boxed{
\chi_m=\frac{M}{H}
}
\]

---

It indicates how easily a material gets magnetised.

---

# 10. Magnetic Materials

Materials are classified as:

1. Diamagnetic
2. Paramagnetic
3. Ferromagnetic

---

# A. Diamagnetic Materials

## Definition

Materials weakly repelled by magnetic fields.

---

Examples:

- Bismuth
- Copper
- Water
- Gold

---

Properties:

- No permanent magnetic moment.
- Susceptibility is negative.

\[
\chi_m<0
\]

---

# B. Paramagnetic Materials

## Definition

Materials weakly attracted by magnetic fields.

---

Examples:

- Aluminium
- Oxygen
- Platinum

---

Properties:

- Have unpaired electrons.
- Small positive susceptibility.

\[
\chi_m>0
\]

---

# C. Ferromagnetic Materials

## Definition

Materials strongly attracted by magnetic fields and can retain magnetism.

---

Examples:

- Iron
- Cobalt
- Nickel

---

Properties:

- Very large positive susceptibility.
- Have magnetic domains.

---

# 11. Magnetic Domains

A ferromagnetic material contains tiny regions called magnetic domains.

Each domain behaves like a small magnet.

---

Without external field:

```
Random domains
```

---

With external field:

```
Aligned domains
```

---

# 12. Curie Temperature

## Definition

The temperature above which a ferromagnetic substance loses its magnetic properties and becomes paramagnetic.

---

Symbol:

\[
T_C
\]

---

Examples:

Iron:

\[
T_C \approx 1043K
\]

---

# 13. Hysteresis

## Definition

The lagging of magnetisation behind the applied magnetic field is called hysteresis.

---

# Hysteresis Loop

Shows relationship between:

- Magnetisation (M)
- Magnetic field (H)

---

# Important Terms

---

## Retentivity

Ability of a material to retain magnetism after removing external field.

---

## Coercivity

Magnetic field required to reduce magnetisation to zero.

---

# Soft and Hard Magnetic Materials

---

# Soft Magnetic Materials

Properties:

- Low coercivity
- Easily magnetised and demagnetised

Examples:

- Soft iron

Uses:

- Electromagnets
- Transformers

---

# Hard Magnetic Materials

Properties:

- High retentivity
- High coercivity

Examples:

- Steel

Uses:

- Permanent magnets

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** Calculate torque on a magnetic dipole of moment \(2Am^2\) placed in a magnetic field of \(0.5T\) at \(90^\circ\).

Given:

\[
M=2
\]

\[
B=0.5
\]

\[
\theta=90^\circ
\]

Formula:

\[
\tau=MB\sin\theta
\]

\[
=2(0.5)(1)
\]

\[
\boxed{\tau=1Nm}
\]

---

## Example 2

**Question:** Why are ferromagnetic materials strongly attracted by magnets?

### Solution

Ferromagnetic materials contain magnetic domains that align strongly in an external magnetic field, producing strong magnetisation.

---

## Example 3

**Question:** What happens above Curie temperature?

### Solution

Above Curie temperature, thermal agitation destroys domain alignment, and ferromagnetic materials become paramagnetic.

---

## Example 4

**Question:** Why are diamagnetic materials repelled by magnetic fields?

### Solution

Diamagnetic substances develop induced magnetic moments opposite to the applied magnetic field, causing weak repulsion.

---

## Example 5

**Question:** Why is soft iron used in electromagnets?

### Solution

Soft iron has low coercivity and can be easily magnetised and demagnetised.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing magnetic poles with electric charges; magnetic monopoles do not exist.
- Forgetting that magnetic field lines form closed loops.
- Mixing magnetic moment and magnetisation.
- Confusing diamagnetic and paramagnetic behaviour.
- Forgetting that ferromagnetism disappears above Curie temperature.
- Using wrong direction of Earth's magnetic field.
- Confusing retentivity with coercivity.
- Forgetting that magnetic force depends on moving charges.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Magnets behave as magnetic dipoles with north and south poles.
- Magnetic dipoles experience torque in external magnetic fields.
- Earth behaves like a giant magnet with measurable declination and inclination.
- Magnetisation describes the magnetic moment per unit volume.
- Materials are classified as diamagnetic, paramagnetic, and ferromagnetic.
- Ferromagnetism is explained by magnetic domains.
- Curie temperature marks the transition from ferromagnetic to paramagnetic behaviour.
- Hysteresis explains the magnetic memory of materials.
- Soft magnetic materials are used for electromagnets, while hard magnetic materials are used for permanent magnets.
