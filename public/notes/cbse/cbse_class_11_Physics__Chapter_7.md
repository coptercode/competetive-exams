# Introduction

Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force called **gravitational force**. This force is responsible for the motion of planets, satellites, stars, galaxies, and even the falling of objects toward the Earth.

The law of gravitation, proposed by **Sir Isaac Newton**, successfully explains celestial as well as terrestrial phenomena.

---

# 1. Gravitation

## Definition

**Gravitation** is the mutual force of attraction between any two masses in the universe.

Examples

- Falling of an apple
- Motion of planets
- Ocean tides
- Artificial satellites

---

# 2. Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion

Johannes Kepler proposed three empirical laws describing the motion of planets around the Sun.

---

## First Law (Law of Orbits)

Every planet moves around the Sun in an **elliptical orbit**, with the **Sun at one focus**.

```
        Planet

      ●

   /         \

 *             *

Sun           Focus
```

---

## Second Law (Law of Areas)

The line joining the Sun and the planet sweeps out **equal areas in equal intervals of time**.

Implication

- Planet moves faster near the Sun.
- Planet moves slower when farther from the Sun.

---

## Third Law (Law of Periods)

The square of the time period of revolution of a planet is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit.

Formula

```
T² ∝ r³
```

or

```
T²/r³ = Constant
```

---

# 3. Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation

## Statement

Every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

---

## Formula

```
          Gm₁m₂
F = ----------------
            r²
```

where

- **F** = Gravitational force
- **G** = Universal gravitational constant
- **m₁, m₂** = Masses
- **r** = Distance between centres

---

## SI Unit of G

```
N m² kg⁻²
```

---

## Dimensional Formula

```
[M⁻¹L³T⁻²]
```

---

## Value of G

```
6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²
```

---

# 4. Characteristics of Gravitational Force

- Always attractive.
- Acts along the line joining the centres.
- Obeys Newton's Third Law.
- Independent of the medium.
- Long-range force.

---

# 5. Acceleration Due to Gravity (g)

## Definition

The acceleration produced in a freely falling body due to Earth's gravitational pull.

---

## Formula

Using Newton's law,

```
          GM
g = ----------------
            R²
```

where

- **M** = Mass of Earth
- **R** = Radius of Earth

---

## Value

```
9.8 m s⁻²
```

Approximation

```
9.81 m s⁻²

or

9.8 m s⁻²
```

---

## Dimensional Formula

```
[LT⁻²]
```

---

### Example 6

A satellite revolves around Earth in a circular orbit. If the radius of the orbit is increased, what happens to its orbital velocity?

**Solution**

Since

```
Vo = √(GM/r)
```

orbital velocity is **inversely proportional to the square root of the orbital radius**.

Therefore, **as the orbital radius increases, the orbital velocity decreases**.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing the **gravitational constant (G)** with the **acceleration due to gravity (g)**.
- Assuming **g** is the same everywhere on Earth; it varies with **altitude**, **depth**, and **latitude**.
- Forgetting the **negative sign** in the general formula for gravitational potential energy.
- Using **mgh** for large distances from Earth; the expression **PE = -GMm/r** should be used instead.
- Mixing up **orbital velocity** and **escape velocity**.
- Assuming the **mass of the satellite** affects its orbital or escape velocity; both are **independent of the satellite's mass**.
- Confusing **geostationary satellites** with **polar satellites**.
- Forgetting that **Kepler's Third Law** relates the **square of the time period** to the **cube of the orbital radius**.

# Chapter Summary

- **Gravitation** is the universal attractive force between all masses and is governed by **Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation**.
- **Kepler's three laws** describe the motion of planets and provide the basis for understanding orbital motion.
- The **acceleration due to gravity (g)** depends on Earth's mass and radius and varies with **altitude**, **depth**, and **Earth's rotation**.
- **Gravitational potential energy** is negative when measured relative to infinity, while **gravitational potential** is the potential energy per unit mass.
- The **escape velocity** is the minimum speed required to leave Earth's gravitational field permanently, whereas the **orbital velocity** keeps a satellite in stable circular motion.
- Artificial satellites are classified into **geostationary** and **polar satellites**, each serving different purposes such as communication, weather forecasting, navigation, mapping, and remote sensing.
- The relation **Ve = √2 Vo** connects escape velocity and orbital velocity.
- The concepts of gravitation explain the motion of planets, satellites, tides, and many other natural and technological phenomena in the universe.
