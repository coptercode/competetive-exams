# Introduction

Many chemical reactions do not proceed to completion. Instead, they reach a state where the **forward** and **backward** reactions occur at equal rates. This state is called **chemical equilibrium**.

Equilibrium also exists in aqueous solutions involving acids, bases, and salts, known as **ionic equilibrium**.

Understanding equilibrium is essential for predicting reaction direction, product yield, pH, and solubility.

---

# 1. Equilibrium

## Definition

Equilibrium is the state at which the **rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the backward reaction**.

At equilibrium:

- Concentrations remain constant.
- Reactions continue in both directions.
- It is a **dynamic** state.

---

## Dynamic Nature

```
N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃

Forward Rate

=

Backward Rate
```

Although no visible change occurs, molecules continue reacting.

---

# 2. Types of Equilibrium

## (A) Physical Equilibrium

Occurs during physical processes.

Examples

- Melting of ice
- Evaporation of water
- Dissolution of sugar

---

## (B) Chemical Equilibrium

Occurs during reversible chemical reactions.

Example

```
H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI
```

---

# 3. Characteristics of Chemical Equilibrium

- Dynamic in nature.
- Established only in a closed system.
- Forward and backward reaction rates are equal.
- Concentrations remain constant.
- Can be approached from either direction.

---

# 4. Law of Mass Action

### Proposed by

**Guldberg and Waage**

### Statement

At constant temperature, the rate of a chemical reaction is proportional to the product of the active masses (concentrations) of the reactants.

---

# 5. Equilibrium Constant (K)

For the reaction:

```
aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD
```

The equilibrium constant is

```
Kc

=

[C]^c [D]^d

──────────────

[A]^a [B]^b
```

where concentrations are taken at equilibrium.

---

# 6. Equilibrium Constant (Kc)

## Characteristics

- Constant at a given temperature.
- Independent of initial concentration.
- Independent of catalyst.
- Indicates the extent of reaction.

---

## Interpretation of Kc

| Value of Kc | Interpretation              |
| ----------- | --------------------------- |
| Kc >> 1     | Products predominate        |
| Kc << 1     | Reactants predominate       |
| Kc ≈ 1      | Appreciable amounts of both |

---

# 7. Equilibrium Constant (Kp)

For gaseous reactions,

```
Kp

=

(Pc)^c (Pd)^d

──────────────

(Pa)^a (Pb)^b
```

where P represents equilibrium partial pressures.

---

## Relationship Between Kp and Kc

```
Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn
```

where

```
Δn

=

Moles of gaseous products

−

Moles of gaseous reactants
```

---

# 8. Reaction Quotient (Q)

Reaction quotient has the same mathematical form as K but uses **current concentrations** instead of equilibrium concentrations.

---

## Comparison

| Condition | Prediction                 |
| --------- | -------------------------- |
| Q < K     | Reaction proceeds forward  |
| Q > K     | Reaction proceeds backward |
| Q = K     | System is at equilibrium   |

---

# 9. Factors Affecting Equilibrium

---

## (A) Concentration

Increasing concentration of reactants shifts equilibrium toward products.

Removing products also shifts equilibrium toward products.

---

## (B) Pressure

Applicable only to gaseous reactions.

Increase in pressure shifts equilibrium toward the side having fewer gaseous moles.

---

## (C) Temperature

For exothermic reactions,

```
Heat + Products
```

Increasing temperature shifts equilibrium backward.

For endothermic reactions,

Increasing temperature shifts equilibrium forward.

---

## (D) Catalyst

A catalyst:

- Does not change K.
- Does not shift equilibrium.
- Helps equilibrium to be attained faster.

---

# 10. Le Chatelier's Principle

## Statement

If a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change in concentration, pressure, or temperature, the equilibrium shifts in such a way as to oppose the imposed change.

---

## Applications

### Haber Process

```
N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃

ΔH = Negative
```

High pressure favors ammonia formation because fewer moles of gas are produced.

Moderately low temperature increases ammonia yield.

---

### Contact Process

```
2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃
```

High pressure and moderate temperature favor sulfur trioxide formation.

---

# 11. Acids and Bases

---

## Arrhenius Concept

### Acid

Produces H⁺ ions in water.

Example

```
HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻
```

---

### Base

Produces OH⁻ ions in water.

Example

```
NaOH → Na⁺ + OH⁻
```

---

## Limitations

- Applicable only in aqueous solution.
- Cannot explain ammonia acting as a base.

---

# 12. Brønsted-Lowry Concept

### Acid

Proton donor.

---

### Base

Proton acceptor.

---

## Example

```
NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻
```

NH₃ acts as a base.

H₂O acts as an acid.

---

## Conjugate Acid-Base Pair

```
NH₃ ⇌ NH₄⁺

Base    Conjugate Acid
```

```
H₂O ⇌ OH⁻

Acid   Conjugate Base
```

---

# 13. Lewis Concept

### Lewis Acid

Electron pair acceptor.

Examples

- BF₃
- AlCl₃
- H⁺

---

### Lewis Base

Electron pair donor.

Examples

- NH₃
- H₂O
- OH⁻

---

# 14. Ionization of Weak Acids

Example

```
CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺
```

Acid dissociation constant,

```
Ka

=

[CH₃COO⁻][H⁺]

────────────────

[CH₃COOH]
```

Larger Ka indicates a stronger acid.

---

# 15. Ionization of Weak Bases

Example

```
NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻
```

Base dissociation constant,

```
Kb

=

[NH₄⁺][OH⁻]

──────────────

[NH₃]
```

Larger Kb indicates a stronger base.

---

# 16. Ionic Product of Water

Water ionizes as

```
H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻
```

At 25°C,

```
Kw

=

[H⁺][OH⁻]

=

1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴
```

---

# 17. pH Scale

Definition

```
pH = −log[H⁺]
```

---

## pOH

```
pOH = −log[OH⁻]
```

---

## Relation

```
pH + pOH = 14
```

(at 25°C)

---

## Nature of Solutions

| pH  | Nature  |
| --- | ------- |
| < 7 | Acidic  |
| = 7 | Neutral |
| > 7 | Basic   |

---

# 18. Buffer Solutions

## Definition

Solutions that resist changes in pH upon addition of small amounts of acid or base.

---

## Types

### Acidic Buffer

Weak acid + its salt

Example

```
CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa
```

---

### Basic Buffer

Weak base + its salt

Example

```
NH₄OH + NH₄Cl
```

---

## Applications

- Blood pH regulation.
- Pharmaceutical preparations.
- Biological systems.

---

# 19. Solubility Product (Ksp)

For

```
AB ⇌ A⁺ + B⁻
```

```
Ksp

=

[A⁺][B⁻]
```

---

## Characteristics

- Constant at a given temperature.
- Used for sparingly soluble salts.

---

# 20. Common Ion Effect

## Definition

Suppression of ionization of a weak electrolyte by adding a strong electrolyte containing a common ion.

Example

```
CH₃COOH

+

CH₃COONa
```

The acetate ion suppresses the ionization of acetic acid.

---

# 21. Salt Hydrolysis

Reaction of salt ions with water producing acidic or basic solutions.

---

## Types

### Strong Acid + Strong Base

Example

```
NaCl
```

Solution is neutral.

---

### Strong Acid + Weak Base

Example

```
NH₄Cl
```

Solution is acidic.

---

### Weak Acid + Strong Base

Example

```
CH₃COONa
```

Solution is basic.

---

### Weak Acid + Weak Base

Example

```
NH₄CH₃COO
```

Nature depends on Ka and Kb.

| --------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Physical change       | Chemical change          |
| Example: Ice ⇌ Water  | Example: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ |
| Composition unchanged | Composition changes      |

---

| Arrhenius                | Brønsted-Lowry  | Lewis                  |
| ------------------------ | --------------- | ---------------------- |
| H⁺ donor in water        | Proton donor    | Electron pair acceptor |
| OH⁻ producer             | Proton acceptor | Electron pair donor    |
| Limited to aqueous media | Wider concept   | Widest concept         |

---

## Strong Acid vs Weak Acid

| Strong Acid         | Weak Acid          |
| ------------------- | ------------------ |
| Complete ionization | Partial ionization |
| Large Ka            | Small Ka           |

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

For the reaction

```
N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃
```

write the expression for Kc.

**Solution**

```
Kc

=

[NH₃]²

────────────

[N₂][H₂]³
```

---

### Example 2

For the reaction

```
H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI
```

If

```
Q < K
```

what is the direction of the reaction?

**Solution**

Since

```
Q < K
```

the forward reaction proceeds until equilibrium is reached.

---

### Example 3

Calculate the pH of a solution having

```
[H⁺]

=

1 × 10⁻³ M
```

**Solution**

```
pH

=

−log(10⁻³)

=

3
```

---

### Example 4

Calculate the pOH of a solution with

```
[OH⁻]

=

1 × 10⁻⁵ M
```

**Solution**

```
pOH

=

−log(10⁻⁵)

=

5
```

Therefore,

```
pH

=

14 − 5

=

9
```

---

### Example 5

State the effect of increasing pressure on the Haber process.

**Solution**

Reaction:

```
N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃
```

Reactants contain 4 moles of gas, while products contain 2 moles.

Increasing pressure shifts the equilibrium toward **NH₃ formation**.

---

### Example 6

A solution contains equal concentrations of CH₃COOH and CH₃COONa. What type of solution is it?

**Solution**

It contains a weak acid and its salt.

Therefore, it is an **acidic buffer solution**, which resists changes in pH on the addition of small amounts of acid or base.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **equilibrium** with the completion of a reaction; at equilibrium, both forward and backward reactions continue at equal rates.
- Assuming a **catalyst** changes the equilibrium constant or equilibrium position; it only speeds up the attainment of equilibrium.
- Forgetting to include only **gaseous species** while calculating **Δn** in the equation **Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn**.
- Mixing up **Ka** and **Kb** or assuming a larger value always means a weaker acid or base.
- Using **concentrations instead of equilibrium concentrations** in the expression for Kc.
- Forgetting the relation **pH + pOH = 14** at **25°C**.
- Confusing **common ion effect** with **buffer action**; the former suppresses ionization, whereas the latter resists changes in pH.
- Incorrectly predicting the nature of salt solutions without considering the strengths of the parent acid and base.

---

# Chapter Summary

- **Chemical equilibrium** is a dynamic state where the rates of the forward and backward reactions are equal.
- The **Law of Mass Action** leads to the expressions for the equilibrium constants **Kc** and **Kp**, which measure the extent of a reaction.
- The **reaction quotient (Q)** helps predict the direction in which a reaction will proceed to attain equilibrium.
- **Le Chatelier's Principle** explains how equilibrium shifts in response to changes in concentration, pressure, and temperature.
- The **Arrhenius**, **Brønsted-Lowry**, and **Lewis** theories provide progressively broader definitions of acids and bases.
- The strength of weak acids and bases is measured using **Ka** and **Kb**, while the **ionic product of water (Kw)** forms the basis of the **pH scale**.
- **Buffer solutions** resist changes in pH and play an important role in biological and industrial systems.
- The **solubility product (Ksp)** describes the solubility of sparingly soluble salts, and the **common ion effect** reduces the ionization or solubility of weak electrolytes.
- **Salt hydrolysis** explains why solutions of certain salts are acidic, basic, or neutral depending on the strengths of the parent acid and base.
