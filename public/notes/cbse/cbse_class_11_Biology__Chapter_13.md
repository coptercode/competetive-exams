# Introduction

**Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants, algae, and certain bacteria prepare food using **sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water** in the presence of chlorophyll. It is one of the most important biological processes because it is the primary source of food and oxygen for life on Earth.

The process occurs mainly in the **chloroplasts** of green plant cells.

---

# 1. Photosynthesis

## Definition

Photosynthesis is the process of converting **light energy into chemical energy**, resulting in the synthesis of carbohydrates from carbon dioxide and water.

### General Equation

```
6CO₂ + 12H₂O
      Light
──────────────►
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O
Chlorophyll
```

# 2. Historical Experiments

## Joseph Priestley (1770)

- Demonstrated that plants restore air made impure by burning candles.
- Showed that plants release oxygen.

---

## Jan Ingenhousz

- Demonstrated that photosynthesis occurs only in the presence of light.
- Green parts of plants release oxygen.

---

## Julius von Sachs

- Demonstrated that starch is formed during photosynthesis.
- Identified chloroplasts as the site of photosynthesis.

---

## T. W. Engelmann

- Used aerobic bacteria to show that maximum oxygen is released in blue and red light.

---

## Cornelius van Niel

- Suggested that oxygen released during photosynthesis comes from water.

---

## Robert Hill

- Demonstrated the **Hill Reaction**, proving that oxygen is evolved from water during the light reaction.

# 4. Photosynthetic Pigments

Pigments absorb light energy.

## Major Pigments

| Pigment       | Colour       | Function          |
| ------------- | ------------ | ----------------- |
| Chlorophyll a | Blue-green   | Primary pigment   |
| Chlorophyll b | Yellow-green | Accessory pigment |
| Carotene      | Orange       | Accessory pigment |
| Xanthophyll   | Yellow       | Accessory pigment |

---

## Importance of Accessory Pigments

- Broaden the range of light absorption.
- Protect chlorophyll from photooxidation.
- Transfer absorbed energy to chlorophyll a.

---

# 5. Absorption Spectrum and Action Spectrum

## Absorption Spectrum

Shows the wavelengths of light absorbed by pigments.

---

## Action Spectrum

Shows the effectiveness of different wavelengths in photosynthesis.

### Maximum photosynthesis occurs in:

- Blue light
- Red light

Green light is least effective because it is mostly reflected.

# 6. Light Reaction (Photochemical Phase)

Occurs in:

```
Grana (Thylakoid Membrane)
```

Requires:

- Light
- Water
- Chlorophyll

---

## Major Events

- Excitation of chlorophyll.
- Photolysis of water.
- ATP synthesis.
- NADPH formation.
- Oxygen evolution.

---

# 7. Photolysis of Water

Water splits into hydrogen ions, electrons, and oxygen.

Equation:

```
2H₂O
   │
4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂
```

Occurs in:

```
Photosystem II
```

---

# 8. Photosystems

Two photosystems participate in photosynthesis.

---

## Photosystem II (PS II)

Reaction centre:

```
P680
```

Functions

- Water splitting.
- Electron release.
- Oxygen evolution.

---

## Photosystem I (PS I)

Reaction centre:

```
P700
```

Functions

- NADPH formation.
- Final electron transfer.

# 10. Photophosphorylation

Formation of ATP using light energy.

---

## Types

### Cyclic Photophosphorylation

- Only PS I involved.
- ATP formed.
- No oxygen evolved.
- No NADPH produced.

---

### Non-Cyclic Photophosphorylation

- PS I and PS II involved.
- ATP formed.
- NADPH formed.
- Oxygen evolved.

---

# Comparison

| Cyclic    | Non-Cyclic   |
| --------- | ------------ |
| PS I only | PS I & PS II |
| ATP only  | ATP + NADPH  |
| No O₂     | O₂ evolved   |

# 11. Chemiosmotic Hypothesis

Proposed by:

**Peter Mitchell**

ATP is synthesized due to the movement of protons (H⁺) through ATP synthase across the thylakoid membrane.

# 13. Calvin Cycle (C₃ Cycle)

Discovered by:

**Melvin Calvin**

Occurs in C₃ plants.

---

## Steps

### (A) Carboxylation

- CO₂ combines with RuBP.
- Catalysed by **RuBisCO**.
- Produces 3-PGA.

---

### (B) Reduction

- ATP and NADPH convert 3-PGA into G3P (triose phosphate).

---

### (C) Regeneration

- RuBP is regenerated.
- Cycle continues.

---

## Important Facts

- First stable product: **3-Phosphoglycerate (3-PGA)**
- CO₂ acceptor: **RuBP (Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate)**

---

# 14. C₄ Pathway (Hatch and Slack Pathway)

Discovered by:

**Hatch and Slack**

Occurs in plants adapted to high temperature and intense light.

Examples

- Maize
- Sugarcane
- Sorghum

---

## Characteristics

- Kranz anatomy present.
- First stable product: Oxaloacetic acid (OAA).
- Initial CO₂ acceptor: Phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP).
- Low photorespiration.
- High photosynthetic efficiency.

---

# 15. Kranz Anatomy

Characteristic feature of C₄ plants.

```
Bundle Sheath Cells
       │
Mesophyll Cells
```

### Features

- Large bundle sheath cells.
- Chloroplast dimorphism.
- Vascular bundles surrounded by bundle sheath cells.

---

# 16. CAM Pathway

CAM stands for:

**Crassulacean Acid Metabolism**

Occurs in xerophytic plants.

Examples

- Opuntia
- Pineapple
- Agave

---

## Characteristics

- Stomata open at night.
- CO₂ fixed at night.
- Water loss reduced.

---

# 17. Photorespiration

Photorespiration is a process in which RuBisCO acts as an oxygenase, leading to oxygen uptake and carbon dioxide release.

Occurs mainly in:

- C₃ plants

---

## Characteristics

- Reduces photosynthetic efficiency.
- Consumes ATP.
- Produces no glucose.

---

# 18. Comparison of C₃ and C₄ Plants

| Feature          | C₃ Plants | C₄ Plants  |
| ---------------- | --------- | ---------- |
| First Product    | 3-PGA     | OAA        |
| CO₂ Acceptor     | RuBP      | PEP        |
| Kranz Anatomy    | Absent    | Present    |
| Photorespiration | High      | Negligible |
| Efficiency       | Lower     | Higher     |

# 20. Blackman's Law of Limiting Factors

Proposed by:

**F. F. Blackman**

When several factors affect a process, the rate is limited by the factor that is in the least favourable amount.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Why is chlorophyll a called the primary pigment?

**Solution**

Chlorophyll a directly participates in the photochemical reactions of photosynthesis by converting light energy into chemical energy. Other pigments transfer absorbed light energy to chlorophyll a.

---

### Example 2

State two differences between cyclic and non-cyclic photophosphorylation.

**Solution**

- Cyclic photophosphorylation involves only Photosystem I and produces only ATP.
- Non-cyclic photophosphorylation involves both Photosystem I and Photosystem II and produces ATP, NADPH, and oxygen.

---

### Example 3

What is the first stable product of the Calvin cycle?

**Solution**

The first stable product of the Calvin cycle is **3-phosphoglycerate (3-PGA)**.

---

### Example 4

Why do C₄ plants show higher photosynthetic efficiency than C₃ plants?

**Solution**

C₄ plants possess **Kranz anatomy** and initially fix CO₂ using PEP, minimizing photorespiration. As a result, photosynthesis remains efficient even under high temperature and low CO₂ conditions.

---

### Example 5

Explain the significance of photolysis of water.

**Solution**

Photolysis provides electrons to replace those lost by Photosystem II, supplies hydrogen ions for ATP synthesis, and releases oxygen as a by-product of photosynthesis.

---

### Example 6

State Blackman's Law of Limiting Factors.

**Solution**

Blackman's Law states that when several factors influence a process, its rate is controlled by the factor that is least available or least favourable.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing the **light reaction** with the **Calvin cycle**.
- Forgetting that **oxygen released during photosynthesis originates from water**, not carbon dioxide.
- Mixing up the reaction centres of the photosystems: **P680** belongs to **Photosystem II**, while **P700** belongs to **Photosystem I**.
- Assuming the **dark reaction** occurs only in darkness; it does not require light directly but depends on ATP and NADPH produced during the light reaction.
- Confusing **RuBP** (CO₂ acceptor in C₃ plants) with **PEP** (CO₂ acceptor in C₄ plants).
- Forgetting that the first stable product of the **Calvin cycle** is **3-PGA**, whereas the first stable product of the **C₄ pathway** is **OAA**.
- Mixing up **photorespiration** with **photosynthesis**.
- Assuming all plants possess **Kranz anatomy**; it is characteristic of **C₄ plants**.

# Chapter Summary

- **Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants convert light energy into chemical energy, producing carbohydrates from carbon dioxide and water.
- The process occurs in **chloroplasts**, where **light reactions** take place in the **grana** and the **Calvin cycle** occurs in the **stroma**.
- **Chlorophyll a** is the primary photosynthetic pigment, while chlorophyll b, carotenoids, and xanthophylls function as accessory pigments.
- During the **light reaction**, water undergoes **photolysis**, ATP and NADPH are synthesized, and oxygen is released.
- **Photophosphorylation** may be **cyclic** or **non-cyclic**, depending on the participation of the photosystems.
- The **Calvin cycle (C₃ pathway)** fixes carbon dioxide using **RuBP**, whereas the **C₄ pathway** initially fixes carbon dioxide using **PEP**, reducing photorespiration and improving efficiency.
- **CAM plants** open their stomata at night to conserve water in dry environments.
- **Photorespiration** decreases photosynthetic efficiency and is much more pronounced in C₃ plants than in C₄ plants.
- Environmental factors such as light, carbon dioxide concentration, temperature, and water availability, along with internal plant factors, regulate the rate of photosynthesis according to **Blackman's Law of Limiting Factors**.
