# Introduction

The nucleus is the central part of an atom containing:

- Protons
- Neutrons

Together, they are called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Nucleons}}
\]

---

# 1. Composition of Nucleus

A nucleus is represented as:

\[
\boxed{
{}^A_ZX
}
\]

where:

- X = element symbol
- Z = atomic number (number of protons)
- A = mass number

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
A=Z+N
}
\]

where:

N = number of neutrons

---

# 2. Nuclear Size

The radius of nucleus is given by:

\[
\boxed{
R=R_0A^{1/3}
}
\]

where:

\[
R_0=1.2\times10^{-15}m
\]

---

## Important Observation

Nuclear radius depends on mass number, not atomic number.

---

# 3. Nuclear Density

Density:

\[
\rho=\frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Volume}}
\]

Since:

\[
R\propto A^{1/3}
\]

Volume:

\[
V\propto A
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\rho=\text{constant}}
\]

---

# 4. Nuclear Force

## Definition

The strong attractive force between nucleons is called nuclear force.

---

# Properties of Nuclear Force

1. Very strong force.
2. Short-range force.

Range:

\[
10^{-15}m
\]

3. Independent of charge.
4. Saturation property.
5. Attractive at normal distances.

---

# 5. Mass Defect

## Definition

The difference between the sum of masses of individual nucleons and actual mass of nucleus is called mass defect.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta m=Zm_p+Nm_n-M
}
\]

where:

- mp = proton mass
- mn = neutron mass
- M = nuclear mass

---

# 6. Binding Energy

## Definition

The energy required to separate a nucleus completely into its nucleons is called binding energy.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
BE=\Delta mc^2
}
\]

---

Using atomic mass unit:

\[
\boxed{
1u=931.5MeV
}
\]

---

# 7. Binding Energy Per Nucleon

\[
\boxed{
\frac{BE}{A}
}
\]

It represents nuclear stability.

---

# Binding Energy Curve

The graph of binding energy per nucleon versus mass number.

---

## Important Features

### Light nuclei

Low binding energy.

Example:

Hydrogen

---

### Medium nuclei

Maximum binding energy.

Around:

\[
A\approx56
\]

Iron is most stable.

---

### Heavy nuclei

Binding energy decreases.

---

# 8. Radioactivity

## Definition

Spontaneous disintegration of unstable nuclei with emission of radiation is called radioactivity.

Discovered by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Henri Becquerel}}
\]

---

# Types of Radioactive Radiation

---

# A. Alpha Decay

Emission of helium nucleus.

\[
{}^A_ZX
\rightarrow
{}^{A-4}_{Z-2}Y +
{}^4_2He
\]

Properties:

- Positive charge
- Low penetration
- High ionisation

---

# B. Beta Decay

Emission of electron.

\[
n\rightarrow p+e^-+\bar\nu
\]

Properties:

- Moderate penetration

---

# C. Gamma Decay

Emission of high-energy photons.

Properties:

- No charge
- Highest penetration

---

# 9. Radioactive Decay Law

Rate of decay is proportional to number of undecayed nuclei.

\[
\boxed{
\frac{dN}{dt}=-\lambda N
}
\]

---

Solution:

\[
\boxed{
N=N_0e^{-\lambda t}
}
\]

where:

- N₀ = initial nuclei
- N = remaining nuclei
- λ = decay constant

---

# 10. Half-Life

## Definition

Time required for half of the radioactive nuclei to decay.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
T_{1/2}=\frac{0.693}{\lambda}
}
\]

---

# Mean Life

\[
\boxed{
\tau=\frac1\lambda
}
\]

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
\tau=1.44T_{1/2}
}
\]

---

# 11. Nuclear Fission

## Definition

The splitting of a heavy nucleus into two lighter nuclei with release of energy is called nuclear fission.

---

Example:

\[
{}^{235}_{92}U
\]

---

Reaction:

\[
{}^{235}_{92}U+n
\rightarrow
{}^{141}_{56}Ba +
{}^{92}_{36}Kr +
3n
+\text{Energy}
\]

---

# Chain Reaction

Neutrons released in one fission cause further fissions.

---

# Applications

- Nuclear reactors
- Atomic bombs

---

# 12. Nuclear Reactor

## Principle

Controlled nuclear fission reaction.

---

# Main Parts

## 1. Fuel

Example:

Uranium-235

---

## 2. Moderator

Slows down neutrons.

Examples:

- Heavy water
- Graphite

---

## 3. Control Rods

Control reaction rate.

Example:

Cadmium

---

## 4. Coolant

Removes heat.

---

# 13. Nuclear Fusion

## Definition

Combining light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus with release of energy.

---

Example:

Hydrogen fusion in the Sun.

\[
{}^2H+{}^3H
\rightarrow
{}^4He+n+Energy
\]

---

# Conditions for Fusion

1. Extremely high temperature.
2. High pressure.
3. Sufficient confinement.

---

# 14. Fission vs Fusion

| Nuclear Fission         | Nuclear Fusion                 |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Heavy nucleus splits    | Light nuclei combine           |
| Uses uranium            | Uses hydrogen isotopes         |
| Used in reactors        | Source of solar energy         |
| Chain reaction possible | Requires very high temperature |

---

# 15. Nuclear Energy

Energy released in nuclear reactions comes from conversion of mass into energy.

Einstein's equation:

\[
\boxed{
E=mc^2
}
\]

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** Find half-life if decay constant is \(0.002s^{-1}\).

Formula:

\[
T_{1/2}=\frac{0.693}{\lambda}
\]

\[
=\frac{0.693}{0.002}
\]

\[
\boxed{T_{1/2}=346.5s}
\]

---

## Example 2

**Question:** Why does iron have maximum stability?

### Solution

Iron has the highest binding energy per nucleon, making its nucleus highly stable.

---

## Example 3

**Question:** What is mass defect?

### Solution

The difference between the mass of separate nucleons and the actual nuclear mass is called mass defect.

---

## Example 4

**Question:** Why is fusion difficult on Earth?

### Solution

Fusion requires extremely high temperature and pressure to overcome repulsive forces between nuclei.

---

## Example 5

**Question:** Why does nuclear fission release energy?

### Solution

The products have greater binding energy per nucleon than the original heavy nucleus, and the mass difference appears as energy.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing atomic number and mass number.
- Forgetting that binding energy is positive.
- Mixing fission and fusion processes.
- Forgetting decay constant units.
- Confusing half-life and mean life formulas.
- Using incorrect mass defect formula.
- Forgetting that gamma rays have no charge.
- Confusing nuclear force with electromagnetic force.

---

# Chapter Summary

- The nucleus contains protons and neutrons.
- Nuclear radius follows:

\[
R=R_0A^{1/3}
\]

- Nuclear force is strong, short-range, and charge independent.
- Mass defect produces binding energy:

\[
BE=\Delta mc^2
\]

- Radioactivity follows exponential decay law.
- Half-life is related to decay constant:

\[
T_{1/2}=\frac{0.693}{\lambda}
\]

- Fission releases energy by splitting heavy nuclei.
- Fusion releases energy by combining light nuclei.
- The Sun produces energy through nuclear fusion.
