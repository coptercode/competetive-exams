# Introduction

Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force called **gravitational force**. This force keeps the planets revolving around the Sun, the Moon revolving around the Earth, and causes objects to fall towards the Earth.

The study of gravitation explains the **Universal Law of Gravitation**, **acceleration due to gravity**, **mass and weight**, **free fall**, **thrust**, **pressure**, **buoyancy**, and **Archimedes' Principle**.

---

# 1. Gravitation

**Gravitation** is the force of attraction between any two objects having mass.

### Examples

- Fruits falling from trees.
- Planets revolving around the Sun.
- Moon revolving around the Earth.
- Ocean tides caused by the Moon.

---

# 2. Universal Law of Gravitation

Sir **Isaac Newton** proposed the **Universal Law of Gravitation**.

### Statement

> **Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres.**

### Formula

```
        Gm₁m₂
F = ------------
         r²
```

where:

- **F** = Gravitational force
- **G** = Universal gravitational constant
- **m₁, m₂** = Masses of the two objects
- **r** = Distance between their centres

### SI Unit of Force

```
newton (N)
```

---

# 3. Universal Gravitational Constant (G)

The **universal gravitational constant** is represented by **G**.

### Value

```
G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg²
```

### Characteristics

- Constant throughout the universe.
- Independent of the nature of objects.

---

# 4. Importance of Gravitation

Gravitation is responsible for:

- Falling of objects towards the Earth.
- Keeping planets in their orbits.
- Keeping satellites in orbit.
- Ocean tides.
- Formation of stars and galaxies.

---

# 5. Free Fall

When an object falls towards the Earth under the influence of gravity alone, it is said to be in **free fall**.

In free fall:

- Air resistance is neglected.
- Acceleration remains constant.

---

# 6. Acceleration Due to Gravity (g)

The acceleration produced in a freely falling object due to Earth's gravity is called **acceleration due to gravity**.

### Symbol

```
g
```

### Average Value

```
9.8 m/s²
```

### Characteristics

- Acts vertically downward.
- Same for all freely falling objects (ignoring air resistance).

---

# 7. Variation in the Value of g

The value of **g** changes under different conditions.

### With Altitude

As altitude increases,

```
g decreases.
```

---

### With Depth

As depth below the Earth's surface increases,

```
g decreases.
```

---

### At the Equator and Poles

- Maximum at the poles.
- Minimum at the equator.

This is due to the Earth's rotation and its slightly flattened shape.

---

# 8. Equations of Motion for Freely Falling Bodies

For objects moving under gravity, the equations of motion become:

### First Equation

```
v = u + gt
```

---

### Second Equation

```
s = ut + ½gt²
```

---

### Third Equation

```
v² = u² + 2gs
```

For upward motion, **g** is taken as **negative** because gravity acts downward.

---

# 9. Mass

**Mass** is the quantity of matter contained in an object.

### Characteristics

- Scalar quantity.
- Constant everywhere.
- Independent of gravity.

### SI Unit

```
kilogram (kg)
```

---

# 10. Weight

**Weight** is the gravitational force acting on an object.

### Formula

```
Weight = Mass × g
```

```
W = mg
```

### SI Unit

```
newton (N)
```

---

# 11. Difference Between Mass and Weight

| Mass                          | Weight                          |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Quantity of matter            | Force due to gravity            |
| Constant everywhere           | Changes with location           |
| SI unit: kilogram (kg)        | SI unit: newton (N)             |
| Scalar quantity               | Vector quantity                 |
| Measured using a beam balance | Measured using a spring balance |

---

# 12. Thrust

**Thrust** is the force acting **perpendicular** to a surface.

### SI Unit

```
newton (N)
```

### Examples

- A person standing on the ground.
- A book placed on a table.

---

# 13. Pressure

**Pressure** is the thrust acting per unit area.

### Formula

```
Pressure = Thrust / Area
```

```
P = F / A
```

### SI Unit

```
pascal (Pa)
```

where

```
1 Pa = 1 N/m²
```

### Applications

- Sharp knives cut easily.
- Broad straps of school bags reduce pressure.
- Buildings have wide foundations.

---

# 14. Buoyancy

When an object is immersed in a fluid, the fluid exerts an upward force on it.

This upward force is called **buoyant force** or **upthrust**.

### Factors Affecting Buoyant Force

- Density of the fluid.
- Volume of the immersed object.
- Acceleration due to gravity.

---

# 15. Archimedes' Principle

Archimedes stated:

> **When a body is partially or completely immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.**

### Applications

- Designing ships.
- Hydrometers.
- Submarines.
- Lactometers.

---

# 16. Relative Density

**Relative density** is the ratio of the density of a substance to the density of water at **4°C**.

### Formula

```
Relative Density
=
Density of Substance
---------------------
Density of Water
```

Relative density has **no unit**.

---

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Calculate the weight of a body of mass **10 kg**.

Take

```
g = 9.8 m/s²
```

**Solution**

```
W = mg
```

```
= 10 × 9.8
```

```
= 98 N
```

---

### Example 2

A freely falling object starts from rest.

Find its velocity after **5 s**.

Take

```
g = 9.8 m/s²
```

**Solution**

```
u = 0
```

Using

```
v = u + gt
```

```
= 0 + 9.8 × 5
```

```
= 49 m/s
```

---

### Example 3

A force of **200 N** acts on an area of **4 m²**.

Find the pressure.

**Solution**

```
P = F / A
```

```
= 200 / 4
```

```
= 50 Pa
```

---

### Example 4

Why do ships made of steel float on water?

**Solution**

Ships are designed to displace a large volume of water. The buoyant force acting on them becomes equal to or greater than their weight, allowing them to float.

---

### Example 5

State one application of Archimedes' Principle.

**Solution**

Archimedes' Principle is used in the design of **ships and submarines**.

---

### Example 6

Why is the weight of an object less on the Moon than on the Earth?

**Solution**

The Moon has much weaker gravitational attraction than the Earth. Therefore, the value of **g** is smaller, making the object's weight less.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **mass** with **weight**.
- Using **g = 9.8 m/s²** with incorrect units.
- Forgetting that **weight** is measured in **newtons**, not kilograms.
- Confusing **thrust** with **pressure**.
- Assuming mass changes from place to place.
- Ignoring the direction of gravity in upward motion problems.
- Mixing up **density** and **relative density**.

# Chapter Summary

- **Gravitation** is the force of attraction between any two masses.
- Newton's **Universal Law of Gravitation** explains the gravitational attraction between objects.
- The **universal gravitational constant (G)** has a fixed value throughout the universe.
- A freely falling object experiences a constant **acceleration due to gravity (g)**, approximately **9.8 m/s²** near the Earth's surface.
- The value of **g** decreases with increasing altitude and depth and is slightly greater at the poles than at the equator.
- **Mass** is the quantity of matter in an object, while **weight** is the gravitational force acting on it.
- **Thrust** is the force acting perpendicular to a surface, and **pressure** is thrust per unit area.
- **Buoyancy** is the upward force exerted by a fluid on an immersed object.
- **Archimedes' Principle** states that the buoyant force on an object is equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.
- Gravitation explains many natural phenomena, including planetary motion, tides, free fall, floating of objects, and satellite motion.
