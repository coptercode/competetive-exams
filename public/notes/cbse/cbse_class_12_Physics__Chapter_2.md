# Introduction

When a charge is placed in an electric field, work is required to move it. This work done per unit charge is called **electric potential**.

---

# 1. Electric Potential

## Definition

Electric potential at a point is defined as the work done in bringing a unit positive test charge from infinity to that point.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
V=\frac{W}{q}
}
\]

where:

- V = electric potential
- W = work done
- q = test charge

---

## SI Unit

\[
\boxed{\text{Volt (V)}}
\]

\[
1V=1JC^{-1}
\]

---

# 2. Potential Difference

## Definition

The potential difference between two points is the work done per unit charge in moving a charge from one point to another.

\[
\boxed{
V_B-V_A=\frac{W}{q}
}
\]

---

# 3. Potential Due to a Point Charge

Consider a charge Q at distance r.

Potential:

\[
\boxed{
V=
\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\frac{Q}{r}
}
\]

---

## Important Points

- Potential is a scalar quantity.
- It can be positive or negative.
- Potential decreases with distance from a positive charge.

---

# 4. Potential Due to a System of Charges

For charges:

\[
q_1,q_2,q_3...
\]

Total potential:

\[
\boxed{
V=
\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\left(
\frac{q_1}{r_1} +
\frac{q_2}{r_2} +
...
\right)
}
\]

---

# 5. Relation Between Electric Field and Potential

Electric field is the negative gradient of potential.

\[
\boxed{
E=-\frac{dV}{dr}
}
\]

---

Meaning:

- Electric field points from high potential to low potential.
- Potential decreases in direction of electric field.

---

# 6. Potential Energy

## Definition

Energy possessed by a charge due to its position in an electric field.

---

## Potential Energy of Two Charges

For charges:

\[
q_1,q_2
\]

separated by distance r:

\[
\boxed{
U=
\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\frac{q_1q_2}{r}
}
\]

---

# 7. Potential Energy of a System of Charges

\[
\boxed{
U=
\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\sum\frac{q_iq_j}{r_{ij}}
}
\]

---

# 8. Equipotential Surfaces

## Definition

A surface where electric potential is the same at every point is called an equipotential surface.

---

# Properties

1. No work is done in moving a charge on an equipotential surface.

\[
W=q\Delta V
\]

Since:

\[
\Delta V=0
\]

\[
W=0
\]

---

2. Electric field is always perpendicular to an equipotential surface.

---

3. Two equipotential surfaces never intersect.

---

# Examples

## Point Charge

Equipotential surfaces are:

```
Concentric spheres
```

---

## Uniform Electric Field

Equipotential surfaces are:

```
Parallel planes
```

---

# 9. Conductors in Electrostatic Equilibrium

## Properties

---

## 1. Electric Field Inside Conductor

\[
\boxed{E=0}
\]

---

## 2. Potential Inside Conductor

Potential remains constant.

---

## 3. Charge Distribution

Charge resides only on the outer surface.

---

## 4. Electric Field at Surface

\[
\boxed{
E=\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}
}
\]

where:

σ = surface charge density

---

# 10. Capacitance

## Definition

The ability of a conductor to store electric charge is called capacitance.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
C=\frac{Q}{V}
}
\]

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{\text{Farad (F)}}
\]

---

# 11. Parallel Plate Capacitor

A capacitor consists of two parallel conducting plates separated by an insulating material.

---

Capacitance:

\[
\boxed{
C=\frac{\epsilon_0A}{d}
}
\]

where:

- A = area of plates
- d = separation

---

# 12. Effect of Dielectric

A dielectric is an insulating material placed between capacitor plates.

Examples:

- Glass
- Mica
- Plastic

---

Dielectric constant:

\[
\boxed{
K=\frac{C}{C_0}
}
\]

---

New capacitance:

\[
\boxed{
C=K C_0
}
\]

---

# 13. Polarisation of Dielectric

When dielectric is placed in an electric field:

- Molecules become polarized.
- Bound charges shift slightly.
- Electric field reduces.

---

# 14. Combination of Capacitors

---

# A. Capacitors in Parallel

Diagram:

```
 |--C1--|
 |--C2--|
 |--C3--|
```

---

Voltage is same:

\[
V=V_1=V_2=V_3
\]

Equivalent capacitance:

\[
\boxed{
C=C_1+C_2+C_3
}
\]

---

# B. Capacitors in Series

Diagram:

```
--C1--C2--C3--
```

---

Charge is same:

\[
Q=Q_1=Q_2=Q_3
\]

Equivalent capacitance:

\[
\boxed{
\frac1C=
\frac1{C_1} +
\frac1{C_2} +
\frac1{C_3}
}
\]

---

# 15. Energy Stored in Capacitor

Energy stored:

\[
\boxed{
U=\frac12CV^2
}
\]

---

Other forms:

\[
\boxed{
U=\frac{Q^2}{2C}
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
U=\frac12QV
}
\]

---

# 16. Energy Density

Energy stored per unit volume:

\[
\boxed{
u=
\frac12\epsilon_0E^2
}
\]

---

With dielectric:

\[
\boxed{
u=
\frac12K\epsilon_0E^2
}
\]

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** Find potential at 2 m from a charge of 4 μC.

Given:

\[
Q=4\times10^{-6}C
\]

\[
r=2m
\]

Formula:

\[
V=
9\times10^9
\frac{4\times10^{-6}}{2}
\]

\[
\boxed{V=18000V}
\]

---

## Example 2

**Question:** A capacitor has charge 5 μC and potential 10 V. Find capacitance.

\[
C=\frac{Q}{V}
\]

\[
=\frac{5\times10^{-6}}{10}
\]

\[
\boxed{C=0.5\mu F}
\]

---

## Example 3

**Question:** Why is electric field zero inside a conductor?

### Solution

If an electric field existed inside a conductor, free electrons would move. In electrostatic equilibrium, charges stop moving; therefore the internal electric field must be zero.

---

## Example 4

**Question:** What happens when a dielectric is inserted between capacitor plates?

### Solution

The dielectric reduces electric field and increases capacitance by a factor equal to its dielectric constant.

\[
C=KC_0
\]

---

## Example 5

**Question:** Calculate energy stored in a 2 μF capacitor charged to 100 V.

\[
U=\frac12CV^2
\]

\[
=\frac12(2\times10^{-6})(100)^2
\]

\[
\boxed{U=0.01J}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing electric potential with electric field.
- Forgetting that potential is a scalar quantity.
- Using vector addition for potentials.
- Forgetting that electric field is zero inside a conductor at electrostatic equilibrium.
- Mixing capacitor series and parallel formulas.
- Forgetting dielectric increases capacitance.
- Using incorrect units while calculating capacitance and energy.
- Forgetting that no work is done on an equipotential surface.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Electric potential describes the work required to move a unit charge.
- Potential due to a point charge depends inversely on distance.
- Equipotential surfaces have constant potential and require no work for charge movement.
- Conductors in electrostatic equilibrium have zero electric field inside.
- Capacitors store electrical energy by accumulating charges on plates.
- Capacitance depends on plate area, separation, and dielectric material.
- Capacitors can be connected in series or parallel.
- Dielectrics increase capacitance through polarization.
- Energy stored in a capacitor is given by:

\[
U=\frac12CV^2
\]

- Van de Graaff generator is an important application of electrostatic principles.
