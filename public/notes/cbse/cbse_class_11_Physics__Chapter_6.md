# Introduction

Many physical bodies, such as wheels, fans, planets, and machines, not only move from one place to another but also **rotate** about an axis. To study such motion, it is necessary to understand the concepts of **centre of mass**, **torque**, **angular momentum**, and **rotational dynamics**.

Rotational motion is the rotational counterpart of translational motion, where quantities like force, momentum, and mass are replaced by torque, angular momentum, and moment of inertia.

---

# 1. System of Particles

## Definition

A **system of particles** is a collection of two or more particles considered together for analyzing their motion.

Examples

- Solar system
- Molecules
- Rigid bodies
- Vehicles

---

# 2. Centre of Mass (COM)

## Definition

The **centre of mass** is the point at which the entire mass of a system may be considered to be concentrated for studying its translational motion.

---

## Centre of Mass of Two Particles

For masses

```
m₁

and

m₂
```

located at

```
x₁

and

x₂
```

the centre of mass is

```
xCM

=

(m₁x₁ + m₂x₂)

────────────────

(m₁ + m₂)
```

---

## Vector Form

```
rCM

=

(m₁r₁ + m₂r₂ + ...)

───────────────────

Σm
```

---

## Special Cases

### Equal Masses

The centre of mass lies midway between them.

---

### Symmetrical Bodies

The centre of mass lies at the geometrical centre.

Examples

- Uniform rod
- Disc
- Sphere
- Ring

---

# 3. Motion of the Centre of Mass

The motion of the centre of mass follows Newton's Second Law.

```
Fext

=

M aCM
```

where

- **Fext** = Net external force
- **M** = Total mass
- **aCM** = Acceleration of the centre of mass

# 11. Torque (Moment of Force)

## Definition

Torque is the turning effect of a force about a fixed axis.

---

## Formula

Vector form

```
τ = r × F
```

Magnitude

```
τ

=

rF sinθ
```

---

## SI Unit

```
N m
```

---

## Dimensional Formula

```
[ML²T⁻²]
```

---

## Direction

Given by the **Right-Hand Thumb Rule**.

---

# 12. Couple

## Definition

A couple consists of two equal and opposite parallel forces separated by a perpendicular distance.

---

## Moment of a Couple

```

=

Fd
```

where

```

```

is the perpendicular distance.

---

## Characteristics

- Produces rotation only.
- Net force is zero.
- Torque is non-zero.

## Statement

If the external torque acting on a system is zero,


| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing the **centre of mass** with the geometric centre; they coincide only for bodies with **uniform mass distribution**.
- Using **force** instead of **torque** while solving rotational problems.
- Forgetting that **torque depends on the perpendicular distance** from the axis of rotation.
- Assuming the **moment of inertia** depends only on mass; it also depends on the **distribution of mass** and the **axis of rotation**.
- Applying the **Perpendicular Axis Theorem** to three-dimensional bodies; it is valid only for **plane laminae**.
- Using the wrong standard formula for the **moment of inertia** of common objects such as discs, rings, and spheres.
- Forgetting that **rolling motion** consists of both **translational** and **rotational** kinetic energies.
- Ignoring the condition **v = rω** for pure rolling without slipping.

# Chapter Summary

- A **system of particles** can be described by its **centre of mass**, whose motion depends only on the net external force.
- **Linear momentum** of an isolated system remains conserved in the absence of external forces.
- **Rotational motion** is characterized by **angular displacement**, **angular velocity**, and **angular acceleration**, analogous to translational motion.
- **Torque** is the turning effect of a force and is the rotational counterpart of force.
- **Angular momentum** is conserved when no external torque acts on a system.
- A rigid body is in equilibrium when both the **net external force** and the **net external torque** are zero.
- The **moment of inertia** measures a body's resistance to rotational motion and depends on the mass distribution relative to the axis of rotation.
- The **Parallel Axis Theorem** and **Perpendicular Axis Theorem** simplify the calculation of moments of inertia for different axes.
- During **rolling motion**, both translational and rotational kinetic energies contribute to the total kinetic energy.
- The principles of rotational motion are essential for understanding the motion of machines, vehicles, satellites, planets, and many engineering systems.
