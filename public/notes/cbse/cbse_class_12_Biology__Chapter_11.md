# Introduction

Every organism is influenced by its surrounding environment. The study of relationships between organisms and their environment is called **ecology**.

Ecology is studied at different levels:

```
Organism

↓

Population

↓

Community

↓

Ecosystem

↓

Biosphere
```

# 1. Organisms and Their Environment

## Environment

The environment consists of:

### A. Abiotic Components

Non-living factors:

- Temperature
- Water
- Light
- Soil
- Humidity

---

### B. Biotic Components

Living organisms:

- Plants
- Animals
- Microorganisms

# 2. Major Abiotic Factors

---

# A. Temperature

Temperature affects:

- Enzyme activity
- Metabolism
- Growth
- Reproduction

---

## Thermal Adaptations

### Eurythermal

Can tolerate a wide range of temperatures.

Example:

- Humans

---

### Stenothermal

Can tolerate only a narrow temperature range.

---

# B. Water

Water availability determines:

- Distribution of organisms
- Productivity
- Survival

---

## Aquatic Adaptations

Organisms may regulate:

- Salt concentration
- Water balance

---

# C. Light

Important for:

- Photosynthesis
- Reproduction
- Biological rhythms

---

## Examples

Plants respond to light through:

- Photoperiodism
- Flowering responses

---

# D. Soil

Soil characteristics affect:

- Plant growth
- Microbial activity
- Animal distribution

Factors:

- pH
- Mineral content
- Texture
- Water-holding capacity

---

# 3. Responses of Organisms to Abiotic Factors

Organisms respond in four major ways:

---

# A. Regulation

Organisms maintain internal conditions despite external changes.

Example:

- Humans maintain body temperature.

---

# B. Conformation

Internal conditions change according to surroundings.

Example:

- Many aquatic animals.

---

# C. Migration

Movement to favorable environments.

Example:

- Birds migrating during winter.

---

# D. Suspension

Temporary reduction of metabolic activity.

Examples:

- Hibernation
- Aestivation
- Diapause

---

# 5. Population

## Definition

A group of individuals of the same species living in a particular area at a particular time.

---

# Population Attributes

Individual organisms do not have these properties, but populations do.

---

## A. Birth Rate (Natality)

Number of births per individual per unit time.

Formula:

```
Natality = Number of births / Total population
```

---

## B. Death Rate (Mortality)

Number of deaths per individual per unit time.

Formula:

```
Mortality = Number of deaths / Total population
```

---

## C. Sex Ratio

Ratio of males to females in a population.

---

## D. Age Structure

Population consists of:

- Pre-reproductive individuals
- Reproductive individuals
- Post-reproductive individuals

---

# 7. Population Growth

Population size changes due to:

- Births
- Deaths
- Immigration
- Emigration

---

## Formula

```
N = Population size
```

Change:

```
dN/dt = (Births + Immigration) - (Deaths + Emigration)
```

---

# 8. Population Growth Models

---

# A. Exponential Growth

Occurs when resources are unlimited.

---

## Equation

```
dN/dt = rN
```

Where:

- N = Population size
- r = Intrinsic growth rate

---

## Curve

J-shaped growth curve.

```
Population

|

|        /
|      /
|    /
|___/________ Time
```

---

# B. Logistic Growth

Occurs when resources are limited.

---

## Equation

```
dN/dt = rN(K-N)/K
```

Where:

- K = Carrying capacity

---

## Curve

S-shaped growth curve.

```
Population

|

|      ______
|    /
|  /
|_/__________ Time
```

---

# 9. Carrying Capacity (K)

## Definition

Maximum population size that an environment can support sustainably.

---

# 10. Population Interactions

Organisms interact with each other for:

- Food
- Shelter
- Reproduction
- Survival

---

# Types of Interactions

## 1. Mutualism

Both species benefit.

Symbol:

```
(+/+)
```

---

## Examples

### Lichen

Algae + Fungus

- Algae provides food.
- Fungus provides shelter.

---

### Mycorrhiza

Fungus + Plant roots

Benefits:

- Better mineral absorption.
- Protection from pathogens.

---

# 2. Commensalism

One species benefits, the other is unaffected.

Symbol:

```
(+/0)
```

---

## Examples

### Orchid on Mango Tree

- Orchid gets support.
- Tree is unaffected.

---

### Cattle Egret and Grazing Animals

- Egret gets insects.
- Cattle unaffected.

---

# 3. Predation

One organism kills and consumes another.

Symbol:

```
(+/-)
```

---

## Examples

- Tiger eating deer.
- Ladybird feeding on aphids.

---

## Importance

Predation:

- Controls population size.
- Maintains ecosystem balance.
- Promotes natural selection.

---

# 4. Parasitism

One organism benefits while harming another.

Symbol:

```
(+/-)
```

---

## Types

### Ectoparasites

Live on host surface.

Example:

- Lice

---

### Endoparasites

Live inside host.

Example:

- Tapeworm

---

# 5. Competition

Occurs when organisms compete for limited resources.

Symbol:

```
(-/-)
```

---

## Examples

- Plants competing for sunlight.
- Animals competing for food.

---

# 6. Amensalism

One species is harmed, other remains unaffected.

Symbol:

```
(-/0)
```

Example:

- Antibiotic production by fungi affecting bacteria.

---

# 11. Gause's Competitive Exclusion Principle

Proposed by:

```
G.F. Gause
```

---

## Statement

Two species competing for exactly the same resources cannot coexist indefinitely.

---

## Result

One species will eliminate or replace the other.

---

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

**Question:** Define population.

**Solution:**

A population is a group of individuals of the same species living in a particular area at a particular time and capable of interbreeding.

---

### Example 2

**Question:** Differentiate between exponential and logistic growth.

**Solution:**

Exponential growth occurs when resources are unlimited and produces a J-shaped curve. Logistic growth occurs when resources are limited and produces an S-shaped curve with a carrying capacity.

---

### Example 3

**Question:** What is mutualism? Give an example.

**Solution:**

Mutualism is an interaction where both species benefit. Example: In lichen, algae provide food while fungi provide shelter and minerals.

---

### Example 4

**Question:** Explain Gause's competitive exclusion principle.

**Solution:**

It states that two species competing for exactly the same resources cannot coexist indefinitely. One species will eventually eliminate the other or occupy a different niche.

---

### Example 5

**Question:** Why is predation important in an ecosystem?

**Solution:**

Predation controls prey population, maintains ecological balance, and acts as a selective force that promotes evolution of adaptations.

---

### Example 6

**Question:** Write the logistic growth equation.

**Solution:**

The logistic growth equation is:

```
dN/dt = rN(K-N)/K
```

where:

- N = population size
- r = intrinsic growth rate
- K = carrying capacity

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing population attributes with individual characteristics; birth rate and sex ratio apply to populations, not individuals.
- Forgetting that logistic growth includes carrying capacity (**K**).
- Mixing up mutualism and commensalism; mutualism benefits both organisms, while commensalism benefits only one.
- Confusing predation and parasitism; predators usually kill prey immediately, whereas parasites depend on hosts for longer periods.
- Assuming competition occurs only between different species; it can also occur within the same species.
- Forgetting that evolution occurs through population changes, not changes in individual organisms.
- Mixing up immigration and emigration; immigration adds individuals, while emigration removes individuals.
- Assuming all adaptations occur during an individual's lifetime; adaptations are inherited traits developed over generations.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Organisms interact continuously with abiotic and biotic components of their environment.
- Abiotic factors such as temperature, water, light, and soil influence the distribution and survival of organisms.
- Organisms respond through regulation, conformity, migration, or suspension of activities.
- Adaptations help organisms survive under specific environmental conditions.
- Populations are characterized by birth rate, death rate, sex ratio, age structure, and population density.
- Population growth may be exponential (unlimited resources) or logistic (limited resources with carrying capacity).
- Organisms interact through mutualism, commensalism, predation, parasitism, competition, and amensalism.
- The **Hardy-Weinberg principle** and evolutionary concepts explain population-level changes over time.
- Understanding organism–environment relationships helps explain biodiversity, ecological balance, and survival strategies.
