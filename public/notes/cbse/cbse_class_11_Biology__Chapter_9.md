# Introduction

Living organisms are composed of a variety of **chemical substances** known as **biomolecules**. These molecules form the structural framework of cells and participate in all metabolic activities. Biomolecules include **carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, nucleic acids, vitamins, enzymes**, and other organic compounds.

This chapter explains the chemical composition of living cells, classification and functions of biomolecules, enzyme action, and factors affecting enzyme activity.

---

# 2. Inorganic Compounds

## Water

Water is the most abundant component of living cells.

### Functions

- Universal solvent.
- Medium for biochemical reactions.
- Maintains body temperature.
- Transport of substances.
- Maintains cell turgidity in plants.

---

## Minerals

Minerals are inorganic elements required in small quantities.

### Functions

- Formation of bones and teeth.
- Enzyme activation.
- Maintenance of osmotic balance.
- Formation of chlorophyll (Mg).
- Formation of haemoglobin (Fe).

# 5. Proteins

Proteins are polymers of amino acids.

---

## Amino Acids

Basic building blocks of proteins.

General structure

```
      H
      │
H₂N—C—COOH
      │
      R
```

Where **R** represents the side chain.

---

## Peptide Bond

A peptide bond is formed between the amino group of one amino acid and the carboxyl group of another.

---

## Levels of Protein Structure

### Primary Structure

Linear sequence of amino acids.

---

### Secondary Structure

Regular folding into:

- α-helix
- β-pleated sheet

---

### Tertiary Structure

Three-dimensional folding of a single polypeptide.

---

### Quaternary Structure

Association of two or more polypeptide chains.

Example

- Haemoglobin

---

## Functions of Proteins

- Enzymes
- Hormones
- Structural support
- Transport
- Defence (antibodies)
- Muscle contraction

# 11. Mechanism of Enzyme Action

According to the **Lock and Key Model** proposed by **Emil Fischer**, the active site of the enzyme has a specific shape that exactly fits the substrate.

```
Enzyme + Substrate
        │
Enzyme-Substrate Complex
        │
 Enzyme + Product
```

The **Induced Fit Model** (proposed by **Daniel Koshland**) states that the active site changes shape slightly to fit the substrate more effectively.

---

# 12. Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity

### Temperature

- Activity increases up to the optimum temperature.
- Very high temperatures denature enzymes.

---

### pH

Each enzyme has an optimum pH.

Example

- Pepsin → acidic pH
- Trypsin → alkaline pH

---

### Substrate Concentration

Increasing substrate concentration increases enzyme activity until all active sites become saturated.

---

### Enzyme Concentration

Higher enzyme concentration generally increases the reaction rate if sufficient substrate is available.

---

### Inhibitors

Certain chemicals reduce or stop enzyme activity by interfering with enzyme function.

---

# 13. Denaturation of Proteins

Denaturation is the loss of the natural three-dimensional structure of proteins due to:

- High temperature
- Extreme pH
- Chemicals
- Heavy metals

Denatured proteins generally lose their biological activity.

---

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Differentiate between monosaccharides and polysaccharides.

**Solution**

- **Monosaccharides** are simple sugars consisting of a single sugar unit (e.g., glucose).
- **Polysaccharides** are complex carbohydrates made of many monosaccharide units (e.g., starch, cellulose).

---

### Example 2

State the function of glycogen.

**Solution**

Glycogen serves as the primary storage form of carbohydrates in animals, especially in the liver and muscles, and provides energy when required.

---

### Example 3

What is a peptide bond?

**Solution**

A peptide bond is a covalent bond formed between the amino group of one amino acid and the carboxyl group of another amino acid during protein synthesis.

---

### Example 4

Differentiate between DNA and RNA.

**Solution**

DNA is double-stranded, contains deoxyribose sugar and thymine, and stores genetic information. RNA is usually single-stranded, contains ribose sugar and uracil, and mainly participates in protein synthesis.

---

### Example 5

What is the role of enzymes in living organisms?

**Solution**

Enzymes act as biological catalysts that accelerate biochemical reactions without being consumed in the process.

---

### Example 6

What happens when a protein is denatured?

**Solution**

During denaturation, the protein loses its native three-dimensional structure due to factors such as high temperature or extreme pH, resulting in the loss of its biological activity.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **starch** with **glycogen** as storage carbohydrates.
- Assuming all lipids are polymers; many lipids are not true polymers like proteins or nucleic acids.
- Mixing up **DNA** and **RNA** bases (thymine vs uracil).
- Forgetting that **enzymes are mostly proteins**, though some RNA molecules (ribozymes) also have catalytic activity.
- Confusing **apoenzyme** with **cofactor**.
- Assuming denaturation breaks peptide bonds; it mainly disrupts the secondary, tertiary, or quaternary structure while the primary structure usually remains intact.
- Mixing up **primary** and **secondary metabolites**.
- Forgetting the correct base pairing in DNA: **A–T** and **G–C**.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Biomolecules are the chemical substances that constitute living organisms and are classified into **inorganic** (water, minerals, gases) and **organic** (carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids) compounds.
- **Carbohydrates** serve as the primary energy source and occur as monosaccharides, disaccharides, and polysaccharides such as starch, glycogen, cellulose, and chitin.
- **Proteins** are polymers of amino acids linked by peptide bonds and exhibit four levels of structural organization: primary, secondary, tertiary, and quaternary.
- **Lipids** function in energy storage, membrane formation, insulation, and hormone synthesis.
- **Nucleic acids** (DNA and RNA) store, transmit, and express genetic information through nucleotides.
- **Enzymes** are biological catalysts that increase the rate of biochemical reactions and function according to substrate specificity, with their activity influenced by temperature, pH, substrate concentration, enzyme concentration, and inhibitors.
- Proteins may undergo **denaturation**, resulting in the loss of their functional three-dimensional structure.
- **Primary metabolites** are essential for growth, whereas **secondary metabolites** play important ecological and defensive roles.
- The study of biomolecules provides the molecular basis for understanding metabolism, genetics, physiology, biotechnology, and all life processes.
