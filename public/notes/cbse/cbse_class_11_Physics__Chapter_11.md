# Introduction

**Thermodynamics** is the branch of physics that deals with the relationship between **heat**, **work**, and **energy**. It studies the transfer of energy and the laws governing physical systems in thermal equilibrium.

Thermodynamics has applications in engines, refrigerators, air conditioners, power plants, and many industrial processes.

---

# 1. Thermodynamic System

## Definition

A **thermodynamic system** is a specified quantity of matter or a region in space chosen for study.

Everything outside the system is called the **surroundings**.

---

# 2. Types of Thermodynamic Systems

---

## (A) Open System

Exchanges both **matter** and **energy** with the surroundings.

Examples

- Boiling water in an open vessel
- Human body

---

## (B) Closed System

Exchanges **energy** but not **matter**.

Examples

- Gas enclosed in a cylinder with a movable piston

---

## (C) Isolated System

Exchanges neither **matter** nor **energy**.

Examples

- Ideal thermos flask
- The universe (approximately)

---

# 3. Thermodynamic Variables

Variables that describe the state of a system.

Examples

- Pressure (P)
- Volume (V)
- Temperature (T)
- Internal Energy (U)

These are called **state variables** because they depend only on the state of the system.

---

# 4. Thermodynamic Equilibrium

A system is said to be in thermodynamic equilibrium when it is simultaneously in:

- Thermal equilibrium
- Mechanical equilibrium
- Chemical equilibrium

---

# 5. Thermal Equilibrium

Two bodies are in thermal equilibrium if there is **no net flow of heat** between them.

---

# 6. Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics

## Statement

If two systems are separately in thermal equilibrium with a third system, then they are in thermal equilibrium with each other.

---

## Importance

- Defines temperature.
- Forms the basis of thermometry.

---

# 7. Internal Energy

## Definition

Internal energy is the total microscopic kinetic and potential energy of the molecules of a system.

Symbol

```
U
```

---

## Characteristics

- State function
- Depends only on the state of the system
- SI unit: Joule (J)

---

# 8. Heat

## Definition

Heat is the energy transferred between systems because of a temperature difference.

Symbol

```
Q
```

---

## Sign Convention

```
Q > 0
```

Heat supplied to the system.

```
Q < 0
```

Heat released by the system.

---

# 9. Work

## Definition

Work is done when the system expands or contracts under external pressure.

For a small change,

```
dW = PdV
```

For constant pressure,

```
W = PΔV
```

---

## Sign Convention

```
W > 0
```

Work done **by** the system.

```
W < 0
```

Work done **on** the system.

---

# 10. First Law of Thermodynamics

## Statement

The heat supplied to a system is used to increase its internal energy and to do external work.

---

## Mathematical Form

```
Q = ΔU + W
```

or

```
ΔU = Q − W
```

---

## Significance

- Based on the law of conservation of energy.
- Heat and work are different forms of energy transfer.

---

# 11. Special Thermodynamic Processes

---

## (A) Isothermal Process

### Definition

A process carried out at constant temperature.

```
ΔT = 0
```

---

### Characteristics

For an ideal gas,

```
PV = Constant
```

(Boyle's law)

Since temperature is constant,

```
ΔU = 0
```

Therefore,

```
Q = W
```

---

## (B) Adiabatic Process

### Definition

A process in which no heat is exchanged with the surroundings.

```
Q = 0
```

---

### Equation

```
PVᵞ = Constant
```

where

```
γ = Cp/Cv
```

---

### First Law

```
ΔU = −W
```

---

## (C) Isochoric Process

### Definition

Volume remains constant.

```
ΔV = 0
```

---

### Work Done

```
W = 0
```

Hence,

```
Q = ΔU
```

---

## (D) Isobaric Process

### Definition

Pressure remains constant.

```
P = Constant
```

---

### Work Done

```
W = PΔV
```

---

# 12. Specific Heat Capacity

Specific heat depends on the process.

---

## At Constant Volume

```
Cv
```

---

## At Constant Pressure

```
Cp
```

---

## Relation

```
Cp > Cv
```

because extra heat is required to perform expansion work.

---

## Mayer's Relation

For an ideal gas,

```
Cp − Cv = R
```

where

```
R
```

is the universal gas constant.

---

# 13. Heat Capacity Ratio

```
γ = Cp/Cv
```

Also called the **adiabatic index**.

---

## Importance

Used in adiabatic processes and the speed of sound in gases.

---

# 14. Second Law of Thermodynamics

---

## Kelvin-Planck Statement

It is impossible to construct a heat engine that converts all the absorbed heat into work.

---

## Clausius Statement

Heat cannot flow spontaneously from a colder body to a hotter body without external work.

---

## Importance

- Explains the direction of heat flow.
- Introduces the concept of efficiency limits.
- Explains irreversibility.

---

# 15. Heat Engine

## Definition

A heat engine converts heat into mechanical work.

---

## Components

- Source
- Working substance
- Sink

---

## Efficiency

```
η

=

W

─────

Q₁
```

where

- **Q₁** = Heat absorbed
- **W** = Work done

Also,

```
η

=

Q₁ − Q₂

──────────

Q₁
```

where

- **Q₂** = Heat rejected

---

# 16. Refrigerator

## Definition

A refrigerator transfers heat from a low-temperature region to a high-temperature region using external work.

---

## Coefficient of Performance (COP)

```
COP

=

Q₂

────

W
```

where

- **Q₂** = Heat removed from the cold reservoir

---

# 17. Heat Pump

A heat pump transfers heat from a colder region to a warmer region for heating purposes.

Examples

- Room heating
- Water heaters

---

# 18. Carnot Engine

## Definition

A Carnot engine is an ideal reversible heat engine operating between two temperatures.

---

## Efficiency

```
η

=

1 − T₂/T₁
```

where

- **T₁** = Temperature of source (K)
- **T₂** = Temperature of sink (K)

---

## Characteristics

- Maximum possible efficiency.
- Reversible process.
- Depends only on temperatures.

---

# 19. Carnot Cycle

Consists of four reversible processes:

1. Isothermal expansion
2. Adiabatic expansion
3. Isothermal compression
4. Adiabatic compression

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

A gas absorbs **500 J** of heat and does **200 J** of work. Calculate the change in internal energy.

**Solution**

Using the first law,

```
ΔU = Q − W
```

```
= 500 − 200

= 300 J
```

---

### Example 2

A gas expands at constant pressure of

```
2 × 10⁵ Pa
```

through a volume change of

```
0.02 m³
```

Calculate the work done.

**Solution**

```
W = PΔV
```

```
= 2 × 10⁵ × 0.02

= 4000 J
```

---

### Example 3

A heat engine absorbs **1200 J** of heat and rejects **500 J**. Calculate its efficiency.

**Solution**

Work done,

```
W = Q₁ − Q₂

= 1200 − 500

= 700 J
```

Efficiency,

```
η

=

700

─────

1200

= 0.583

= 58.3%
```

---

### Example 4

A refrigerator removes **800 J** of heat while consuming **200 J** of work. Find its coefficient of performance.

**Solution**

```
COP

=

Q₂/W

=

800/200

=

4
```

---

### Example 5

A Carnot engine operates between **600 K** and **300 K**. Calculate its efficiency.

**Solution**

```
η

=

1 − T₂/T₁
```

```
=

1 − 300/600

=

0.5

=

50%
```

---

### Example 6

A gas undergoes an isochoric process and absorbs **250 J** of heat. Calculate the work done and change in internal energy.

**Solution**

For an isochoric process,

```
ΔV = 0
```

Hence,

```
W = 0
```

Using the first law,

```
ΔU = Q − W

= 250 − 0

= 250 J
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **heat** with **internal energy**; heat is energy in transit, while internal energy is a property of the system.
- Using the wrong **sign convention** for heat and work in the first law of thermodynamics.
- Assuming **ΔU = 0** for all processes; it is true only for an **ideal gas** undergoing an **isothermal process**.
- Forgetting that **work done is zero** in an **isochoric process** because the volume remains constant.
- Mixing up **isothermal** and **adiabatic** processes.
- Using temperatures in **°C** instead of **Kelvin** while calculating Carnot efficiency.
- Assuming a heat engine can have **100% efficiency**, which violates the second law of thermodynamics.
- Confusing **efficiency** of a heat engine with the **coefficient of performance (COP)** of a refrigerator.

# Chapter Summary

- **Thermodynamics** deals with the relationship between heat, work, and internal energy.
- A thermodynamic system may be **open**, **closed**, or **isolated**, depending on the exchange of matter and energy.
- The **Zeroth Law** defines thermal equilibrium and forms the basis of temperature measurement.
- The **First Law of Thermodynamics** is a statement of the conservation of energy: **Q = ΔU + W**.
- The four important thermodynamic processes are **isothermal**, **adiabatic**, **isobaric**, and **isochoric**, each having characteristic equations and properties.
- The **specific heat capacities** at constant pressure and constant volume are related by **Mayer's relation**, and their ratio is called the **adiabatic index**.
- The **Second Law of Thermodynamics** establishes the direction of natural processes and sets the upper limit on the efficiency of heat engines.
- **Heat engines**, **refrigerators**, and **heat pumps** operate by transferring heat between reservoirs while performing or consuming work.
- The **Carnot engine** is an ideal reversible engine with the maximum possible efficiency, depending only on the temperatures of the source and sink.
- Thermodynamic principles are fundamental to power generation, refrigeration, industrial processes, and many natural phenomena.
