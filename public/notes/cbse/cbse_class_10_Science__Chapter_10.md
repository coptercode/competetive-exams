# Introduction

The **human eye** is one of the most important sense organs. It enables us to see objects by detecting light reflected from them. The eye works like a camera by focusing light onto the retina. This chapter explains the structure and working of the human eye, defects of vision and their correction, the phenomenon of **dispersion**, **scattering of light**, and various natural phenomena such as the **blue colour of the sky** and **rainbows**.

---

# 1. Human Eye

The **human eye** is a natural optical instrument that helps us see objects.

### Functions

- Receives light from objects.
- Forms images on the retina.
- Sends visual information to the brain through the optic nerve.

# 3. Accommodation of the Eye

**Accommodation** is the ability of the eye lens to change its focal length to focus on objects at different distances.

### For Distant Objects

- Ciliary muscles relax.
- Lens becomes thinner.
- Focal length increases.

---

### For Near Objects

- Ciliary muscles contract.
- Lens becomes thicker.
- Focal length decreases.

---

# 4. Near Point and Far Point

### Near Point

The **near point** is the minimum distance at which an object can be seen clearly.

For a normal eye,

```
25 cm
```

---

### Far Point

The **far point** is the farthest point that can be seen clearly.

For a normal eye,

```
Infinity
```

---

# 5. Persistence of Vision

The image formed on the retina remains for about

```
1/16 second
```

This phenomenon is called **persistence of vision**.

### Applications

- Motion pictures.
- Television.
- Animation.

---

# 6. Defects of Vision

The eye may develop defects that affect clear vision.

Major defects include:

- Myopia
- Hypermetropia
- Presbyopia

# 7. Myopia (Short-Sightedness)

A person with **myopia** can see nearby objects clearly but distant objects appear blurred.

### Causes

- Eyeball becomes too long.
- Eye lens has excessive converging power.

### Image Formation

Image forms

```

```

# 8. Hypermetropia (Long-Sightedness)

A person with **hypermetropia** can see distant objects clearly but nearby objects appear blurred.

### Causes

- Eyeball becomes too short.
- Eye lens has insufficient converging power.

### Image Formation

Image forms

```
Behind Retina
```

---

### Correction

Hypermetropia is corrected using a

```
Convex Lens
```

# 9. Presbyopia

**Presbyopia** is an age-related defect in which the eye gradually loses its power of accommodation.

### Causes

- Weakening of ciliary muscles.
- Loss of elasticity of the eye lens.

### Correction

- Convex lenses.
- Bifocal lenses.
- Progressive lenses.

---

# 10. Cataract

A **cataract** is the clouding of the eye lens.

### Symptoms

- Blurred vision.
- Difficulty seeing at night.
- Reduced brightness.

### Treatment

The cloudy lens is removed surgically and replaced with an artificial lens.

---

# 11. Refraction Through a Prism

A **prism** is a transparent refracting medium bounded by two inclined plane surfaces.

When light passes through a prism, it undergoes refraction at both surfaces.

---

# 12. Dispersion of Light

**Dispersion** is the splitting of white light into its constituent colours when it passes through a prism.

### Colours Produced

```
VIBGYOR

V - Violet
I - Indigo
B - Blue
G - Green
Y - Yellow
O - Orange
R - Red
```

---

### Diagram

```
White Light

↓

 /\
/  \

↓

V I B G Y O R
```

---

## Why Does Dispersion Occur?

Different colours travel with different speeds in glass and therefore bend by different amounts.

- Violet deviates the most.
- Red deviates the least.

---

# 13. Rainbow

A **rainbow** is a natural spectrum produced by the dispersion of sunlight by tiny water droplets.

### Formation

The formation of a rainbow involves:

- Refraction
- Dispersion
- Internal reflection
- Refraction again

### Conditions

- Sun behind the observer.
- Rain in front of the observer.

---

# 14. Atmospheric Refraction

**Atmospheric refraction** is the bending of light as it passes through different layers of the Earth's atmosphere.

---

## Twinkling of Stars

Stars appear to twinkle because their light is continuously refracted by the Earth's atmosphere.

Planets do not twinkle noticeably because they appear as extended sources of light.

---

## Advanced Sunrise and Delayed Sunset

The Sun appears about **2 minutes earlier** before the actual sunrise and remains visible for about **2 minutes after** the actual sunset.

This is due to atmospheric refraction.

---

# 15. Scattering of Light

**Scattering** is the phenomenon in which light is redirected in different directions by tiny particles in the atmosphere.

---

## Tyndall Effect

The scattering of light by colloidal particles is called the **Tyndall effect**.

Examples:

- Dust particles visible in a beam of sunlight.
- Fog illuminated by vehicle headlights.

---

# 16. Blue Colour of the Sky

The sky appears blue because:

- Air molecules scatter shorter wavelengths of light more effectively.
- Blue light is scattered much more than red light.

---

# 17. Reddish Appearance of the Sun

## During Sunrise and Sunset

The Sun appears reddish because:

- Sunlight travels a longer distance through the atmosphere.
- Most of the blue light is scattered away.
- Red light reaches our eyes.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **myopia** with **hypermetropia**.
- Forgetting which lens corrects which eye defect.
- Assuming stars twinkle because of clouds (they twinkle due to atmospheric refraction).
- Mixing up **dispersion** with **scattering**.
- Forgetting the order of colours in **VIBGYOR**.
- Assuming the sky is blue because it reflects the ocean.
- Confusing **persistence of vision** with **accommodation**.
- Forgetting that violet light deviates more than red light in a prism.

# Chapter Summary

- The **human eye** forms images on the **retina** and sends visual information to the brain through the **optic nerve**.
- **Accommodation** enables the eye to focus on objects at different distances by changing the focal length of the eye lens.
- The **near point** of a normal eye is **25 cm**, while the **far point** is at **infinity**.
- **Myopia** is corrected using a **concave lens**, **hypermetropia** using a **convex lens**, and **presbyopia** using suitable corrective lenses such as bifocals.
- **Dispersion** of white light through a prism produces the seven colours of the visible spectrum (**VIBGYOR**).
- A **rainbow** is formed due to **refraction, dispersion, internal reflection,** and **refraction** of sunlight in water droplets.
- **Atmospheric refraction** causes the **twinkling of stars** and the **advanced sunrise and delayed sunset**.
- The **blue colour of the sky** and the **reddish appearance of the Sun** during sunrise and sunset are explained by the **scattering of light** in the Earth's atmosphere.
