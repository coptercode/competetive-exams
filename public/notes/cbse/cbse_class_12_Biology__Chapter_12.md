# Introduction

An **ecosystem** is a functional unit of nature where living organisms interact with each other and with the physical environment.

The term **ecosystem** was coined by:

```
A.G. Tansley (1935)
```

---

# 1. Structure of Ecosystem

An ecosystem consists of:

```
Ecosystem

├── Abiotic Components

└── Biotic Components
```

---

# A. Abiotic Components

Non-living components include:

- Temperature
- Light
- Water
- Soil
- Minerals
- Atmospheric gases

---

# B. Biotic Components

Living components include:

1. Producers
2. Consumers
3. Decomposers

---

# 2. Components of Ecosystem

---

# A. Producers

## Definition

Organisms that synthesize their own food using sunlight or chemical energy.

---

## Examples

- Green plants
- Algae
- Cyanobacteria

---

## Function

Convert solar energy into chemical energy.

---

# B. Consumers

Organisms that depend on other organisms for food.

---

## Types

### Primary Consumers

Herbivores.

Examples:

- Deer
- Rabbit

---

### Secondary Consumers

Primary carnivores.

Examples:

- Frog
- Small fish

---

### Tertiary Consumers

Top carnivores.

Examples:

- Tiger
- Eagle

---

# C. Decomposers

## Definition

Organisms that break down dead organic matter.

---

## Examples

- Bacteria
- Fungi

---

## Functions

- Nutrient recycling
- Soil formation
- Decomposition of organic matter

---

# 6. Productivity

## Definition

The rate at which biomass is produced in an ecosystem.

---

# A. Primary Productivity

Rate of biomass production by producers.

---

## Gross Primary Productivity (GPP)

Total amount of organic matter produced by plants during photosynthesis.

---

## Net Primary Productivity (NPP)

Organic matter remaining after respiration losses.

Formula:

```
NPP = GPP - Respiration
```

---

# B. Secondary Productivity

Rate of biomass production by consumers.

---

# Comparison

| GPP                       | NPP                    |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Total production          | Available production   |
| Includes respiration loss | After respiration loss |
| Higher value              | Lower value            |

---

# 7. Decomposition

## Definition

Breakdown of dead organic matter into simpler substances.

---

## Decomposers

- Bacteria
- Fungi

---

# Steps of Decomposition

---

# 1. Fragmentation

Detritivores break dead matter into smaller pieces.

Example:

- Earthworms

---

# 2. Leaching

Water dissolves soluble nutrients and carries them into soil.

---

# 3. Catabolism

Microbial enzymes break complex substances into simpler molecules.

---

# 4. Humification

Formation of dark-coloured organic matter called humus.

---

# 5. Mineralization

Release of inorganic nutrients from organic matter.

---

## Decomposition Flowchart

```
Dead Matter

↓

Fragmentation

↓

Leaching

↓

Catabolism

↓

Humification

↓

Mineralization
```

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

**Question:** Define ecosystem.

**Solution:**

An ecosystem is a functional unit of nature where living organisms interact with each other and with the physical environment.

---

### Example 2

**Question:** Differentiate between GPP and NPP.

**Solution:**

GPP is the total organic matter produced by plants through photosynthesis, while NPP is the remaining biomass after subtracting energy used in respiration.

Formula:

```
NPP = GPP - Respiration
```

---

### Example 3

**Question:** Explain the 10% law.

**Solution:**

According to Lindeman's 10% law, only about 10% of energy from one trophic level is transferred to the next trophic level. The remaining energy is lost mainly as heat during metabolic activities.

---

### Example 4

**Question:** Why is the pyramid of energy always upright?

**Solution:**

Energy decreases at each trophic level due to heat loss during metabolism. Since higher trophic levels always contain less energy, the energy pyramid cannot be inverted.

---

### Example 5

**Question:** Name the steps of decomposition.

**Solution:**

The steps are:

1. Fragmentation
2. Leaching
3. Catabolism
4. Humification
5. Mineralization

---

### Example 6

**Question:** What is the role of decomposers?

**Solution:**

Decomposers break down dead organic matter and recycle nutrients back into the ecosystem, maintaining soil fertility and nutrient cycles.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **GPP** with **NPP**; NPP is always lower because respiration losses are subtracted.
- Forgetting that energy flow in ecosystems is **unidirectional**.
- Assuming all ecological pyramids are upright; biomass pyramids can be inverted in aquatic ecosystems.
- Confusing food chains with food webs; food webs contain multiple interconnected food chains.
- Forgetting that decomposers play a major role in nutrient recycling.
- Mixing up primary and secondary succession.
- Assuming energy is recycled; energy flows one way, while nutrients are recycled.
- Forgetting the order of decomposition steps.

---

# Chapter Summary

- An ecosystem consists of abiotic and biotic components interacting as a functional unit.
- Producers capture solar energy, consumers transfer energy through trophic levels, and decomposers recycle nutrients.
- Food chains and food webs describe energy transfer pathways in ecosystems.
- Productivity is measured as **GPP** and **NPP**, with NPP representing the energy available to consumers.
- Decomposition involves fragmentation, leaching, catabolism, humification, and mineralization.
- Energy flow is unidirectional and follows the **10% law**, with only a small fraction transferred between trophic levels.
- Ecological pyramids represent trophic relationships, and the pyramid of energy is always upright.
- Nutrient cycles such as carbon and phosphorus cycles maintain ecosystem balance.
- Ecosystem functioning depends on continuous energy flow and recycling of essential nutrients.
