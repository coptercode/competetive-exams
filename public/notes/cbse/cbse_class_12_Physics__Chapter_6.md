# Introduction

The phenomenon of production of an induced electromotive force (EMF) due to a change in magnetic flux linked with a circuit is called **electromagnetic induction**.

It was discovered by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Michael Faraday}}
\]

---

# 1. Magnetic Flux

## Definition

Magnetic flux is the amount of magnetic field passing through a surface.

Symbol:

\[
\Phi_B
\]

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_B=BA\cos\theta
}
\]

where:

- B = magnetic field
- A = area
- θ = angle between magnetic field and area vector

---

## SI Unit

\[
\boxed{\text{Weber (Wb)}}
\]

---

# 2. Faraday's Laws of Electromagnetic Induction

---

# First Law

## Statement

Whenever the magnetic flux linked with a circuit changes, an induced EMF is produced in the circuit.

---

# Second Law

## Statement

The magnitude of induced EMF is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux.

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon\propto\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}
}
\]

For a coil of N turns:

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon=-N\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}
}
\]

---

# 3. Negative Sign in Faraday's Law

The negative sign represents:

\[
\boxed{\text{Lenz's Law}}
\]

It shows that induced current opposes the change producing it.

---

# 4. Lenz's Law

## Statement

The direction of induced current is such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux responsible for producing it.

---

## Conservation of Energy

Lenz's law ensures that energy is conserved because induced current opposes the cause producing it.

---

# 5. Motional EMF

## Definition

EMF produced due to motion of a conductor in a magnetic field is called motional EMF.

---

Consider a rod of length l moving with velocity v in magnetic field B.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon=Blv
}
\]

(for perpendicular motion)

---

# 6. Induced Current

Using Ohm's law:

\[
\boxed{
I=\frac{\epsilon}{R}
}
\]

---

# 7. Force on Moving Rod

A current-carrying rod experiences magnetic force:

\[
F=BIl
\]

Substituting:

\[
\boxed{
F=\frac{B^2l^2v}{R}
}
\]

---

# 8. Eddy Currents

## Definition

Circular currents induced in bulk conductors due to changing magnetic flux are called eddy currents.

---

# Applications of Eddy Currents

## 1. Electromagnetic Brakes

Used in trains and vehicles.

---

## 2. Induction Furnace

Used for melting metals.

---

## 3. Speedometers

Used for measuring speed.

---

## 4. Galvanometer Damping

Helps stop coil movement quickly.

---

# Disadvantages

Eddy currents cause:

- Energy loss
- Heating

---

# Reduction of Eddy Currents

Using:

- Laminated iron cores
- Thin insulated sheets

---

# 9. Self-Induction

## Definition

The phenomenon in which a changing current in a coil induces EMF in the same coil is called self-induction.

---

Induced EMF:

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon=-L\frac{dI}{dt}
}
\]

where:

L = self-inductance

---

# 10. Self-Inductance

## Definition

The property of a coil by which it opposes any change in current through it.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
L=\frac{N\Phi}{I}
}
\]

---

## SI Unit

\[
\boxed{\text{Henry (H)}}
\]

---

# 11. Factors Affecting Self-Inductance

Depends on:

1. Number of turns (N)
2. Area of coil (A)
3. Length of coil
4. Magnetic permeability of core

---

# 12. Mutual Induction

## Definition

The phenomenon in which a changing current in one coil induces EMF in another nearby coil is called mutual induction.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon_2=-M\frac{dI_1}{dt}
}
\]

where:

M = mutual inductance

---

# 13. Mutual Inductance

## Definition

Measure of the ability of one coil to induce EMF in another coil.

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{\text{Henry (H)}}
\]

---

# 14. Energy Stored in an Inductor

When current flows through an inductor, energy is stored in its magnetic field.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
U=\frac12LI^2
}
\]

---

# 15. AC Generator

## Principle

Based on:

\[
\boxed{\text{Electromagnetic induction}}
\]

---

# Construction

Main parts:

- Armature coil
- Magnetic field
- Slip rings
- Brushes

---

# Working

When a coil rotates in a magnetic field:

- Magnetic flux changes.
- EMF is induced.
- Alternating current is produced.

---

# Generated EMF

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon=NBA\omega\sin\omega t
}
\]

---

Maximum EMF:

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon_0=NBA\omega
}
\]

---

# 16. Transformer

## Definition

A device that changes AC voltage using mutual induction.

---

# Principle

\[
\boxed{\text{Mutual induction}}
\]

---

# Types

---

## Step-Up Transformer

Increases voltage.

\[
N_s>N_p
\]

---

## Step-Down Transformer

Decreases voltage.

\[
N_s<N_p
\]

---

# Transformer Equation

\[
\boxed{
\frac{V_s}{V_p}
=

\frac{N_s}{N_p}
}
\]

---

# Current Relation

For ideal transformer:

\[
\boxed{
V_pI_p=V_sI_s
}
\]

---

# Efficiency

\[
\boxed{
\eta=
\frac{\text{Output power}}
{\text{Input power}}
\times100
}
\]

---

# 17. Losses in Transformer

---

## 1. Copper Loss

Due to resistance of coils.

---

## 2. Eddy Current Loss

Reduced by laminated core.

---

## 3. Hysteresis Loss

Due to repeated magnetisation.

---

## 4. Flux Leakage

Due to incomplete coupling.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** A rod of length 2 m moves with velocity 5 m/s perpendicular to a magnetic field of 0.4 T. Find induced EMF.

Given:

\[
l=2m
\]

\[
v=5m/s
\]

\[
B=0.4T
\]

Formula:

\[
\epsilon=Blv
\]

\[
=0.4\times2\times5
\]

\[
\boxed{\epsilon=4V}
\]

---

## Example 2

**Question:** State Faraday's second law.

### Solution

The induced EMF is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linked with the circuit.

\[
\epsilon=-N\frac{d\Phi}{dt}
\]

---

## Example 3

**Question:** Why are transformer cores laminated?

### Solution

Lamination reduces eddy current losses and prevents unnecessary heating of the core.

---

## Example 4

**Question:** Calculate energy stored in an inductor of inductance 2 H carrying current 3 A.

\[
U=\frac12LI^2
\]

\[
=\frac12(2)(9)
\]

\[
\boxed{U=9J}
\]

---

## Example 5

**Question:** Why does Lenz's law obey conservation of energy?

### Solution

The induced current always opposes the change in flux, requiring external work. This prevents creation of energy without input.

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting the negative sign in Faraday's law.
- Confusing magnetic flux with magnetic field.
- Mixing self-induction and mutual induction.
- Forgetting that transformers work only on AC.
- Using transformer equations for DC circuits.
- Confusing step-up and step-down transformers.
- Ignoring the direction given by Lenz's law.
- Forgetting units of inductance (Henry).

---

# Chapter Summary

- Electromagnetic induction is the production of EMF due to changing magnetic flux.
- Faraday's law gives the magnitude of induced EMF.
- Lenz's law determines the direction of induced current.
- Motional EMF is produced when a conductor moves in a magnetic field.
- Self-induction occurs in the same coil, while mutual induction occurs between two coils.
- Inductors store energy in magnetic fields.
- AC generators produce alternating current using electromagnetic induction.
- Transformers work on mutual induction to increase or decrease AC voltage.
- Eddy currents have both useful applications and energy-loss effects.
