# Introduction

A **circle** is one of the most important geometric figures. It is the collection of all points in a plane that are at a fixed distance from a fixed point. The fixed point is called the **centre**, and the fixed distance is called the **radius**.

Circles are found everywhere in daily life, such as in wheels, clocks, coins, rings, and planets. They are widely used in engineering, architecture, astronomy, and design.

In this chapter, we study the different parts of a circle and the relationships between chords, arcs, and angles.

---

# 1. Circle

A **circle** is the set of all points in a plane that are at an equal distance from a fixed point.

Example:

```
          •
      *       *
    *     O     *
      *       *
          •
```

Here,

- **O** is the centre.
- Every point on the boundary is at the same distance from **O**.

---

# 2. Centre

The **centre** is the fixed point inside the circle from which every point on the circle is equidistant.

Example:

```
O
```

---

# 3. Radius

A **radius** is the line segment joining the centre of the circle to any point on the circle.

Example:

```
OA
```

Properties:

- All radii of a circle are equal.
- Radius is half of the diameter.

Relationship:

```
Radius = Diameter ÷ 2
```

---

# 4. Diameter

A **diameter** is a chord that passes through the centre of the circle.

Properties:

- It is the longest chord of the circle.
- It divides the circle into two equal parts called semicircles.

Relationship:

```
Diameter = 2 × Radius
```

---

# 5. Chord

A **chord** is a line segment joining any two points on the circle.

Properties:

- Every diameter is a chord.
- Every chord is not a diameter.

---

# 6. Arc

An **arc** is a part of the circumference of a circle.

Types of arcs:

### Minor Arc

The smaller arc between two points.

### Major Arc

The larger arc between the same two points.

### Semicircular Arc

An arc formed by a diameter.

Its measure is

```
180°
```

---

# 7. Circumference

The **circumference** is the boundary or perimeter of a circle.

It is the complete distance around the circle.

---

# 8. Sector

A **sector** is the region enclosed by two radii and the intercepted arc.

Types:

- Minor Sector
- Major Sector

---

# 9. Segment

A **segment** is the region enclosed by a chord and its corresponding arc.

Types:

- Minor Segment
- Major Segment

---

# 10. Concentric Circles

Two or more circles having the same centre but different radii are called **concentric circles**.

Example:

```
     _______
   /         \
  |  _____    |
  | /     \   |
  | \_____/   |
   \_________/
```

Both circles have the same centre.

---

# 11. Equal Chords and Equal Arcs

### Property 1

Equal chords of the same circle subtend equal arcs.

### Property 2

Equal arcs of the same circle correspond to equal chords.

---

# 12. Angle Subtended by a Chord

A chord of a circle forms an angle at the centre.

### Property

Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre.

Conversely,

Equal angles at the centre stand on equal chords.

---

# 13. Perpendicular from the Centre to a Chord

### Theorem

The perpendicular drawn from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.

If **OM** is perpendicular to chord **AB**, then

```
AM = MB
```

---

# 14. Converse of the Theorem

If a line from the centre of a circle bisects a chord, then it is perpendicular to the chord.

---

# 15. Equal Chords are Equidistant from the Centre

### Theorem

Equal chords are at equal distances from the centre.

---

# 16. Chords Equidistant from the Centre

### Converse

Chords that are at equal distances from the centre are equal.

---

Circles are used in:

- Ferris wheels.
- Satellite communication.
- Architecture and construction.
- Engineering design.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

A circle has a radius of

```
7 cm
```

Find its diameter.

Solution

```
Diameter = 2 × Radius
```

```
=2×7
```

```
=14 cm
```

---

### Example 2

The diameter of a circle is

```
18 cm
```

Find its radius.

Solution

```
Radius = Diameter ÷ 2
```

```
=18÷2
```

```
=9 cm
```

---

### Example 3

State whether the following is true or false.

"Every diameter is a chord."

Solution

**True**

A diameter joins two points on the circle and is therefore a chord.

---

### Example 4

The perpendicular from the centre to a chord divides the chord into two parts.

What can you conclude?

Solution

The two parts are equal.

The perpendicular bisects the chord.

---

### Example 5

Two chords are equal.

What can be said about their distances from the centre?

Solution

Equal chords are equidistant from the centre.

---

### Example 6

Identify the figure enclosed by two radii and an arc.

Solution

**Sector**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing a chord with a diameter.
- Assuming every chord passes through the centre.
- Mixing up sectors and segments.
- Forgetting that the diameter is the longest chord.
- Thinking concentric circles have the same radius.
- Assuming all arcs have the same length.

# Chapter Summary

- A circle is the set of all points that are at a fixed distance from a fixed point called the **centre**.
- A **radius** joins the centre to any point on the circle, while a **diameter** passes through the centre and is the longest chord.
- A **chord** joins any two points on the circle.
- An **arc** is a part of the circumference.
- A **sector** is enclosed by two radii and an arc, whereas a **segment** is enclosed by a chord and an arc.
- Concentric circles have the same centre but different radii.
- Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre and are equidistant from the centre.
- A perpendicular drawn from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.
- The properties of circles are widely used in geometry, engineering, architecture, astronomy, and everyday life.
