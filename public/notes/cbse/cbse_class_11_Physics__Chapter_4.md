# Introduction

The motion of an object changes only when an external force acts on it. The relationship between **force** and **motion** was established by **Sir Isaac Newton** through his three famous laws of motion.

These laws form the foundation of **classical mechanics** and explain the motion of bodies ranging from everyday objects to planets.

---

# 1. Force

## Definition

A **force** is an external influence that changes or tends to change the state of rest or uniform motion of a body.

It may also change the:

- Shape
- Size
- Direction of motion

---

## SI Unit

```
newton (N)
```

---

## Dimensional Formula

```
[MLT⁻²]
```

---

## Types of Forces

### Contact Forces

Require physical contact.

Examples

- Friction
- Normal reaction
- Tension
- Spring force

---

### Non-Contact Forces

Act without physical contact.

Examples

- Gravitational force
- Magnetic force
- Electrostatic force

---

# 2. Inertia

## Definition

**Inertia** is the property of a body by virtue of which it resists any change in its state of rest or uniform motion.

---

## Measure of Inertia

```
Mass
```

Greater the mass, greater the inertia.

---

## Types of Inertia

### (A) Inertia of Rest

A body at rest tends to remain at rest.

Example

Passengers fall backward when a bus starts suddenly.

---

### (B) Inertia of Motion

A moving body tends to continue moving.

Example

Passengers fall forward when a moving bus stops suddenly.

---

### (C) Inertia of Direction

A body tends to maintain its direction of motion.

Example

Passengers lean outward while taking a sharp turn.

---

# 3. Newton's First Law of Motion

## Statement

Every body continues in its state of rest or uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force.

---

## Significance

- Defines force.
- Explains inertia.
- Introduces inertial frames.

---

# 4. Momentum

## Definition

Momentum is the product of mass and velocity.

Formula

```
p = mv
```

---

## SI Unit

```
kg m s⁻¹
```

---

## Dimensional Formula

```
[MLT⁻¹]
```

---

# 5. Newton's Second Law of Motion

## Statement

The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied force and occurs in the direction of the force.

Mathematically,

```
F = dp/dt
```

For constant mass,

```
F = ma
```

---

## SI Definition of One Newton

One newton is the force that produces an acceleration of

```
1 m s⁻²
```

in a body of mass

```
1 kg
```

---

# 6. Impulse

## Definition

Impulse is the product of force and the time interval during which it acts.

Formula

```
J = Ft
```

---

## Impulse-Momentum Theorem

```
Impulse

=

Change in Momentum
```

```
J = Δp
```

---

## SI Unit

```
N s
```

or

```
kg m s⁻¹
```

---

## Applications

- Airbags
- Seat belts
- Catching a ball by moving hands backward
- Landing on sand

---

# 7. Newton's Third Law of Motion

## Statement

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Characteristics

- Equal magnitude.
- Opposite direction.
- Act on different bodies.
- Simultaneous.

---

## Examples

- Walking
- Swimming
- Rocket propulsion
- Recoil of a gun

---

# 8. Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum

## Statement

If no external force acts on a system, the total linear momentum remains constant.

Mathematically,

```
Initial Momentum

=

Final Momentum
```

```
m₁u₁ + m₂u₂

=

m₁v₁ + m₂v₂
```

---

## Applications

- Explosion
- Rocket motion
- Collisions
- Recoil of a gun

---

# 9. Equilibrium of Concurrent Forces

## Definition

A body is in equilibrium when the vector sum of all forces acting on it is zero.

Condition

```
ΣF = 0
```

For two-dimensional equilibrium,

```
ΣFx = 0
```

```
ΣFy = 0
```

# 11. Types of Friction

## Definition

Friction is the force that opposes the relative motion or tendency of motion between two surfaces in contact.

---

## (A) Static Friction

Acts before motion starts.

Maximum static friction

```
Fs(max)

=

μs N
```

---

## (B) Kinetic Friction

Acts when surfaces slide over each other.

```
Fk

=

μk N
```

---

## (C) Rolling Friction

Acts when a body rolls over a surface.

Smallest among all types.

---

# 12. Laws of Friction

1. Friction acts opposite to motion.
2. Friction is proportional to the normal reaction.
3. It is independent of the apparent area of contact.
4. Kinetic friction is less than limiting friction.

---

Formula

```
=

μ
```

where

```
λ
```

is the angle of friction.

# 14. Angle of Repose

## Definition

The angle of inclination at which a body just begins to slide.

Relation

```
tan θ

=

μ
```

Hence,

```
Angle of Repose

=

Angle of Friction
```

# 15. Dynamics of Circular Motion

A body moving in a circular path requires an inward force called **centripetal force**.

Formula

```
Fc

=

mv²/r
```

Direction

```
Towards the Centre
```

---

# 16. Banking of Roads

## Definition

The outer edge of a curved road is raised above the inner edge to reduce dependence on friction while negotiating turns.

---

## Angle of Banking

```
tan θ

=

v²

────

rg
```

where

- v = Speed
- r = Radius
- g = Acceleration due to gravity

---

## Advantages

- Reduces skidding.
- Allows vehicles to turn safely.
- Reduces wear of tyres.

---

# 17. Circular Motion on Level Roads

On an unbanked road, friction provides the centripetal force.

```
Friction

=

mv²/r
```

Maximum safe speed

```
v

=

√(μrg)
```

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

A force of **20 N** acts on a body of mass **5 kg**. Find the acceleration.

**Solution**

Using

```
F = ma
```

```
a

=

20/5

=

4 m s⁻²
```

---

### Example 2

A **2 kg** object moves with a velocity of **6 m s⁻¹**. Find its momentum.

**Solution**

```
p = mv

= 2 × 6

= 12 kg m s⁻¹
```

### Example 3

A force of **40 N** acts for **0.5 s**. Calculate the impulse.

**Solution**

```
= 20 N s
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **mass** with **weight**; mass remains constant, while weight depends on gravity.
- Assuming that **action and reaction forces act on the same body**. They always act on **different bodies**.
- Applying **F = ma** without first considering whether the net force is zero.
- Forgetting to draw a proper **Free Body Diagram (FBD)** before solving force-related problems.
- Using the coefficient of **static friction** in situations involving motion, where **kinetic friction** should be used.
- Assuming friction always acts opposite to the applied force; it actually opposes the **relative motion or tendency of motion**.
- Ignoring the role of **centripetal force** in circular motion.
- Using the banking formula without ensuring the correct values of **radius**, **speed**, and **angle**.

# Chapter Summary

- A **force** is an external agent that changes or tends to change the state of motion of a body.
- **Inertia** is the tendency of a body to resist changes in its state of rest or uniform motion, and it is measured by **mass**.
- **Newton's First Law** explains inertia, **Newton's Second Law** relates force to the rate of change of momentum (**F = ma** for constant mass), and **Newton's Third Law** describes action-reaction force pairs.
- **Momentum** is the product of mass and velocity, while **impulse** equals the change in momentum.
- The **law of conservation of linear momentum** states that the total momentum of an isolated system remains constant.
- A body is in equilibrium when the **net external force is zero**, which is analyzed using **Free Body Diagrams**.
- **Friction** opposes relative motion and exists as **static**, **kinetic**, and **rolling friction**, each governed by specific laws.
- In **uniform circular motion**, a body requires a **centripetal force** directed toward the centre of the circular path.
- **Banking of roads** allows vehicles to negotiate curves safely by reducing dependence on friction.
- Newton's laws provide the foundation for understanding mechanics and are extensively used in engineering, astronomy, transportation, and everyday life.
