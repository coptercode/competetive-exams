# Introduction

A **chemical reaction** is a process in which one or more substances (reactants) are converted into new substances (products) with different chemical properties. During a chemical reaction, atoms are rearranged, but the total number of atoms of each element remains unchanged according to the **Law of Conservation of Mass**.

Chemical reactions occur all around us, such as the rusting of iron, digestion of food, burning of fuels, cooking, respiration, and photosynthesis.

---

# 1. Chemical Reaction

A **chemical reaction** is a process in which new substances are formed with different physical and chemical properties.

### Characteristics of a Chemical Reaction

- Evolution of a gas.
- Change in colour.
- Formation of a precipitate.
- Change in temperature.
- Change in state.
- Emission of light or sound (in some reactions).

### Examples

1. Burning of magnesium ribbon

```
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO
```

Observation:

- Bright white flame.
- White ash (magnesium oxide) is formed.

---

2. Reaction between zinc and hydrochloric acid

```
Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂↑
```

Observation:

- Hydrogen gas is evolved.
- Zinc gradually dissolves.

---

# 2. Chemical Equations

A **chemical equation** represents a chemical reaction using chemical symbols and formulae.

### Word Equation

```
Magnesium + Oxygen
↓

Magnesium Oxide
```

### Chemical Equation

```
Mg + O₂ → MgO
```

This equation is **unbalanced**.

---

# 3. Balanced Chemical Equation

A **balanced chemical equation** has an equal number of atoms of each element on both sides.

### Balanced Equation

```
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO
```

### Importance of Balancing

- Obeys the Law of Conservation of Mass.
- Shows correct proportions of reactants and products.
- Helps in quantitative calculations.

---

# 4. Writing and Balancing Chemical Equations

### Steps

1. Write the word equation.
2. Convert it into a chemical equation.
3. Count the atoms on both sides.
4. Balance by adjusting coefficients only.
5. Verify the equation.

### Example

Unbalanced equation

```
Fe + O₂ → Fe₂O₃
```

Balanced equation

```
4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃
```

---

# 5. Types of Chemical Reactions

## (A) Combination Reaction

Two or more substances combine to form a single product.

### General Equation

```
A + B → AB
```

### Examples

Formation of water

```
2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
```

Formation of calcium oxide

```
CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + Heat
```

This reaction is also **exothermic**.

---

## (B) Decomposition Reaction

A single compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances.

### General Equation

```
AB → A + B
```

### Types of Decomposition

### (i) Thermal Decomposition

Occurs on heating.

Example

```
CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
```

---

### (ii) Electrolytic Decomposition

Occurs by passing electricity.

Example

```
2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂
```

---

### (iii) Photolytic Decomposition

Occurs in the presence of sunlight.

Example

```
2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl₂
```

Silver chloride changes from **white to grey**.

Another example

```
2AgBr → 2Ag + Br₂
```

These reactions are used in **black-and-white photography**.

---

## (C) Displacement Reaction

A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound.

### General Equation

```
A + BC → AC + B
```

### Example

```
Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
```

Observation

- Blue solution becomes colourless.
- Copper is deposited.

---

## (D) Double Displacement Reaction

Two compounds exchange ions to form new compounds.

### General Equation

```
AB + CD → AD + CB
```

### Example

```
Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + 2NaCl
```

A white precipitate of **barium sulfate** is formed.

---

### Precipitation Reaction

A reaction in which an insoluble solid (precipitate) is formed.

Example

```
Pb(NO₃)₂ + 2KI → PbI₂↓ + 2KNO₃
```

Observation

- Yellow precipitate of lead iodide is formed.

# 6. Oxidation and Reduction (Redox Reactions)

## Oxidation

A reaction involving:

- Addition of oxygen.
- Removal of hydrogen.
- Loss of electrons.

### Example

```
2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO
```

Copper is oxidized.

---

## Reduction

A reaction involving:

- Removal of oxygen.
- Addition of hydrogen.
- Gain of electrons.

### Example

```
CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O
```

Copper oxide is reduced to copper.

---

## Redox Reaction

A reaction in which oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously.

### Example

```
CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O
```

Here,

- Hydrogen is oxidized to water.
- Copper oxide is reduced to copper.

---

# 7. Oxidizing and Reducing Agents

## Oxidizing Agent

An oxidizing agent causes oxidation by supplying oxygen or accepting electrons.

### Example

```
CuO
```

acts as an oxidizing agent.

---

## Reducing Agent

A reducing agent causes reduction by removing oxygen or donating electrons.

### Example

```
H₂
```

acts as a reducing agent.

---

# 8. Exothermic and Endothermic Reactions

## Exothermic Reaction

A reaction that releases heat.

### Example

Combustion of methane

```
CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O + Heat
```

Other examples

- Respiration
- Burning of fuels

---

## Endothermic Reaction

A reaction that absorbs heat.

### Example

Thermal decomposition

```
CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
```

---

# 9. Effects of Oxidation in Daily Life

## (A) Corrosion

**Corrosion** is the gradual destruction of metals due to reaction with oxygen, moisture, or other substances in the environment.

### Example

Rusting of iron

```
4Fe + 3O₂ + xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O
```

### Prevention of Corrosion

- Painting
- Oiling and greasing
- Galvanization
- Alloying
- Electroplating

---

## (B) Rancidity

**Rancidity** is the oxidation of fats and oils, causing unpleasant smell and taste.

### Prevention

- Store food in airtight containers.
- Refrigeration.
- Vacuum packing.
- Use antioxidants.
- Pack food with nitrogen gas.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Balance the equation:

```
H₂ + O₂ → H₂O
```

**Solution**

Balanced equation

```
2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
```

---

### Example 2

Identify the type of reaction:

```
CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂
```

**Solution**

Two reactants combine to form one product.

**Answer:** Combination reaction.

---

### Example 3

Identify the type of reaction:

```
CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
```

**Solution**

One compound breaks into simpler substances.

**Answer:** Thermal decomposition reaction.

---

### Example 4

Which substance is oxidized?

```
2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO
```

**Solution**

Copper gains oxygen.

**Answer:** Copper is oxidized.

---

### Example 5

Identify the oxidizing agent:

```
CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O
```

**Solution**

Copper oxide supplies oxygen.

**Answer:** CuO is the oxidizing agent.

---

### Example 6

Why does iron rust?

**Solution**

Iron reacts with oxygen and moisture in the presence of air to form hydrated iron(III) oxide (rust).

# Common Mistakes

- Writing chemical formulae incorrectly before balancing equations.
- Changing subscripts instead of coefficients while balancing equations.
- Confusing **combination** and **decomposition** reactions.
- Assuming every double displacement reaction produces a precipitate.
- Mixing up oxidation with reduction.
- Forgetting that oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously in a redox reaction.
- Confusing corrosion with rancidity.
- Ignoring the physical states of reactants and products when writing equations.

# Chapter Summary

- A **chemical reaction** converts reactants into products with new properties.
- A **balanced chemical equation** obeys the **Law of Conservation of Mass** by having equal numbers of atoms on both sides.
- **Combination reactions** produce a single product, while **decomposition reactions** break compounds into simpler substances.
- **Displacement reactions** involve a more reactive element replacing a less reactive one, whereas **double displacement reactions** involve the exchange of ions between compounds.
- **Oxidation** involves the addition of oxygen or removal of hydrogen, while **reduction** involves the removal of oxygen or addition of hydrogen.
- In a **redox reaction**, oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously.
- **Exothermic reactions** release heat, whereas **endothermic reactions** absorb heat.
- **Corrosion** damages metals through oxidation, while **rancidity** spoils food containing fats and oils due to oxidation.
- Chemical reactions play a vital role in everyday life, biological processes, and industrial applications.
