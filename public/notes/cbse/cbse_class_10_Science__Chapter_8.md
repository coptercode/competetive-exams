# Introduction

**Heredity** is the process by which characteristics are passed from parents to their offspring. These inherited characteristics are controlled by **genes**, which are present on **chromosomes**. Variations that arise during reproduction make individuals different from one another and play an important role in **evolution**. This chapter explains heredity, Mendel's experiments, inheritance of traits, sex determination, and the significance of variation.

---

# 1. Heredity

**Heredity** is the transmission of genetic traits from parents to offspring.

### Importance of Heredity

- Maintains continuity of species.
- Passes genetic information to the next generation.
- Determines inherited characteristics.
- Helps in the study of evolution.

---

# 2. Variation

**Variation** refers to the differences in characteristics among individuals of the same species.

### Causes of Variation

- Sexual reproduction.
- Crossing over during meiosis.
- Mutation.
- Environmental influences.

### Importance of Variation

- Helps organisms adapt to changing environments.
- Increases the chances of survival.
- Forms the basis of evolution.

---

# 3. Genetics

**Genetics** is the branch of biology that deals with the study of heredity and variation.

The scientist known as the **Father of Genetics** is **Gregor Johann Mendel**.

---

# 4. Mendel's Experiments

Mendel conducted experiments on **pea plants (Pisum sativum)** because they:

- Have distinct contrasting traits.
- Have a short life cycle.
- Produce many seeds.
- Can self-pollinate as well as cross-pollinate.

---

# 5. Important Genetic Terms

| Term            | Definition                                                         |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Gene            | Unit of heredity controlling a specific trait.                     |
| Chromosome      | Thread-like structure carrying genes.                              |
| DNA             | Deoxyribonucleic Acid; hereditary material of living organisms.    |
| Trait           | A characteristic feature of an organism.                           |
| Allele          | Alternative form of a gene.                                        |
| Genotype        | Genetic makeup of an organism.                                     |
| Phenotype       | Observable characteristics of an organism.                         |
| Dominant Trait  | Trait expressed even in the presence of another contrasting trait. |
| Recessive Trait | Trait expressed only when both alleles are recessive.              |

# 6. Monohybrid Cross

A **monohybrid cross** involves the inheritance of a single pair of contrasting traits.

### Example

Tall pea plant (**TT**) × Dwarf pea plant (**tt**)

### Parental Generation (P)

```
TT × tt
```

### Gametes

```
T     T

t     t
```

### F₁ Generation

```
All Tt
```

All plants are **Tall** because tallness is dominant.

---

## Self-Pollination of F₁ Generation

```
Tt × Tt
```

### Punnett Square

|       | T   | t   |
| ----- | --- | --- |
| **T** | TT  | Tt  |
| **t** | Tt  | tt  |

---

### Genotypic Ratio

```
1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt
```

---

### Phenotypic Ratio

```
3 Tall : 1 Dwarf
```

---

# 7. Law of Dominance

**Law of Dominance**

When two contrasting traits are present together, only one trait (dominant) is expressed, while the other (recessive) remains hidden.

Example:

Tall (T) is dominant over dwarf (t).

---

# 8. Law of Segregation

During gamete formation, the two alleles of a gene separate so that each gamete receives only one allele.

This is also called the **Law of Purity of Gametes**.

---

# 9. Dihybrid Cross

A **dihybrid cross** studies the inheritance of two pairs of contrasting traits simultaneously.

### Example

Round Yellow Seeds

×

Wrinkled Green Seeds

Mendel observed the **9 : 3 : 3 : 1** phenotypic ratio in the F₂ generation.

### Phenotypic Ratio

```
9 : 3 : 3 : 1
```

---

# 10. Law of Independent Assortment

The inheritance of one pair of traits is independent of the inheritance of another pair.

This law explains why new combinations of traits appear in offspring.

---

# 11. Chromosomes

**Chromosomes** are thread-like structures found in the nucleus of cells.

They contain:

- DNA
- Genes

### Human Chromosomes

| Cell Type | Number of Chromosomes |
| --------- | --------------------: |
| Body Cell |         46 (23 pairs) |
| Gamete    |                    23 |

---

# 12. DNA

**DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)** is the hereditary material that stores genetic information.

### Functions

- Carries hereditary information.
- Controls protein synthesis.
- Transfers genetic traits to offspring.

---

# 13. Sex Determination in Humans

Humans have **23 pairs of chromosomes**.

One pair determines the sex of the child.

### Sex Chromosomes

- Female: **XX**
- Male: **XY**

---

## Formation of Gametes

### Female

All eggs contain:

```
X
```

### Male

Sperms contain either:

```
X
```

or

```
Y
```

---

## Sex Determination

### Case 1

```
X (Egg)

+

X (Sperm)

↓

XX

Girl
```

---

### Case 2

```
X (Egg)

+

Y (Sperm)

↓

XY

Boy
```

Thus, the **father's sperm determines the sex of the child**.

# 14. Difference Between Inherited Traits and Acquired Traits

| Inherited Traits    | Acquired Traits                        |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Passed from parents | Developed during lifetime              |
| Controlled by genes | Not controlled by genes                |
| Passed to offspring | Not passed to offspring                |
| Example: Eye colour | Example: Muscular body due to exercise |

---

# 15. Evolution and Variation

Variation is essential for evolution.

Organisms with favourable variations have a greater chance of survival and reproduction.

This process over many generations leads to the evolution of new species.

---

# 16. Fossils

**Fossils** are the preserved remains or impressions of ancient organisms.

### Importance

- Provide evidence of evolution.
- Help determine the age of rocks.
- Show the history of life on Earth.
- Reveal extinct organisms.

---

# 17. Evolution

**Evolution** is the gradual change in living organisms over many generations, leading to the formation of new species.

### Evidence for Evolution

- Fossils.
- Homologous organs.
- Analogous organs.
- Comparative anatomy.
- Embryological studies.

---

## Homologous Organs

Organs having the **same basic structure** but performing **different functions**.

### Examples

- Forelimbs of humans.
- Wings of bats.
- Flippers of whales.

These indicate **common ancestry**.

---

## Analogous Organs

Organs having **different structures** but performing **similar functions**.

### Examples

- Wings of birds.
- Wings of insects.

These indicate **convergent evolution**.

Who determines the sex of a child in humans?

**Solution**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **genotype** with **phenotype**.
- Assuming dominant traits are always more common in a population.
- Mixing up **homologous** and **analogous** organs.
- Confusing **genes** with **chromosomes**.
- Assuming acquired traits are inherited.
- Forgetting that **gametes contain 23 chromosomes**, while body cells contain **46 chromosomes**.
- Assuming the mother determines the sex of the child (the father determines it in humans).
- Confusing the **Law of Dominance** with the **Law of Segregation**.

# Chapter Summary

- **Heredity** is the transmission of genetic traits from parents to offspring, while **variation** refers to differences among individuals of the same species.
- **Gregor Johann Mendel**, known as the **Father of Genetics**, explained inheritance through his experiments on pea plants and proposed the **Laws of Dominance, Segregation,** and **Independent Assortment**.
- A **monohybrid cross** gives a **3 : 1 phenotypic ratio**, while a **dihybrid cross** gives a **9 : 3 : 3 : 1 phenotypic ratio**.
- **Genes**, present on **chromosomes**, are made of **DNA**, the hereditary material that carries genetic information.
- In humans, **body cells contain 46 chromosomes**, whereas **gametes contain 23 chromosomes**.
- The **father determines the sex of the child**, as sperm carry either an **X** or **Y** chromosome.
- **Inherited traits** are passed from parents to offspring, whereas **acquired traits** are not inherited.
- **Variation** provides the raw material for **evolution**, and evidence such as **fossils, homologous organs,** and **analogous organs** supports the theory of evolution.
