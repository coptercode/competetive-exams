# Introduction

Thermodynamics is the branch of chemistry that deals with the **relationship between heat, work, and energy** during physical and chemical changes.

It helps answer questions such as:

- Why do some reactions occur spontaneously?
- How much heat is absorbed or released?
- What determines the direction of a chemical reaction?

Thermodynamics is based on four fundamental laws, of which the **First** and **Second Laws** are most important at the Class 11 level.

---

# 1. Basic Terminology

## System

The part of the universe under study.

Example

- A chemical reaction in a beaker.
- Water in a flask.

---

## Surroundings

Everything outside the system.

---

## Universe

```
Universe

=

System + Surroundings
```

---

# 2. Types of Systems

## (A) Open System

Exchanges both matter and energy with surroundings.

Example

- Boiling water in an open vessel.

---

## (B) Closed System

Exchanges energy but not matter.

Example

- Water in a sealed bottle.

---

## (C) Isolated System

Exchanges neither matter nor energy.

Example

- An ideal thermos flask.

---

# 3. State of a System

The condition of a system described by measurable properties such as:

- Pressure (P)
- Volume (V)
- Temperature (T)
- Composition

These properties are called **state variables**.

---

# 4. State Functions and Path Functions

## State Functions

Depend only on the initial and final states.

Examples

- Internal energy (U)
- Enthalpy (H)
- Entropy (S)
- Gibbs energy (G)

---

## Path Functions

Depend on the path followed.

Examples

- Heat (q)
- Work (w)

---

# Comparison

| State Function        | Path Function   |
| --------------------- | --------------- |
| Depends only on state | Depends on path |
| U, H, S, G            | q, w            |

---

# 5. Internal Energy (U)

## Definition

The total energy possessed by a system due to the motion and interactions of its particles.

It includes:

- Kinetic energy of particles.
- Potential energy of particles.

---

## Change in Internal Energy

```
ΔU

=

U(final) − U(initial)
```

---

# 6. Heat (q)

Heat is the energy transferred due to a temperature difference.

---

## Sign Convention

### Heat Absorbed

```
q = +ve
```

---

### Heat Released

```
q = −ve
```

---

# 7. Work (w)

Work is done when a force causes displacement.

For expansion work,

```
w = −Pext ΔV
```

where

- Pext = External pressure
- ΔV = Change in volume

---

## Sign Convention

### Expansion

```
w = Negative
```

System does work on surroundings.

---

### Compression

```
w = Positive
```

Surroundings do work on system.

---

# 8. First Law of Thermodynamics

## Statement

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed.

It can only be converted from one form to another.

---

## Mathematical Expression

```
ΔU = q + w
```

where

- ΔU = Change in internal energy
- q = Heat
- w = Work

---

## Special Cases

### Constant Volume

```
w = 0

Therefore,

ΔU = qv
```

---

### Adiabatic Process

```
q = 0

Therefore,

ΔU = w
```

---

# 9. Enthalpy (H)

## Definition

Heat content of a system.

```
H = U + PV
```

---

## Enthalpy Change

```
ΔH

=

H(products)

−

H(reactants)
```

---

## Relationship between ΔH and ΔU

For gaseous reactions,

```
ΔH = ΔU + Δn(g)RT
```

where

- Δn(g) = Change in moles of gaseous substances
- R = Gas constant
- T = Absolute temperature

---

# 10. Endothermic and Exothermic Reactions

---

## Exothermic Reaction

Heat is released.

```
ΔH < 0
```

Examples

- Combustion
- Neutralization

---

## Endothermic Reaction

Heat is absorbed.

```
ΔH > 0
```

Examples

- Photosynthesis
- Thermal decomposition of CaCO₃

---

# 11. Thermochemical Equations

A balanced chemical equation showing the heat change.

Example

```
H₂ + ½O₂

↓

H₂O

ΔH = −286 kJ mol⁻¹
```

---

# 12. Standard Enthalpy Changes

---

## (A) Standard Enthalpy of Formation (ΔH°f)

Enthalpy change when **one mole** of a compound is formed from its constituent elements in their standard states.

Example

```
H₂ + ½O₂

↓

H₂O
```

---

## (B) Standard Enthalpy of Combustion (ΔH°c)

Heat evolved when one mole of a substance burns completely in oxygen.

---

## (C) Standard Enthalpy of Atomization

Enthalpy change when one mole of gaseous atoms is formed from the element in its standard state.

Example

```
½Cl₂(g)

↓

Cl(g)
```

---

## (D) Standard Enthalpy of Neutralization

Heat evolved when one mole of water is formed by neutralization.

For strong acid–strong base,

```
≈ −57.1 kJ mol⁻¹
```

---

## (E) Standard Enthalpy of Solution

Heat change when one mole of solute dissolves in excess solvent.

---

# 13. Hess's Law of Constant Heat Summation

## Statement

The total enthalpy change for a reaction is independent of the pathway.

---

## Mathematical Representation

```
ΔH(total)

=

ΣΔH(products)

−

ΣΔH(reactants)
```

---

## Applications

- Calculation of unknown enthalpies.
- Determination of bond enthalpies.
- Thermochemical calculations.

---

# 14. Bond Enthalpy

## Definition

Energy required to break one mole of a specific bond in the gaseous state.

---

## Relation

Greater bond enthalpy means:

- Stronger bond.
- Shorter bond.

---

## Enthalpy Using Bond Energies

```
ΔH

=

Σ(Bond Energies Broken)

−

Σ(Bond Energies Formed)
```

---

# 15. Spontaneous Process

A spontaneous process occurs naturally under given conditions without continuous external assistance.

Examples

- Rusting of iron.
- Melting of ice above 0°C.
- Flow of heat from hot to cold body.

---

# 16. Second Law of Thermodynamics

## Statement

The entropy of an isolated system always increases during a spontaneous process.

---

# 17. Entropy (S)

## Definition

Entropy is a measure of randomness or disorder in a system.

---

## General Trends

```
Solid

↓

Liquid

↓

Gas
```

Entropy increases in the above order.

---

## Factors Affecting Entropy

- Temperature
- Physical state
- Number of gaseous molecules
- Molecular complexity

---

# 18. Gibbs Free Energy (G)

## Definition

A thermodynamic function used to predict spontaneity.

---

## Gibbs Equation

```
ΔG

=

ΔH − TΔS
```

where

- ΔG = Gibbs free energy change
- ΔH = Enthalpy change
- T = Absolute temperature
- ΔS = Entropy change

---

# 19. Conditions for Spontaneity

| ΔG       | Process         |
| -------- | --------------- |
| Negative | Spontaneous     |
| Positive | Non-spontaneous |
| Zero     | Equilibrium     |

---

## Effect of ΔH and ΔS

| ΔH  | ΔS  | Spontaneity        |
| --- | --- | ------------------ |
| −   | +   | Always spontaneous |
| +   | −   | Never spontaneous  |
| −   | −   | Low temperature    |
| +   | +   | High temperature   |

---

# 20. Relationship between Gibbs Energy and Equilibrium

At equilibrium,

```
ΔG = 0
```

The relationship between standard Gibbs free energy and the equilibrium constant is:

```
ΔG° = −RT ln K
```

where

- R = Gas constant
- T = Temperature (K)
- K = Equilibrium constant

Interpretation:

- **K > 1** → ΔG° is negative (products favored).
- **K < 1** → ΔG° is positive (reactants favored).
- **K = 1** → ΔG° = 0.

---

# 21. Heat Capacity

## Heat Capacity (C)

Amount of heat required to raise the temperature of a substance by 1 K.

```
C = q / ΔT
```

---

## Specific Heat Capacity

Heat required to raise the temperature of **1 g** of substance by 1 K.

---

## Molar Heat Capacity

Heat required to raise the temperature of **1 mole** of substance by 1 K.

---

# 22. Calorimetry

Principle:

```
Heat Lost

=

Heat Gained
```

---

## Formula

```
q

=

mcΔT
```

where

- m = Mass
- c = Specific heat capacity
- ΔT = Temperature change

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

A system absorbs **150 J** of heat and does **50 J** of work. Calculate the change in internal energy.

**Solution**

Using the First Law:

```
ΔU = q + w
```

Here,

```
q = +150 J

w = −50 J
```

Therefore,

```
ΔU

= 150 − 50

= 100 J
```

---

### Example 2

A reaction has **ΔH = −250 kJ**. Is it exothermic or endothermic?

**Solution**

Since ΔH is negative,

```
ΔH < 0
```

the reaction releases heat.

Therefore, it is an **exothermic reaction**.

---

### Example 3

Calculate the heat required to raise the temperature of **100 g of water** from **25°C to 35°C**.

Specific heat capacity of water = **4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹**

**Solution**

Using

```
q = mcΔT
```

```
m = 100 g

c = 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹

ΔT = 10 K
```

```
q

= 100 × 4.18 × 10

= 4180 J
```

---

### Example 4

A process has:

```
ΔH = −100 kJ

ΔS = +200 J K⁻¹
```

Will it always be spontaneous?

**Solution**

Negative ΔH and positive ΔS always make

```
ΔG = ΔH − TΔS
```

negative at all temperatures.

Hence, the process is **always spontaneous**.

---

### Example 5

State Hess's Law.

**Solution**

Hess's Law states that the total enthalpy change for a reaction depends only on the initial and final states of the system and is independent of the path by which the reaction occurs.

---

### Example 6

At equilibrium, what is the value of ΔG?

**Solution**

At equilibrium,

```
ΔG = 0
```

because there is no net tendency for the reaction to proceed in either direction.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **heat (q)** with **temperature**; heat is energy in transit, whereas temperature measures the average kinetic energy of particles.
- Mixing up **internal energy (U)** and **enthalpy (H)**.
- Using the wrong **sign convention** for heat and work.
- Forgetting that **heat** and **work** are **path functions**, while **U**, **H**, **S**, and **G** are **state functions**.
- Assuming every exothermic reaction is spontaneous; **entropy** must also be considered.
- Forgetting the relationship **ΔG = ΔH − TΔS** while predicting spontaneity.
- Using **°C** instead of **Kelvin** in thermodynamic equations involving temperature.
- Ignoring the sign of **Δn(g)** when applying the equation **ΔH = ΔU + Δn(g)RT**.

# Chapter Summary

- **Thermodynamics** studies the interconversion of heat and other forms of energy during physical and chemical processes.
- A **system** may be open, closed, or isolated, depending on whether it exchanges matter and/or energy with its surroundings.
- The **First Law of Thermodynamics** expresses the conservation of energy through the equation **ΔU = q + w**.
- **Internal energy (U)** and **enthalpy (H)** are state functions used to describe the energy content of a system.
- Various standard enthalpy changes, including **formation**, **combustion**, **atomization**, **neutralization**, and **solution**, help quantify heat changes in chemical reactions.
- **Hess's Law** enables the calculation of reaction enthalpies by combining known thermochemical equations.
- The **Second Law of Thermodynamics** introduces **entropy (S)** as a measure of disorder and explains the direction of spontaneous processes.
- **Gibbs free energy (ΔG)** combines enthalpy and entropy to predict spontaneity, with **ΔG < 0** indicating a spontaneous process.
- The relationship **ΔG° = −RT ln K** connects thermodynamics with chemical equilibrium, linking free energy changes to the equilibrium constant.
- **Calorimetry** and the equation **q = mcΔT** are widely used to measure heat changes experimentally.
