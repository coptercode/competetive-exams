# Introduction

All living organisms require energy to perform vital life processes such as growth, reproduction, transport, biosynthesis, and movement. In plants, this energy is obtained through **cellular respiration**, a process in which food molecules are broken down to release energy in the form of **ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate).**

Respiration occurs continuously in all living cells and is independent of photosynthesis. While photosynthesis stores energy, respiration releases it for cellular activities.

---

# 1. Respiration

## Definition

Respiration is the biochemical process by which food substances are oxidized to release energy in the form of ATP.

---

## General Equation (Aerobic Respiration)

```
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
        │
        ▼
6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy (ATP)
```

---

## Characteristics

- Catabolic process.
- Releases energy.
- Occurs in all living cells.
- ATP is produced.
- Controlled by enzymes.

---

# 2. Types of Respiration

```
Respiration
│
├── Aerobic Respiration
└── Anaerobic Respiration
```

---

## (A) Aerobic Respiration

Occurs in the presence of oxygen.

### Characteristics

- Oxygen required.
- Complete oxidation of glucose.
- High ATP production.
- End products:
  - Carbon dioxide
  - Water

Occurs mainly in:

- Mitochondria

---

## (B) Anaerobic Respiration

Occurs in the absence of oxygen.

### Characteristics

- Oxygen not required.
- Incomplete oxidation.
- Less ATP produced.

Occurs in:

- Yeast
- Some bacteria
- Muscle cells (during vigorous exercise)

---

# Comparison

| Aerobic Respiration | Anaerobic Respiration      |
| ------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Oxygen required     | Oxygen absent              |
| Complete oxidation  | Incomplete oxidation       |
| High ATP yield      | Low ATP yield              |
| CO₂ + H₂O formed    | Alcohol/Lactic acid formed |

---

# 3. Respiratory Substrates

Respiration may utilize:

- Carbohydrates (main substrate)
- Fats
- Proteins
- Organic acids

Glucose is the preferred respiratory substrate.

---

# 4. Glycolysis

Also called:

```
EMP Pathway
```

Named after:

- Embden
- Meyerhof
- Parnas

---

## Site

```
Cytoplasm
```

---

## Definition

Glycolysis is the breakdown of one molecule of glucose into two molecules of pyruvate.

---

## Overall Reaction

```
Glucose
   │
   ▼
2 Pyruvate
```

---

## Products

From one molecule of glucose:

- 2 Pyruvate
- 2 ATP (Net gain)
- 2 NADH

---

## Importance

- First stage of respiration.
- Occurs in both aerobic and anaerobic respiration.
- Does not require oxygen directly.

---

# 6. Link Reaction

The conversion of pyruvate into acetyl CoA is called the **link reaction**.

---

## Site

```
Mitochondrial Matrix
```

---

## Products

From one glucose molecule:

- 2 Acetyl CoA
- 2 CO₂
- 2 NADH

# 7. Krebs Cycle (Citric Acid Cycle)

Discovered by:

```
Sir Hans Krebs
```

---

## Site

```
Mitochondrial Matrix
```

---

## Beginning

```
Acetyl CoA
     │
Oxaloacetic Acid
     │
Citrate
```

---

## Products (Per Glucose)

- 4 CO₂
- 6 NADH
- 2 FADH₂
- 2 ATP (or GTP)

---

## Importance

- Complete oxidation of acetyl CoA.
- Produces reducing power (NADH, FADH₂).
- Generates ATP.

Oxidative phosphorylation is the synthesis of ATP using the energy released during electron transport.

---

ATP is synthesized by the enzyme:

```
ATP Synthase
```

# 10. ATP Yield

Approximate ATP production from one glucose molecule:

| Stage                     | ATP Produced     |
| ------------------------- | ---------------- |
| Glycolysis                | 2                |
| Krebs Cycle               | 2                |
| Electron Transport System | About 28         |
| **Total**                 | **About 32 ATP** |

_(Actual ATP yield may vary depending on cell type and shuttle systems.)_

# 12. Amphibolic Pathway

Respiration is called an **amphibolic pathway** because it functions in both:

- Catabolism
- Anabolism

Examples

- Amino acids formed from respiratory intermediates.
- Fatty acid synthesis from acetyl CoA.

---

# 13. Respiratory Quotient (RQ)

RQ is the ratio of carbon dioxide evolved to oxygen consumed during respiration.

Formula

```
RQ = CO₂ Produced
     ─────────────
     O₂ Consumed
```

---

## RQ Values

| Respiratory Substrate | RQ             |
| --------------------- | -------------- |
| Carbohydrates         | 1.0            |
| Fats                  | Less than 1    |
| Proteins              | About 0.8      |
| Organic acids         | Greater than 1 |

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Why is glycolysis called the EMP pathway?

**Solution**

Glycolysis is called the EMP pathway because it was discovered and described by **Embden, Meyerhof, and Parnas**.

---

### Example 2

State the site of the Krebs cycle.

**Solution**

The Krebs cycle occurs in the **mitochondrial matrix** of eukaryotic cells.

---

### Example 3

Differentiate between aerobic and anaerobic respiration.

**Solution**

Aerobic respiration requires oxygen, completely oxidizes glucose, and produces a large amount of ATP. Anaerobic respiration occurs without oxygen, partially oxidizes glucose, and produces much less ATP.

---

### Example 4

Why is oxygen called the final electron acceptor?

**Solution**

In the electron transport system, oxygen accepts electrons and hydrogen ions at the end of the chain to form water, allowing electron transport and ATP synthesis to continue.

---

### Example 5

Calculate the respiratory quotient (RQ) for carbohydrate respiration.

**Solution**

For carbohydrates:

```
RQ = CO₂ Produced / O₂ Consumed = 6 / 6 = 1
```

Therefore, the respiratory quotient of carbohydrates is **1.0**.

---

### Example 6

Why is respiration considered an amphibolic pathway?

**Solution**

Respiration is amphibolic because it participates in both the breakdown of food molecules (catabolism) and the synthesis of important biomolecules such as amino acids and fatty acids (anabolism).

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **photosynthesis** with **respiration**.
- Assuming glycolysis occurs in mitochondria; it occurs in the **cytoplasm**.
- Forgetting that the **Krebs cycle** occurs in the **mitochondrial matrix**.
- Mixing up the **link reaction** with the **Krebs cycle**.
- Assuming oxygen is required for glycolysis; glycolysis itself does **not** directly require oxygen.
- Confusing **alcoholic fermentation** with **lactic acid fermentation**.
- Forgetting that **oxygen** is the **final electron acceptor** in aerobic respiration.
- Assuming every cell produces exactly 38 ATP from one glucose molecule; in eukaryotic cells, the yield is generally **about 32 ATP**.

---

# Chapter Summary

- **Respiration** is the process by which living cells oxidize food molecules to release energy in the form of ATP.
- It may occur as **aerobic respiration**, requiring oxygen and producing carbon dioxide and water, or as **anaerobic respiration**, producing ethanol or lactic acid with a lower ATP yield.
- **Glycolysis (EMP pathway)** occurs in the cytoplasm and converts glucose into two molecules of pyruvate with a net gain of ATP and NADH.
- Pyruvate undergoes the **link reaction** to form acetyl CoA, which enters the **Krebs cycle** in the mitochondrial matrix for complete oxidation.
- The **electron transport system (ETS)** on the inner mitochondrial membrane transfers electrons to oxygen, the final electron acceptor, driving ATP synthesis through **oxidative phosphorylation**.
- Cellular respiration functions as an **amphibolic pathway**, participating in both energy production and biosynthesis.
- The **respiratory quotient (RQ)** varies depending on the respiratory substrate being oxidized.
- Temperature, oxygen availability, water, respiratory substrates, and enzyme activity influence the rate of respiration.
- Respiration supplies the energy required for all metabolic activities, making it essential for plant growth, maintenance, and survival.
