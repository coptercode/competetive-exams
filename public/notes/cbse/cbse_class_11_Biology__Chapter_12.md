# Introduction

Plants require **mineral elements** in addition to water, carbon dioxide, and sunlight for proper growth and development. These minerals are absorbed from the soil through roots and participate in various physiological and biochemical processes.

Depending on the quantity required, mineral nutrients are classified into **macronutrients** and **micronutrients**. A deficiency or excess of these nutrients affects plant growth, metabolism, and productivity.

---

# 1. Mineral Nutrition

**Mineral nutrition** is the study of the absorption, transport, assimilation, and functions of mineral elements in plants.

Plants obtain minerals mainly from:

- Soil
- Water

Carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen are obtained from:

- Carbon dioxide
- Water

---

# 2. Essential Mineral Elements

An element is considered **essential** if:

- It is absolutely necessary for normal growth and reproduction.
- Its deficiency prevents the plant from completing its life cycle.
- It has a direct role in plant metabolism.
- Its function cannot be replaced by another element.

These criteria were proposed by **Arnon and Stout (1939).**

---

# 3. Criteria for Essentiality

A mineral element is essential if:

1. The plant cannot complete its life cycle without it.
2. The deficiency is specific and can be corrected only by supplying that element.
3. It directly participates in plant metabolism.

---

# 4. Classification of Mineral Elements

```
Mineral Elements
│
├── Macronutrients
└── Micronutrients
```

---

## (A) Macronutrients

Required in relatively large amounts (generally more than **10 mmol kg⁻¹ dry matter**).

### Macronutrients

- Carbon (C)
- Hydrogen (H)
- Oxygen (O)
- Nitrogen (N)
- Phosphorus (P)
- Potassium (K)
- Calcium (Ca)
- Magnesium (Mg)
- Sulphur (S)

---

## (B) Micronutrients

Required in very small amounts (generally less than **10 mmol kg⁻¹ dry matter**).

### Micronutrients

- Iron (Fe)
- Manganese (Mn)
- Zinc (Zn)
- Copper (Cu)
- Boron (B)
- Molybdenum (Mo)
- Chlorine (Cl)
- Nickel (Ni)

---

# 5. Hydroponics

**Hydroponics** is the technique of growing plants in a **nutrient solution without soil**.

It is useful for:

- Studying mineral nutrition.
- Commercial cultivation.
- Identifying deficiency symptoms.

---

## Advantages

- Better control of nutrients.
- Faster growth.
- Water conservation.
- Reduced soil-borne diseases.

---

# 6. Functions of Essential Mineral Elements

---

## Nitrogen (N)

### Functions

- Component of amino acids.
- Component of proteins.
- Component of nucleic acids.

---

- Root development.

- Maintains osmotic balance.

### Functions

- Membrane stability.

---

### Functions

---

---

## Boron (B)

### Functions

- Cell wall formation.

## Molybdenum (Mo)

### Functions

# 7. Deficiency Symptoms

Mineral deficiency produces characteristic symptoms in plants.

---

## Common Deficiency Symptoms

### Chlorosis

Yellowing of leaves due to loss of chlorophyll.

Caused by deficiency of:

- Nitrogen
- Magnesium
- Iron
- Sulphur

---

### Necrosis

Death of plant tissues.

Caused by deficiency of:

- Calcium
- Potassium
- Copper

---

### Inhibition of Cell Division

Occurs due to deficiency of:

- Calcium
- Boron

---

### Delayed Flowering

Caused by deficiency of:

- Phosphorus

---

### Stunted Growth

Occurs due to deficiency of:

- Nitrogen
- Potassium
- Zinc

---

# 8. Mobility of Nutrients

## Mobile Elements

Can move from older leaves to younger leaves.

Examples

- Nitrogen
- Phosphorus
- Potassium
- Magnesium

Deficiency symptoms first appear in **older leaves**.

---

## Immobile Elements

Cannot move easily within the plant.

Examples

- Calcium
- Iron
- Boron

Deficiency symptoms first appear in **younger leaves**.

---

# Comparison

| Mobile Elements          | Immobile Elements          |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Symptoms in older leaves | Symptoms in younger leaves |
| N, P, K, Mg              | Ca, Fe, B                  |

---

# 9. Toxicity of Micronutrients

Excess absorption of micronutrients may become toxic.

Effects include:

- Reduced growth.
- Chlorosis.
- Necrosis.
- Enzyme inhibition.

# 11. Nitrogen Fixation

Nitrogen fixation is the conversion of atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms.

---

## Types

### (A) Physical Fixation

Occurs through:

- Lightning
- UV radiation

---

### (B) Industrial Fixation

Haber-Bosch process.

---

### (C) Biological Nitrogen Fixation

Performed by microorganisms.

Examples

- Rhizobium
- Azotobacter
- Clostridium
- Frankia
- Cyanobacteria (Anabaena, Nostoc)

---

# 12. Biological Nitrogen Fixation

Occurs in root nodules of leguminous plants.

Symbiotic partners:

- Legume plant
- Rhizobium bacteria

---

## Steps

1. Rhizobium infects root hairs.
2. Infection thread develops.
3. Root nodules form.
4. Nitrogenase enzyme converts atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia.

---

# 13. Nitrogenase Enzyme

Nitrogenase catalyzes nitrogen fixation.

Requires

- ATP
- Iron
- Molybdenum

It functions only under **anaerobic (oxygen-free)** conditions.

---

# 14. Leghemoglobin

Leghemoglobin is a **pink pigment** present in root nodules.

### Functions

- Binds oxygen.
- Maintains low oxygen concentration.
- Protects nitrogenase from oxygen damage.

---

# 15. Nitrification

Conversion of ammonia into nitrate.

Occurs in two steps.

---

## Step 1

```
Ammonia
   │
Nitrosomonas
   │
Nitrite
```

---

## Step 2

```
Nitrite
   │
Nitrobacter
   │
Nitrate
```

---

# 16. Ammonification

Conversion of organic nitrogen into ammonia by decomposer microorganisms.

---

# 17. Denitrification

Conversion of nitrates into atmospheric nitrogen.

Performed by bacteria such as:

- Pseudomonas
- Thiobacillus

---

# 18. Nitrogen Assimilation

Plants absorb nitrogen mainly as:

- Nitrate (NO₃⁻)
- Ammonium (NH₄⁺)

These forms are converted into amino acids and proteins.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Define hydroponics and mention one application.

**Solution**

Hydroponics is the technique of growing plants in a nutrient solution without soil. It is widely used to study mineral nutrient requirements and deficiency symptoms.

---

### Example 2

Why is nitrogen considered an essential element?

**Solution**

Nitrogen is a component of amino acids, proteins, nucleic acids, and chlorophyll. Without nitrogen, plants cannot complete their life cycle.

---

### Example 3

What is the role of leghemoglobin in root nodules?

**Solution**

Leghemoglobin binds oxygen and maintains a low oxygen concentration inside root nodules, protecting the oxygen-sensitive nitrogenase enzyme.

---

### Example 4

Differentiate between nitrification and denitrification.

**Solution**

Nitrification converts ammonia into nitrate through the action of Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter, whereas denitrification converts nitrate into atmospheric nitrogen through bacteria such as Pseudomonas.

---

### Example 5

Why do deficiency symptoms of nitrogen first appear in older leaves?

**Solution**

Nitrogen is a mobile element. During deficiency, it is transported from older leaves to younger growing tissues, causing older leaves to show symptoms first.

---

### Example 6

State two functions of potassium in plants.

**Solution**

Potassium activates many enzymes and regulates the opening and closing of stomata, helping maintain osmotic balance.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **macronutrients** with **micronutrients** based on their importance rather than the quantity required.
- Assuming **iron** is a constituent of chlorophyll; it is required for chlorophyll synthesis but is **not** part of the chlorophyll molecule.
- Mixing up **mobile** and **immobile** mineral elements.
- Forgetting that **leghemoglobin** protects **nitrogenase** by maintaining a low oxygen concentration.
- Confusing **nitrification**, **ammonification**, and **denitrification**.
- Assuming plants absorb atmospheric nitrogen directly; most plants absorb nitrogen mainly as **nitrate** or **ammonium ions**.
- Forgetting the role of **Rhizobium** in symbiotic nitrogen fixation.
- Confusing **hydroponics** with **aeroponics**.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Plants require essential **mineral elements** for normal growth, development, and completion of their life cycle.
- Essential elements are classified into **macronutrients** and **micronutrients** based on the quantity required.
- **Hydroponics** is a useful technique for studying mineral nutrition and identifying nutrient deficiencies.
- Each mineral performs specific functions, including enzyme activation, chlorophyll formation, protein synthesis, membrane stability, and energy transfer.
- Deficiency symptoms such as **chlorosis**, **necrosis**, and **stunted growth** vary depending on the nutrient and its mobility within the plant.
- The **nitrogen cycle** involves nitrogen fixation, ammonification, nitrification, assimilation, and denitrification, ensuring the continuous availability of nitrogen in ecosystems.
- **Biological nitrogen fixation** is carried out by microorganisms such as **Rhizobium**, using the enzyme **nitrogenase**, which functions under low oxygen conditions maintained by **leghemoglobin**.
- Plants absorb nitrogen mainly in the form of **nitrate (NO₃⁻)** and **ammonium (NH₄⁺)** and convert it into amino acids and proteins.
- Understanding mineral nutrition is essential for improving agricultural productivity, maintaining soil fertility, and ensuring healthy plant growth.
