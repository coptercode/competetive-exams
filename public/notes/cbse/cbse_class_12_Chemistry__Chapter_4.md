# Introduction

Elements in which the last electron enters the **d-orbital** are called **d-block elements**, while elements in which the last electron enters the **f-orbital** are called **f-block elements**.

---

# 1. Position in Periodic Table

## d-Block Elements

Located between:

```
s-block and p-block
```

Groups:

```
3 to 12
```

---

## f-Block Elements

Located separately at the bottom of the periodic table.

Two series:

1. Lanthanoids
2. Actinoids

---

# 2. Transition Elements

## Definition

Elements having partially filled d-orbitals in their atoms or in one of their oxidation states are called transition elements.

---

## General Electronic Configuration

\[
(n-1)d^{1-10}ns^{0-2}
\]

---

## Examples

| Element | Configuration |
| ------- | ------------- |
| Sc      | [Ar]3d¹4s²    |
| Ti      | [Ar]3d²4s²    |
| Fe      | [Ar]3d⁶4s²    |
| Cu      | [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹   |

---

# 3. Exceptions in Electronic Configuration

Some elements show unusual configurations due to extra stability of half-filled and completely filled orbitals.

---

## Chromium

Expected:

\[
3d^4 4s^2
\]

Actual:

\[
3d^5 4s^1
\]

---

## Copper

Expected:

\[
3d^9 4s^2
\]

Actual:

\[
3d^{10}4s^1
\]

---

# 4. General Properties of Transition Elements

---

# A. Metallic Character

Transition metals are:

- Hard
- Strong
- Good conductors

---

# B. Variable Oxidation States

Due to similar energies of:

- ns electrons
- (n−1)d electrons

---

## Examples

### Iron

\[
Fe^{2+}, Fe^{3+}
\]

---

### Manganese

Shows:

\[
+2,+3,+4,+6,+7
\]

---

# C. Coloured Ions

## Reason

Presence of partially filled d-orbitals.

d-electrons absorb visible light and undergo d-d transition.

---

Examples:

| Ion   | Colour |
| ----- | ------ |
| Cu²⁺  | Blue   |
| Fe³⁺  | Yellow |
| MnO₄⁻ | Purple |

---

# D. Magnetic Properties

Due to presence of unpaired electrons.

---

## Types

### Paramagnetic

Contains unpaired electrons.

Example:

\[
Fe^{3+}
\]

---

### Diamagnetic

All electrons paired.

Example:

\[
Zn^{2+}
\]

---

## Magnetic Moment Formula

\[
\mu=\sqrt{n(n+2)}
\]

where:

n = number of unpaired electrons

Unit:

```
Bohr Magneton (BM)
```

---

# E. Catalytic Activity

Transition metals and compounds act as catalysts due to:

- Variable oxidation states.
- Formation of intermediate compounds.
- Surface adsorption.

---

Examples:

| Catalyst | Reaction        |
| -------- | --------------- |
| Fe       | Haber process   |
| V₂O₅     | Contact process |
| Ni       | Hydrogenation   |

---

# F. Formation of Alloys

Transition metals form alloys because of:

- Similar atomic sizes.
- Similar crystal structures.

---

Examples:

| Alloy           | Composition  |
| --------------- | ------------ |
| Brass           | Cu + Zn      |
| Bronze          | Cu + Sn      |
| Stainless steel | Fe + Cr + Ni |

---

# G. Interstitial Compounds

Small atoms occupy spaces between metal atoms.

---

Examples:

- TiC
- WC

---

Properties:

- Hard
- High melting point
- Conducting nature

---

# 5. Atomic and Ionic Radii

Across a transition series:

- Atomic radii decrease initially.
- Become almost constant later.

---

## Reason

Increase in nuclear charge is balanced by shielding effect of d-electrons.

---

# 6. Lanthanoids

## Definition

Elements in which 4f orbitals are progressively filled.

---

General configuration:

\[
[Xe]4f^{1-14}5d^{0-1}6s^2
\]

---

# Examples

- La
- Ce
- Nd
- Eu

---

# Common Oxidation State

\[
+3
\]

---

# 7. Lanthanoid Contraction

## Definition

Gradual decrease in atomic and ionic size of lanthanoids from La to Lu.

---

## Cause

Poor shielding ability of 4f electrons.

---

# Consequences

## 1. Similarity of Zr and Hf

Due to almost same size.

---

## 2. Difficulty in separation of lanthanoids

Because of similar properties.

---

## 3. Basicity decreases

From La(OH)₃ to Lu(OH)₃.

---

# 8. Actinoids

## Definition

Elements involving filling of 5f orbitals.

---

Configuration:

\[
[Rn]5f^{1-14}6d^{0-1}7s^2
\]

---

Examples:

- Th
- U
- Pu

---

# Properties

- All radioactive.
- Show variable oxidation states.
- Form coloured ions.

---

# Comparison: Lanthanoids vs Actinoids

| Lanthanoids               | Actinoids                 |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| 4f filling                | 5f filling                |
| Mostly +3 oxidation state | Multiple oxidation states |
| Less radioactive          | All radioactive           |
| Less reactive             | More reactive             |

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** Why do transition elements show variable oxidation states?

### Solution

Transition elements have similar energies of ns and (n−1)d orbitals. Both types of electrons can participate in bonding, resulting in different oxidation states.

---

## Example 2

**Question:** Why are transition metal ions coloured?

### Solution

Partially filled d-orbitals allow d-d electronic transitions. Absorption of visible light produces characteristic colours.

---

## Example 3

**Question:** Explain lanthanoid contraction.

### Solution

Lanthanoid contraction is the gradual decrease in atomic and ionic radii from La to Lu due to poor shielding by 4f electrons.

---

## Example 4

**Question:** Calculate magnetic moment of Fe³⁺.

Configuration:

\[
Fe^{3+}=3d^5
\]

Number of unpaired electrons:

\[
n=5
\]

\[
\mu=\sqrt{5(5+2)}
\]

\[
=\sqrt{35}
\]

\[
\boxed{5.92BM}
\]

---

## Example 5

**Question:** Why are transition metals good catalysts?

### Solution

They show variable oxidation states and form intermediate compounds, which provide alternate reaction pathways with lower activation energy.

# Common Mistakes

- Assuming all d-block elements are transition elements; elements like Zn, Cd, and Hg are not considered transition elements because they have completely filled d-orbitals.
- Forgetting that colour arises due to d-d transitions.
- Confusing lanthanoid contraction with normal atomic size decrease.
- Mixing oxidation states of lanthanoids and actinoids.
- Forgetting that all actinoids are radioactive.
- Using incorrect oxidation reactions for KMnO₄ and K₂Cr₂O₇.
- Confusing paramagnetic and diamagnetic behaviour.
- Forgetting that chromium and copper show exceptional electronic configurations.

---

# Chapter Summary

- d-block elements are transition elements with partially filled d-orbitals.
- Transition metals show variable oxidation states, coloured ions, magnetic behaviour, catalytic activity, alloy formation, and interstitial compound formation.
- Lanthanoids involve filling of 4f orbitals and show mainly +3 oxidation states.
- Actinoids involve filling of 5f orbitals and are radioactive with variable oxidation states.
- Lanthanoid contraction explains the gradual decrease in atomic size across the series.
- Potassium dichromate and potassium permanganate are important oxidizing agents.
- Magnetic properties depend on the number of unpaired electrons.
- Transition and inner transition elements have important industrial and biological applications.
