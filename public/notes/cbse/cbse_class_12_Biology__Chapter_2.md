# Introduction

Human reproduction is a **sexual mode of reproduction** involving the formation and fusion of male and female gametes. Fertilization results in the formation of a diploid zygote, which develops into an embryo and later into a fetus. Human reproduction is regulated by a complex interaction of hormones produced by the hypothalamus, pituitary gland, gonads, and placenta.

## Testes

- Located in the **scrotum** outside the abdominal cavity.
- Maintain a temperature about **2–2.5°C below body temperature**, essential for sperm production.

### Functions

- Produce sperms.
- Secrete the hormone **testosterone**.

---

# 2. Microscopic Structure of Testis

Each testis contains numerous **seminiferous tubules**.

---

## Seminiferous Tubules

Contain:

- Spermatogonia
- Sertoli cells

---

## Sertoli Cells

Functions:

- Nourish developing sperms.
- Form the blood-testis barrier.
- Secrete inhibin.

---

## Interstitial (Leydig) Cells

Located between seminiferous tubules.

Functions:

- Secrete testosterone.
- Stimulated by LH.

| Spermatogenesis      | Oogenesis                          |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------- |

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

**Question:** Why are testes located outside the abdominal cavity?

**Solution:**

The testes are present in the scrotum, where the temperature is about **2–2.5°C lower** than body temperature. This lower temperature is essential for normal spermatogenesis.

---

### Example 2

**Question:** Differentiate between Sertoli cells and Leydig cells.

**Solution:**

| Sertoli Cells                       | Leydig Cells                         |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Present inside seminiferous tubules | Present between seminiferous tubules |
| Nourish developing sperms           | Secrete testosterone                 |
| Stimulated mainly by FSH            | Stimulated mainly by LH              |

---

### Example 3

**Question:** Name the site of fertilization in humans.

**Solution:**

Fertilization normally occurs at the **ampullary-isthmic junction** of the fallopian tube, where the sperm fuses with the secondary oocyte to form a diploid zygote.

---

### Example 4

**Question:** What is the function of the corpus luteum?

**Solution:**

The corpus luteum is formed from the ruptured Graafian follicle after ovulation. It secretes **progesterone**, which maintains the uterine endometrium for implantation and early pregnancy.

---

### Example 5

**Question:** What is the importance of colostrum?

**Solution:**

Colostrum is the first milk secreted after childbirth. It is rich in proteins and antibodies, especially **IgA**, and provides **passive immunity** to the newborn against infections.

---

### Example 6

**Question:** Arrange the following stages in the correct sequence:

Blastocyst, Morula, Zygote, Embryo.

**Solution:**

Correct sequence:

```
Zygote

↓

Morula

↓

Blastocyst

↓

Embryo
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **seminiferous tubules** with **Leydig cells**; seminiferous tubules produce sperms, while Leydig cells produce testosterone.
- Assuming spermatogenesis and oogenesis begin at the same stage of life; oogenesis begins before birth, whereas spermatogenesis starts at puberty.
- Forgetting that fertilization occurs in the **ampullary-isthmic junction**, not in the uterus.
- Confusing **morula** with **blastocyst**; the blastocyst contains a trophoblast and an inner cell mass.
- Mixing up the functions of **FSH** and **LH** during gametogenesis.
- Incorrectly stating that prolactin causes milk ejection; **prolactin** stimulates milk production, while **oxytocin** causes milk ejection.
- Forgetting that **colostrum** provides passive immunity due to its high antibody content.
- Assuming implantation occurs immediately after fertilization; it generally occurs about **6–7 days** later.

# Chapter Summary

- The human reproductive system consists of specialized organs that produce gametes and facilitate fertilization and development.
- The **male reproductive system** includes the testes, accessory ducts, glands, and penis, while the **female reproductive system** includes the ovaries, fallopian tubes, uterus, cervix, and vagina.
- **Spermatogenesis** produces four functional sperms and begins at puberty, whereas **oogenesis** begins before birth and produces one functional ovum.
- The **menstrual cycle** consists of menstrual, follicular, ovulatory, and luteal phases regulated by **GnRH, FSH, LH, estrogen, and progesterone**.
- Fertilization occurs in the **ampullary-isthmic junction** of the fallopian tube, followed by cleavage, blastocyst formation, and implantation.
- The **placenta** supports fetal growth by facilitating nutrient exchange, gas exchange, waste removal, and hormone secretion.
- **Parturition** is regulated by oxytocin and other hormones, while **lactation** provides nutrition and passive immunity to the newborn through colostrum.
- Human reproduction is a coordinated process involving hormonal regulation, successful fertilization, embryonic development, pregnancy, childbirth, and postnatal nourishment.
