# Introduction

A **coordination compound** is a compound in which a central metal atom or ion is bonded to surrounding ions or molecules called ligands through coordinate bonds.

Examples:

\[
[Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}
\]

\[
K_4[Fe(CN)_6]
\]

---

# 1. Basic Terms

---

# A. Coordination Entity

A species containing:

- Central metal atom/ion
- Ligands attached to it

Example:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}
\]

---

# B. Central Metal Ion

The atom or ion to which ligands are attached.

Examples:

- Fe³⁺
- Co³⁺
- Cu²⁺

---

# C. Ligands

## Definition

Ions or molecules that donate electron pairs to the central metal atom.

---

## Examples

### Neutral Ligands

| Ligand | Name     |
| ------ | -------- |
| NH₃    | Ammine   |
| H₂O    | Aqua     |
| CO     | Carbonyl |

---

### Negative Ligands

| Ligand | Name    |
| ------ | ------- |
| Cl⁻    | Chloro  |
| CN⁻    | Cyano   |
| OH⁻    | Hydroxo |

---

# Types of Ligands

---

# 1. Monodentate Ligands

Donate one lone pair.

Examples:

- NH₃
- Cl⁻

---

# 2. Bidentate Ligands

Donate two lone pairs.

Examples:

- Ethylenediamine (en)
- Oxalate ion

---

# 3. Polydentate Ligands

Donate multiple lone pairs.

Example:

EDTA

---

# Chelating Ligands

Ligands forming ring structures with metal ions.

Example:

\[
[Cu(en)_2]^{2+}
\]

---

# 2. Coordination Number

## Definition

Number of ligand donor atoms directly attached to the central metal ion.

---

Examples:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}
\]

Coordination number:

\[
6
\]

---

\[
[Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2]
\]

Coordination number:

\[
4
\]

---

# 3. Werner's Theory

Proposed by:

```
Alfred Werner (1893)
```

---

# Main Postulates

## 1. Primary Valency

- Equal to oxidation state.
- Ionizable.

Example:

\[
CoCl_3
\]

Primary valency:

\[
+3
\]

---

## 2. Secondary Valency

- Equal to coordination number.
- Non-ionizable.
- Satisfied by ligands.

---

Example:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_6]Cl_3
\]

Primary valency:

\[
3
\]

Secondary valency:

\[
6
\]

---

# 4. IUPAC Nomenclature of Coordination Compounds

Rules:

---

## Rule 1

Name cation first, then anion.

Example:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_6]Cl_3
\]

Hexaamminecobalt(III) chloride

---

## Rule 2

Ligands are named alphabetically.

---

## Rule 3

Number of ligands:

| Number | Prefix |
| ------ | ------ |
| 2      | di     |
| 3      | tri    |
| 4      | tetra  |
| 5      | penta  |
| 6      | hexa   |

---

## Rule 4

Oxidation state of metal is written in Roman numerals.

---

## Rule 5

For anionic complexes, metal name ends with:

```
-ate
```

Examples:

Fe → ferrate

Cu → cuprate

---

# Examples

\[
K_4[Fe(CN)_6]
\]

Name:

Potassium hexacyanidoferrate(II)

---

\[
[Cu(NH_3)_4]SO_4
\]

Name:

Tetraamminecopper(II) sulfate

---

# 5. Isomerism in Coordination Compounds

Coordination compounds show two major types:

```
Isomerism

├── Structural Isomerism

└── Stereoisomerism
```

---

# A. Structural Isomerism

---

# 1. Ionisation Isomerism

Different ions are produced in solution.

Example:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_5Br]SO_4
\]

and

\[
[Co(NH_3)_5SO_4]Br
\]

---

# 2. Hydrate Isomerism

Different number of water molecules inside and outside coordination sphere.

Example:

\[
CrCl_3.6H_2O
\]

---

# 3. Linkage Isomerism

Occurs with ambidentate ligands.

Example:

NO₂⁻

Nitro:

\[
M-NO_2
\]

Nitrito:

\[
M-ONO
\]

---

# 4. Coordination Isomerism

Exchange of ligands between cationic and anionic complexes.

---

# B. Stereoisomerism

Same bonding but different arrangement.

---

# 1. Geometrical Isomerism

Different spatial arrangement.

---

Example:

\[
[Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2]
\]

cis:

```
NH3   Cl

 \    /

  Pt

 /    \

NH3   Cl
```

trans:

```
NH3   NH3

 \    /

  Pt

 /    \

Cl     Cl
```

---

# 2. Optical Isomerism

Non-superimposable mirror images.

Example:

\[
[Co(en)_3]^{3+}
\]

---

# 6. Valence Bond Theory (VBT)

Explains bonding in coordination compounds.

---

## Main Idea

Ligands donate lone pairs into empty hybrid orbitals of metal ion.

---

# Hybridization and Geometry

| Hybridization | Geometry        |
| ------------- | --------------- |
| sp            | Linear          |
| sp²           | Trigonal planar |
| sp³           | Tetrahedral     |
| dsp²          | Square planar   |
| d²sp³         | Octahedral      |

---

# Example:

\[
[Ni(CN)_4]^{2-}
\]

Hybridization:

\[
dsp^2
\]

Geometry:

Square planar

---

# 7. Inner Orbital and Outer Orbital Complexes

---

# Inner Orbital Complex

Uses inner d-orbitals.

Example:

\[
d^2sp^3
\]

---

# Outer Orbital Complex

Uses outer d-orbitals.

Example:

\[
sp^3d^2
\]

---

# 8. Magnetic Properties

Depends on:

- Number of unpaired electrons.

---

# Paramagnetic

Contains unpaired electrons.

Example:

\[
[FeCl_6]^{3-}
\]

---

# Diamagnetic

All electrons paired.

Example:

\[
[Ni(CN)_4]^{2-}
\]

---

# Magnetic Moment

\[
\mu=\sqrt{n(n+2)}
\]

where:

n = unpaired electrons

---

# 9. Crystal Field Theory (CFT)

Explains:

- Bonding
- Colour
- Magnetic behaviour

---

# Basic Idea

Ligands are treated as point charges.

They repel d-electrons of metal ion.

---

# Octahedral Complex

Five d-orbitals split into:

```
Higher energy

eg

-----

Lower energy

t2g
```

---

# Splitting Energy

\[
\Delta_o
\]

---

# Tetrahedral Complex

Splitting:

\[
\Delta_t
\]

Relation:

\[
\Delta_t=\frac{4}{9}\Delta_o
\]

---

# 10. Strong Field and Weak Field Ligands

---

# Weak Field Ligands

Cause less pairing.

Examples:

- Cl⁻
- F⁻

---

# Strong Field Ligands

Cause electron pairing.

Examples:

- CN⁻
- CO
- NH₃

---

# Spectrochemical Series

Increasing field strength:

\[
I^-<Br^-<Cl^-<F^-<OH^-<H_2O<NH_3<CN^-<CO
\]

---

# 11. Colour in Coordination Compounds

Due to:

```
d-d transition
```

---

Example:

\[
[Cu(H_2O)_6]^{2+}
\]

appears blue.

| ------------------ | ------------------- |
| More pairing       | Less pairing        |
| Low spin complexes | High spin complexes |
| CN⁻, CO            | F⁻, Cl⁻             |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

Find oxidation state of Co in:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_6]Cl_3
\]

### Solution

NH₃ is neutral.

Complex charge:

\[
+3
\]

Therefore:

\[
Co=+3
\]

---

## Example 2

Name:

\[
K_4[Fe(CN)_6]
\]

### Solution

Metal:

Fe

Ligand:

CN⁻ = cyano

Number:

6 = hexa

Name:

\[
\boxed{\text{Potassium hexacyanidoferrate(II)}}
\]

---

## Example 3

Why is:

\[
[Ni(CN)_4]^{2-}
\]

diamagnetic?

### Solution

CN⁻ is a strong field ligand. It causes pairing of electrons, leaving no unpaired electrons. Therefore the complex is diamagnetic.

---

## Example 4

Calculate magnetic moment of Fe³⁺.

Configuration:

\[
3d^5
\]

Unpaired electrons:

\[
n=5
\]

\[
\mu=\sqrt{5(7)}
\]

\[
=5.92BM
\]

---

## Example 5

Explain optical isomerism.

### Solution

Optical isomerism occurs when complexes exist as non-superimposable mirror images and rotate plane polarized light.

Example:

\[
[Co(en)_3]^{3+}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing coordination number with oxidation state.
- Forgetting that ligands donate lone pairs to metal ions.
- Using incorrect IUPAC prefixes for ligand numbers.
- Forgetting that anionic complexes end with "-ate".
- Mixing strong field and weak field ligands.
- Assuming all coordination compounds are coloured; colour depends on d-electron transitions.
- Confusing geometrical isomerism with optical isomerism.
- Forgetting that magnetic behaviour depends on unpaired electrons.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Coordination compounds contain a central metal ion surrounded by ligands.
- Werner's theory explains primary and secondary valencies.
- Proper IUPAC naming requires correct ligand names, prefixes, and oxidation states.
- Coordination compounds show structural and stereoisomerism.
- Valence Bond Theory explains hybridization and magnetic properties.
- Crystal Field Theory explains d-orbital splitting, colour, and high-spin/low-spin behaviour.
- Strong field ligands cause electron pairing, while weak field ligands do not.
- Coordination compounds have important biological, medical, and industrial applications.
