# Introduction

Living organisms continuously respond to changes in their surroundings. These changes are called **stimuli**, and the responses help organisms survive. The processes that regulate and coordinate these responses are known as **control and coordination**.

In animals, coordination is brought about by the **nervous system** and the **endocrine system (hormones)**. In plants, coordination occurs through **plant hormones** and various types of movements.

---

# 1. Stimulus and Response

A **stimulus** is any change in the environment that causes an organism to react.

A **response** is the reaction of an organism to a stimulus.

### Examples

| Stimulus     | Response              |
| ------------ | --------------------- |
| Bright light | Closing of eyes       |
| Heat         | Pulling the hand away |
| Touch        | Mimosa leaves fold    |
| Food smell   | Secretion of saliva   |

---

# 2. Nervous System

The **nervous system** controls and coordinates body activities by transmitting electrical impulses.

### Main Parts

- Central Nervous System (CNS)
- Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)

---

## Central Nervous System (CNS)

The CNS consists of:

- Brain
- Spinal Cord

It receives information, processes it, and sends appropriate responses.

---

## Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)

The PNS consists of nerves that connect the CNS with different parts of the body.

It includes:

- Sensory nerves
- Motor nerves

---

# 3. Neuron (Nerve Cell)

A **neuron** is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system.

### Structure of a Neuron

```
      Dendrites
          /|\
         / | \
        /  |  \
      Cell Body
          |
        Axon
          |
   Axon Terminals
```

### Parts and Functions

| Part           | Function                                  |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Dendrites      | Receive nerve impulses                    |
| Cell Body      | Contains nucleus and cytoplasm            |
| Axon           | Carries impulses away from the cell body  |
| Axon Terminals | Pass impulses to the next neuron or organ |

---

# 4. Reflex Action

A **reflex action** is a quick, automatic, and involuntary response to a stimulus.

### Examples

- Pulling the hand away from a hot object.
- Blinking of eyes.
- Sneezing.

---

## Reflex Arc

A **reflex arc** is the pathway followed by a nerve impulse during a reflex action.

### Reflex Arc Pathway

```
Stimulus
   ↓
Receptor
   ↓
Sensory Neuron
   ↓
Spinal Cord
   ↓
Motor Neuron
   ↓
Effector (Muscle)
   ↓
Response
```

### Importance

- Provides rapid protection from injury.
- Reduces response time.
- Does not require immediate involvement of the brain.

---

# 5. Human Brain

The **brain** is the main coordinating centre of the nervous system.

It is protected by:

- Skull (cranium)
- Three protective membranes (meninges)
- Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)

---

## Main Parts of the Brain

### (A) Forebrain

Functions:

- Thinking
- Intelligence
- Memory
- Learning
- Decision-making
- Interpretation of sensory information

---

### (B) Midbrain

Functions:

- Controls visual and auditory reflexes.
- Connects the forebrain and hindbrain.

---

### (C) Hindbrain

Includes:

- Cerebellum
- Pons
- Medulla Oblongata

#### Cerebellum

- Maintains body balance.
- Coordinates muscular movements.

#### Medulla Oblongata

Controls involuntary actions such as:

- Heartbeat
- Breathing
- Blood pressure
- Swallowing

---

# 6. Coordination in Plants

Plants do not have nerves or muscles, yet they respond to stimuli through **plant hormones**.

---

# 7. Plant Hormones (Phytohormones)

Plant hormones are chemical substances that regulate growth and development.

---

## (A) Auxins

Functions:

- Promote cell elongation.
- Control phototropism.
- Stimulate root formation.

---

## (B) Gibberellins

Functions:

- Promote stem elongation.
- Stimulate seed germination.
- Increase fruit size.

---

## (C) Cytokinins

Functions:

- Promote cell division.
- Delay ageing of leaves.
- Stimulate shoot growth.

---

## (D) Abscisic Acid (ABA)

Functions:

- Inhibits growth.
- Causes dormancy in seeds and buds.
- Closes stomata during water shortage.

---

# 8. Plant Movements

Plant movements are responses to external stimuli.

---

## (A) Tropic Movements

**Tropic movements** are directional growth movements towards or away from a stimulus.

### Types

#### Phototropism

Growth in response to light.

Example:

- Shoot grows towards light.

---

#### Geotropism

Growth in response to gravity.

Example:

- Roots grow downward.
- Shoots grow upward.

---

#### Hydrotropism

Growth in response to water.

Example:

- Roots grow towards moisture.

---

#### Thigmotropism

Growth in response to touch.

Example:

- Tendrils of climbers coil around a support.

---

## (B) Nastic Movements

**Nastic movements** are non-directional responses to stimuli.

### Example

Touch-me-not plant (**Mimosa pudica**)

- Leaves fold when touched.

---

## Difference Between Tropic and Nastic Movements

| Tropic Movement                  | Nastic Movement                      |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Directional                      | Non-directional                      |
| Involves growth                  | Usually does not involve growth      |
| Depends on direction of stimulus | Independent of direction of stimulus |
| Usually slow                     | Usually faster                       |

---

# 9. Endocrine System

The **endocrine system** consists of glands that secrete hormones directly into the bloodstream.

Hormones regulate:

- Growth
- Metabolism
- Reproduction
- Stress response
- Blood sugar levels

---

# 10. Major Endocrine Glands and Hormones

| Gland     | Hormone                | Function                                            |
| --------- | ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Pituitary | Growth Hormone         | Controls body growth                                |
| Thyroid   | Thyroxine              | Regulates metabolism                                |
| Pancreas  | Insulin                | Controls blood glucose level                        |
| Adrenal   | Adrenaline             | Prepares body for emergencies                       |
| Testes    | Testosterone           | Male secondary sexual characters                    |
| Ovaries   | Estrogen, Progesterone | Female secondary sexual characters and reproduction |

---

## Adrenaline

Also called the **emergency hormone**.

Functions:

- Increases heartbeat.
- Increases breathing rate.
- Raises blood pressure.
- Increases blood supply to muscles.

This prepares the body for **fight or flight** situations.

---

## Thyroxine

Produced by the **thyroid gland**.

Functions:

- Regulates metabolism.
- Supports growth and development.

### Iodine

Iodine is necessary for the production of thyroxine.

Deficiency of iodine causes:

```
Goitre
```

---

## Insulin

Produced by the **pancreas**.

Functions:

- Maintains normal blood glucose level.

Deficiency of insulin causes:

```
Diabetes Mellitus
```

# Solved Examples

What is a reflex action?

**Solution**

A reflex action is a quick, automatic, and involuntary response to a stimulus without conscious thought.

---

### Example 2

Name the structural and functional unit of the nervous system.

**Solution**

The **neuron**.

---

### Example 3

Which part of the brain controls body balance?

**Solution**

The **cerebellum**.

---

### Example 4

Name the hormone responsible for regulating blood sugar.

**Solution**

**Insulin**, produced by the pancreas.

---

### Example 5

Which plant hormone promotes cell elongation?

**Solution**

**Auxin**.

---

Differentiate between phototropism and thigmotropism.

**Solution**

- **Thigmotropism** is growth in response to touch.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **reflex action** with voluntary action.
- Assuming the brain alone controls all reflexes (most reflex actions are coordinated by the spinal cord).
- Mixing up the functions of the **forebrain**, **midbrain**, and **hindbrain**.
- Confusing **tropic movements** with **nastic movements**.
- Assuming all plant movements involve growth.
- Confusing **auxins** with **abscisic acid**.
- Mixing up the functions of **insulin**, **thyroxine**, and **adrenaline**.
- Forgetting that hormones are transported through the **bloodstream**, not nerves.

# Chapter Summary

- **Control and coordination** enable organisms to respond appropriately to internal and external stimuli.
- The **nervous system** consists of the **brain, spinal cord,** and **nerves**, with the **neuron** as its basic structural and functional unit.
- **Reflex actions** are rapid, involuntary responses coordinated mainly by the **spinal cord** through a **reflex arc**.
- The **human brain** is divided into the **forebrain, midbrain,** and **hindbrain**, each performing specific functions such as thinking, coordination, and control of involuntary activities.
- Plants coordinate their responses through **plant hormones**, including **auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins,** and **abscisic acid**.
- Plant movements are classified into **tropic movements** (directional growth responses) and **nastic movements** (non-directional responses).
- The **endocrine system** regulates body functions using hormones such as **adrenaline, thyroxine,** and **insulin**, which control stress response, metabolism, and blood sugar levels respectively.
- Proper coordination between the nervous and endocrine systems ensures the smooth functioning and survival of living organisms.
