# Introduction

The study of atoms began with attempts to understand the structure and stability of matter.

Major atomic models:

1. Thomson's model
2. Rutherford's model
3. Bohr's model

---

# 1. Rutherford's Atomic Model

## Alpha Particle Scattering Experiment

Performed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ernest Rutherford}}
\]

---

# Experimental Setup

A beam of alpha particles was directed towards a thin gold foil.

Observations were recorded on a fluorescent screen.

---

# Observations

1. Most alpha particles passed straight through.

2. Some were slightly deflected.

3. Very few bounced back.

---

# Conclusions

## 1. Atom is Mostly Empty Space

Most particles passed without deflection.

---

## 2. Positive Charge is Concentrated in Nucleus

A small, dense nucleus exists at the centre.

---

## 3. Electrons Revolve Around Nucleus

Like planets around the Sun.

---

# Limitations of Rutherford Model

## 1. Stability Problem

According to electromagnetic theory, accelerating electrons should radiate energy and fall into the nucleus.

---

## 2. Spectral Problem

Could not explain discrete atomic spectra.

---

# 2. Bohr's Atomic Model

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Niels Bohr}}
\]

---

# Bohr's Postulates

---

## Postulate 1: Stationary Orbits

Electrons revolve in certain stable orbits without radiating energy.

---

## Postulate 2: Quantisation of Angular Momentum

Only those orbits are allowed where:

\[
\boxed{
mvr=\frac{nh}{2\pi}
}
\]

where:

- n = principal quantum number

---

## Postulate 3: Radiation During Transition

Energy is emitted or absorbed when an electron jumps between energy levels.

\[
\boxed{
h\nu=E_2-E_1
}
\]

---

# 3. Radius of Bohr Orbit

For hydrogen atom:

\[
\boxed{
r_n=n^2a_0
}
\]

where:

\[
a_0=0.529\AA
\]

is Bohr radius.

---

For hydrogen-like atoms:

\[
\boxed{
r_n=
\frac{n^2a_0}{Z}
}
\]

where:

Z = atomic number

---

# 4. Velocity of Electron in Orbit

For hydrogen:

\[
\boxed{
v_n=
\frac{2.18\times10^6}{n}
m/s
}
\]

---

For hydrogen-like atoms:

\[
\boxed{
v_n\propto\frac Zn
}
\]

---

# 5. Energy of Electron in Orbit

Total energy:

\[
\boxed{
E_n=
-\frac{13.6Z^2}{n^2}eV
}
\]

---

For hydrogen:

\[
\boxed{
E_n=
-\frac{13.6}{n^2}eV
}
\]

---

# 6. Hydrogen Spectrum

When electrons transition between energy levels, electromagnetic radiation is emitted.

---

# Rydberg Formula

\[
\boxed{
\frac1\lambda
=

R
\left(
\frac1{n_1^2}
-

\frac1{n_2^2}
\right)
}
\]

where:

- R = Rydberg constant
- \(n_2>n_1\)

# 8. Ionisation Energy

## Definition

Minimum energy required to remove an electron from the ground state of an atom.

---

For hydrogen:

\[
\boxed{
13.6eV
}
\]

---

# 9. Excitation Energy

## Definition

Energy required to excite an electron from a lower energy state to a higher energy state.

---

# 10. De Broglie Explanation of Bohr Orbit

According to de Broglie:

Electron behaves like a wave.

For stable orbit:

\[
\boxed{
2\pi r=n\lambda
}
\]

---

This means:

Only those orbits are allowed where electron wave forms a standing wave.

---

## Example 2

**Question:** Why could Rutherford model not explain atomic stability?

### Solution

According to electromagnetic theory, accelerating electrons should continuously radiate energy and spiral into the nucleus.

---

## Example 3

**Question:** Which series lies in visible region?

### Solution

Balmer series.

Transitions:

\[
n_2\rightarrow2
\]

---

## Example 4

**Question:** What is ionisation energy of hydrogen?

### Solution

Energy required to remove electron from:

\[
n=1
\]

to infinity.

\[
\boxed{13.6eV}
\]

---

## Example 5

**Question:** Why are only certain orbits allowed in Bohr model?

### Solution

Because electron waves must form standing waves:

\[
2\pi r=n\lambda
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing atomic number Z with atomic mass.
- Forgetting negative sign in energy equation.
- Mixing spectral series transitions.
- Using Bohr model for multi-electron atoms.
- Confusing excitation energy with ionisation energy.
- Forgetting that electron energy increases with increasing n.
- Using wavelength and frequency relations incorrectly.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Rutherford discovered the nuclear structure of atoms.
- Bohr explained atomic stability using quantised energy levels.
- Electrons occupy only certain allowed orbits.
- Energy of hydrogen atom levels is:

\[
E_n=-\frac{13.6}{n^2}eV
\]

- Spectral lines arise due to transitions between energy levels.
- Hydrogen spectrum consists of Lyman, Balmer, Paschen, Brackett, and Pfund series.
- de Broglie’s hypothesis explains the stability of Bohr orbits through standing electron waves.
