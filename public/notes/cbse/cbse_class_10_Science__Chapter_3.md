# Introduction

Elements are broadly classified into **metals** and **non-metals** based on their physical and chemical properties. Metals are generally strong, lustrous, malleable, and good conductors of heat and electricity, while non-metals usually have opposite properties. This chapter explains the properties of metals and non-metals, the **reactivity series**, **ionic compounds**, **metallurgical processes**, and methods of **preventing corrosion**.

---

# 1. Metals

**Metals** are elements that readily lose electrons to form **positive ions (cations)**.

### Examples

- Iron (Fe)
- Copper (Cu)
- Aluminium (Al)
- Zinc (Zn)
- Sodium (Na)
- Magnesium (Mg)
- Silver (Ag)
- Gold (Au)

---

# 2. Physical Properties of Metals

| Property           | Description                               |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| Lustrous           | Shiny surface                             |
| Malleable          | Can be beaten into thin sheets            |
| Ductile            | Can be drawn into wires                   |
| Sonorous           | Produce sound when struck                 |
| Conductors         | Good conductors of heat and electricity   |
| Hard               | Most metals are hard and strong           |
| High Melting Point | Most have high melting and boiling points |

### Exceptions

- **Mercury (Hg)** is a liquid metal at room temperature.
- **Sodium (Na)** and **Potassium (K)** are soft and can be cut with a knife.
- **Gallium (Ga)** and **Caesium (Cs)** have low melting points.

---

# 3. Physical Properties of Non-Metals

| Property          | Description                                               |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dull              | Non-lustrous (except iodine)                              |
| Brittle           | Break easily when hammered                                |
| Non-sonorous      | Do not produce ringing sound                              |
| Poor Conductors   | Poor conductors of heat and electricity (except graphite) |
| Low Density       | Generally lighter than metals                             |
| Low Melting Point | Most have low melting and boiling points                  |

### Exceptions

- **Graphite** conducts electricity.
- **Diamond** is extremely hard.
- **Iodine** is lustrous.

---

# 4. Chemical Properties of Metals

## (A) Reaction with Oxygen

Metals react with oxygen to form **metal oxides**.

### General Equation

```
Metal + Oxygen → Metal Oxide
```

### Example

```
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO
```

Metal oxides are generally **basic** in nature.

---

## (B) Amphoteric Oxides

Some metal oxides react with both acids and bases.

Examples:

- Aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃)
- Zinc oxide (ZnO)

### Example

```
ZnO + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂O
```

```
ZnO + 2NaOH → Na₂ZnO₂ + H₂O
```

---

## (C) Reaction with Water

Different metals react differently with water.

| Metal     | Reaction with Water          |
| --------- | ---------------------------- |
| Potassium | Reacts violently             |
| Sodium    | Reacts vigorously            |
| Calcium   | Reacts readily               |
| Magnesium | Reacts slowly with hot water |
| Iron      | Reacts with steam            |
| Copper    | No reaction                  |

### Example

```
2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂↑
```

---

## (D) Reaction with Acids

Metals react with dilute acids to form **salt** and **hydrogen gas**.

### General Equation

```
Metal + Acid → Salt + Hydrogen
```

### Example

```
Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂↑
```

**Note:** Copper, silver, gold, and platinum do not react with dilute acids.

---

## (E) Reaction with Metal Salt Solutions

A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its salt solution.

### Example

```
Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
```

Observation:

- Blue colour of copper sulphate fades.
- Copper is deposited.

---

# 5. Chemical Properties of Non-Metals

Non-metals generally gain electrons to form **negative ions (anions)**.

### Reaction with Oxygen

Non-metals form **acidic** or **neutral oxides**.

### Example

```
C + O₂ → CO₂
```

Carbon dioxide is an acidic oxide.

---

### Reaction with Hydrogen

Some non-metals combine with hydrogen to form covalent compounds.

Example

```
H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl
```

---

# 6. Reactivity Series

The **reactivity series** is the arrangement of metals in decreasing order of their reactivity.

| Reactivity Order |
| ---------------- |
| Potassium (K)    |
| Sodium (Na)      |
| Calcium (Ca)     |
| Magnesium (Mg)   |
| Aluminium (Al)   |
| Zinc (Zn)        |
| Iron (Fe)        |
| Lead (Pb)        |
| Hydrogen (H)     |
| Copper (Cu)      |
| Mercury (Hg)     |
| Silver (Ag)      |
| Gold (Au)        |

### Uses of the Reactivity Series

- Predict displacement reactions.
- Determine extraction methods.
- Compare metal reactivity.

---

# 7. Displacement Reactions

A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its compound.

### Example

```
Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu
```

Since iron is more reactive than copper, it displaces copper.

---

# 8. Ionic Compounds

**Ionic compounds** are formed by the transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal.

### Example

Formation of sodium chloride

```
Na → Na⁺ + e⁻
```

```
Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻
```

```
Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl
```

---

## Properties of Ionic Compounds

- Hard and brittle.
- High melting and boiling points.
- Conduct electricity in molten or aqueous state.
- Usually soluble in water.
- Consist of oppositely charged ions held together by electrostatic forces.

---

# 9. Metallurgy

**Metallurgy** is the process of extracting metals from their ores and refining them.

---

## (A) Roasting

Heating sulphide ores in the presence of excess air.

### Example

```
2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂
```

---

## (B) Calcination

Heating carbonate or hydrated ores in the absence or limited supply of air.

### Example

```
ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂
```

---

## (C) Reduction

Metal oxides are reduced to metals using reducing agents like carbon.

### Example

```
ZnO + C → Zn + CO
```

---

## (D) Refining

Purification of metals after extraction.

### Electrolytic Refining

Used for metals such as:

- Copper
- Zinc
- Silver
- Gold

---

# 10. Corrosion

**Corrosion** is the gradual destruction of metals due to reaction with air, moisture, or chemicals.

### Example

Rusting of iron

```
4Fe + 3O₂ + xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O
```

---

# 11. Prevention of Corrosion

## (A) Painting

Protects metal from air and moisture.

---

## (B) Oiling and Greasing

Forms a protective layer on the metal surface.

---

## (C) Galvanization

Coating iron with zinc to prevent rusting.

---

## (D) Electroplating

Depositing a thin layer of one metal over another using electricity.

---

## (E) Alloying

An **alloy** is a homogeneous mixture of two or more metals or a metal with a non-metal.

### Examples

| Alloy           | Composition              | Uses                                   |
| --------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| Brass           | Copper + Zinc            | Utensils, musical instruments          |
| Bronze          | Copper + Tin             | Statues, medals                        |
| Steel           | Iron + Carbon            | Construction, machinery                |
| Stainless Steel | Iron + Chromium + Nickel | Kitchen utensils, surgical instruments |
| Solder          | Lead + Tin               | Electrical connections                 |

---

# 12. Uses of Metals and Non-Metals

## Uses of Metals

- Electrical wiring.
- Construction.
- Transport.
- Machinery.
- Cooking utensils.
- Jewellery.

---

## Uses of Non-Metals

- Oxygen for respiration.
- Nitrogen in fertilizers.
- Chlorine for water purification.
- Carbon in fuels and pencils.
- Sulphur in medicines and vulcanization.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Why is sodium stored under kerosene?

**Solution**

Sodium reacts vigorously with oxygen and moisture. Kerosene prevents contact with air and water.

---

### Example 2

Identify the type of oxide formed by magnesium.

**Solution**

```
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO
```

**Answer:** Basic oxide.

---

### Example 3

What happens when zinc is added to copper sulphate solution?

**Solution**

```
Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
```

Zinc displaces copper because it is more reactive.

---

### Example 4

Why do ionic compounds have high melting points?

**Solution**

Strong electrostatic forces between oppositely charged ions require a large amount of energy to break.

---

### Example 5

Differentiate between roasting and calcination.

**Solution**

- **Roasting:** Heating sulphide ores in excess air.
- **Calcination:** Heating carbonate ores in the absence or limited supply of air.

---

### Example 6

What is galvanization?

**Solution**

Galvanization is the process of coating iron with zinc to protect it from corrosion.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **malleability** with **ductility**.
- Assuming all metals react with water in the same way.
- Forgetting that **graphite** conducts electricity.
- Confusing **roasting** with **calcination**.
- Writing ionic compounds as sharing electrons instead of transferring electrons.
- Assuming all metal oxides are basic (some are amphoteric).
- Confusing galvanization with electroplating.
- Forgetting that metals below hydrogen in the reactivity series do not displace hydrogen from dilute acids.

# Key Definitions

| Term                  | Definition                                                                |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Metal                 | Element that loses electrons to form positive ions.                       |
| Non-metal             | Element that gains electrons to form negative ions.                       |
| Reactivity Series     | Arrangement of metals in decreasing order of reactivity.                  |
| Displacement Reaction | A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its compound.  |
| Ionic Bond            | Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.                 |
| Ionic Compound        | Compound formed by transfer of electrons between a metal and a non-metal. |
| Metallurgy            | Extraction and purification of metals from ores.                          |
| Roasting              | Heating sulphide ores in excess air.                                      |
| Calcination           | Heating carbonate ores in limited or no air.                              |
| Refining              | Purification of extracted metals.                                         |
| Corrosion             | Gradual destruction of metals due to environmental reactions.             |
| Galvanization         | Coating iron with zinc to prevent rusting.                                |
| Alloy                 | Homogeneous mixture of metals or a metal and a non-metal.                 |
| Amphoteric Oxide      | Oxide that reacts with both acids and bases.                              |

# Chapter Summary

- **Metals** are generally lustrous, malleable, ductile, sonorous, and good conductors of heat and electricity, whereas **non-metals** usually show opposite properties.
- Metals react with **oxygen, water, acids,** and **salt solutions**, while non-metals generally form acidic or neutral oxides.
- The **reactivity series** helps predict the reactivity of metals and displacement reactions.
- **Ionic compounds** are formed by the transfer of electrons from metals to non-metals and have high melting points and good electrical conductivity in molten or aqueous states.
- **Metallurgy** involves the extraction of metals through processes such as **roasting, calcination, reduction,** and **refining**.
- **Corrosion** damages metals, especially iron, and can be prevented by **painting, oiling, galvanization, electroplating,** and **alloying**.
- **Alloys** improve the properties of metals and are widely used in construction, transportation, electrical applications, and household items.
