# Introduction

Food provides energy and raw materials for growth, repair, and maintenance of the body. However, most food substances are complex and cannot be absorbed directly. They must first be broken down into simpler, soluble molecules by the process of **digestion**.

The **digestive system** consists of the **alimentary canal** and **associated digestive glands**, which work together to digest food, absorb nutrients, and eliminate undigested waste.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Why is hydrochloric acid secreted in the stomach?

**Solution**

Hydrochloric acid provides an acidic medium for the activation of pepsinogen into pepsin, kills harmful microorganisms, and maintains the optimum pH for gastric enzymes.

---

### Example 2

Differentiate between bile and pancreatic juice.

**Solution**

Bile contains no digestive enzymes and mainly emulsifies fats, whereas pancreatic juice contains enzymes that digest carbohydrates, proteins, fats, and nucleic acids.

---

### Example 3

What is the role of villi in digestion?

**Solution**

Villi increase the surface area of the small intestine, enabling efficient absorption of digested nutrients into blood capillaries and lacteals.

---

### Example 4

How are fats digested in the small intestine?

**Solution**

Bile salts first emulsify fats into tiny droplets, increasing the surface area. Pancreatic lipase then breaks them down into fatty acids and glycerol, which are absorbed through the lacteals.

---

### Example 5

What is peristalsis?

**Solution**

Peristalsis is the wave-like contraction and relaxation of the muscles of the alimentary canal that pushes food forward through the digestive tract.

---

### Example 6

Why are lacteals important?

**Solution**

Lacteals are lymphatic vessels present inside intestinal villi. They absorb fatty acids, glycerol, and fat-soluble vitamins before transporting them into the lymphatic system.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **digestion** with **absorption**; digestion breaks complex food into simpler molecules, whereas absorption transfers these molecules into blood or lymph.
- Assuming **bile contains digestive enzymes**; bile contains bile salts and pigments but **no digestive enzymes**.
- Forgetting that **pepsinogen** is inactive and is activated by **hydrochloric acid** to form pepsin.
- Mixing up the functions of the **liver** and **pancreas**.
- Assuming digestion is completed in the stomach; most digestion and nearly all absorption occur in the **small intestine**.
- Confusing **villi** with **microvilli**.
- Forgetting that **fatty acids and glycerol** are absorbed through **lacteals**, while glucose and amino acids enter **blood capillaries**.
- Assuming the adult human dental formula represents the total number of teeth on both jaws together; **2123/2123** represents one half of the upper and lower jaws.

# Chapter Summary

- The **digestive system** consists of the alimentary canal and associated digestive glands, which work together to digest food, absorb nutrients, and eliminate waste.
- Digestion begins in the **mouth**, where salivary amylase initiates starch digestion, continues in the **stomach** with protein digestion by pepsin, and is completed in the **small intestine** through the action of pancreatic and intestinal enzymes.
- The **liver** produces bile, which emulsifies fats, while the **pancreas** secretes enzymes for the digestion of carbohydrates, proteins, fats, and nucleic acids.
- The **small intestine**, with its **villi** and **microvilli**, provides a large surface area for efficient absorption of nutrients.
- Glucose and amino acids are absorbed into **blood capillaries**, whereas fatty acids, glycerol, and fat-soluble vitamins enter the **lacteals**.
- Absorbed nutrients are utilized by body cells through **assimilation**, and undigested food is eliminated by **egestion**.
- Disorders such as **indigestion**, **constipation**, **vomiting**, **jaundice**, and **diarrhoea** affect the normal functioning of the digestive system.
- Efficient digestion and absorption ensure a continuous supply of energy and nutrients necessary for growth, repair, and maintenance of the human body.
