# Introduction

The transport of oxygen, nutrients, hormones, metabolic wastes, and other substances is essential for maintaining life. In humans, this transport is carried out by the **circulatory system**, which consists of **blood**, **blood vessels**, **heart**, and **lymph**.

The circulatory system ensures the maintenance of **homeostasis** by supplying nutrients to tissues, removing waste products, and regulating body temperature.

---

# 1. Circulatory System

The human circulatory system consists of:

```
Circulatory System
│
├── Heart
├── Blood
├── Blood Vessels
└── Lymphatic System
```

Humans possess a **closed circulatory system** and **double circulation**.

---

# 2. Blood

Blood is a fluid connective tissue.

### Composition

```
Blood
│
├── Plasma (≈55%)
└── Formed Elements (≈45%)
```

---

# 3. Plasma

Plasma is a straw-coloured fluid.

### Composition

- Water (90–92%)
- Plasma proteins
- Mineral salts
- Nutrients
- Hormones
- Gases
- Waste products

---

## Plasma Proteins

| Protein    | Function                   |
| ---------- | -------------------------- |
| Albumin    | Maintains osmotic pressure |
| Globulins  | Immunity                   |
| Fibrinogen | Blood clotting             |

---

# 4. Formed Elements

These include:

- Red Blood Cells (RBCs)
- White Blood Cells (WBCs)
- Platelets

---

# 5. Red Blood Cells (Erythrocytes)

### Characteristics

- Biconcave discs.
- No nucleus in mammals.
- Contain haemoglobin.
- Life span: about **120 days**.
- Produced in **red bone marrow**.
- Destroyed mainly in the **spleen** and **liver**.

---

## Functions

- Transport oxygen.
- Transport a small amount of carbon dioxide.

---

## Haemoglobin

Normal haemoglobin level:

- Men: about **13–17 g/dL**
- Women: about **12–15 g/dL**

---

# 6. White Blood Cells (Leucocytes)

White blood cells protect the body against infection.

They are divided into:

```
WBCs
│
├── Granulocytes
└── Agranulocytes
```

---

## Granulocytes

### Neutrophils

- Most abundant WBCs.
- Phagocytic.

---

### Eosinophils

- Defend against parasites.
- Involved in allergic reactions.

---

### Basophils

- Release histamine and heparin.
- Participate in inflammatory responses.

---

## Agranulocytes

### Lymphocytes

Types:

- B lymphocytes
- T lymphocytes

Functions:

- Immunity.
- Antibody production (B cells).
- Cell-mediated immunity (T cells).

---

### Monocytes

- Largest WBCs.
- Highly phagocytic.
- Differentiate into macrophages in tissues.

---

# 7. Platelets (Thrombocytes)

### Characteristics

- Cell fragments.
- Produced from megakaryocytes.
- Life span: about **7–10 days**.

---

## Functions

- Blood clotting.
- Repair of damaged blood vessels.

---

# 10. Blood Clotting

Blood clotting prevents excessive blood loss.

---

## Steps

```
Injury
   │
Platelet Activation
   │
Thromboplastin
   │
Prothrombin
   │
Thrombin

Fibrinogen
```

# 11. Lymph

Lymph is a colourless tissue fluid.

### Characteristics

- No RBCs.
- Fewer proteins than plasma.
- Rich in lymphocytes.

---

## Functions

- Returns excess tissue fluid to blood.
- Absorbs fats from intestinal lacteals.
- Provides immune defence.

---

# 16. Cardiac Output

Cardiac Output is the volume of blood pumped by one ventricle in one minute.

Formula

```
Cardiac Output

= Stroke Volume × Heart Rate
```

Average values

- Stroke Volume = **70 mL**
- Heart Rate = **72 beats/min**

Cardiac Output ≈ **5 litres/min**

# 18. Blood Vessels

---

## Arteries

Characteristics

- Carry blood away from the heart.
- Thick elastic walls.
- High pressure.
- Usually oxygenated (except pulmonary artery).

---

## Veins

Characteristics

- Carry blood toward the heart.
- Thin walls.
- Valves present.
- Usually deoxygenated (except pulmonary veins).

---

## Capillaries

Characteristics

- One-cell-thick walls.
- Exchange of substances.
- Connect arteries and veins.

---

# Comparison

| Arteries         | Veins                    |
| ---------------- | ------------------------ |
| Thick walls      | Thin walls               |
| High pressure    | Low pressure             |
| No valves        | Valves present           |
| Carry blood away | Carry blood toward heart |

---

# 19. Double Circulation

Blood passes through the heart twice during one complete circulation.

---

## Pulmonary Circulation

```
Right Ventricle
      │
Lungs
      │
Left Atrium
```

---

## Systemic Circulation

```
Left Ventricle
      │
Body
      │
Right Atrium
```

---

## Advantages

- Complete separation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood.
- Efficient oxygen supply.
- High metabolic efficiency.

---

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Why is the SA node called the natural pacemaker of the heart?

**Solution**

The sinoatrial (SA) node generates rhythmic electrical impulses that initiate each heartbeat and regulate the heart rate without requiring external stimulation.

---

### Example 2

Differentiate between arteries and veins.

**Solution**

Arteries carry blood away from the heart under high pressure and have thick elastic walls. Veins carry blood toward the heart under lower pressure, possess thinner walls, and contain valves that prevent backflow.

---

### Example 3

State the significance of double circulation.

**Solution**

Double circulation ensures complete separation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood, allowing efficient oxygen delivery to tissues and supporting a high metabolic rate.

---

### Example 4

Why is AB⁺ called the universal recipient?

**Solution**

Individuals with the AB⁺ blood group possess both A and B antigens and do not have anti-A or anti-B antibodies. Being Rh-positive, they can receive blood from all ABO and Rh blood groups.

---

### Example 5

What is the role of platelets in blood clotting?

**Solution**

Platelets adhere to the site of injury and release clotting factors that initiate the coagulation cascade, leading to the formation of a fibrin clot and preventing excessive blood loss.

---

### Example 6

Calculate the cardiac output of a person with a stroke volume of 70 mL and a heart rate of 72 beats per minute.

**Solution**

Using the formula:

```
Cardiac Output = Stroke Volume × Heart Rate
```

```
= 70 mL × 72/min
= 5040 mL/min
≈ 5.0 L/min
```

Therefore, the cardiac output is approximately **5 litres per minute**.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **plasma** with **serum**; serum is plasma without clotting factors.
- Assuming all arteries carry oxygenated blood; the **pulmonary artery** carries deoxygenated blood.
- Assuming all veins carry deoxygenated blood; the **pulmonary veins** carry oxygenated blood.
- Mixing up the **tricuspid** and **bicuspid (mitral)** valves.
- Forgetting that the **SA node** is the natural pacemaker of the heart.
- Confusing the **P wave**, **QRS complex**, and **T wave** in an ECG.
- Assuming lymph contains red blood cells; lymph normally **does not contain RBCs**.
- Mixing up **hypertension**, **coronary artery disease**, **angina**, and **heart failure**.

# Chapter Summary

- The **circulatory system** consists of the heart, blood, blood vessels, and lymphatic system, ensuring the transport of gases, nutrients, hormones, and wastes throughout the body.
- **Blood** is composed of **plasma** and **formed elements** (RBCs, WBCs, and platelets). RBCs transport oxygen, WBCs provide immunity, and platelets help in blood clotting.
- The **ABO** and **Rh** blood group systems are important for safe blood transfusions and pregnancy management.
- **Lymph** returns excess tissue fluid to the bloodstream, transports absorbed fats, and contributes to immune defence.
- The **human heart** has four chambers and a specialized conducting system, with the **SA node** acting as the natural pacemaker.
- The **cardiac cycle** includes atrial systole, ventricular systole, and joint diastole, producing the characteristic **lub** and **dub** heart sounds.
- The **ECG** records the electrical activity of the heart and helps assess cardiac function.
- **Arteries**, **veins**, and **capillaries** differ in structure and function, enabling efficient circulation.
- Humans exhibit **double circulation**, ensuring complete separation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood and supporting efficient metabolism.
- Disorders such as **hypertension**, **coronary artery disease**, **angina pectoris**, and **heart failure** affect cardiovascular health and require timely diagnosis and management.
