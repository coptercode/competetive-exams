# Introduction

A moving charge produces a magnetic field around itself. The branch of physics dealing with magnetic effects of moving charges is called **magnetism due to current**.

---

# 1. Magnetic Field

## Definition

The region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where magnetic force can be experienced is called a magnetic field.

Symbol:

\[
B
\]

---

## SI Unit

\[
\boxed{\text{Tesla (T)}}
\]

---

# 2. Magnetic Force on a Moving Charge

When a charge moves in a magnetic field, it experiences a force.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\vec F=q(\vec v\times \vec B)
}
\]

Magnitude:

\[
\boxed{
F=qvB\sin\theta
}
\]

where:

- q = charge
- v = velocity
- B = magnetic field
- θ = angle between v and B

---

# Special Cases

## 1. Charge at Rest

\[
v=0
\]

Therefore:

\[
F=0
\]

---

## 2. Motion Parallel to Magnetic Field

\[
\theta=0^\circ
\]

\[
F=0
\]

---

## 3. Motion Perpendicular to Magnetic Field

\[
\theta=90^\circ
\]

\[
\boxed{
F=qvB
}
\]

Maximum force.

---

# 3. Lorentz Force

When electric and magnetic fields are both present:

\[
\boxed{
\vec F=q(\vec E+\vec v\times\vec B)
}
\]

---

# 4. Motion of Charged Particle in Magnetic Field

When charged particle enters perpendicular to magnetic field:

Magnetic force provides centripetal force.

\[
qvB=\frac{mv^2}{r}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
r=\frac{mv}{qB}
}
\]

---

# Time Period

\[
\boxed{
T=\frac{2\pi m}{qB}
}
\]

---

# Frequency

\[
\boxed{
\nu=\frac{qB}{2\pi m}
}
\]

---

# 5. Cyclotron

## Definition

A device used to accelerate charged particles using electric and magnetic fields.

---

# Principle

Charged particles moving perpendicular to a magnetic field experience circular motion.

---

# Cyclotron Frequency

\[
\boxed{
\nu=\frac{qB}{2\pi m}
}
\]

---

# Limitations

- Cannot accelerate neutral particles.
- Not suitable for very high velocities due to relativistic effects.

---

# 6. Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor

A current-carrying wire placed in a magnetic field experiences force.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\vec F=I(\vec l\times\vec B)
}
\]

Magnitude:

\[
\boxed{
F=IlB\sin\theta
}
\]

---

# Fleming's Left-Hand Rule

Used to find direction of force.

```
Thumb → Force

First finger → Magnetic field

Second finger → Current
```

---

# 7. Force Between Two Parallel Current-Carrying Conductors

Consider two parallel wires carrying currents:

\[
I_1,I_2
\]

separated by distance:

\[
d
\]

Force per unit length:

\[
\boxed{
\frac FL=
\frac{\mu_0 I_1I_2}{2\pi d}
}
\]

---

# Definition of Ampere

One ampere is the current which produces a force of:

\[
2\times10^{-7}N/m
\]

between two parallel conductors separated by 1 m in vacuum.

---

# 8. Biot-Savart Law

## Statement

The magnetic field due to a small current element is directly proportional to current element and inversely proportional to square of distance.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
dB=
\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}
\frac{Idl\sin\theta}{r^2}
}
\]

---

where:

- μ₀ = permeability of free space
- dl = current element

---

# 9. Magnetic Field Due to Straight Current-Carrying Wire

For a long straight wire:

\[
\boxed{
B=
\frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}
}
\]

---

Direction:

Right-hand thumb rule.

---

# Right-Hand Thumb Rule

If thumb points in direction of current, curled fingers show magnetic field direction.

---

# 10. Magnetic Field Due to Circular Loop

At centre:

\[
\boxed{
B=
\frac{\mu_0NI}{2R}
}
\]

where:

- N = number of turns
- R = radius

---

# At any point on axis:

\[
\boxed{
B=
\frac{\mu_0NIR^2}
{2(R^2+x^2)^{3/2}}
}
\]

---

# 11. Ampere's Circuital Law

## Statement

The line integral of magnetic field around a closed path equals μ₀ times current enclosed.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\oint B.dl=\mu_0I
}
\]

---

# Applications

## A. Long Straight Wire

\[
B=
\frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}
\]

---

## B. Solenoid

Magnetic field inside long solenoid:

\[
\boxed{
B=\mu_0 nI
}
\]

where:

n = number of turns per unit length

---

# 12. Solenoid

## Definition

A long cylindrical coil containing many turns of wire.

---

Magnetic field:

\[
B=\mu_0nI
\]

---

Properties:

- Strong uniform magnetic field inside.
- Weak field outside.

---

# 13. Toroid

A circular solenoid is called a toroid.

Magnetic field:

\[
\boxed{
B=
\frac{\mu_0NI}{2\pi r}
}
\]

---

# 14. Moving Coil Galvanometer

## Definition

A device used to detect and measure small electric currents.

---

# Principle

A current-carrying coil placed in a magnetic field experiences torque.

---

Torque:

\[
\boxed{
\tau=NIAB
}
\]

---

where:

- N = number of turns
- I = current
- A = area

---

# Current Sensitivity

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\theta}{I}
=

\frac{NAB}{k}
}
\]

---

# Voltage Sensitivity

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\theta}{V}
=

\frac{NAB}{kG}
}
\]

---

# Conversion of Galvanometer

---

# Galvanometer to Ammeter

A low resistance shunt is connected in parallel.

---

# Galvanometer to Voltmeter

A high resistance is connected in series.

---

# 15. Magnetic Dipole Moment of Current Loop

A current loop behaves like a magnetic dipole.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
M=NIA
}
\]

---

# Torque on Magnetic Dipole

\[
\boxed{
\tau=MB\sin\theta
}
\]

---

# Comparison Tables

## Electric Force vs Magnetic Force

| Electric Force             | Magnetic Force                      |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Acts on stationary charges | Acts on moving charges              |
| \(F=qE\)                   | \(F=qvB\sin\theta\)                 |
| Along electric field       | Perpendicular to velocity and field |

---

## Ammeter vs Voltmeter

| Ammeter             | Voltmeter             |
| ------------------- | --------------------- |
| Measures current    | Measures voltage      |
| Low resistance      | High resistance       |
| Connected in series | Connected in parallel |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** Find force on a charge of \(2\times10^{-6}C\) moving with velocity \(3\times10^5m/s\) perpendicular to a magnetic field of 0.5 T.

\[
F=qvB
\]

\[
=2\times10^{-6}\times3\times10^5\times0.5
\]

\[
\boxed{F=0.3N}
\]

---

## Example 2

**Question:** Find magnetic field at 2 cm from a wire carrying 5 A current.

\[
B=
\frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}
\]

\[
=

\frac{4\pi\times10^{-7}\times5}
{2\pi\times0.02}
\]

\[
\boxed{
B=5\times10^{-5}T
}
\]

---

## Example 3

**Question:** Why does magnetic force not do work on a charged particle?

### Solution

Magnetic force is always perpendicular to velocity, so displacement is perpendicular to force.

\[
W=Fs\cos90^\circ=0
\]

---

## Example 4

**Question:** State Ampere's circuital law.

### Solution

The line integral of magnetic field around a closed path is equal to μ₀ times the total current enclosed.

\[
\oint B.dl=\mu_0I
\]

---

## Example 5

**Question:** How is a galvanometer converted into an ammeter?

### Solution

A low resistance shunt is connected parallel to the galvanometer so that most current passes through the shunt.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting that magnetic force acts only on moving charges.
- Using electric force formula instead of Lorentz force.
- Forgetting the angle term in magnetic force.
- Confusing right-hand thumb rule with Fleming's left-hand rule.
- Using incorrect direction for magnetic field around wires.
- Forgetting magnetic force does no work.
- Mixing ammeter and voltmeter conversions.
- Confusing magnetic moment with magnetic field.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Moving charges produce magnetic fields.
- Magnetic force on a charge is given by Lorentz force.
- Charged particles move in circular paths when entering magnetic fields perpendicular to velocity.
- Biot-Savart law calculates magnetic fields due to current elements.
- Ampere's law is useful for highly symmetric current distributions.
- Current-carrying conductors experience forces in magnetic fields.
- Solenoids and toroids produce controlled magnetic fields.
- Moving coil galvanometers measure small currents and can be converted into ammeters and voltmeters.
- Current loops behave like magnetic dipoles with magnetic moment:

\[
M=NIA
\]
