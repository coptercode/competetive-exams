# Introduction

Light shows a **dual nature**:

1. Wave nature
   - Interference
   - Diffraction
   - Polarisation

2. Particle nature
   - Photoelectric effect

Similarly, matter also shows wave nature.

---

# 1. Photoelectric Effect

## Definition

The phenomenon of emission of electrons from a metal surface when electromagnetic radiation of suitable frequency falls on it is called the photoelectric effect.

The emitted electrons are called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Photoelectrons}}
\]

---

# 2. Experimental Setup

Main parts:

- Photosensitive metal surface
- Light source
- Collector electrode
- Battery
- Ammeter

---

# 3. Important Terms

---

# A. Work Function

## Definition

Minimum energy required to remove an electron from the surface of a metal.

Symbol:

\[
\phi
\]

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\phi=h\nu_0
}
\]

where:

- h = Planck's constant
- ν₀ = threshold frequency

---

# B. Threshold Frequency

## Definition

Minimum frequency of incident radiation required to emit electrons from a metal surface.

Symbol:

\[
\nu_0
\]

---

If:

\[
\nu<\nu_0
\]

No photoelectric emission occurs.

---

# C. Stopping Potential

## Definition

Minimum negative potential applied to stop the fastest photoelectrons.

Symbol:

\[
V_0
\]

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
K_{max}=eV_0
}
\]

---

# D. Photoelectric Current

The current produced due to photoelectrons is called photoelectric current.

---

# 4. Laws of Photoelectric Effect

---

## Law 1: Effect of Intensity

Photoelectric current is directly proportional to intensity of incident light.

\[
I\propto \text{Intensity}
\]

---

## Law 2: Effect of Frequency

Maximum kinetic energy depends on frequency, not intensity.

\[
K_{max}\propto\nu
\]

---

## Law 3: Instantaneous Emission

Photoelectric emission occurs immediately after light falls on the surface.

---

## Law 4: Threshold Frequency

Every metal has a minimum frequency below which emission does not occur.

---

# 5. Einstein's Photoelectric Equation

Einstein explained photoelectric effect using photon theory.

Energy of photon:

\[
E=h\nu
\]

---

Energy conservation:

\[
\boxed{
h\nu=\phi+K_{max}
}
\]

---

Since:

\[
K_{max}=\frac12mv^2
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
h\nu=\phi+\frac12mv^2
}
\]

---

# In Terms of Stopping Potential

\[
\boxed{
h\nu=\phi+eV_0
}
\]

---

# 6. Graphs of Photoelectric Effect

---

# A. Kinetic Energy vs Frequency

Straight line:

\[
K_{max}=h\nu-\phi
\]

Slope:

\[
\boxed{h}
\]

---

# B. Stopping Potential vs Frequency

\[
V_0=\frac he(\nu-\nu_0)
\]

Slope:

\[
\boxed{\frac he}
\]

---

# 7. Photon

## Definition

A photon is a packet of electromagnetic energy.

Energy:

\[
\boxed{
E=h\nu
}
\]

---

Momentum:

\[
\boxed{
p=\frac hc}
\]

or:

\[
\boxed{
p=\frac h\lambda
}
\]

---

# Properties of Photon

1. Zero rest mass.
2. Travels with speed of light.
3. Energy depends on frequency.
4. Momentum depends on wavelength.

---

# 8. Wave Nature of Matter

## de Broglie Hypothesis

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Louis de Broglie}}
\]

---

## Statement

Every moving particle has an associated wave.

---

# de Broglie Wavelength

\[
\boxed{
\lambda=\frac hp
}
\]

Since:

\[
p=mv
\]

\[
\boxed{
\lambda=\frac h{mv}
}
\]

---

# 9. de Broglie Wavelength of Electron

For electron accelerated through potential V:

Energy gained:

\[
eV=\frac12mv^2
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\lambda=
\frac h{\sqrt{2meV}}
}
\]

---

# 10. Davisson-Germer Experiment

## Purpose

To prove the wave nature of electrons.

---

# Experiment

Electrons were scattered from a nickel crystal.

Diffraction pattern was observed.

---

# Conclusion

Electrons behave as waves.

| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------- |

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** Find energy of a photon of frequency \(5\times10^{14}Hz\).

Given:

\[
h=6.626\times10^{-34}Js
\]

\[
\nu=5\times10^{14}Hz
\]

Formula:

\[
E=h\nu
\]

\[
=6.626\times10^{-34}\times5\times10^{14}
\]

\[
\boxed{E=3.313\times10^{-19}J}
\]

---

## Example 2

**Question:** Why does increasing intensity not increase maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons?

### Solution

Maximum kinetic energy depends on photon energy:

\[
K_{max}=h\nu-\phi
\]

which depends only on frequency.

---

## Example 3

**Question:** Calculate de Broglie wavelength of a particle.

Given:

Mass:

\[
m
\]

Velocity:

\[
v
\]

Formula:

\[
\lambda=\frac h{mv}
\]

---

## Example 4

**Question:** What is threshold frequency?

### Solution

The minimum frequency required for photoelectric emission from a metal surface is called threshold frequency.

---

## Example 5

**Question:** What did Davisson-Germer experiment prove?

### Solution

It proved that electrons show wave nature through diffraction.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing intensity with frequency effects.
- Forgetting that photons have zero rest mass.
- Using \(E=mc^2\) for photon energy instead of \(E=h\nu\).
- Forgetting threshold frequency condition.
- Mixing stopping potential with work function.
- Forgetting that de Broglie wavelength applies only to moving particles.
- Confusing photon momentum and electron momentum.
- Ignoring unit conversion from eV to joules.
