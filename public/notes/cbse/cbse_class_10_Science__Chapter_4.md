# Introduction

**Carbon** is one of the most important elements found in nature. It is present in all living organisms and forms a vast number of compounds because of its unique properties such as **tetravalency** and **catenation**. Carbon compounds are widely used as fuels, medicines, plastics, fibres, detergents, and many other useful materials. This chapter explains the properties of carbon, covalent bonding, homologous series, important reactions of carbon compounds, and the cleansing action of soaps and detergents.

---

# 1. Carbon

**Carbon (C)** is a non-metal with atomic number **6**.

### Electronic Configuration

```
2, 4
```

Carbon has **4 valence electrons**, so it is **tetravalent**.

---

# 2. Covalent Bonding

Carbon neither loses nor gains four electrons easily because doing so requires a large amount of energy. Instead, it **shares electrons** with other atoms to complete its outermost shell.

A **covalent bond** is formed by the **sharing of electrons** between atoms.

### Example: Hydrogen Molecule

```
H : H
```

Each hydrogen atom shares one electron.

---

### Example: Chlorine Molecule

```
Cl : Cl
```

Each chlorine atom shares one electron.

---

### Example: Methane (CH₄)

```
        H
        |
    H - C - H
        |
        H
```

Carbon shares one electron with each hydrogen atom, forming four covalent bonds.

---

# 3. Versatile Nature of Carbon

Carbon forms a large number of compounds because of two important properties.

## (A) Tetravalency

Carbon has **four valence electrons** and forms four covalent bonds.

Example:

```
CH₄
```

---

## (B) Catenation

**Catenation** is the property of carbon atoms to bond with one another to form long chains, branched chains, and rings.

Examples

```
C-C-C-C
```

```
C=C
```

```
C≡C
```

This ability gives rise to millions of carbon compounds.

---

# 4. Allotropes of Carbon

**Allotropes** are different forms of the same element having different structures and properties.

### Diamond

- Hardest natural substance.
- Each carbon atom is bonded to four other carbon atoms.
- Poor conductor of electricity.

---

### Graphite

- Soft and slippery.
- Carbon atoms are arranged in layers.
- Good conductor of electricity.

---

### Fullerenes

- Molecules made of carbon atoms arranged in hollow spherical structures.
- Example: Buckminsterfullerene (C₆₀).

---

# 5. Saturated and Unsaturated Carbon Compounds

## Saturated Compounds

Contain only **single covalent bonds**.

Example:

```
CH₄
```

```
C₂H₆
```

These are called **alkanes**.

---

## Unsaturated Compounds

Contain one or more **double** or **triple bonds**.

### Alkenes

Contain at least one double bond.

Example:

```
C₂H₄
```

---

### Alkynes

Contain at least one triple bond.

Example:

```
C₂H₂
```

---

# 6. Homologous Series

A **homologous series** is a group of organic compounds having:

- The same functional group.
- Similar chemical properties.
- Successive members differing by **–CH₂–** (14 u).

### Example: Alkanes

| Compound | Molecular Formula |
| -------- | ----------------- |
| Methane  | CH₄               |
| Ethane   | C₂H₆              |
| Propane  | C₃H₈              |
| Butane   | C₄H₁₀             |

---

# 8. Chemical Properties of Carbon Compounds

## (A) Combustion

Carbon compounds burn in oxygen to produce **carbon dioxide**, **water**, and **heat**.

### Example

```
CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O + Heat
```

---

## (B) Oxidation

Alcohols are oxidized to carboxylic acids using oxidizing agents such as:

- Alkaline KMnO₄
- Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇

### Example

```
CH₃CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃COOH + H₂O
```

---

## (C) Addition Reaction

Unsaturated compounds add hydrogen in the presence of a catalyst.

### Example

```
CH₂=CH₂ + H₂

Ni

→ CH₃-CH₃
```

This process is called **hydrogenation**.

**Application:** Manufacture of vanaspati ghee from vegetable oils.

---

## (D) Substitution Reaction

In saturated hydrocarbons, hydrogen atoms are replaced by other atoms such as chlorine.

### Example

```
CH₄ + Cl₂

Sunlight

→ CH₃Cl + HCl
```

---

# 9. Ethanol

### Molecular Formula

```
C₂H₅OH
```

### Preparation

Ethanol is produced by the fermentation of sugar.

---

### Properties

- Colourless liquid.
- Pleasant smell.
- Miscible with water.
- Neutral in nature.

---

### Uses

- Solvent.
- Medicines.
- Perfumes.
- Fuel.
- Alcoholic beverages.
- Hand sanitizers.

---

### Reaction with Sodium

```
2C₂H₅OH + 2Na → 2C₂H₅ONa + H₂↑
```

Hydrogen gas is evolved.

---

# 10. Ethanoic Acid (Acetic Acid)

### Molecular Formula

```
CH₃COOH
```

### Properties

- Colourless liquid.
- Sour taste.
- Weak acid.
- Pure form is called **glacial acetic acid**.

---

### Uses

- Vinegar.
- Food preservation.
- Textile industry.
- Medicines.
- Manufacture of esters.

---

### Reaction with Sodium Bicarbonate

```
CH₃COOH + NaHCO₃

↓

CH₃COONa + CO₂ + H₂O
```

Carbon dioxide gas is evolved.

---

# 11. Esterification

An **ester** is formed when an alcohol reacts with a carboxylic acid in the presence of concentrated sulphuric acid.

### Example

```
CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH

Conc. H₂SO₄

→ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O
```

Esters have a **pleasant fruity smell**.

---

# 12. Saponification

The alkaline hydrolysis of an ester produces **soap** and **alcohol**.

### General Reaction

```
Ester + NaOH → Soap + Alcohol
```

---

# 13. Soaps

Soaps are sodium or potassium salts of long-chain fatty acids.

### Structure of Soap Molecule

```
Hydrophilic Head

O⁻ Na⁺

|

Long Hydrophobic Hydrocarbon Tail
```

- **Hydrophilic head** dissolves in water.
- **Hydrophobic tail** dissolves in grease and oil.

---

## Cleansing Action of Soap

Soap molecules form **micelles** around grease particles.

```
      Water

  ○ ○ ○ ○ ○
 ○         ○
○  Grease   ○
 ○         ○
  ○ ○ ○ ○ ○

(Hydrophilic heads outside,
Hydrophobic tails inside)
```

The grease is surrounded by soap molecules and washed away with water.

---

# 14. Detergents

Detergents are synthetic cleansing agents.

### Advantages over Soaps

- Work in hard water.
- Do not form scum.
- More effective in acidic conditions.

### Uses

- Washing clothes.
- Dishwashing.
- Cleaning floors.
- Industrial cleaning.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Why does carbon form covalent bonds?

**Solution**

Carbon has four valence electrons. It shares electrons instead of losing or gaining four electrons, forming covalent bonds.

---

### Example 2

Differentiate between saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons.

**Solution**

- Saturated hydrocarbons contain only single bonds.
- Unsaturated hydrocarbons contain double or triple bonds.

---

### Example 3

Name the functional group in ethanol.

**Solution**

The functional group is

```
–OH
```

called the **hydroxyl group**.

---

### Example 4

Write the balanced equation for the combustion of methane.

**Solution**

```
CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O + Heat
```

---

### Example 5

What is formed when ethanoic acid reacts with ethanol?

**Solution**

An ester (ethyl ethanoate) and water are formed.

---

### Example 6

Why are detergents preferred over soaps in hard water?

**Solution**

Detergents do not react with calcium and magnesium ions present in hard water, so they clean effectively without forming scum.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **catenation** with **tetravalency**.
- Assuming covalent compounds conduct electricity (most do not).
- Mixing up **addition** and **substitution** reactions.
- Forgetting that **ethanol** is an alcohol while **ethanoic acid** is a carboxylic acid.
- Confusing **soap** with **detergent**.
- Writing incorrect suffixes while naming organic compounds.
- Forgetting that successive members of a homologous series differ by **–CH₂–**.

# Key Definitions

| Term                    | Definition                                                                                   |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Covalent Bond           | Bond formed by sharing of electrons between atoms.                                           |
| Tetravalency            | Property of carbon to form four covalent bonds.                                              |
| Catenation              | Ability of carbon atoms to form long chains and rings by bonding with one another.           |
| Allotrope               | Different structural form of the same element.                                               |
| Homologous Series       | Group of organic compounds with the same functional group and similar chemical properties.   |
| Functional Group        | Atom or group of atoms responsible for the characteristic properties of an organic compound. |
| Saturated Hydrocarbon   | Hydrocarbon containing only single bonds.                                                    |
| Unsaturated Hydrocarbon | Hydrocarbon containing one or more double or triple bonds.                                   |
| Esterification          | Reaction between an alcohol and a carboxylic acid to form an ester and water.                |
| Saponification          | Hydrolysis of an ester in the presence of an alkali to form soap and alcohol.                |
| Micelle                 | Aggregate of soap molecules that traps grease and dirt during cleaning.                      |

# Chapter Summary

- **Carbon** forms a vast number of compounds because of its **tetravalency** and **catenation**.
- Carbon forms **covalent bonds** by sharing electrons.
- The major allotropes of carbon include **diamond, graphite,** and **fullerenes**.
- Carbon compounds are classified as **saturated** or **unsaturated** based on the type of carbon-carbon bonds.
- A **homologous series** consists of compounds with the same functional group, and successive members differ by **–CH₂–**.
- Carbon compounds undergo **combustion, oxidation, addition,** and **substitution** reactions.
- **Ethanol** is an important alcohol used as a fuel, solvent, and antiseptic, while **ethanoic acid** is widely used as vinegar and in industry.
- **Esters** are pleasant-smelling compounds formed by **esterification**, while **saponification** produces soap from esters.
- **Soap** cleans by forming **micelles**, whereas **detergents** are more effective in hard water because they do not form scum.
