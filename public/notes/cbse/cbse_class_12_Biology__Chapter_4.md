# Introduction

**Inheritance** is the transmission of genetic information from parents to offspring, while **variation** refers to differences among individuals of the same species. The study of heredity began with the experiments of **Gregor Johann Mendel**, who is known as the **Father of Genetics**.

Modern genetics explains inheritance through genes located on chromosomes, which are transmitted during reproduction according to specific principles.

---

# 1. Basic Genetic Terminology

## Gene

A **gene** is the functional unit of heredity located on a chromosome.

---

## Allele

Alternative forms of the same gene.

Example:

```
T → Tall

t → Dwarf
```

---

## Locus

The specific position of a gene on a chromosome.

---

## Genotype

The genetic constitution of an organism.

Examples:

```
TT

Tt

tt
```

---

## Phenotype

The observable characteristics produced by the genotype and environment.

Example:

- Tall plant
- Dwarf plant

---

## Homozygous

Both alleles are identical.

Examples:

```
TT

tt
```

---

## Heterozygous

Alleles are different.

Example:

```
Tt
```

---

## Dominant Trait

Expressed even in the heterozygous condition.

---

## Recessive Trait

Expressed only in the homozygous condition.

# 5. Mendel's Laws of Inheritance

## A. Law of Dominance

When two contrasting alleles are present together, only one is expressed.

---

## B. Law of Segregation

The two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation, and each gamete receives only one allele.

Also called:

**Law of Purity of Gametes**

---

## C. Law of Independent Assortment

Genes controlling different characters assort independently during gamete formation.

Applicable only to genes located on different chromosomes or far apart on the same chromosome.

---

# 10. Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance

Proposed by:

- Walter Sutton
- Theodore Boveri

---

## Main Points

- Genes are located on chromosomes.
- Chromosomes occur in pairs.
- Chromosomes segregate during meiosis.
- Fertilization restores chromosome number.

---

# 11. Linkage

## Definition

The tendency of genes located on the same chromosome to be inherited together.

---

## Types

### Complete Linkage

No crossing over.

---

### Incomplete Linkage

Crossing over occurs.

---

# 12. Crossing Over

## Definition

Exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes during **Pachytene stage of Prophase I** of meiosis.

---

## Importance

- Produces genetic variation.
- Creates new gene combinations.
- Basis of genetic mapping.

---

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

**Question:** State the phenotypic and genotypic ratios of a monohybrid cross.

**Solution:**

For the cross:

```
Tt × Tt
```

Genotypic ratio:

```
1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt
```

Phenotypic ratio:

```
3 Tall : 1 Dwarf
```

---

### Example 2

**Question:** Why is the ABO blood group an example of co-dominance?

**Solution:**

In individuals with genotype **IAIB**, both A and B alleles express themselves equally. As a result, both A and B antigens are present on red blood cells, producing blood group **AB**.

---

### Example 3

**Question:** Differentiate between linkage and crossing over.

**Solution:**

Linkage is the tendency of genes on the same chromosome to be inherited together. Crossing over is the exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes during meiosis, producing new gene combinations.

---

### Example 4

**Question:** Who determines the sex of the child in humans?

**Solution:**

The father determines the sex of the child because males produce two types of sperms:

- X-bearing sperm
- Y-bearing sperm

An X-bearing sperm produces an **XX (female)** offspring, while a Y-bearing sperm produces an **XY (male)** offspring.

---

### Example 5

**Question:** Name the chromosomal abnormalities in Down syndrome, Turner's syndrome, and Klinefelter syndrome.

**Solution:**

| Disorder             | Chromosomal Condition       |
| -------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Down syndrome        | Trisomy 21 (47 chromosomes) |
| Turner's syndrome    | XO (45 chromosomes)         |
| Klinefelter syndrome | XXY (47 chromosomes)        |

---

### Example 6

**Question:** What is the significance of crossing over?

**Solution:**

Crossing over creates new combinations of genes, increases genetic variation, contributes to evolution, and forms the basis for genetic mapping.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **genotype** with **phenotype**.
- Assuming **dominance** and **co-dominance** are the same; in co-dominance, both alleles are expressed equally.
- Mixing up **incomplete dominance** with **co-dominance**; incomplete dominance produces an intermediate phenotype.
- Forgetting that the **Law of Independent Assortment** applies mainly to genes located on different chromosomes or far apart on the same chromosome.
- Confusing **linkage** with **crossing over**; linkage keeps genes together, whereas crossing over exchanges genetic material.
- Incorrectly stating that the mother determines the sex of the child in humans; the **father** determines the sex through X- or Y-bearing sperm.
- Mixing up the chromosomal constitutions of **Turner's syndrome (XO)** and **Klinefelter syndrome (XXY)**.
- Assuming all genetic disorders are inherited; **Down syndrome** usually results from chromosomal nondisjunction rather than inheritance.

# Chapter Summary

- **Inheritance** is the transmission of genetic traits from parents to offspring, while **variation** introduces differences among individuals.
- **Gregor Mendel** established the fundamental laws of inheritance through experiments on pea plants, leading to the **Law of Dominance**, **Law of Segregation**, and **Law of Independent Assortment**.
- A **monohybrid cross** produces a **3:1 phenotypic ratio**, while a **dihybrid cross** produces a **9:3:3:1 phenotypic ratio** under Mendelian inheritance.
- **Incomplete dominance**, **co-dominance**, and **multiple alleles** are important deviations from simple Mendelian inheritance.
- The **Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance** explains that genes are located on chromosomes, which segregate during meiosis.
- **Linkage** and **crossing over** influence the inheritance of genes and contribute to genetic variation and gene mapping.
- Sex determination differs among organisms: **XX-XY** in humans, **ZZ-ZW** in birds, and **haplodiploidy** in honey bees.
- Human genetic disorders such as **thalassemia**, **Down syndrome**, **Turner's syndrome**, and **Klinefelter syndrome** arise due to gene mutations or chromosomal abnormalities.
- Understanding inheritance patterns is essential for genetics, medicine, breeding programmes, and genetic counseling.
