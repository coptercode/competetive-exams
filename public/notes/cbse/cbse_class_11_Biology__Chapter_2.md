# Introduction

The Earth is home to millions of organisms with remarkable diversity in size, structure, nutrition, reproduction, and habitat. To study this diversity systematically, scientists have developed various systems of classification. One of the most widely accepted systems is the **Five Kingdom Classification** proposed by **R.H. Whittaker (1969)**.

This chapter explains the **Five Kingdom Classification**, the characteristics of **Kingdom Monera**, **Protista**, and **Fungi**, and introduces important acellular entities such as **Viruses**, **Viroids**, **Prions**, and the symbiotic association called **Lichens**.

---

# 1. Biological Classification

**Biological classification** is the systematic arrangement of organisms into groups based on their similarities, differences, and evolutionary relationships.

### Objectives

- To simplify the study of organisms.
- To identify organisms accurately.
- To understand evolutionary relationships.
- To provide a universal system of classification.

---

# 2. Basis of Five Kingdom Classification

R.H. Whittaker classified all living organisms into five kingdoms based on:

- Cell structure
- Cell organization
- Mode of nutrition
- Reproduction
- Phylogenetic (evolutionary) relationships

---

# 3. Five Kingdom Classification

The five kingdoms are:

1. Monera
2. Protista
3. Fungi
4. Plantae
5. Animalia

```
├── Monera
├── Protista
├── Fungi
├── Plantae
└── Animalia
```

# 4. Kingdom Monera

Kingdom **Monera** includes all **prokaryotic organisms**.

### General Characteristics

- Unicellular
- Prokaryotic cells
- No true nucleus
- No membrane-bound organelles
- Cell wall usually made of peptidoglycan
- Reproduction mainly by binary fission

---

### (B) Heterotrophic

## Archaebacteria

Archaebacteria possess a unique cell wall and live in extreme environments.

### Types

- Methanogens
- Halophiles
- Thermoacidophiles

---

## Cyanobacteria

Also called **Blue-Green Algae**.

### Characteristics

- Photosynthetic
- Contain chlorophyll-a
- Fix atmospheric nitrogen
- Form blooms in water

Example

- Nostoc
- Anabaena

---

## Mycoplasma

- Smallest living cells
- Lack a cell wall
- Can survive without a rigid shape
- Cause diseases in plants and animals

# 5. Kingdom Protista

Kingdom Protista includes **unicellular eukaryotic organisms**.

### Characteristics

- True nucleus
- Membrane-bound organelles
- Mostly aquatic
- Sexual and asexual reproduction

---

## (A) Chrysophytes

Include

- Diatoms
- Golden algae

### Characteristics

- Photosynthetic
- Silica cell walls
- Cell walls form diatomaceous earth

Example

- Navicula

---

## (B) Dinoflagellates

### Characteristics

- Two unequal flagella
- Cell wall contains cellulose plates
- Marine organisms
- Cause red tides

Example

- Gonyaulax

---

## (C) Euglenoids

### Characteristics

- No cell wall
- Protein-rich pellicle
- Photosynthetic in sunlight
- Heterotrophic in darkness

Example

- Euglena

---

## (D) Slime Moulds

### Characteristics

- Saprophytic
- Form multinucleate plasmodium
- Produce spores during unfavorable conditions

Example

- Physarum

---

## (E) Protozoans

Animal-like protists.

---

### Amoeboid Protozoans

Move by pseudopodia.

Example

- Amoeba

---

### Flagellated Protozoans

Move by flagella.

Example

- Trypanosoma

---

### Ciliated Protozoans

Move by cilia.

Example

- Paramecium

---

### Sporozoans

Non-motile parasites.

Example

- Plasmodium

# 6. Kingdom Fungi

Fungi are eukaryotic organisms that obtain food by absorption.

---

## General Characteristics

- Eukaryotic
- Mostly multicellular
- Cell wall made of chitin
- Heterotrophic
- Body made of hyphae
- Hyphae collectively form mycelium

---

## Nutrition

### Saprophytic

Feed on dead organic matter.

Example

- Mushroom

---

### Parasitic

Obtain food from living organisms.

Example

- Puccinia

---

### Symbiotic

Live in association with algae or plant roots.

Examples

- Lichens
- Mycorrhiza

---

## Reproduction in Fungi

### Vegetative Reproduction

- Fragmentation
- Budding
- Fission

---

### Asexual Reproduction

By spores

- Conidia
- Sporangiospores
- Zoospores

---

### Sexual Reproduction

Occurs through

- Plasmogamy
- Karyogamy
- Meiosis

# 8. Viruses

Viruses are **acellular infectious particles**.

They show characteristics of both living and non-living organisms.

---

## Characteristics

- No cellular organization
- DNA or RNA (never both)
- Protein coat called **Capsid**
- Multiply only inside host cells

```
      Capsid
   ┌─────────┐
   │ DNA/RNA │
   └─────────┘
```

---

## Examples

- Tobacco Mosaic Virus (TMV)
- HIV
- Influenza Virus
- Coronavirus

---

# 9. Viroids

Viroids are infectious agents made only of **small circular RNA**.

### Characteristics

- No protein coat
- Smaller than viruses
- Infect plants

Example

- Potato Spindle Tuber Viroid (PSTV)

Discovered by

**T.O. Diener**

---

# 10. Prions

Prions are infectious **protein particles**.

### Characteristics

- No DNA
- No RNA
- Cause neurological diseases

Example

- Mad Cow Disease
- Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease (CJD)

Discovered by

**Stanley Prusiner**

---

# 11. Lichens

Lichens are a **symbiotic association** between

- Algae (Phycobiont)
- Fungus (Mycobiont)

---

## Roles

### Alga

Performs photosynthesis.

### Fungus

Provides

- Water
- Minerals
- Shelter

---

## Importance

- Indicator of air pollution
- Soil formation
- Food for reindeer
- Medicinal uses

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Why are bacteria placed in Kingdom Monera?

**Solution**

Because bacteria are **prokaryotic**, lack a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, and are unicellular.

---

### Example 2

Differentiate between viruses and viroids.

**Solution**

Viruses contain nucleic acid enclosed within a protein coat, whereas viroids consist only of naked RNA without a protein coat.

---

### Example 3

Why are lichens considered a symbiotic association?

**Solution**

The alga prepares food by photosynthesis, while the fungus absorbs water and minerals and provides protection. Both partners benefit.

---

### Example 4

Name one organism responsible for red tide.

**Solution**

**Gonyaulax**, a dinoflagellate, is responsible for red tides.

---

### Example 5

Why are Deuteromycetes called imperfect fungi?

**Solution**

Because their sexual stage is absent or has not yet been observed.

---

### Example 6

State one important difference between fungi and plants.

**Solution**

Plants contain chlorophyll and are autotrophic, whereas fungi lack chlorophyll and are heterotrophic, obtaining nutrients by absorption.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Monera** (prokaryotes) with **Protista** (eukaryotes).
- Thinking all bacteria are harmful; many are beneficial.
- Confusing **cyanobacteria** with true algae.
- Assuming viruses are complete living organisms—they reproduce only inside host cells.
- Mixing up **viroids** (RNA only) and **prions** (protein only).
- Forgetting that fungal cell walls are made of **chitin**, not cellulose.
- Confusing **mycelium** with **hyphae** (mycelium is a network of hyphae).
- Believing that all protists are photosynthetic; many are heterotrophic.

# Chapter Summary

- **Biological classification** organizes living organisms based on similarities, differences, and evolutionary relationships.
- **R.H. Whittaker's Five Kingdom Classification** groups organisms into **Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae,** and **Animalia** based on cell structure, organization, nutrition, reproduction, and phylogeny.
- **Kingdom Monera** includes prokaryotic organisms such as bacteria, archaea, cyanobacteria, and mycoplasma.
- **Kingdom Protista** consists of unicellular eukaryotes, including **chrysophytes, dinoflagellates, euglenoids, slime moulds,** and **protozoans**.
- **Kingdom Fungi** comprises heterotrophic eukaryotes with chitinous cell walls that reproduce by vegetative, asexual, and sexual methods.
- **Viruses** are acellular particles containing either DNA or RNA enclosed in a protein coat, while **viroids** contain only RNA and **prions** contain only protein.
- **Lichens** represent a mutualistic association between algae and fungi, serving as important ecological indicators of air quality.
- Understanding biological classification helps explain the diversity of life and forms the basis for advanced studies in microbiology, botany, zoology, and evolutionary biology.
