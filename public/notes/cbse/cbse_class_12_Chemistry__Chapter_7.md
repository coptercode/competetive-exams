# Introduction

Organic compounds containing oxygen as a functional group are called oxygen-containing compounds.

Important classes:

1. Alcohols
2. Phenols
3. Ethers

---

# 1. Alcohols

## Definition

Alcohols are compounds in which a hydroxyl group (-OH) is attached to an sp³ hybridised carbon atom.

General formula:

\[
R-OH
\]

Example:

\[
CH_3OH
\]

(methanol)

---

# 2. Classification of Alcohols

Based on number of hydroxyl groups:

---

## A. Monohydric Alcohols

Contain one -OH group.

Example:

\[
CH_3CH_2OH
\]

---

## B. Dihydric Alcohols

Contain two -OH groups.

Example:

Ethylene glycol:

\[
HO-CH_2-CH_2-OH
\]

---

## C. Trihydric Alcohols

Contain three -OH groups.

Example:

Glycerol:

\[
CH_2OH-CHOH-CH_2OH
\]

---

# Classification Based on Carbon Attached to -OH

---

## Primary Alcohol (1°)

Carbon with -OH attached to one carbon.

Example:

\[
CH_3CH_2OH
\]

---

## Secondary Alcohol (2°)

Carbon attached to two carbons.

Example:

\[
CH_3CHOHCH_3
\]

---

## Tertiary Alcohol (3°)

Carbon attached to three carbons.

Example:

\[
(CH_3)_3COH
\]

---

# 3. Nomenclature of Alcohols

Rules:

1. Select longest chain containing -OH.
2. Replace "e" of alkane with "ol".
3. Give lowest number to -OH group.

---

Examples:

\[
CH_3OH
\]

Methanol

---

\[
CH_3CH_2OH
\]

Ethanol

---

\[
CH_3CHOHCH_3
\]

Propan-2-ol

---

# 4. Preparation of Alcohols

---

# A. From Alkenes

Hydration of alkenes:

\[
CH_2=CH_2+H_2O
\rightarrow
CH_3CH_2OH
\]

Catalyst:

\[
H_3PO_4
\]

---

# B. Reduction of Carbonyl Compounds

## Aldehydes

\[
RCHO
\xrightarrow{NaBH_4}
RCH_2OH
\]

---

## Ketones

\[
RCOR'
\xrightarrow{NaBH_4}
RCHOHR'
\]

---

# C. From Grignard Reagents

\[
RMgX+HCHO
\rightarrow
RCH_2OH
\]

---

# 5. Physical Properties of Alcohols

## Hydrogen Bonding

Alcohols form hydrogen bonds because of -OH group.

Effects:

- Higher boiling points.
- Greater solubility in water.

---

## Solubility

Lower alcohols:

- Soluble in water.

Higher alcohols:

- Solubility decreases with increasing carbon chain.

---

# 6. Chemical Reactions of Alcohols

---

# A. Reaction with Active Metals

Alcohol reacts with sodium:

\[
2ROH+2Na
\rightarrow
2RONa+H_2
\]

---

# B. Dehydration

Removal of water forms alkene.

\[
CH_3CH_2OH
\xrightarrow{conc.H_2SO_4}
CH_2=CH_2+H_2O
\]

---

## Ease of dehydration:

\[
3^\circ>2^\circ>1^\circ
\]

---

# C. Oxidation

## Primary Alcohol

Produces aldehyde:

\[
RCH_2OH
\rightarrow
RCHO
\]

Further oxidation:

\[
RCHO
\rightarrow
RCOOH
\]

---

## Secondary Alcohol

Produces ketone:

\[
R_2CHOH
\rightarrow
R_2CO
\]

---

## Tertiary Alcohol

Resistant to oxidation.

---

# 7. Phenols

## Definition

Phenols are compounds where -OH group is directly attached to an aromatic ring.

General formula:

\[
Ar-OH
\]

Example:

\[
C_6H_5OH
\]

(phenol)

---

# 8. Preparation of Phenol

---

# A. From Chlorobenzene

Dow process:

\[
C_6H_5Cl
\xrightarrow{NaOH}
C_6H_5ONa
\]

then:

\[
C_6H_5ONa+H^+
\rightarrow C_6H_5OH
\]

---

# B. From Benzene Diazonium Salt

\[
C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^-
+H_2O
\rightarrow
C_6H_5OH+N_2
\]

---

# 9. Acidity of Alcohols and Phenols

Phenols are more acidic than alcohols.

Reason:

Phenoxide ion is stabilized by resonance.

---

Comparison:

\[
Phenol > Alcohol
\]

---

# 10. Reactions of Phenol

---

# A. Reaction with Sodium

\[
2C_6H_5OH+2Na
\rightarrow
2C_6H_5ONa+H_2
\]

---

# B. Electrophilic Substitution Reactions

-OH group is:

- Activating
- Ortho/para directing

---

# 1. Bromination

\[
C_6H_5OH+3Br_2
\rightarrow
C_6H_2Br_3OH
\]

Product:

2,4,6-tribromophenol

---

# 2. Nitration

Dilute nitric acid:

Forms:

o-nitrophenol + p-nitrophenol

---

# 3. Kolbe Reaction

## Definition

Phenoxide ion reacts with CO₂ to form salicylic acid.

Reaction:

\[
C_6H_5ONa+CO_2
\rightarrow
o-HOC_6H_4COOH
\]

---

# 4. Reimer-Tiemann Reaction

Phenol with chloroform gives salicylaldehyde.

\[
C_6H_5OH+CHCl_3
\rightarrow
o-HOC_6H_4CHO
\]

---

# 11. Ethers

## Definition

Compounds containing oxygen atom bonded to two alkyl/aryl groups.

General formula:

\[
R-O-R'
\]

Example:

\[
CH_3-O-CH_3
\]

(dimethyl ether)

---

# 12. Nomenclature of Ethers

Named as:

alkoxyalkanes

Example:

\[
CH_3OCH_2CH_3
\]

Methoxyethane

---

# 13. Preparation of Ethers

---

# Williamson Ether Synthesis

## Definition

Preparation of ethers by reaction of alkoxide ion with alkyl halide.

Reaction:

\[
RONa+R'X
\rightarrow
ROR'+NaX
\]

---

## Mechanism

Occurs through:

\[
SN2
\]

reaction.

---

# 14. Cleavage of Ethers

Ethers react with HI or HBr.

Example:

\[
CH_3OCH_3+HI
\rightarrow
CH_3I+CH_3OH
\]

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** Why is phenol more acidic than ethanol?

### Solution

Phenol forms phenoxide ion, which is stabilized by resonance. Ethoxide ion formed from ethanol has no resonance stabilization. Therefore phenol is more acidic.

---

## Example 2

**Question:** Write Williamson ether synthesis.

### Solution

Ethers are prepared by reaction of sodium alkoxide with alkyl halide:

\[
RONa+R'X
\rightarrow
ROR'+NaX
\]

---

## Example 3

**Question:** What is Reimer-Tiemann reaction?

### Solution

Phenol reacts with chloroform in presence of NaOH to form salicylaldehyde.

---

## Example 4

**Question:** Why do alcohols have high boiling points?

### Solution

Alcohols form strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds due to the presence of the hydroxyl group, requiring more energy for boiling.

---

## Example 5

**Question:** What happens when ethanol is oxidised?

### Solution

Ethanol is oxidised first to ethanal and then to ethanoic acid.

\[
C_2H_5OH
\rightarrow
CH_3CHO
\rightarrow
CH_3COOH
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing alcohols and phenols; phenol has -OH directly attached to benzene.
- Forgetting that phenols are more acidic due to resonance stabilization.
- Mixing up Kolbe reaction and Reimer-Tiemann reaction.
- Forgetting Williamson synthesis follows SN2 mechanism.
- Assuming all alcohols oxidise in the same way; primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols behave differently.
- Forgetting that dehydration order is tertiary > secondary > primary.
- Confusing methanol and ethanol toxicity.
- Forgetting that ethers can be cleaved by HI/HBr.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Alcohols contain hydroxyl groups attached to sp³ carbon atoms.
- Phenols contain hydroxyl groups directly attached to aromatic rings.
- Alcohols are prepared by hydration of alkenes and reduction of carbonyl compounds.
- Phenols are more acidic than alcohols due to resonance stabilization.
- Phenols undergo electrophilic substitution reactions due to activation by -OH group.
- Kolbe reaction produces salicylic acid, while Reimer-Tiemann reaction produces salicylaldehyde.
- Ethers contain an oxygen atom between two carbon groups and are prepared mainly by Williamson synthesis.
- Methanol is highly toxic, while ethanol has important industrial and pharmaceutical applications.
