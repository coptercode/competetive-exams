# Introduction

All substances expand when heated and contract when cooled. Heat also flows from a hotter body to a colder body until thermal equilibrium is reached. The study of these phenomena forms the basis of **Thermal Properties of Matter**.

Understanding heat transfer and thermal expansion is essential in engineering, architecture, meteorology, and everyday life.

---

# 1. Temperature

## Definition

**Temperature** is the physical quantity that indicates the degree of hotness or coldness of a body.

It determines the direction of heat flow.

---

## SI Unit

```
kelvin (K)
```

---

## Common Temperature Scales

- Celsius (°C)
- Fahrenheit (°F)
- Kelvin (K)

---

## Conversion Formulae

### Celsius to Kelvin

```
K = °C + 273.15
```

---

### Kelvin to Celsius

```
°C = K − 273.15
```

---

### Celsius to Fahrenheit

```
°F = (9/5)°C + 32
```

---

### Fahrenheit to Celsius

```
°C = (5/9)(°F − 32)
```

---

# 2. Heat

## Definition

Heat is the energy transferred from a body at higher temperature to another body at lower temperature.

---

## SI Unit

```
joule (J)
```

---

## Other Unit

```
calorie (cal)
```

Relation

```
1 cal = 4.186 J
```

---

# 3. Thermal Expansion

## Definition

Thermal expansion is the increase in dimensions of a body due to an increase in temperature.

---

## Types of Expansion

- Linear expansion
- Superficial (Area) expansion
- Cubical (Volume) expansion

---

# 4. Linear Expansion

Applicable mainly to rods and wires.

Formula

```
ΔL = αLΔT
```

where

- **ΔL** = Change in length
- **L** = Original length
- **α** = Coefficient of linear expansion
- **ΔT** = Rise in temperature

---

## SI Unit of α

```
K⁻¹
```

---

# 5. Area Expansion

Formula

```
ΔA = βAΔT
```

where

- **β** = Coefficient of area expansion

---

## Relation

```
β = 2α
```

(for isotropic solids)

---

# 6. Volume Expansion

Formula

```
ΔV = γVΔT
```

where

- **γ** = Coefficient of volume expansion

---

## Relation

```
γ = 3α
```

(for isotropic solids)

---

# 7. Anomalous Expansion of Water

Water behaves differently between

```
0°C

and

4°C
```

---

## Observation

- Water contracts when heated from **0°C to 4°C**.
- Water expands when heated above **4°C**.
- Density of water is maximum at **4°C**.

---

## Importance

- Lakes freeze from the top.
- Aquatic life survives during winter.
- Ice floats on water.

---

# 8. Thermal Stress

If the expansion of a body is prevented, internal stress develops.

Formula

```
Thermal Stress

=

YαΔT
```

where

- **Y** = Young's modulus

---

## SI Unit

# 10. Specific Heat Capacity

## Definition

The amount of heat required to raise the temperature of

```
1 kg
```

of a substance by

```
1 K
```

---

## Formula

```
Q = mcΔT
```

where

- **m** = Mass
- **c** = Specific heat capacity

---

## SI Unit

```
J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹
```

---

# 11. Molar Heat Capacity

Heat required to raise the temperature of one mole of a substance by

```
1 K
```

Formula

```
Q = nCΔT
```

---

## SI Unit

```
J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹
```

# 12. Principle of Calorimetry

## Statement

When hot and cold bodies are mixed in an insulated system,

```
Heat Lost

=

Heat Gained
```

Mathematically,

```
m₁c₁(T₁ − T)

=

m₂c₂(T − T₂)
```

---

# 13. Change of State

Matter changes from one state to another by absorbing or releasing heat.

---

## Melting

Solid → Liquid

---

## Freezing

Liquid → Solid

---

## Vaporisation

Liquid → Gas

---

## Condensation

Gas → Liquid

---

## Sublimation

Solid → Gas directly

Examples

- Camphor
- Dry ice
- Naphthalene

---

# 14. Latent Heat

## Definition

Heat required to change the state of a substance without changing its temperature.

---

## Formula

```
Q = mL
```

where

- **L** = Latent heat

---

## Types

### Latent Heat of Fusion

Solid → Liquid

---

### Latent Heat of Vaporisation

Liquid → Gas

---

# 15. Heat Transfer

Heat is transferred by three methods.

---

## (A) Conduction

Heat transfer without movement of matter.

Examples

- Metal spoon in hot tea
- Iron rod

---

## Fourier's Law

```
       kAΔT
Q = -----------
         L
```

Rate of heat flow,

```
H

=

kAΔT/L
```

where

- **k** = Thermal conductivity

---

## SI Unit of Thermal Conductivity

```
W m⁻¹ K⁻¹
```

---

# 16. Convection

Heat transfer through actual movement of fluid particles.

Examples

- Sea breeze
- Land breeze
- Chimneys
- Boiling water

---

# 17. Radiation

Heat transfer through electromagnetic waves.

No medium required.

Examples

- Heat from Sun
- Electric heater
- Fire

---

# 18. Black Body

## Definition

A black body absorbs all the incident radiation falling on it.

Characteristics

- Best absorber
- Best emitter

---

# 19. Stefan-Boltzmann Law

The rate of emission of radiant energy is proportional to the fourth power of the absolute temperature.

Formula

```
P = σAT⁴
```

where

- **σ** = Stefan-Boltzmann constant

---

# 20. Newton's Law of Cooling

## Statement

The rate of loss of heat is directly proportional to the temperature difference between the body and its surroundings, provided the temperature difference is small.

Formula

```
Rate of Cooling

∝

(T − Ts)
```

---

# 21. Greenhouse Effect

Certain gases in the atmosphere trap outgoing infrared radiation, increasing Earth's average temperature.

---

## Greenhouse Gases

- Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
- Methane (CH₄)
- Water vapour
- Nitrous oxide (N₂O)

---

## Effects

- Global warming
- Climate change
- Melting glaciers
- Rising sea level

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

A steel rod of length **2 m** is heated through **50°C**. If the coefficient of linear expansion is

```
12 × 10⁻⁶ K⁻¹
```

calculate the increase in length.

**Solution**

Using

```
ΔL = αLΔT
```

```
=

12 × 10⁻⁶ × 2 × 50

=

1.2 × 10⁻³ m

=

1.2 mm
```

---

### Example 2

Calculate the heat required to raise the temperature of **2 kg** of water from **20°C** to **30°C**.

Take

```
c = 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹
```

**Solution**

```
Q = mcΔT
```

```
= 2 × 4200 × 10

= 84000 J
```

---

### Example 3

A **0.5 kg** block of ice melts completely. If the latent heat of fusion is

```
3.34 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹
```

calculate the heat absorbed.

**Solution**

```
Q = mL
```

```
= 0.5 × 3.34 × 10⁵

= 1.67 × 10⁵ J
```

---

### Example 4

Convert

```
27°C
```

to Kelvin.

**Solution**

```
K = °C + 273.15
```

```
= 27 + 273.15

= 300.15 K
```

---

### Example 5

A body has a heat capacity of **500 J K⁻¹**. Calculate the heat required to increase its temperature by **8 K**.

**Solution**

```
Q = CΔT
```

```
= 500 × 8

= 4000 J
```

---

### Example 6

A hot metal block of mass **0.2 kg** at **150°C** is placed in **0.4 kg** of water at **25°C**. Assuming no heat loss, explain how the final temperature is determined.

**Solution**

Apply the **principle of calorimetry**:

```
Heat Lost by Metal

=

Heat Gained by Water
```

Using

```
m₁c₁(T₁ − T)

=

m₂c₂(T − T₂)
```

the common final temperature **T** can be calculated after substituting the given values and the specific heat capacities of the metal and water.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **heat** with **temperature**; heat is energy transfer, while temperature measures the thermal state.
- Forgetting to convert temperatures to **Kelvin** where absolute temperature is required.
- Mixing up **heat capacity** and **specific heat capacity**.
- Applying **Q = mcΔT** during a **change of state**, where **Q = mL** should be used instead.
- Ignoring the **anomalous expansion of water** between **0°C and 4°C**.
- Assuming **radiation** requires a medium; electromagnetic radiation can travel through a vacuum.
- Forgetting that **convection occurs only in fluids** (liquids and gases).
- Using the formulas **β = 2α** and **γ = 3α** for materials that are not isotropic.

# Chapter Summary

- **Temperature** measures the degree of hotness of a body, while **heat** is the energy transferred due to a temperature difference.
- Most solids expand on heating and contract on cooling, showing **linear**, **area**, and **volume expansion**.
- Water exhibits **anomalous expansion**, having its maximum density at **4°C**.
- **Heat capacity**, **specific heat capacity**, and **molar heat capacity** describe the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of a substance.
- The **principle of calorimetry** is based on the conservation of energy, where heat lost equals heat gained in an insulated system.
- During a **change of state**, heat is absorbed or released as **latent heat** without changing the temperature.
- Heat is transferred by **conduction**, **convection**, and **radiation**, each governed by different physical principles.
- **Stefan-Boltzmann law** describes thermal radiation from hot bodies, while **Newton's law of cooling** explains the cooling of objects under small temperature differences.
- The concepts of thermal expansion and heat transfer have numerous applications in engineering, climate science, construction, and everyday life.
