# Introduction

Geometry helps us understand the shapes and structures around us. Two of the most fundamental concepts in geometry are **lines** and **angles**. Roads intersect at junctions, railway tracks run parallel to each other, and the corners of buildings form different angles. Understanding these concepts is essential for studying triangles, polygons, circles, and other geometric figures.

In this chapter, we learn about different types of lines and angles, the relationship between angles formed by intersecting and parallel lines, and important angle properties used in geometric proofs.

---

# 1. Basic Concepts

## Line

A **line** is a straight path that extends infinitely in both directions.

Properties:

- Has no endpoints.
- Has infinite length.
- Can be named using two points.

Example:

```
←──────────────→
A              B
```

---

## Line Segment

A **line segment** is a part of a line with two fixed endpoints.

Example:

```
A────────B
```

Unlike a line, a line segment has a definite length.

---

## Ray

A **ray** starts from one fixed point and extends infinitely in one direction.

Example:

```
A────────────→
```

Point **A** is called the initial point of the ray.

---

# 2. Angles

An **angle** is formed when two rays meet at a common endpoint.

The common endpoint is called the **vertex**.

Example:

```
      B
     /
    /
A──O
```

Here,

- **OA** and **OB** are the arms of the angle.
- **O** is the vertex.

---

# 3. Types of Angles

## Acute Angle

An angle measuring **less than 90°**.

Example:

```
35°
```

```
60°
```

---

## Right Angle

An angle measuring exactly

```
90°
```

Example:

```
∟
```

---

## Obtuse Angle

An angle measuring **more than 90° but less than 180°**.

Examples:

```
120°
```

```
145°
```

---

## Straight Angle

An angle measuring exactly

```
180°
```

It forms a straight line.

---

## Reflex Angle

An angle measuring **greater than 180° but less than 360°**.

Example:

```
240°
```

---

## Complete Angle

An angle measuring

```
360°
```

It represents one complete revolution.

---

# 4. Pair of Angles

## Adjacent Angles

Two angles are adjacent if:

- They have a common vertex.
- They share a common arm.
- Their interiors do not overlap.

Example:

```
∠AOB and ∠BOC
```

---

## Linear Pair

A **linear pair** consists of two adjacent angles whose non-common arms form a straight line.

Property:

```
Sum of angles = 180°
```

### Example

If one angle is

```
70°
```

then the other angle is

```
180°−70°=110°
```

---

## Vertically Opposite Angles

When two lines intersect, opposite angles are called **vertically opposite angles**.

Property:

```
Vertically opposite angles are equal.
```

Example:

If one angle is

```
50°
```

then its vertically opposite angle is also

```
50°
```

---

# 5. Parallel Lines and a Transversal

Two lines are **parallel** if they never meet, even when extended indefinitely.

A **transversal** is a line that intersects two or more lines at distinct points.

```
l₁  ─────────────

        /

       /

l₂  ─────────────
```

The slanting line is the transversal.

---

# 6. Angles Formed by a Transversal

When a transversal cuts two parallel lines, several pairs of angles are formed.

---

## Corresponding Angles

These occupy the same relative position.

Property:

```
Corresponding angles are equal.
```

---

## Alternate Interior Angles

These lie inside the parallel lines on opposite sides of the transversal.

Property:

```
Alternate interior angles are equal.
```

---

## Alternate Exterior Angles

These lie outside the parallel lines on opposite sides of the transversal.

Property:

```
Alternate exterior angles are equal.
```

---

## Interior Angles on the Same Side of the Transversal

These are also called **co-interior angles**.

Property:

```
Sum = 180°
```

---

# 7. Criteria for Parallel Lines

If a transversal intersects two lines and any one of the following conditions is true, then the lines are parallel.

- Corresponding angles are equal.
- Alternate interior angles are equal.
- Alternate exterior angles are equal.
- Interior angles on the same side add up to

```
180°
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing complementary angles with supplementary angles.
- Assuming every adjacent angle forms a linear pair.
- Mixing corresponding angles with alternate angles.
- Forgetting that vertically opposite angles are always equal.
- Using angle properties for non-parallel lines.
- Incorrectly identifying the type of an angle based on its measure.

# Chapter Summary

- A line extends infinitely in both directions, while a line segment has two endpoints.
- An angle is formed by two rays meeting at a common vertex.
- Angles are classified as acute, right, obtuse, straight, reflex, and complete angles.
- Adjacent angles share a common vertex and arm.
- A linear pair consists of two adjacent angles whose sum is **180°**.
- Vertically opposite angles formed by intersecting lines are always equal.
- A transversal intersects two or more lines at distinct points.
- When a transversal cuts parallel lines, corresponding, alternate interior, and alternate exterior angles are equal.
- Interior angles on the same side of a transversal are supplementary.
- The properties of lines and angles are fundamental to the study of triangles, polygons, and other geometric figures.
