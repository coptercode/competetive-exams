# Introduction

In geometry, **perimeter** and **area** are two important measurements used to describe plane figures.

- **Perimeter** is the total length of the boundary of a closed figure.
- **Area** is the amount of surface enclosed by a closed figure.

These concepts are widely used in everyday life, such as measuring land, designing parks, constructing buildings, laying tiles, fencing gardens, and estimating the amount of paint required for walls.

---

# 1. Perimeter

The **perimeter** of a closed figure is the total length of its boundary.

### Examples

- Length of fencing around a garden.
- Boundary of a playground.
- Frame around a picture.

The unit of perimeter is measured in **units of length**, such as:

- cm
- m
- km

---

# 2. Area

The **area** of a closed figure is the region enclosed within its boundary.

The unit of area is measured in **square units**, such as:

- cm²
- m²
- km²

---

# 3. Perimeter and Area of a Rectangle

A **rectangle** has opposite sides equal and all angles equal to **90°**.

### Perimeter

```
Perimeter = 2(Length + Breadth)
```

### Area

```
Area = Length × Breadth
```

---

# 4. Perimeter and Area of a Square

A **square** has all sides equal.

### Perimeter

```
Perimeter = 4 × Side
```

### Area

```
Area = Side²
```

---

# 5. Area of a Parallelogram

A **parallelogram** is a quadrilateral with opposite sides parallel.

The area depends on its **base** and **height**.

### Formula

```
Area = Base × Height
```

The height must always be **perpendicular** to the base.

---

# 6. Area of a Triangle

The area of a triangle depends on its base and the corresponding perpendicular height.

### Formula

```
Area = ½ × Base × Height
```

The height is the perpendicular distance from the vertex to the base.

---

# 7. Area of a Rhombus

A **rhombus** is a quadrilateral with all sides equal.

Its area can be found using its diagonals.

### Formula

```
Area = ½ × Diagonal₁ × Diagonal₂
```

---

# 8. Area of a Trapezium

A **trapezium** has one pair of opposite sides parallel.

### Formula

```
Area = ½ × (Sum of Parallel Sides) × Height
```

If the parallel sides are **a** and **b**,

```
Area = ½ × (a + b) × h
```

---

# 9. Conversion of Units

Sometimes measurements must be converted into the same unit before applying formulas.

### Length

```
1 m = 100 cm
```

```
1 km = 1000 m
```

### Area

```
1 m² = 10,000 cm²
```

```
1 km² = 1,000,000 m²
```

Always convert the given values to the same unit before calculation.

---

# 10. Applications of Area and Perimeter

Area and perimeter are used in:

- Fencing agricultural fields.
- Tiling floors.
- Painting walls.
- Constructing buildings.
- Designing parks and gardens.
- Estimating carpet requirements.
- Land measurement.
- Architecture and engineering.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Find the perimeter of a rectangle whose length is

```
12 cm
```

and breadth is

```
8 cm
```

Solution

```
Perimeter = 2(12 + 8)
```

```
=2×20
```

```
=40 cm
```

### Example 2

Find the area of a rectangle whose length is

Solution

```

```

```
=135 cm²

Find the area of a trapezium whose parallel sides are

```
10 cm
```

```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **area** with **perimeter**.
- Forgetting to convert all measurements into the same unit.
- Using the slant height instead of the perpendicular height.
- Omitting square units while writing the area.
- Applying the wrong formula for the given figure.
- Calculating the area of a parallelogram using the slant side instead of the perpendicular height.

# Chapter Summary

- **Perimeter** is the total length of the boundary of a closed figure.
- **Area** is the measure of the surface enclosed by a figure.
- The perimeter of a rectangle is **2(Length + Breadth)**, and its area is **Length × Breadth**.
- The perimeter of a square is **4 × Side**, and its area is **Side²**.
- The area of a triangle is **½ × Base × Height**.
- The area of a parallelogram is **Base × Height**.
- The area of a rhombus is **½ × Diagonal₁ × Diagonal₂**.
- The area of a trapezium is **½ × (Sum of Parallel Sides) × Height**.
- Always use consistent units before performing calculations.
- Area and perimeter have numerous applications in construction, agriculture, architecture, engineering, and everyday measurements.
