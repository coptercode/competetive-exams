# Introduction

The **Animal Kingdom (Kingdom Animalia)** includes a vast diversity of multicellular, eukaryotic, heterotrophic organisms. Animals differ in body organization, symmetry, body cavity, segmentation, and the presence or absence of a notochord. These characteristics form the basis for their classification.

This chapter explains the **basis of animal classification**, the important characteristics of **non-chordates and chordates**, and the distinguishing features of different classes of vertebrates.

---

# 1. General Characteristics of Kingdom Animalia

Animals possess the following common characteristics:

- Multicellular and eukaryotic.
- Heterotrophic mode of nutrition.
- Cells lack cell walls.
- Mostly capable of movement.
- Well-developed nervous and muscular systems.
- Mostly reproduce sexually.
- Embryonic development begins from a zygote.

---

# 2. Basis of Classification

Animals are classified on the basis of several structural and developmental features.

---

## (A) Levels of Organisation

### Cellular Level

Cells are loosely arranged and perform different functions.

Example

- Sponges (Porifera)

---

### Tissue Level

Similar cells form tissues.

Example

- Cnidaria
- Ctenophora

---

### Organ Level

Different tissues combine to form organs.

Example

- Platyhelminthes

---

### Organ System Level

Organs work together as systems.

Example

- Nematoda onwards

---

## Comparison

| Level        | Example              |
| ------------ | -------------------- |
| Cellular     | Porifera             |
| Tissue       | Cnidaria             |
| Organ        | Platyhelminthes      |
| Organ System | Nematoda to Chordata |

---

# 3. Body Symmetry

---

## Asymmetrical

Body cannot be divided into equal halves.

Example

- Sponges

---

## Radial Symmetry

Body can be divided into equal halves through many planes passing through the central axis.

Example

- Hydra
- Starfish (adult)

---

## Bilateral Symmetry

Only one plane divides the body into equal halves.

Example

- Human
- Earthworm

---

# 4. Germ Layers

Animals develop from embryonic germ layers.

---

## Diploblastic Animals

Have two germ layers.

- Ectoderm
- Endoderm

Example

- Hydra

---

## Triploblastic Animals

Have three germ layers.

- Ectoderm
- Mesoderm
- Endoderm

Example

- Earthworm
- Human

---

# 5. Coelom (Body Cavity)

The coelom is a fluid-filled cavity between the body wall and the alimentary canal.

---

## Acoelomates

No body cavity.

Example

- Flatworms

---

## Pseudocoelomates

False body cavity.

Example

- Ascaris

---

## True Coelomates

True body cavity completely lined by mesoderm.

Example

- Earthworm
- Human

---

# 6. Segmentation

Segmentation is the division of the body into repeated units called **metameres**.

Examples

- Earthworm
- Cockroach
- Human (vertebral column)

---

# 7. Notochord

The **notochord** is a flexible rod-like structure present in chordates.

Functions

- Provides support.
- Acts as the primary skeletal structure during development.

---

# 8. Classification of Non-Chordates

---

# Phylum 1: Porifera

Common Name

**Sponges**

## Characteristics

- Cellular level of organization.
- Asymmetrical.
- Body with pores (Ostia).
- Canal system present.
- Skeleton made of spicules or spongin fibers.

Examples

- Sycon
- Spongilla
- Euspongia

---

# Phylum 2: Cnidaria (Coelenterata)

## Characteristics

- Tissue level organization.
- Radial symmetry.
- Diploblastic.
- Cnidoblasts (stinging cells) present.

Examples

- Hydra
- Aurelia
- Adamsia

---

# Phylum 3: Ctenophora

## Characteristics

- Marine.
- Eight rows of ciliary comb plates.
- Bioluminescent.
- Diploblastic.

Examples

- Pleurobrachia
- Ctenoplana

---

# Phylum 4: Platyhelminthes

Common Name

**Flatworms**

## Characteristics

- Bilateral symmetry.
- Triploblastic.
- Acoelomate.
- Dorsoventrally flattened body.

Examples

- Taenia
- Fasciola
- Planaria

---

# Phylum 5: Nematoda (Aschelminthes)

Common Name

**Roundworms**

## Characteristics

- Cylindrical body.
- Pseudocoelom.
- Complete digestive system.
- Sexes separate.

Examples

- Ascaris
- Wuchereria
- Ancylostoma

---

# Phylum 6: Annelida

## Characteristics

- Segmented body.
- True coelom.
- Closed circulatory system.
- Organ-system level.

Examples

- Earthworm
- Leech
- Nereis

---

# Phylum 7: Arthropoda

Largest animal phylum.

## Characteristics

- Jointed appendages.
- Chitinous exoskeleton.
- Open circulatory system.
- Compound eyes.

Examples

- Cockroach
- Butterfly
- Crab
- Honeybee
- Scorpion

---

# Phylum 8: Mollusca

## Characteristics

- Soft unsegmented body.
- Muscular foot.
- Mantle present.
- Shell generally present.

Examples

- Pila
- Octopus
- Unio

---

# Phylum 9: Echinodermata

## Characteristics

- Exclusively marine.
- Endoskeleton made of calcareous plates.
- Water vascular system.
- Adult radial symmetry.

Examples

- Starfish
- Sea Urchin
- Sea Cucumber

---

# Phylum 10: Hemichordata

## Characteristics

- Marine.
- Worm-like.
- Body divided into

  - Proboscis
  - Collar
  - Trunk

- Gill slits present.
- Notochord absent (possess stomochord).

Examples

- Balanoglossus
- Saccoglossus

---

# 9. Chordata

## General Characteristics

- Notochord present at some stage.
- Dorsal hollow nerve cord.
- Pharyngeal gill slits.
- Post-anal tail.
- Closed circulatory system.

---

## Distinguishing Features: Chordates vs Non-Chordates

| Chordates                     | Non-Chordates                   |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Notochord present             | Notochord absent                |
| Dorsal nerve cord             | Ventral nerve cord              |
| Pharyngeal gill slits present | Gill slits absent               |
| Closed circulatory system     | Usually open circulatory system |

---

# 11. Vertebrata

Vertebrates possess a vertebral column and cranium.

---

## Class 1: Pisces

### Characteristics

- Aquatic.
- Gills for respiration.
- Fins for locomotion.
- Cold-blooded.
- Two-chambered heart.

Examples

- Rohu
- Shark

---

## Class 2: Amphibia

### Characteristics

- Live on land and in water.
- Moist skin.
- Three-chambered heart.
- Respire through lungs and skin.

Examples

- Frog
- Salamander

---

## Class 3: Reptilia

### Characteristics

- Dry scaly skin.
- Internal fertilization.
- Mostly oviparous.
- Three-chambered heart (except crocodile).

Examples

- Snake
- Lizard
- Turtle
- Crocodile

---

## Class 4: Aves

### Characteristics

- Feathers present.
- Forelimbs modified into wings.
- Warm-blooded.
- Four-chambered heart.
- Oviparous.

Examples

- Pigeon
- Peacock
- Crow

---

## Class 5: Mammalia

### Characteristics

- Mammary glands.
- Hair on body.
- External ears.
- Four-chambered heart.
- Mostly viviparous.

Examples

- Human
- Cow
- Dog
- Whale

---

# 12. Diagnostic Characters of Vertebrates

| Class    | Body Covering | Respiration  | Heart       | Example |
| -------- | ------------- | ------------ | ----------- | ------- |
| Pisces   | Scales        | Gills        | 2 chambers  | Rohu    |
| Amphibia | Moist skin    | Lungs & Skin | 3 chambers  | Frog    |
| Reptilia | Dry scales    | Lungs        | 3 chambers* | Snake   |
| Aves     | Feathers      | Lungs        | 4 chambers  | Pigeon  |
| Mammalia | Hair          | Lungs        | 4 chambers  | Human   |

\*Crocodiles possess a four-chambered heart.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Why are arthropods considered the largest phylum?

**Solution**

Arthropoda is the largest phylum because it contains the maximum number of described animal species, including insects, crustaceans, and arachnids.

---

### Example 2

Name one characteristic feature of echinoderms.

**Solution**

Echinoderms possess a **water vascular system**, which helps in locomotion, feeding, and respiration.

---

### Example 3

Differentiate between radial and bilateral symmetry.

**Solution**

- **Radial symmetry:** The body can be divided into equal halves through many planes passing through the central axis (e.g., Hydra).
- **Bilateral symmetry:** Only one plane divides the body into equal halves (e.g., Human).

---

### Example 4

Why are amphibians called amphibians?

**Solution**

Amphibians spend part of their life in water and part on land, and therefore are called amphibians.

---

### Example 5

State two distinguishing features of mammals.

**Solution**

- Presence of **mammary glands** for feeding young ones.
- Body covered with **hair**.

---

### Example 6

What is the main difference between chordates and non-chordates?

**Solution**

Chordates possess a **notochord**, a **dorsal hollow nerve cord**, and **pharyngeal gill slits** at some stage of development, whereas non-chordates lack these features.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Hemichordata** with **Chordata**; hemichordates possess a **stomochord**, not a true notochord.
- Assuming all animals have bilateral symmetry; poriferans are asymmetrical and cnidarians show radial symmetry.
- Confusing **acoelomates**, **pseudocoelomates**, and **coelomates**.
- Forgetting that **Arthropoda** has an **open circulatory system**.
- Assuming all reptiles have a three-chambered heart; **crocodiles** have a four-chambered heart.
- Mixing up the body coverings of vertebrate classes (scales, feathers, hair).
- Believing all mammals are viviparous; **monotremes** (e.g., platypus) are oviparous.
- Confusing the examples of different phyla during classification.

# Chapter Summary

- **Kingdom Animalia** includes multicellular, eukaryotic, heterotrophic organisms lacking cell walls.
- Animals are classified based on **levels of organization**, **symmetry**, **germ layers**, **coelom**, **segmentation**, and the presence or absence of a **notochord**.
- **Non-chordates** comprise ten major phyla, from **Porifera** to **Hemichordata**, each with distinct structural and functional characteristics.
- **Chordates** are characterized by a **notochord**, **dorsal hollow nerve cord**, **pharyngeal gill slits**, **post-anal tail**, and a **closed circulatory system**.
- Vertebrates are classified into **Pisces, Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves,** and **Mammalia**, each distinguished by unique body coverings, respiratory organs, heart structure, and reproductive adaptations.
- Comparative studies of animal groups help in understanding their evolutionary relationships and biological diversity.
- The classification of animals forms the foundation for advanced topics in zoology, anatomy, physiology, ecology, and evolutionary biology.
