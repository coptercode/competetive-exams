# Introduction

Electricity and magnetism are closely related. Whenever an electric current flows through a conductor, it produces a **magnetic field** around it. This principle is used in devices such as **electric motors, generators, loudspeakers,** and **electromagnets**. This chapter explains magnetic fields, the magnetic effect of electric current, Fleming's rules, the working of electric motors and generators, and domestic electric safety.

---

# 1. Magnet

A **magnet** is a material that attracts magnetic substances such as iron, nickel, and cobalt.

### Properties of a Magnet

- Has two poles: **North (N)** and **South (S)**.
- Like poles repel each other.
- Unlike poles attract each other.
- Freely suspended magnet aligns itself in the north-south direction.

---

# 2. Magnetic Field

A **magnetic field** is the region around a magnet or a current-carrying conductor where its magnetic effect can be experienced.

### Representation

Magnetic fields are represented by **magnetic field lines**.

---

## Characteristics of Magnetic Field Lines

- Originate from the north pole and enter the south pole outside the magnet.
- Form closed continuous curves.
- Never intersect each other.
- Closer field lines indicate a stronger magnetic field.

---

### Magnetic Field Around a Bar Magnet

```
       N -------- S

   → → → → → → →

  Curved magnetic
     field lines
```

# 4. Right-Hand Thumb Rule

The **Right-Hand Thumb Rule** is used to determine the direction of the magnetic field around a current-carrying conductor.

### Statement

If the conductor is held in the **right hand** with the **thumb pointing in the direction of current**, then the curled fingers indicate the direction of the magnetic field.

---

### Diagram

```
Thumb

↑ Current

Curled Fingers

↺ Magnetic Field
```

---

# 5. Magnetic Field Due to a Circular Current Loop

When current flows through a circular loop:

- Magnetic field lines are circular near the wire.
- At the centre, the magnetic field becomes nearly straight and parallel.
- The strength of the magnetic field increases with the number of turns.

---

### Factors Affecting Magnetic Field Strength

- Magnitude of current.
- Number of turns.
- Radius of the loop.

---

# 6. Solenoid

A **solenoid** is a long cylindrical coil made of many turns of insulated copper wire.

---

### Magnetic Field of a Solenoid

```
 N |||||||||| S
```

A current-carrying solenoid behaves like a **bar magnet**.

---

## Characteristics

- Produces a strong and nearly uniform magnetic field inside.
- Magnetic field is weak outside.
- One end behaves as the north pole and the other as the south pole.

---

# 7. Electromagnet

An **electromagnet** is a temporary magnet produced by passing electric current through a solenoid wound around a soft iron core.

---

## Advantages

- Magnetism can be switched on and off.
- Strength can be controlled.
- Temporary in nature.

---

## Uses

- Electric bells.
- Cranes for lifting scrap iron.
- Electric relays.
- Loudspeakers.

---

# 8. Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor

A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force.

The direction of the force depends on:

- Direction of current.
- Direction of magnetic field.

---

# 9. Fleming's Left-Hand Rule

This rule determines the direction of the force acting on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field.

### Statement

Stretch the thumb, forefinger, and middle finger of the **left hand** mutually perpendicular.

- **Forefinger** → Direction of magnetic field.
- **Middle finger** → Direction of current.
- **Thumb** → Direction of force (motion).

---

### Diagram

```
Forefinger → Magnetic Field

Middle Finger ↓ Current

Thumb ← Force
```

---

# 10. Electric Motor

An **electric motor** is a device that converts **electrical energy into mechanical energy**.

---

## Principle

A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force.

---

## Main Parts

- Armature coil.
- Permanent magnet.
- Split-ring commutator.
- Carbon brushes.
- DC power supply.

---

### Simplified Diagram

```
N          S

|----------|

   Coil

Split Ring

Battery
```

---

## Working

- Current flows through the coil.
- Magnetic field exerts opposite forces on the two sides of the coil.
- The coil rotates.
- The split-ring commutator reverses the current every half rotation, allowing continuous rotation.

---

## Applications

- Electric fans.
- Mixers.
- Water pumps.
- Washing machines.
- Electric vehicles.

---

# 11. Electromagnetic Induction

**Electromagnetic induction** is the phenomenon of producing an electric current in a conductor by changing the magnetic field around it.

This phenomenon was discovered by **Michael Faraday**.

---

## Methods of Producing Induced Current

- Moving a magnet near a coil.
- Moving a coil near a magnet.
- Changing the magnetic field through a stationary coil.

---

# 12. Fleming's Right-Hand Rule

This rule determines the direction of the induced current.

### Statement

Stretch the thumb, forefinger, and middle finger of the **right hand** mutually perpendicular.

- **Thumb** → Direction of motion.
- **Forefinger** → Direction of magnetic field.
- **Middle finger** → Direction of induced current.

---

### Diagram

```
Thumb → Motion

Forefinger ↑ Magnetic Field

Middle Finger ↓ Current
```

---

# 13. Electric Generator

An **electric generator** converts **mechanical energy into electrical energy**.

---

## Principle

Electric generators work on the principle of **electromagnetic induction**.

---

## Main Parts

- Armature coil.
- Strong magnet.
- Slip rings.
- Carbon brushes.
- External circuit.

---

### Simplified Diagram

```
N          S

Rotating Coil

Slip Rings

Brushes
```

---

## Types of Generators

### (A) AC Generator

Produces **alternating current (AC)**.

Uses:

- Power stations.
- Household electricity supply.

---

### (B) DC Generator

Produces **direct current (DC)**.

Uses:

- Battery charging.
- Electroplating.
- Laboratory equipment.

---

## Difference Between Electric Motor and Electric Generator

| Electric Motor                                    | Electric Generator                                |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Converts electrical energy into mechanical energy | Converts mechanical energy into electrical energy |
| Works on Fleming's Left-Hand Rule                 | Works on Fleming's Right-Hand Rule                |
| Uses split-ring commutator                        | Uses slip rings (AC generator)                    |
| Consumes electricity                              | Produces electricity                              |

# 14. Domestic Electric Circuits

A domestic electric circuit distributes electricity safely throughout a building.

---

## Main Components

- Live wire.
- Neutral wire.
- Earth wire.
- Main fuse.
- Electric meter.
- Miniature Circuit Breaker (MCB).

---

## Earth Wire

Functions:

- Prevents electric shock.
- Carries leakage current safely to the ground.
- Connected to the metal body of electrical appliances.

---

## Electric Fuse

A **fuse** protects electrical appliances from:

- Overloading.
- Short circuit.

It works due to the **heating effect of electric current**.

---

## Miniature Circuit Breaker (MCB)

An **MCB** automatically switches off the circuit when excessive current flows.

### Advantages over Fuse

- Can be reset after tripping.
- Operates quickly.
- More reliable.
- Safer and reusable.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **Fleming's Left-Hand Rule** with **Fleming's Right-Hand Rule**.
- Mixing up the functions of an **electric motor** and an **electric generator**.
- Assuming a solenoid and an electromagnet are the same (an electromagnet includes a soft iron core).
- Forgetting the direction of magnetic field lines around a current-carrying conductor.
- Confusing **split-ring commutator** with **slip rings**.
- Assuming the earth wire carries current during normal operation (it carries current only during leakage).
- Confusing **electromagnetic induction** with the magnetic effect of electric current.
- Assuming an MCB works by melting like a fuse.

# Chapter Summary

- A **current-carrying conductor** produces a **magnetic field** around it, whose direction can be determined using the **Right-Hand Thumb Rule**.
- A **solenoid** produces a strong, nearly uniform magnetic field and behaves like a bar magnet. With a soft iron core, it forms an **electromagnet**.
- A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force, explained by **Fleming's Left-Hand Rule**, which is the working principle of an **electric motor**.
- **Electromagnetic induction**, discovered by **Michael Faraday**, is the production of electric current due to a changing magnetic field.
- **Fleming's Right-Hand Rule** helps determine the direction of induced current in a generator.
- An **electric generator** converts **mechanical energy into electrical energy**, while an **electric motor** converts **electrical energy into mechanical energy**.
- Domestic electrical safety is ensured through the use of **earth wires, fuses,** and **Miniature Circuit Breakers (MCBs)**, which protect users and appliances from electric shock, overload, and short circuits.
