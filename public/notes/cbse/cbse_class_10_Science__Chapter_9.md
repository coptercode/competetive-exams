# Introduction

**Light** is a form of energy that enables us to see objects. It travels in a straight line and exhibits phenomena such as **reflection** and **refraction**. Reflection is the bouncing back of light from a surface, while refraction is the bending of light when it passes from one medium to another. This chapter explains the laws of reflection, spherical mirrors, refraction through lenses, image formation, and related numerical concepts.

---

# 1. Light

**Light** is a form of electromagnetic energy that produces the sensation of vision.

### Characteristics of Light

- Travels in a straight line.
- Travels fastest in vacuum.
- Undergoes reflection and refraction.
- Does not require a material medium for propagation.

### Speed of Light

In vacuum,

```
c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
```

**SI Unit of Speed:** metre per second (m/s)

---

# 2. Reflection of Light

**Reflection** is the bouncing back of light into the same medium after striking a surface.

---

## Laws of Reflection

### First Law

The **incident ray**, **reflected ray**, and the **normal** at the point of incidence lie in the same plane.

---

### Second Law

The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.

```
∠i = ∠r
```

---

### Reflection from a Plane Mirror

```
          Reflected Ray
               /
              /
             /
------------|------------- Mirror
            |
            | Normal
            |
           /
          /
 Incident Ray
```

---

# 3. Spherical Mirrors

A **spherical mirror** is a mirror whose reflecting surface forms part of a sphere.

### Types

- Concave Mirror
- Convex Mirror

---

## Concave Mirror

A **concave mirror** has its reflecting surface curved inward.

### Characteristics

- Converging mirror.
- Can form real or virtual images.
- Produces magnified images for nearby objects.

### Uses

- Shaving mirrors.
- Dentist's mirrors.
- Solar furnaces.
- Vehicle headlights.

---

## Convex Mirror

A **convex mirror** has its reflecting surface curved outward.

### Characteristics

- Diverging mirror.
- Always forms virtual, erect, and diminished images.
- Provides a wider field of view.

### Uses

- Rear-view mirrors in vehicles.
- Security mirrors in shops.
- Road intersections.

---

# 4. Important Terms Related to Mirrors

| Term                    | Definition                                                            |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pole (P)                | Centre of the mirror's reflecting surface.                            |
| Centre of Curvature (C) | Centre of the sphere of which the mirror is a part.                   |
| Radius of Curvature (R) | Distance between P and C.                                             |
| Principal Axis          | Straight line passing through P and C.                                |
| Principal Focus (F)     | Point where parallel rays meet or appear to diverge after reflection. |
| Focal Length (f)        | Distance between P and F.                                             |

---

## Relation Between Radius of Curvature and Focal Length

```
R = 2f
```

or

```
f = R/2
```

---

# 5. Image Formation by Concave Mirror

| Position of Object | Position of Image | Nature of Image                   |
| ------------------ | ----------------- | --------------------------------- |
| At infinity        | At F              | Real, inverted, highly diminished |
| Beyond C           | Between C and F   | Real, inverted, diminished        |
| At C               | At C              | Real, inverted, same size         |
| Between C and F    | Beyond C          | Real, inverted, magnified         |
| At F               | At infinity       | Real, inverted, highly enlarged   |
| Between F and P    | Behind mirror     | Virtual, erect, magnified         |

---

# 6. Image Formation by Convex Mirror

| Position of Object | Position of Image                 | Nature of Image            |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Anywhere           | Between P and F behind the mirror | Virtual, erect, diminished |

---

# 7. Mirror Formula

For spherical mirrors,

```
1/f = 1/v + 1/u
```

Where:

- **f** = Focal length
- **u** = Object distance
- **v** = Image distance

**SI Unit:** metre (m)

---

# 8. Magnification by Mirrors

Magnification is the ratio of the height of the image to the height of the object.

### Formula

```
m = hᵢ / hₒ
```

Also,

```
m = -v/u
```

Where:

- **m** = Magnification
- **hᵢ** = Height of image
- **hₒ** = Height of object

---

# 9. Sign Convention for Mirrors

According to the **New Cartesian Sign Convention**:

- All distances are measured from the pole.
- Distances measured in the direction of incident light are positive.
- Distances measured opposite to the direction of incident light are negative.
- Heights above the principal axis are positive.
- Heights below the principal axis are negative.

---

# 10. Refraction of Light

**Refraction** is the bending of light when it passes from one transparent medium to another due to a change in its speed.

---

## Laws of Refraction (Snell's Laws)

### First Law

The incident ray, refracted ray, and the normal lie in the same plane.

---

### Second Law

```
n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r
```

Where:

- **n₁** = Refractive index of first medium
- **n₂** = Refractive index of second medium

---

# 11. Refractive Index

The **refractive index** of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to its speed in that medium.

### Formula

```
n = c/v
```

Where:

- **n** = Refractive index
- **c** = Speed of light in vacuum
- **v** = Speed of light in the medium

---

# 12. Lenses

A **lens** is a transparent optical device bounded by two refracting surfaces.

---

## Types of Lenses

### Convex Lens

A **convex lens** is thicker at the centre than at the edges.

### Characteristics

- Converging lens.
- Can produce real or virtual images.

### Uses

- Magnifying glass.
- Camera.
- Microscope.
- Human eye correction (hypermetropia).

---

### Concave Lens

A **concave lens** is thinner at the centre than at the edges.

### Characteristics

- Diverging lens.
- Always forms virtual, erect, and diminished images.

### Uses

- Correcting myopia.
- Door viewers.

---

# 13. Important Terms Related to Lenses

| Term                | Definition                                                              |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Optical Centre (O)  | Central point of the lens through which light passes without deviation. |
| Principal Focus (F) | Point where parallel rays meet or appear to diverge after refraction.   |
| Focal Length (f)    | Distance between the optical centre and the principal focus.            |
| Principal Axis      | Straight line passing through the optical centre and principal foci.    |

---

# 14. Image Formation by Convex Lens

| Position of Object | Position of Image  | Nature of Image                   |
| ------------------ | ------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| At infinity        | At F₂              | Real, inverted, highly diminished |
| Beyond 2F₁         | Between F₂ and 2F₂ | Real, inverted, diminished        |
| At 2F₁             | At 2F₂             | Real, inverted, same size         |
| Between F₁ and 2F₁ | Beyond 2F₂         | Real, inverted, magnified         |
| At F₁              | At infinity        | Real, inverted, highly enlarged   |
| Between F₁ and O   | Same side of lens  | Virtual, erect, magnified         |

---

# 15. Image Formation by Concave Lens

| Position of Object | Position of Image                | Nature of Image            |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Anywhere           | Between O and F on the same side | Virtual, erect, diminished |

---

# 16. Lens Formula

For spherical lenses,

```
1/f = 1/v − 1/u
```

Where:

- **f** = Focal length
- **u** = Object distance
- **v** = Image distance

**SI Unit:** metre (m)

---

# 17. Magnification by Lenses

### Formula

```
m = hᵢ / hₒ
```

Also,

```
m = v/u
```

---

# 18. Power of a Lens

The **power** of a lens is the ability of the lens to converge or diverge light rays.

### Formula

```
P = 1/f
```

Where:

- **P** = Power (in dioptre)
- **f** = Focal length (in metre)

### SI Unit

```
Dioptre (D)
```

### Notes

- Convex lens → Positive power.
- Concave lens → Negative power.

---

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

State the laws of reflection.

**Solution**

1. The incident ray, reflected ray, and normal lie in the same plane.
2. The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.

---

### Example 2

A mirror has a radius of curvature of **40 cm**. Find its focal length.

**Solution**

Using,

```
f = R/2
```

```
f = 40/2 = 20 cm
```

**Answer:** **20 cm**

---

### Example 3

State one use each of a concave mirror and a convex mirror.

**Solution**

- Concave mirror: Dentist's mirror.
- Convex mirror: Rear-view mirror in vehicles.

---

### Example 4

Write the mirror formula.

**Solution**

```
1/f = 1/v + 1/u
```

---

### Example 5

What is the SI unit of power of a lens?

**Solution**

The SI unit is the **dioptre (D)**.

---

### Example 6

Calculate the power of a lens having a focal length of **0.5 m**.

**Solution**

Using,

```
P = 1/f
```

```
P = 1/0.5 = 2 D
```

**Answer:** **+2 D**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **reflection** with **refraction**.
- Using the wrong sign convention in mirror and lens formulae.
- Mixing up **concave** and **convex** mirrors.
- Forgetting that **R = 2f** for spherical mirrors.
- Confusing **magnification** formulae for mirrors and lenses.
- Using focal length in **centimetres** while calculating power (it must be in **metres**).
- Assuming a convex lens always forms a magnified image (it depends on the object's position).
- Forgetting that a concave lens always forms a **virtual, erect, and diminished** image.

# Chapter Summary

- **Light** is a form of energy that travels in a straight line and undergoes **reflection** and **refraction**.
- **Reflection** follows two laws, including the equality of the angles of incidence and reflection.
- **Concave mirrors** are converging mirrors, while **convex mirrors** are diverging mirrors with a wider field of view.
- The **mirror formula** and **magnification formula** are used to determine image position and size.
- **Refraction** is the bending of light due to a change in its speed when moving between different media and is governed by **Snell's Law**.
- **Convex lenses** converge light rays, whereas **concave lenses** diverge them.
- The **lens formula** and **power of a lens** are important in solving numerical problems related to image formation.
- Understanding the properties of mirrors and lenses is essential for explaining the working of optical instruments such as cameras, microscopes, telescopes, spectacles, and the human eye.
