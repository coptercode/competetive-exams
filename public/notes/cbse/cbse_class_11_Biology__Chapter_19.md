# Introduction

Metabolic activities in the body produce waste substances that can be harmful if allowed to accumulate. The process of removing these wastes is called **excretion**. The human excretory system maintains the internal environment by eliminating nitrogenous wastes, regulating water and electrolyte balance, and maintaining acid-base balance.

The principal excretory organs are the **kidneys**, which produce urine through millions of microscopic filtering units called **nephrons**.

---

# 1. Excretion

## Definition

Excretion is the process of removing metabolic waste products and excess substances from the body.

---

## Functions

- Removes nitrogenous wastes.
- Maintains water balance.
- Maintains electrolyte balance.
- Regulates blood pH.
- Maintains homeostasis.

---

# 2. Nitrogenous Wastes

The major nitrogenous wastes are:

- Ammonia
- Urea
- Uric acid

---

## Types of Animals

### Ammonotelic Animals

Excrete:

```
Ammonia
```

Examples

- Bony fishes
- Aquatic invertebrates

---

### Ureotelic Animals

Excrete:

```
Urea
```

Examples

- Humans
- Mammals
- Adult amphibians

---

### Uricotelic Animals

Excrete:

```
Uric Acid
```

Examples

- Birds
- Reptiles
- Insects

# 4. Kidneys

Humans possess a pair of bean-shaped kidneys located in the abdominal cavity.

### Characteristics

- Reddish-brown.
- Retroperitoneal.
- Protected by adipose tissue.
- Each kidney weighs about **120–170 g**.

---

# Structure of Kidney

```
Kidney
│
├── Cortex
├── Medulla
└── Renal Pelvis
```

---

## Cortex

- Outer region.
- Contains renal corpuscles.

---

## Medulla

- Inner region.
- Contains renal pyramids.

---

## Renal Pelvis

- Funnel-shaped cavity.
- Collects urine before it enters the ureter.

---

# 5. Nephron

The nephron is the **structural and functional unit** of the kidney.

Each kidney contains approximately **1 million nephrons**.

---

## Types of Nephrons

### Cortical Nephrons

- Short loop of Henle.
- Majority of nephrons.

---

### Juxtamedullary Nephrons

- Long loop of Henle.
- Important for concentrating urine.

---

# 6. Renal Corpuscle

The renal corpuscle consists of:

- Glomerulus
- Bowman's capsule

---

## Glomerulus

A network of capillaries where blood filtration occurs.

---

## Bowman's Capsule

Cup-shaped structure that surrounds the glomerulus and receives the filtrate.

---

# 7. Parts of Renal Tubule

---

## Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)

Functions

- Reabsorption of glucose.
- Reabsorption of amino acids.
- Reabsorption of sodium.
- Reabsorption of water.

---

## Loop of Henle

### Descending Limb

- Permeable to water.
- Impermeable to salts.

---

### Ascending Limb

- Impermeable to water.
- Actively transports sodium and chloride ions.

---

## Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)

Functions

- Selective reabsorption.
- Secretion of potassium and hydrogen ions.

---

## Collecting Duct

Functions

- Final concentration of urine.
- Water reabsorption under ADH influence.

---

# 8. Juxtaglomerular Apparatus (JGA)

Located where the DCT comes in close contact with the afferent arteriole.

Functions

- Secretes **renin**.
- Regulates blood pressure.
- Maintains glomerular filtration rate (GFR).

---

# 9. Urine Formation

Urine formation occurs in three steps.

```
Glomerular Filtration
        │
Tubular Reabsorption
        │
Tubular Secretion
```

---

# 10. Glomerular Filtration

Blood is filtered through the glomerulus into Bowman's capsule.

The filtrate formed is called **glomerular filtrate**.

---

## Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR)

Average GFR:

```
Approximately 125 mL/min
```

About **180 litres** of filtrate are formed daily.

---

# 11. Tubular Reabsorption

Useful substances are reabsorbed into the blood.

Examples

- Glucose
- Amino acids
- Water
- Sodium
- Bicarbonate ions

About **99%** of the filtrate is reabsorbed.

---

# 12. Tubular Secretion

Certain substances are actively secreted into the filtrate.

Examples

- Potassium ions
- Hydrogen ions
- Ammonia
- Drugs
- Creatinine

---

# 13. Counter Current Mechanism

The counter current mechanism helps concentrate urine.

Components

- Loop of Henle
- Vasa recta

---

## Importance

- Maintains medullary osmotic gradient.
- Conserves water.
- Produces concentrated urine.

---

# 14. Regulation of Kidney Function

---

## Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH)

Secreted by:

- Posterior pituitary

Functions

- Increases water reabsorption.
- Produces concentrated urine.

---

## Aldosterone

Secreted by:

- Adrenal cortex

Functions

- Increases sodium reabsorption.
- Increases potassium excretion.

---

## Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS)

```
Low Blood Pressure
        │
Renin
        │
Angiotensin I
        │
Angiotensin II
        │
Aldosterone
```

Functions

- Raises blood pressure.
- Increases sodium and water reabsorption.

---

## Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF)

Released by:

- Atria of the heart

Functions

- Decreases blood pressure.
- Increases sodium excretion.
- Reduces renin and aldosterone secretion.

---

# 15. Micturition

Micturition is the process of expelling urine from the urinary bladder.

It is regulated by the **micturition reflex**, which is coordinated by the spinal cord and controlled voluntarily by higher brain centres.

---

# 16. Composition of Urine

Normal urine contains:

- Water (about 95%)
- Urea
- Uric acid
- Creatinine
- Sodium ions
- Potassium ions

Normal urine does **not** contain:

- Glucose
- Proteins
- Blood cells

---

# 17. Role of the Kidneys in Homeostasis

The kidneys help maintain:

- Water balance.
- Electrolyte balance.
- Blood pressure.
- Blood pH.
- Osmotic balance.

---

# 18. Artificial Kidney (Haemodialysis)

Haemodialysis removes waste products from the blood when the kidneys fail.

### Principle

- Diffusion through a semipermeable membrane.

### Uses

- Kidney failure.
- Chronic renal disease.

---

# 19. Disorders of the Excretory System

---

## Uremia

Accumulation of urea in the blood due to kidney failure.

Treatment

- Haemodialysis
- Kidney transplantation

---

## Renal Calculi (Kidney Stones)

Hard deposits of calcium salts, uric acid, or oxalates formed in the kidneys.

Symptoms

- Severe pain.
- Difficulty in urination.
- Blood in urine.

---

## Glomerulonephritis

Inflammation of the glomeruli due to infection or immune reactions.

---

## Acute Renal Failure

Sudden loss of kidney function leading to reduced urine production and accumulation of wastes.

---

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Why are nephrons called the structural and functional units of the kidney?

**Solution**

Each nephron filters blood, reabsorbs useful substances, secretes unwanted substances, and produces urine. Since every kidney function is performed by nephrons, they are the structural and functional units of the kidney.

---

### Example 2

Differentiate between cortical and juxtamedullary nephrons.

**Solution**

Cortical nephrons have short loops of Henle and are more numerous, whereas juxtamedullary nephrons possess long loops of Henle that help produce highly concentrated urine.

---

### Example 3

What are the three major steps of urine formation?

**Solution**

Urine formation involves:

1. Glomerular filtration.
2. Tubular reabsorption.
3. Tubular secretion.

---

### Example 4

What is the role of ADH?

**Solution**

ADH increases the permeability of the collecting ducts to water, promoting water reabsorption and producing concentrated urine during dehydration.

---

### Example 5

Explain the function of the counter current mechanism.

**Solution**

The counter current mechanism of the loop of Henle and vasa recta maintains a high osmotic gradient in the renal medulla, allowing maximum water reabsorption and the formation of concentrated urine.

---

### Example 6

What is haemodialysis?

**Solution**

Haemodialysis is an artificial method of removing metabolic wastes, excess salts, and water from the blood using a semipermeable membrane when the kidneys fail to function properly.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **excretion** with **egestion**; excretion removes metabolic wastes, whereas egestion removes undigested food.
- Assuming all animals excrete **urea**; many aquatic animals are **ammonotelic**, while birds and reptiles are **uricotelic**.
- Mixing up the **proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)** and the **distal convoluted tubule (DCT)**.
- Forgetting that the **loop of Henle** plays a major role in concentrating urine through the counter current mechanism.
- Assuming glucose is normally present in urine; under healthy conditions, glucose is almost completely reabsorbed.
- Confusing the actions of **ADH** and **aldosterone**.
- Forgetting that the **juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA)** secretes **renin**.
- Assuming haemodialysis cures kidney disease; it only replaces some kidney functions until recovery or transplantation.

---

# Chapter Summary

- **Excretion** is the removal of metabolic waste products, helping maintain homeostasis.
- Nitrogenous wastes include **ammonia**, **urea**, and **uric acid**, giving rise to ammonotelic, ureotelic, and uricotelic animals.
- The human excretory system consists of the **kidneys**, **ureters**, **urinary bladder**, and **urethra**.
- The **nephron** is the structural and functional unit of the kidney and performs **glomerular filtration**, **tubular reabsorption**, and **tubular secretion** to form urine.
- The **counter current mechanism** in the loop of Henle and vasa recta enables the kidneys to produce concentrated urine and conserve water.
- Hormones such as **ADH**, **aldosterone**, **renin**, and **ANF** regulate water balance, electrolyte balance, and blood pressure.
- The kidneys play a vital role in maintaining blood pH, osmotic balance, electrolyte balance, and overall internal homeostasis.
- **Haemodialysis** temporarily replaces kidney function in patients with severe renal failure.
- Disorders such as **uremia**, **kidney stones**, **glomerulonephritis**, and **acute renal failure** impair excretory function and require appropriate medical management.
