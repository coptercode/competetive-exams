# Introduction

Food is one of the basic necessities of life. With the rapid increase in population, there is a growing demand for food. To meet this demand, agricultural production and animal farming must be improved using scientific methods.

**Improvement in Food Resources** involves increasing the quantity and quality of food by adopting better farming practices, crop protection methods, proper nutrient management, irrigation techniques, and scientific animal husbandry.

---

# 1. Food Resources

Food resources are obtained mainly from:

- Crops
- Livestock
- Fisheries
- Poultry
- Bee-keeping

The main objectives are:

- Increase food production.
- Improve food quality.
- Reduce crop losses.
- Ensure sustainable farming.

---

# 2. Crop Variety Improvement

Crop variety improvement aims to develop better crop plants with desirable characteristics.

### Objectives

- Higher yield.
- Improved quality.
- Resistance to diseases.
- Resistance to pests.
- Drought resistance.
- Flood resistance.
- Early maturity.
- Better adaptability.
- Improved nutritional value.

### Methods

- Hybridization
- Selection
- Genetic improvement

---

# 3. Crop Production Management

Crop production management involves practices that increase crop yield.

It includes:

- Nutrient management.
- Irrigation.
- Cropping patterns.

---

# 4. Nutrient Management

Plants require nutrients for proper growth and development.

### Types of Nutrients

---

## Macronutrients

Required in large quantities.

Examples:

- Nitrogen (N)
- Phosphorus (P)
- Potassium (K)
- Calcium (Ca)
- Magnesium (Mg)
- Sulphur (S)

---

## Micronutrients

Required in very small quantities.

Examples:

- Iron (Fe)
- Zinc (Zn)
- Copper (Cu)
- Manganese (Mn)
- Boron (B)
- Molybdenum (Mo)

---

# 5. Manure

**Manure** is a natural organic substance obtained from the decomposition of plant and animal wastes.

### Advantages

- Improves soil fertility.
- Increases water-holding capacity.
- Adds humus.
- Improves soil texture.
- Environment-friendly.

### Types

- Compost
- Vermicompost
- Green manure

---

# 6. Fertilizers

**Fertilizers** are chemical substances rich in essential plant nutrients.

### Examples

- Urea
- Ammonium sulphate
- Superphosphate
- Potash

### Advantages

- Rapid plant growth.
- Higher crop yield.
- Easy to apply.

### Disadvantages

- Soil pollution.
- Water pollution.
- Reduced soil fertility if overused.

---

# 7. Difference Between Manure and Fertilizers

| Manure                    | Fertilizers                               |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Organic                   | Chemical                                  |
| Prepared naturally        | Manufactured in industries                |
| Improves soil texture     | Does not improve soil texture             |
| Releases nutrients slowly | Releases nutrients quickly                |
| Eco-friendly              | Excessive use may pollute the environment |

---

# 8. Irrigation

**Irrigation** is the artificial supply of water to crops at regular intervals.

### Importance

- Ensures healthy plant growth.
- Prevents crop failure.
- Increases yield.
- Maintains soil moisture.

---

## Sources of Irrigation

- Wells
- Tubewells
- Rivers
- Lakes
- Canals
- Dams

---

## Modern Irrigation Methods

### Sprinkler System

Water is sprayed over crops like rainfall.

#### Advantages

- Suitable for uneven land.
- Saves water.
- Uniform distribution.

---

### Drip Irrigation

Water is supplied directly to plant roots through pipes.

#### Advantages

- Maximum water conservation.
- Suitable for fruit plants.
- Reduces weed growth.

---

# 9. Cropping Patterns

Scientific cropping patterns help improve productivity and maintain soil fertility.

---

## Mixed Cropping

Two or more crops are grown together on the same field.

### Advantages

- Reduces risk of crop failure.
- Better utilization of resources.

Example:

- Wheat + Gram

---

## Intercropping

Two or more crops are grown in alternate rows.

### Advantages

- Efficient use of nutrients.
- Reduced spread of pests.
- Higher productivity.

Example:

- Soybean + Maize

---

## Crop Rotation

Different crops are grown alternately on the same field in different seasons.

### Advantages

- Maintains soil fertility.
- Prevents pest infestation.
- Improves yield.

Example:

- Rice → Wheat → Pulses

---

# 10. Crop Protection Management

Crop protection prevents damage caused by:

- Weeds
- Insects
- Diseases

---

## Weeds

Weeds are unwanted plants growing along with crops.

### Harmful Effects

- Compete for nutrients.
- Reduce crop yield.
- Harbour pests.

### Weed Control

- Manual removal.
- Tilling.
- Weedicides.

Example:

- 2,4-D

---

## Pest Control

Pests include insects, rodents, fungi, bacteria, and viruses.

### Methods

- Biological control.
- Chemical pesticides.
- Crop rotation.
- Resistant crop varieties.

---

# 11. Storage of Grains

Proper storage prevents damage caused by:

- Moisture.
- Insects.
- Rodents.
- Fungi.

### Storage Structures

- Silos.
- Granaries.
- Warehouses.

---

# 12. Animal Husbandry

**Animal husbandry** is the scientific management of domestic animals for useful products such as milk, meat, eggs, wool, and labour.

---

# 13. Cattle Farming

Cattle are mainly reared for:

- Milk production.
- Agricultural work.

### Breeds

#### Indigenous Breeds

Examples:

- Sahiwal
- Red Sindhi
- Gir

#### Exotic Breeds

Examples:

- Jersey
- Holstein Friesian

Cross-breeding combines desirable traits of both.

---

## Dairy Management

Good milk production requires:

- Balanced nutrition.
- Clean housing.
- Disease prevention.
- Regular veterinary care.

---

# 14. Poultry Farming

Poultry farming involves rearing birds such as chickens for:

- Eggs.
- Meat.

### Types

#### Layer

Raised for egg production.

#### Broiler

Raised for meat production.

### Good Practices

- Balanced feed.
- Vaccination.
- Proper ventilation.
- Disease control.

---

# 15. Fish Production

Fish production is known as **Pisciculture**.

### Types

#### Capture Fishing

Fish are caught from natural water bodies.

#### Culture Fishery

Fish are scientifically reared in ponds, lakes, or reservoirs.

Examples:

- Rohu
- Catla
- Mrigal

---

# 16. Bee-keeping (Apiculture)

**Apiculture** is the scientific rearing of honeybees.

### Products

- Honey.
- Beeswax.

### Advantages

- Increases pollination.
- Improves crop yield.
- Provides additional income.

### Common Honeybee Species

- _Apis cerana indica_
- _Apis mellifera_

**Solution**

**Solution**

### Example 4

Differentiate between mixed cropping and intercropping.

**Solution**

- **Mixed cropping:** Two or more crops are grown together without a definite row arrangement.
- **Intercropping:** Two or more crops are grown in a definite row pattern.

---

**Solution**

- Indigenous: Sahiwal
- Exotic: Jersey

What is apiculture?

**Solution**

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **manure** with **fertilizers**.
- Mixing up **mixed cropping** and **intercropping**.
- Assuming all fertilizers improve soil texture.
- Confusing **capture fishing** with **culture fishery**.
- Forgetting that **drip irrigation** supplies water directly to the roots.
- Mixing up **layers** and **broilers** in poultry farming.
- Assuming weeds are beneficial to crop growth.

# Chapter Summary

- **Improvement in food resources** aims to increase food production and quality using scientific farming methods.
- **Crop variety improvement** develops high-yielding, disease-resistant, and climate-tolerant crop varieties.
- Proper **nutrient management** involves the balanced use of **manure** and **fertilizers**.
- Efficient **irrigation systems**, such as **sprinkler** and **drip irrigation**, help conserve water and improve crop growth.
- **Cropping patterns** like mixed cropping, intercropping, and crop rotation increase productivity and maintain soil fertility.
- **Crop protection management** reduces losses caused by weeds, pests, and diseases.
- Scientific **storage methods** protect harvested grains from moisture, insects, fungi, and rodents.
- **Animal husbandry** includes cattle farming, poultry farming, fish production, and bee-keeping to improve the production of milk, meat, eggs, fish, honey, and other useful products.
- Sustainable agricultural practices ensure food security while protecting natural resources and the environment.
