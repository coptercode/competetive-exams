# Introduction

Matter exists in three common states: **solid, liquid, and gas**. Liquids and gases are collectively known as **fluids** because they can flow.

The study of fluid mechanics is essential in understanding hydraulic machines, aeroplanes, ships, blood circulation, weather systems, and many natural phenomena.

---

# 1. Fluids

## Definition

A **fluid** is a substance that can flow and has no fixed shape.

Examples

- Water
- Oil
- Air
- Mercury

---

## Characteristics of Fluids

- They flow easily.
- They cannot resist shear stress.
- They take the shape of their container.
- Liquids have a fixed volume but no fixed shape.
- Gases have neither fixed shape nor fixed volume.

# 5. Pressure in a Fluid

Pressure at a depth **h** in a liquid is

```
P = hρg
```

where

- **ρ** = Density
- **g** = Acceleration due to gravity
- **h** = Depth

---

## Important Observations

- Pressure increases with depth.
- Pressure is the same at the same horizontal level.
- Pressure acts equally in all directions.

---

# 6. Pascal's Law

## Statement

Pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted equally and undiminished in all directions.

---

# 7. Archimedes' Principle

## Statement

A body wholly or partially immersed in a fluid experiences an upward force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced.

---

## Buoyant Force

Formula

```
FB = ρVg
```

where

- **ρ** = Density of fluid
- **V** = Volume displaced
- **g** = Acceleration due to gravity

---

## Applications

- Ships float
- Hydrometer
- Submarines
- Balloons

---

# 8. Conditions for Floatation

A body floats when

```
Weight

=

Buoyant Force
```

or

```
mg = ρVg
```

---

## Floating Body

Weight equals upthrust.

---

## Sinking Body

Weight exceeds upthrust.

---

## Rising Body

Upthrust exceeds weight.

---

# 9. Equation of Continuity

For an incompressible fluid,

```
A₁v₁

=

A₂v₂
```

where

- **A** = Cross-sectional area
- **v** = Velocity

---

## Interpretation

Area decreases

↓

Velocity increases

# 10. Streamline Flow

## Definition

Flow in which every particle follows a definite smooth path.

Characteristics

- No mixing of layers
- Smooth motion
- Low velocity

---

# 11. Turbulent Flow

## Definition

Flow in which fluid particles move irregularly.

Characteristics

- Mixing occurs
- High velocity
- Formation of eddies

---

Formula

- **r** = Radius of tube

Applicable to a small sphere moving slowly through a viscous fluid.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Calculate the pressure exerted by a force of **100 N** acting on an area of **0.02 m²**.

**Solution**

Using

```
P = F/A
```

```
=

100

─────

0.02

=

5000 Pa
```

---

### Example 2

A liquid has a density of **1000 kg m⁻³**. Find the pressure at a depth of **5 m**.

Take

```
g = 9.8 m s⁻²
```

**Solution**

```
P = hρg
```

```
= 5 × 1000 × 9.8

= 49000 Pa
```

---

### Example 3

A body displaces **0.02 m³** of water. Calculate the buoyant force.

Take

```
ρ = 1000 kg m⁻³

g = 9.8 m s⁻²
```

**Solution**

```
FB = ρVg
```

```
= 1000 × 0.02 × 9.8

= 196 N
```

---

### Example 4

Water flows through a pipe whose cross-sectional area decreases from **4 m²** to **2 m²**. If the initial speed is **3 m s⁻¹**, find the final speed.

**Solution**

Using the continuity equation,

```
A₁v₁ = A₂v₂
```

```
4 × 3

=

2v₂
```

```
v₂ = 6 m s⁻¹
```

---

### Example 5

A force of **0.3 N** acts along a liquid surface of length **0.05 m**. Calculate the surface tension.

**Solution**

```
T = F/l
```

```
=

0.3

────

0.05

=

6 N m⁻¹
```

---

### Example 6

A small sphere moves through a viscous liquid with a radius of **0.01 m**, viscosity **0.5 Pa s**, and speed **2 m s⁻¹**. Calculate the viscous drag.

Use

```
π = 3.14
```

**Solution**

Using Stokes' law,

```
F = 6πηrv
```

```
= 6 × 3.14 × 0.5 × 0.01 × 2

≈ 0.188 N
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **density** with **relative density**; relative density has **no unit**.
- Assuming pressure depends on the **shape of the container**; it depends only on **depth, density, and gravity**.
- Forgetting that **Pascal's law** applies only to **confined fluids**.
- Mixing up **buoyant force** with the **weight of the body**.
- Using **Bernoulli's theorem** for viscous or turbulent flow, where its ideal assumptions are not satisfied.
- Confusing **streamline flow** with **turbulent flow**.
- Assuming **viscosity** and **surface tension** are the same property; viscosity acts throughout the fluid, while surface tension acts only at the surface.
- Ignoring the angle of contact while solving **capillarity** problems.

# Chapter Summary

- **Fluids** are substances that can flow and include both liquids and gases.
- **Pressure** is the normal force per unit area and increases with the depth of a liquid.
- **Pascal's law** states that pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted equally in all directions, forming the basis of hydraulic machines.
- **Archimedes' principle** explains buoyancy and the floating of bodies by relating the buoyant force to the weight of the displaced fluid.
- The **continuity equation** expresses the conservation of mass during fluid flow.
- **Bernoulli's theorem** relates pressure, velocity, and height in the streamline flow of an ideal fluid and has numerous engineering applications.
- **Viscosity** is the resistance offered by a fluid to the relative motion of its layers, while **Stokes' law** describes the viscous drag on a sphere moving through a fluid.
- **Terminal velocity** is the constant maximum speed attained by an object falling through a viscous medium.
- **Surface tension** arises due to cohesive forces between liquid molecules and is responsible for phenomena such as droplets, capillarity, and wetting.
- The mechanical properties of fluids have wide applications in hydraulic systems, aviation, medicine, meteorology, transportation, and everyday life.
