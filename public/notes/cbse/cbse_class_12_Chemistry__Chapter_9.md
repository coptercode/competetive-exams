# Introduction

Amines are organic compounds derived from ammonia (**NH₃**) by replacing one or more hydrogen atoms with alkyl or aryl groups.

General formula:

\[
NH_3 \rightarrow RNH_2,\ R_2NH,\ R_3N
\]

Functional group:

\[
-NH_2
\]

---

# 1. Classification of Amines

Amines are classified based on the number of carbon groups attached to nitrogen.

---

# A. Primary Amine (1°)

One alkyl/aryl group attached to nitrogen.

General formula:

\[
R-NH_2
\]

Examples:

\[
CH_3NH_2
\]

(Methylamine)

\[
C_6H_5NH_2
\]

(Aniline)

---

# B. Secondary Amine (2°)

Two carbon groups attached to nitrogen.

Formula:

\[
R_2NH
\]

Example:

\[
(CH_3)_2NH
\]

---

# C. Tertiary Amine (3°)

Three carbon groups attached to nitrogen.

Formula:

\[
R_3N
\]

Example:

\[
(CH_3)_3N
\]

---

# 2. Classification Based on Carbon Group

## A. Aliphatic Amines

Nitrogen attached to alkyl group.

Example:

\[
CH_3NH_2
\]

---

## B. Aromatic Amines

Nitrogen attached directly to benzene ring.

Example:

\[
C_6H_5NH_2
\]

(Aniline)

---

# 3. Nomenclature of Amines

## Common Naming

Name alkyl groups followed by:

```
amine
```

Example:

\[
CH_3NH_2
\]

Methylamine

---

## IUPAC Naming

Replace "e" of alkane with:

```
amine
```

Example:

\[
CH_3CH_2NH_2
\]

Ethanamine

---

# 4. Structure of Amines

Nitrogen is:

- sp³ hybridised
- Contains a lone pair of electrons

Shape:

```
      H

      |

H — N — R

     :
 Lone pair
```

---

# 5. Preparation of Amines

---

# A. Reduction of Nitro Compounds

Nitro compounds are reduced to amines.

Reaction:

\[
RNO_2+6[H]
\rightarrow
RNH_2+2H_2O
\]

Reagents:

- Sn/HCl
- Fe/HCl
- H₂/Ni

---

# B. Ammonolysis of Alkyl Halides

Alkyl halides react with ammonia.

\[
RX+NH_3
\rightarrow
RNH_2
\]

---

# C. Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis

Used for preparation of primary amines.

Reaction:

\[
\text{Potassium phthalimide}+RX
\rightarrow
RNH_2
\]

---

## Advantage

Produces pure primary amines.

---

## Limitation

Cannot prepare aromatic amines.

---

# D. Hoffmann Bromamide Reaction

## Definition

Conversion of amide into primary amine with one less carbon atom.

Reaction:

\[
RCONH_2+Br_2+4NaOH
\rightarrow
RNH_2+Na_2CO_3+2NaBr+2H_2O
\]

---

# 6. Physical Properties

## Boiling Point

Amines have higher boiling points than hydrocarbons due to hydrogen bonding.

---

Order:

\[
Primary>Secondary>Tertiary
\]

---

## Solubility

Lower amines are soluble in water due to hydrogen bonding.

Solubility decreases with increasing carbon chain.

---

# 7. Basic Character of Amines

Amines are bases because nitrogen has a lone pair that accepts protons.

Reaction:

\[
RNH_2+H^+
\rightarrow
RNH_3^+
\]

---

# Factors Affecting Basicity

---

## A. Electron Donating Groups

Increase basic strength.

Example:

\[
CH_3NH_2 > NH_3
\]

---

## B. Electron Withdrawing Groups

Decrease basic strength.

---

## C. Resonance

Aniline is less basic than alkyl amines because lone pair is delocalised.

---

# Basic Strength Order in Aqueous Solution

For aliphatic amines:

\[
2^\circ > 1^\circ > 3^\circ > NH_3
\]

---

For aromatic amines:

\[
Aniline < NH_3
\]

---

# 8. Chemical Reactions of Amines

---

# A. Reaction with Acids

Amines form salts.

Example:

\[
CH_3NH_2+HCl
\rightarrow
CH_3NH_3^+Cl^-
\]

---

# B. Alkylation

Amines react with alkyl halides.

\[
RNH_2+RX
\rightarrow
R_2NH
\]

Further alkylation gives tertiary amines and quaternary salts.

---

# C. Acylation

Primary and secondary amines react with acid chlorides.

Example:

\[
RNH_2+RCOCl
\rightarrow
RCONHR
\]

---

# 9. Identification Tests for Amines

---

# A. Carbylamine Test

## Given by:

Only primary amines.

Reaction:

\[
RNH_2+CHCl_3+KOH
\rightarrow
RNC
\]

Product:

```
Isocyanide (foul smell)
```

---

# B. Hinsberg Test

Used to distinguish primary, secondary, and tertiary amines.

Reagent:

\[
C_6H_5SO_2Cl
\]

(benzenesulphonyl chloride)

---

## Results

### Primary Amine

Forms soluble sulphonamide.

---

### Secondary Amine

Forms insoluble sulphonamide.

---

### Tertiary Amine

No sulphonamide formation.

---

# C. Azo Dye Test

Aromatic primary amines form coloured azo dyes.

---

# 10. Diazonium Salts

## Definition

Compounds containing:

\[
-ArN_2^+
\]

group.

Example:

\[
C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^-
\]

(Benzenediazonium chloride)

---

# 11. Preparation of Diazonium Salts

## Diazotisation Reaction

Primary aromatic amine reacts with nitrous acid at 273–278 K.

Reaction:

\[
C_6H_5NH_2+NaNO_2+2HCl
\]

\[
\rightarrow C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^-+NaCl+2H_2O
\]

---

# Conditions

Temperature:

\[
273-278K
\]

---

# 12. Reactions of Diazonium Salts

---

# A. Sandmeyer Reaction

Replacement of diazonium group by halogen.

Example:

\[
ArN_2^+Cl^-+CuCl
\rightarrow
ArCl+N_2
\]

---

# B. Gattermann Reaction

Uses copper powder and HCl/HBr.

\[
ArN_2^+Cl^-
\rightarrow
ArCl+N_2
\]

---

# C. Hydrolysis

Produces phenol.

\[
ArN_2^+Cl^-+H_2O
\rightarrow
ArOH+N_2
\]

---

# D. Azo Coupling Reaction

Formation of azo dyes.

General:

\[
ArN_2^++ArOH
\rightarrow
Azo\ compound
\]

---

# 13. Aniline

Formula:

\[
C_6H_5NH_2
\]

---

Properties:

- Aromatic primary amine.
- Less basic than ammonia.
- Used in dyes and medicines.

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** Why is aniline less basic than methylamine?

### Solution

In aniline, the nitrogen lone pair is delocalised into the benzene ring through resonance. This reduces availability of the lone pair for proton acceptance, making it less basic than methylamine.

---

## Example 2

**Question:** What is Gabriel phthalimide synthesis?

### Solution

It is a method for preparing primary amines by reacting potassium phthalimide with alkyl halides followed by hydrolysis.

---

## Example 3

**Question:** Which amines give carbylamine test?

### Solution

Only primary amines (both aliphatic and aromatic) give the carbylamine test.

---

## Example 4

**Question:** What is diazotisation?

### Solution

Conversion of primary aromatic amines into diazonium salts using sodium nitrite and HCl at 273–278 K is called diazotisation.

---

## Example 5

**Question:** Arrange amines in basic strength order in aqueous solution.

### Solution:

\[
2^\circ>1^\circ>3^\circ>NH_3
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing primary, secondary, and tertiary amines with primary, secondary, and tertiary carbons.
- Forgetting that aromatic amines are less basic due to resonance.
- Applying carbylamine test to secondary or tertiary amines.
- Forgetting the low temperature requirement in diazotisation.
- Mixing Sandmeyer and Gattermann reactions.
- Forgetting Gabriel synthesis gives only primary amines.
- Confusing diazonium salts with ordinary ammonium salts.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Amines are derivatives of ammonia where hydrogen atoms are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups.
- They are classified as primary, secondary, and tertiary amines.
- Amines are basic due to the lone pair on nitrogen.
- Basicity depends on electronic effects and resonance.
- Important preparation methods include nitro reduction, Gabriel synthesis, and Hoffmann bromamide reaction.
- Primary aromatic amines form diazonium salts through diazotisation.
- Diazonium salts are useful in Sandmeyer, Gattermann, hydrolysis, and azo coupling reactions.
- Identification tests such as carbylamine and Hinsberg tests distinguish different types of amines.
