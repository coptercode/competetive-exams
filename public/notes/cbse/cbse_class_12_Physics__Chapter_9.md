# Introduction

Ray optics (geometrical optics) studies the behaviour of light using the concept of rays.

A light ray represents the direction of propagation of light.

---

# 1. Reflection of Light

## Definition

The bouncing back of light into the same medium after striking a surface is called reflection.

---

# Laws of Reflection

## First Law

The incident ray, reflected ray, and normal at the point of incidence lie in the same plane.

---

## Second Law

The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.

\[
\boxed{i=r}
\]

---

# 2. Spherical Mirrors

A spherical mirror is a reflecting surface that forms part of a sphere.

Types:

1. Concave mirror
2. Convex mirror

---

# Important Terms

## Pole (P)

Centre point of mirror surface.

---

## Centre of Curvature (C)

Centre of the sphere of which mirror is a part.

---

## Radius of Curvature (R)

Distance between pole and centre of curvature.

\[
R=PC
\]

---

## Principal Axis

Line passing through P and C.

---

## Focus (F)

Point where parallel rays meet after reflection.

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
f=\frac R2
}
\]

---

# 3. Mirror Formula

For spherical mirrors:

\[
\boxed{
\frac1f=
\frac1v+\frac1u
}
\]

where:

- f = focal length
- v = image distance
- u = object distance

---

# 4. Magnification of Mirror

\[
\boxed{
m=\frac{h_i}{h_o}
}
\]

Also:

\[
\boxed{
m=-\frac vu
}
\]

---

# Sign Convention (Cartesian)

- All distances measured from pole.
- Direction of incident light is negative.
- Heights above axis are positive.
- Heights below axis are negative.

---

# 5. Refraction of Light

## Definition

The bending of light when it passes from one medium to another is called refraction.

---

# Snell's Law

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\sin i}{\sin r}=n
}
\]

where:

n = refractive index

---

# 6. Refractive Index

## Definition

Ratio of speed of light in vacuum to speed in medium.

\[
\boxed{
n=\frac cv
}
\]

---

# Relative Refractive Index

\[
\boxed{
n_{21}=\frac{n_2}{n_1}
}
\]

---

# 7. Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

## Definition

Complete reflection of light back into the denser medium when it travels from denser to rarer medium.

---

# Conditions for TIR

1. Light must travel from denser to rarer medium.

2. Angle of incidence must be greater than critical angle.

\[
i>C
\]

---

# Critical Angle

\[
\boxed{
\sin C=\frac1n
}
\]

(for denser medium to air)

---

# Applications of TIR

## 1. Optical Fibre

Used in:

- Communication
- Medical endoscopy

---

## 2. Diamond Brilliance

Due to repeated total internal reflection.

---

## 3. Mirage

Formation in deserts and hot regions.

---

# 8. Refraction Through a Prism

A prism is a transparent refracting medium bounded by two inclined surfaces.

---

# Angle of Deviation

The angle between incident ray direction and emergent ray direction.

Symbol:

\[
\delta
\]

---

# Minimum Deviation

At minimum deviation:

\[
i=e
\]

and:

\[
r_1=r_2
\]

---

# Prism Formula

\[
\boxed{
n=
\frac{\sin\frac{A+\delta_m}{2}}
{\sin\frac A2}
}
\]

where:

- A = angle of prism
- δm = minimum deviation

---

# 9. Dispersion of Light

## Definition

Splitting of white light into its constituent colours is called dispersion.

---

Cause:

Different colours have different refractive indices.

---

Order:

\[
VIBGYOR
\]

---

# 13. Power of Lens

## Definition

Ability of lens to converge or diverge light.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
P=\frac1f
}
\]

where f is in metres.

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{\text{Dioptre (D)}}
\]

---

# Combination of Lenses

For lenses in contact:

\[
\boxed{
P=P_1+P_2
}
\]

---

# 14. Lens Maker's Formula

For a lens:

\[
\boxed{
\frac1f=
(n-1)
\left(
\frac1{R_1}
-\frac1{R_2}
\right)
}
\]

---

where:

- n = refractive index
- R₁, R₂ = radii of curvature

---

# 16. Optical Instruments

# A. Simple Microscope

A magnifying glass using a convex lens.

---

Magnifying power:

\[
\boxed{
M=1+\frac Df
}
\]

(for final image at near point)

---

For normal adjustment:

\[
\boxed{
M=\frac Df
}
\]

---

where:

D = least distance of distinct vision

\[
D=25cm
\]

---

# B. Compound Microscope

Uses:

- Objective lens
- Eyepiece lens

---

Magnifying power:

\[
\boxed{
M=m_o m_e
}
\]

---

For normal adjustment:

\[
\boxed{
M=\frac{L}{f_o}\frac D{f_e}
}
\]

where:

- L = tube length
- fo = objective focal length
- fe = eyepiece focal length

---

# C. Astronomical Telescope

Used to observe distant objects.

---

Magnifying power:

\[
\boxed{
M=\frac{f_o}{f_e}
}
\]

---

For normal adjustment.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** A convex lens has focal length 20 cm. Find its power.

Convert:

\[
f=0.2m
\]

Formula:

\[
P=\frac1f
\]

\[
P=\frac1{0.2}
\]

\[
\boxed{P=5D}
\]

---

## Example 2

**Question:** Why does a diamond sparkle?

### Solution

Diamond has a very high refractive index and small critical angle. Multiple total internal reflections produce brilliance.

---

## Example 3

**Question:** State the conditions for total internal reflection.

### Solution

1. Light must travel from denser to rarer medium.
2. Angle of incidence must be greater than critical angle.

---

## Example 4

**Question:** Find magnification of a mirror where u = -20 cm and v = 40 cm.

\[
m=-\frac vu
\]

\[
=-\frac{40}{-20}
\]

\[
\boxed{m=2}
\]

---

## Example 5

**Question:** Why are optical fibres used in communication?

### Solution

They transmit signals with low loss using total internal reflection.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing reflection and refraction.
- Forgetting sign conventions in mirror and lens formulas.
- Using centimetres instead of metres in lens power.
- Confusing critical angle with angle of incidence.
- Forgetting that convex mirrors always form virtual images.
- Mixing magnification formulas of mirrors and lenses.
- Forgetting prism minimum deviation conditions.
- Using incorrect direction of light in ray diagrams.
