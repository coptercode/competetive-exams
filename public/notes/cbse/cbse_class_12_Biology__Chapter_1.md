# Introduction

Sexual reproduction in flowering plants (angiosperms) involves the formation of male and female gametes, their transfer through pollination, fertilization, and the development of seeds and fruits. A unique feature of angiosperms is **double fertilization**, in which one male gamete fuses with the egg to form the zygote, while the other fuses with the polar nuclei to form the endosperm.

Sexual reproduction ensures **genetic variation**, adaptation, and continuity of plant species.

---

# 1. Flower: The Reproductive Organ

## Definition

A **flower** is the reproductive structure of angiosperms that bears the male and female reproductive organs.

---

## Parts of a Typical Flower

- **Calyx (Sepals):** Protects the flower bud.
- **Corolla (Petals):** Attracts pollinators.
- **Androecium:** Male reproductive whorl.
- **Gynoecium (Pistil/Carpel):** Female reproductive whorl.

# 2. Structure of the Male Reproductive Organ (Stamen)

Each stamen consists of:

- **Filament** – Slender stalk.
- **Anther** – Produces pollen grains.

---

## Structure of Anther

A mature anther is:

- Bilobed
- Dithecous (two lobes)
- Four pollen sacs (microsporangia)

---

## Layers of Microsporangium

From outside to inside:

1. Epidermis
2. Endothecium
3. Middle layers
4. Tapetum

---

### Functions

| Layer         | Function                           |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Epidermis     | Protection                         |
| Endothecium   | Helps in anther dehiscence         |
| Middle layers | Temporary nourishment              |
| Tapetum       | Nourishes developing pollen grains |

---

# 3. Microsporogenesis

## Definition

The process of formation of **microspores (pollen grains)** from pollen mother cells (PMCs).

---

## Steps

1. Archesporial cell differentiates.
2. Forms sporogenous tissue.
3. Sporogenous cells become pollen mother cells.
4. Each PMC undergoes meiosis.
5. Four haploid microspores are formed (microspore tetrad).

---

## Importance

- Produces haploid pollen grains.
- Introduces genetic variation through meiosis.

---

# 4. Development of Male Gametophyte

A pollen grain develops into the male gametophyte.

---

## Structure of Mature Pollen Grain

- **Exine** – Outer wall made of sporopollenin.
- **Intine** – Inner cellulose-pectin layer.

Contains:

- Vegetative (tube) cell
- Generative cell

The generative cell divides mitotically to form **two male gametes**.

---

```
  \_____________/

○ Vegetative nucleus

```

# 5. Pollen Viability

- Varies among species.
- Can range from a few minutes to several months.
- Preserved in **liquid nitrogen (-196°C)** in pollen banks.

# 8. Megasporogenesis

## Definition

Formation of **megaspores** from the megaspore mother cell (MMC).

---

## Process

1. MMC undergoes meiosis.
2. Four haploid megaspores are formed.
3. Usually, only one megaspore remains functional.
4. Remaining three degenerate.

---

# 9. Development of Female Gametophyte (Embryo Sac)

The functional megaspore undergoes **three mitotic divisions**.

---

## Result

- Eight nuclei
- Seven cells

---

## Arrangement

### Micropylar End

- Egg cell
- Two synergids

---

### Centre

- Two polar nuclei

---

### Chalazal End

- Three antipodal cells

---

# 10. Pollination

## Definition

Transfer of pollen grains from anther to stigma.

---

## Types

### A. Autogamy

Pollen reaches stigma of the **same flower**.

Requirements:

- Bisexual flower
- Anther and stigma mature simultaneously.

---

### B. Geitonogamy

Transfer between different flowers of the **same plant**.

Genetically similar but requires a pollinating agent.

---

### C. Xenogamy

Transfer between flowers of **different plants** of the same species.

Produces maximum genetic variation.

---

# Comparison of Pollination Types

| Feature                          | Autogamy | Geitonogamy | Xenogamy |
| -------------------------------- | -------- | ----------- | -------- |
| Same flower                      | ✓        | ✗           | ✗        |
| Same plant                       | ✓        | ✓           | ✗        |
| Cross-pollination agent required | No       | Yes         | Yes      |
| Genetic variation                | Very low | Low         | High     |

---

# 11. Agents of Pollination

## Abiotic Agents

- Wind (Anemophily)
- Water (Hydrophily)

---

## Biotic Agents

- Insects (Entomophily)
- Birds (Ornithophily)
- Bats (Chiropterophily)
- Animals (Zoophily)

---

## Adaptations

### Wind-pollinated Flowers

- Small flowers
- Dry pollen
- Feathery stigma
- Long stamens

---

### Insect-pollinated Flowers

- Bright petals
- Nectar
- Fragrance
- Sticky pollen

---

# 12. Outbreeding Devices

Mechanisms preventing self-pollination:

- Dichogamy
- Herkogamy
- Self-incompatibility
- Unisexuality

---

## Importance

- Promotes cross-pollination.
- Maintains genetic diversity.

---

# 13. Pollen-Pistil Interaction

Steps:

1. Pollen lands on stigma.
2. Recognition of compatible pollen.
3. Hydration of pollen.
4. Germination.
5. Pollen tube formation.
6. Growth through style.
7. Entry into ovule through micropyle.
8. Discharge of male gametes into embryo sac.

---

# 14. Double Fertilization

A unique feature of angiosperms.

---

## Syngamy

```
Male Gamete + Egg

↓

Zygote (2n)
```

---

## Triple Fusion

```
Male Gamete

+

Two Polar Nuclei

↓

Primary Endosperm Nucleus (3n)
```

---

Together they constitute **double fertilization**.

# 15. Endosperm Development

The primary endosperm nucleus develops into the **endosperm**, which nourishes the embryo.

---

## Types

- Cellular

(Nuclear type is the most common.)

# 18. Fruit Development

The ovary develops into the fruit.

### Types

- True fruit
- False fruit
- Parthenocarpic fruit (formed without fertilization)

---

# 19. Apomixis

## Definition

Seed formation **without fertilization**.

---

## Importance

- Produces genetically identical offspring.
- Preserves hybrid vigor.
- Useful in agriculture.

---

# 20. Polyembryony

## Definition

Formation of **more than one embryo** in a single seed.

---

## Example

- Citrus
- Mango

---

## Importance

- Produces multiple seedlings.
- Useful in horticulture.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

**Question:** Why is double fertilization considered unique to angiosperms?

**Solution:**

Double fertilization involves:

- One male gamete fusing with the egg to form the diploid zygote (syngamy).
- The second male gamete fusing with the two polar nuclei to form the triploid primary endosperm nucleus (triple fusion).

This phenomenon occurs only in flowering plants (angiosperms).

---

### Example 2

**Question:** Differentiate between autogamy and xenogamy.

**Solution:**

| Autogamy                           | Xenogamy                                        |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Pollination within the same flower | Pollination between flowers of different plants |
| No pollinating agent required      | Pollinating agent required                      |
| Very little genetic variation      | High genetic variation                          |

---

### Example 3

**Question:** What is the function of the tapetum?

**Solution:**

The tapetum is the innermost layer of the anther wall. It nourishes developing pollen grains and supplies materials required for pollen wall formation.

---

### Example 4

**Question:** Why are synergids important?

**Solution:**

Synergids possess a **filiform apparatus**, which guides the pollen tube into the embryo sac and facilitates the discharge of male gametes.

---

### Example 5

**Question:** State one advantage of apomixis in agriculture.

**Solution:**

Apomixis allows the production of seeds without fertilization, preserving desirable hybrid characteristics across generations without repeated hybridization.

---

### Example 6

**Question:** What develops from the following after fertilization?

- Ovule
- Ovary
- Zygote
- Integuments

**Solution:**

| Structure   | Develops Into |
| ----------- | ------------- |
| Ovule       | Seed          |
| Ovary       | Fruit         |
| Zygote      | Embryo        |
| Integuments | Seed coat     |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **microsporogenesis** (formation of microspores in the anther) with **megasporogenesis** (formation of megaspores in the ovule).
- Assuming **autogamy** and **geitonogamy** are identical; geitonogamy occurs between different flowers of the same plant.
- Forgetting that the mature embryo sac is **7-celled and 8-nucleate**.
- Mixing up **syngamy** and **triple fusion** during double fertilization.
- Incorrectly stating that the endosperm is diploid; in most angiosperms, it is **triploid (3n)**.
- Confusing **apomixis** with **parthenocarpy**; apomixis produces seeds without fertilization, while parthenocarpy produces fruits without fertilization.
- Forgetting the functions of the **tapetum** and **synergids**, which are commonly asked in examinations.
- Assuming all pollination by insects results in xenogamy; insect pollination can also cause geitonogamy.

# Chapter Summary

- The **flower** is the reproductive organ of angiosperms and consists of the androecium (male) and gynoecium (female).
- **Microsporogenesis** produces pollen grains (male gametophytes), while **megasporogenesis** produces the functional megaspore that develops into the embryo sac (female gametophyte).
- The mature embryo sac is **7-celled and 8-nucleate**, containing the egg apparatus, polar nuclei, and antipodal cells.
- Pollination may be **autogamy**, **geitonogamy**, or **xenogamy**, and is carried out by abiotic or biotic agents.
- Plants possess several **outbreeding devices** to prevent self-pollination and promote genetic diversity.
- During **pollen-pistil interaction**, the pollen tube grows through the style and delivers two male gametes into the embryo sac.
- **Double fertilization**, comprising **syngamy** and **triple fusion**, is a unique characteristic of flowering plants.
- After fertilization, the **zygote develops into the embryo**, the **primary endosperm nucleus forms the endosperm**, the **ovule becomes the seed**, and the **ovary develops into the fruit**.
- **Apomixis** and **polyembryony** are important reproductive phenomena with significant agricultural and horticultural applications.
