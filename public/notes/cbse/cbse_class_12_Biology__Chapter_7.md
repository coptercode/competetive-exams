# Introduction

**Health** is a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease. Human health is affected by genetic factors, lifestyle, environment, nutrition, pathogens, and immune responses.

Diseases may be:

- **Infectious diseases** – caused by pathogens and transmitted between individuals.
- **Non-infectious diseases** – caused by genetic, lifestyle, or environmental factors.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

**Question:** Why does malaria cause recurring fever?

**Solution:**

Malaria causes recurring fever because Plasmodium multiplies inside red blood cells. When infected RBCs rupture, parasites and toxins are released, producing periodic fever and chills.

---

### Example 2

**Question:** Differentiate between innate and acquired immunity.

**Solution:**

Innate immunity is present from birth and provides non-specific defense without memory. Acquired immunity develops after exposure to antigens and provides specific responses with immunological memory.

---

### Example 3

**Question:** Why are antibiotics ineffective against viruses?

**Solution:**

Antibiotics target bacterial structures and processes such as cell walls and bacterial metabolism. Viruses lack these structures and reproduce using host cells, so antibiotics do not affect them.

---

### Example 4

**Question:** Name the immune cells targeted by HIV.

**Solution:**

HIV primarily infects and destroys **CD4+ T-helper lymphocytes**, weakening the immune system and leading to AIDS.

---

### Example 5

**Question:** What is the difference between benign and malignant tumours?

**Solution:**

Benign tumours remain localized and usually do not invade other tissues, whereas malignant tumours spread to other parts of the body through metastasis.

---

### Example 6

**Question:** Why is vaccination effective?

**Solution:**

Vaccination introduces harmless forms of antigens that stimulate the immune system to produce antibodies and memory cells. On future exposure, the immune response becomes faster and stronger.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **infection** with **disease**; infection refers to entry and multiplication of pathogens, while disease involves symptoms and damage.
- Assuming all microorganisms are harmful; many microbes are beneficial.
- Mixing up **innate immunity** and **acquired immunity**.
- Forgetting that **B-cells produce antibodies**, while **T-cells are responsible for cell-mediated immunity**.
- Assuming antibiotics can treat viral infections; they work mainly against bacteria.
- Confusing HIV infection with AIDS; HIV is the virus, while AIDS is the advanced disease condition.
- Forgetting that cancer results from uncontrolled cell division and may involve mutations.
- Assuming vaccines provide immediate protection; immune memory develops after activation of the immune system.

# Chapter Summary

- Human health depends on physical, mental, and social well-being and is influenced by nutrition, lifestyle, environment, and immunity.
- Infectious diseases are caused by pathogens such as bacteria, viruses, protozoans, fungi, and worms.
- Diseases like malaria, typhoid, pneumonia, and amoebiasis spread through different modes of transmission and can be controlled through hygiene and preventive measures.
- **Innate immunity** provides non-specific defense, while **acquired immunity** provides specific responses with memory.
- Vaccination stimulates immune memory and provides protection against infectious diseases.
- Allergies occur due to exaggerated immune responses, mainly involving **IgE antibodies**.
- Cancer results from uncontrolled cell division and can be detected and treated using surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy.
- HIV attacks **CD4+ T-cells**, weakening immunity and causing AIDS.
- Drug and alcohol abuse negatively affect physical and mental health and can be prevented through awareness, counseling, and healthy lifestyle choices.
