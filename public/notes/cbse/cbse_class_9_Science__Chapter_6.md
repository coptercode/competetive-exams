# Introduction

In multicellular organisms, similar cells group together to perform a specific function. Such a group of similar cells, along with their intercellular material, is called a **tissue**.

The study of tissues is known as **Histology**.

Plants and animals have different types of tissues because their body structures and functions differ. Plant tissues mainly provide growth, support, and transport, whereas animal tissues are specialized for movement, protection, communication, and coordination.

---

# 1. Tissue

A **tissue** is a group of similar cells having a common origin that work together to perform a specific function.

### Advantages of Tissues

- Division of labour.
- Increased efficiency.
- Better coordination.
- Growth and repair.
- Specialized functions.

---

# 2. Plant Tissues

Plant tissues are broadly classified into:

- **Meristematic Tissue**
- **Permanent Tissue**

---

# 3. Meristematic Tissue

**Meristematic tissue** consists of actively dividing cells responsible for plant growth.

### Characteristics

- Cells divide continuously.
- Thin cell walls.
- Dense cytoplasm.
- Prominent nucleus.
- Vacuoles absent or very small.
- Cells are compactly arranged.

### Types of Meristematic Tissue

---

## Apical Meristem

Located at the tips of roots and shoots.

### Function

- Increases the length of the plant.
- Responsible for primary growth.

---

## Intercalary Meristem

Located at the base of leaves or internodes.

### Function

- Helps in the growth of grasses.
- Regenerates damaged parts.

---

## Lateral Meristem

Located along the sides of stems and roots.

### Function

- Increases the girth (thickness) of the plant.
- Responsible for secondary growth.

---

# 4. Permanent Tissue

Permanent tissues are formed from meristematic tissues after cell division stops.

### Characteristics

- Cells lose the ability to divide.
- Cells become specialized.
- Perform specific functions.

Permanent tissues are classified into:

- Simple Permanent Tissue
- Complex Permanent Tissue

---

# 5. Simple Permanent Tissue

Simple permanent tissues are made up of only one type of cells.

They include:

- Parenchyma
- Collenchyma
- Sclerenchyma

---

## Parenchyma

Parenchyma consists of living, thin-walled cells.

### Functions

- Storage of food and water.
- Photosynthesis (when chloroplasts are present).
- Healing and repair.
- Provides support through turgidity.

### Special Types

#### Chlorenchyma

Contains chlorophyll and performs photosynthesis.

#### Aerenchyma

Contains large air spaces and provides buoyancy in aquatic plants.

---

## Collenchyma

Collenchyma consists of living cells with unevenly thickened corners.

### Functions

- Provides flexibility.
- Gives mechanical support.
- Allows bending without breaking.

Found in:

- Leaf stalks
- Young stems

---

## Sclerenchyma

Sclerenchyma consists of dead cells with thick, lignified walls.

### Functions

- Provides strength.
- Gives rigidity.
- Protects plant parts.

Examples:

- Coconut husk.
- Hard seed coats.

---

# 6. Complex Permanent Tissue

Complex tissues consist of more than one type of cell working together.

They include:

- Xylem
- Phloem

---

## Xylem

Xylem transports water and minerals from roots to other parts of the plant.

### Components

- Tracheids
- Vessels
- Xylem fibres
- Xylem parenchyma

### Functions

- Conducts water.
- Provides mechanical support.

---

## Phloem

Phloem transports prepared food from leaves to all parts of the plant.

### Components

- Sieve tubes
- Companion cells
- Phloem parenchyma
- Phloem fibres

### Functions

- Conducts food.
- Stores food.

Unlike xylem, phloem transport can occur in both upward and downward directions.

---

# 7. Difference Between Xylem and Phloem

| Xylem                              | Phloem                             |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Transports water and minerals      | Transports food                    |
| Mostly dead cells                  | Mostly living cells                |
| Movement is mainly upward          | Movement occurs in both directions |
| Provides strong mechanical support | Provides limited support           |

---

# 8. Animal Tissues

Animal tissues are classified into:

- Epithelial Tissue
- Connective Tissue
- Muscular Tissue
- Nervous Tissue

---

# 9. Epithelial Tissue

Epithelial tissue forms the outer covering of the body and lines internal organs.

### Functions

- Protection.
- Absorption.
- Secretion.
- Excretion.
- Exchange of materials.

### Types

---

## Squamous Epithelium

Thin and flat cells.

### Function

Allows rapid diffusion.

Examples:

- Air sacs of lungs.
- Blood capillaries.

---

## Cuboidal Epithelium

Cube-shaped cells.

### Function

- Secretion.
- Absorption.

Found in:

- Kidney tubules.
- Glands.

---

## Columnar Epithelium

Tall, pillar-like cells.

### Function

- Absorption.
- Secretion.

Found in:

- Stomach.
- Intestine.

---

## Ciliated Epithelium

Columnar cells with hair-like cilia.

### Function

Moves mucus or other substances.

Found in:

- Respiratory tract.
- Oviduct.

---

## Glandular Epithelium

Modified epithelial tissue specialized for secretion.

Examples:

- Sweat glands.
- Salivary glands.

---

# 10. Connective Tissue

Connective tissue connects, supports, and binds different body parts.

### Functions

- Support.
- Protection.
- Transport.
- Storage.

### Types

---

## Areolar Tissue

Found beneath the skin.

### Function

- Connects skin to muscles.
- Fills spaces between organs.

---

## Adipose Tissue

Stores fat.

### Functions

- Energy storage.
- Heat insulation.
- Protection of organs.

---

## Tendons

Connect muscles to bones.

Characteristics:

- Strong.
- Less elastic.

---

## Ligaments

Connect bones to bones.

Characteristics:

- Strong.
- Elastic.

---

## Cartilage

Flexible connective tissue.

Found in:

- Nose.
- Ear.
- Trachea.
- Joints.

---

## Bone

Hard connective tissue rich in calcium salts.

### Functions

- Provides support.
- Protects organs.
- Helps in movement.

---

## Blood

Blood is a fluid connective tissue.

### Components

- Plasma
- Red Blood Cells (RBCs)
- White Blood Cells (WBCs)
- Platelets

### Functions

- Transport of oxygen and nutrients.
- Protection against diseases.
- Blood clotting.

---

# 11. Muscular Tissue

Muscular tissue enables movement by contraction and relaxation.

### Types

---

## Striated Muscle

Also called skeletal muscle.

### Characteristics

- Long and cylindrical.
- Voluntary.
- Striped appearance.

Examples:

- Muscles of arms and legs.

---

## Smooth Muscle

Also called unstriated muscle.

### Characteristics

- Spindle-shaped.
- Involuntary.
- No stripes.

Found in:

- Stomach.
- Intestine.
- Blood vessels.

---

## Cardiac Muscle

Found only in the heart.

### Characteristics

- Branched.
- Striated.
- Involuntary.
- Contracts continuously.

---

# 12. Nervous Tissue

Nervous tissue consists of specialized cells called **neurons**.

### Structure of a Neuron

A neuron has:

- Cell body
- Dendrites
- Axon

### Functions

- Receives stimuli.
- Transmits nerve impulses.
- Controls body activities.
- Coordinates responses.

---

# 13. Difference Between Plant and Animal Tissues

| Plant Tissues                              | Animal Tissues                                         |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Growth occurs only in meristematic regions | Growth occurs throughout the body                      |
| Mostly provide support and transport       | Specialized for movement, coordination, and protection |
| Cells often have rigid cell walls          | Cells lack cell walls                                  |
| Fewer tissue types                         | Greater variety of tissue types                        |

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

What is a tissue?

**Solution**

A tissue is a group of similar cells that work together to perform a specific function.

---

### Example 2

Name the tissue responsible for increasing the length of a plant.

**Solution**

Apical meristem.

---

### Example 3

Which tissue transports water in plants?

**Solution**

Xylem.

---

### Example 4

Which tissue transports food in plants?

**Solution**

Phloem.

---

### Example 5

Name the muscle found only in the heart.

**Solution**

Cardiac muscle.

---

### Example 6

Which connective tissue stores fat?

**Solution**

Adipose tissue.

---

### Example 7

What is the function of nervous tissue?

**Solution**

It receives stimuli and transmits nerve impulses throughout the body.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **xylem** with **phloem**.
- Mixing up **tendons** and **ligaments**.
- Assuming all muscles are voluntary.
- Confusing **parenchyma** with **collenchyma**.
- Believing blood is not a connective tissue.
- Mixing up **striated** and **cardiac** muscles.
- Forgetting that sclerenchyma cells are dead at maturity.

# Chapter Summary

- A **tissue** is a group of similar cells performing a common function.
- Plant tissues are classified into **meristematic** and **permanent tissues**.
- Permanent tissues are of two types: **simple** (parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma) and **complex** (xylem and phloem).
- **Xylem** transports water and minerals, while **phloem** transports food throughout the plant.
- Animal tissues include **epithelial, connective, muscular,** and **nervous tissues**, each specialized for different functions.
- **Epithelial tissue** protects and lines body surfaces, **connective tissue** provides support and transport, **muscular tissue** enables movement, and **nervous tissue** coordinates body activities.
- **Neurons** are the functional units of the nervous system responsible for transmitting nerve impulses.
- The organization of cells into tissues allows multicellular organisms to perform complex functions efficiently.
