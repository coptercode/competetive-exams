# Introduction

**Acids, bases, and salts** are important classes of chemical substances that play a vital role in everyday life and industries. They are used in medicines, agriculture, food preparation, cleaning products, water treatment, and manufacturing. This chapter explains their properties, reactions, the **pH scale**, and the preparation and uses of important compounds such as **sodium hydroxide, bleaching powder, baking soda,** and **plaster of Paris**.

---

# 1. Acids

An **acid** is a substance that produces **hydrogen ions (H⁺)** in aqueous solution.

### Characteristics of Acids

- Sour in taste.
- Turn **blue litmus red**.
- Conduct electricity in aqueous solution.
- React with metals to produce hydrogen gas.
- React with bases to form salt and water.

### Examples

- Hydrochloric acid (HCl)
- Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄)
- Nitric acid (HNO₃)
- Acetic acid (CH₃COOH)
- Citric acid (in lemons)
- Tartaric acid (in tamarind)

---

# 2. Bases

A **base** is a substance that produces **hydroxide ions (OH⁻)** in aqueous solution.

### Characteristics of Bases

- Bitter in taste.
- Feel soapy to touch.
- Turn **red litmus blue**.
- Conduct electricity in aqueous solution.
- Neutralize acids.

### Examples

- Sodium hydroxide (NaOH)
- Potassium hydroxide (KOH)
- Calcium hydroxide [Ca(OH)₂]
- Magnesium hydroxide [Mg(OH)₂]

---

# 3. Indicators

**Indicators** are substances that change colour in acidic or basic solutions.

### Types of Indicators

| Indicator           | Acid                              | Base                              |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Blue Litmus         | Turns Red                         | No Change                         |
| Red Litmus          | No Change                         | Turns Blue                        |
| Phenolphthalein     | Colourless                        | Pink                              |
| Methyl Orange       | Red                               | Yellow                            |
| Universal Indicator | Different colours depending on pH | Different colours depending on pH |

# 4. Chemical Properties of Acids and Bases

## (A) Reaction of Acids with Metals

Acids react with metals to produce **salt** and **hydrogen gas**.

### General Equation

```
Metal + Acid → Salt + Hydrogen
```

### Example

```
Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂↑
```

Observation:

- Hydrogen gas is evolved.
- Zinc gradually dissolves.

---

## (B) Reaction of Bases with Certain Metals

Strong bases react with metals like **zinc** and **aluminium** to produce hydrogen gas.

### Example

```
2NaOH + Zn → Na₂ZnO₂ + H₂↑
```

---

## (C) Reaction of Acids with Metal Carbonates

Acids react with metal carbonates to produce **salt, carbon dioxide,** and **water**.

### General Equation

```
Metal Carbonate + Acid → Salt + CO₂ + H₂O
```

### Example

```
Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + CO₂ + H₂O
```

---

## (D) Reaction of Acids with Metal Hydrogen Carbonates

### General Equation

```
Metal Hydrogen Carbonate + Acid

↓

Salt + CO₂ + H₂O
```

### Example

```
NaHCO₃ + HCl → NaCl + CO₂ + H₂O
```

---

## (E) Neutralization Reaction

An acid reacts with a base to form **salt** and **water**.

### General Equation

```
Acid + Base → Salt + Water
```

### Example

```
HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
```

This is an **exothermic reaction**.

---

# 5. pH Scale

The **pH scale** measures the acidity or basicity of a solution.

```
pH = -log[H⁺]
```

The pH scale ranges from **0 to 14**.

|       pH Range | Nature  |
| -------------: | ------- |
|    Less than 7 | Acidic  |
|              7 | Neutral |
| Greater than 7 | Basic   |

---

# 6. Importance of pH in Daily Life

## (A) Digestion

- Hydrochloric acid in the stomach helps digestion.
- Excess acid causes indigestion.
- Antacids such as **milk of magnesia** neutralize excess acid.

---

## (B) Tooth Decay

- Tooth enamel begins to dissolve when the pH falls below **5.5**.
- Basic toothpaste helps neutralize acids.

---

## (C) Soil Treatment

- Acidic soil is treated with **quicklime (CaO)** or **slaked lime [Ca(OH)₂]**.
- Basic soil is treated with organic matter.

---

## (D) Self-Defence by Animals and Plants

- Bee and ant stings contain **formic acid**.
- Baking soda relieves the effect of the sting.
- Nettle leaves also contain acidic substances.

# 8. Preparation of Sodium Hydroxide (Chlor-Alkali Process)

Sodium hydroxide is prepared by the **electrolysis of brine (aqueous sodium chloride solution).**

### Chemical Equation

```
2NaCl + 2H₂O

Electrolysis

→ 2NaOH + Cl₂ + H₂
```

### Products Formed

- Sodium hydroxide (NaOH)
- Chlorine gas (Cl₂)
- Hydrogen gas (H₂)

The process is called the **Chlor-alkali Process** because chlorine and alkali (NaOH) are produced together.

---

# 9. Uses of Sodium Hydroxide

- Manufacture of soaps and detergents.
- Paper industry.
- Textile industry.
- Artificial fibres.
- Cleaning agents.
- Petroleum refining.

# 10. Bleaching Powder

### Chemical Formula

```
CaOCl₂
```

### Preparation

Bleaching powder is prepared by passing chlorine gas over dry slaked lime.

### Chemical Equation

```
Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂ → CaOCl₂ + H₂O
```

### Properties

- White powder.
- Has a strong smell of chlorine.
- Acts as a bleaching and disinfecting agent.

### Uses

- Disinfecting drinking water.
- Bleaching cotton and linen.
- Bleaching wood pulp.
- Oxidizing agent.
- Sanitizing swimming pools.

---

# 11. Baking Soda

### Chemical Formula

```
NaHCO₃
```

### Preparation

Prepared by the Solvay process.

### Properties

- White crystalline solid.
- Mild base.
- Produces carbon dioxide on heating.

### Reaction on Heating

```
2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + CO₂ + H₂O
```

### Uses

- Baking powder.
- Antacid.
- Soda-acid fire extinguishers.
- Making soft and fluffy cakes.

---

# 12. Washing Soda

### Chemical Formula

```
Na₂CO₃·10H₂O
```

### Preparation

```
Na₂CO₃ + 10H₂O → Na₂CO₃·10H₂O
```

### Uses

- Cleaning agent.
- Softening hard water.
- Glass industry.
- Soap and paper industries.

---

# 13. Plaster of Paris (POP)

### Chemical Formula

```
CaSO₄·½H₂O
```

### Preparation

Prepared by heating gypsum.

```
CaSO₄·2H₂O

Heat

→ CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O
```

---

### Properties

- White powder.
- Sets into a hard mass on mixing with water.

### Setting Reaction

```
CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O

↓

CaSO₄·2H₂O
```

---

### Uses

- Making statues.
- Surgical casts.
- Decorative materials.
- False ceilings.
- Blackboard chalk.

```
pH = 7
```

### Example 3

Name the process used to manufacture sodium hydroxide.

**Solution**

**Answer:** Chlor-alkali process.

---

### Example 4

Write the chemical formula of bleaching powder.

**Solution**

```
CaOCl₂
```

---

### Example 5

Why is baking soda used in baking?

**Solution**

On heating, baking soda releases **carbon dioxide gas**, which makes dough rise and gives cakes and bread a soft, spongy texture.

---

### Example 6

Why is Plaster of Paris stored in moisture-proof containers?

**Solution**

Plaster of Paris reacts with moisture to form gypsum and becomes hard. Therefore, it must be stored in airtight, moisture-proof containers.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **strong acids** with **concentrated acids**.
- Assuming all bases are alkalis (only water-soluble bases are alkalis).
- Forgetting that acids produce **H⁺ ions only in aqueous solution**.
- Mixing up the formulas of **baking soda (NaHCO₃)** and **washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O)**.
- Confusing **gypsum** with **Plaster of Paris**.
- Writing incorrect products for neutralization reactions.
- Assuming a lower pH means a stronger base (it indicates a stronger acid).

# Key Definitions

| Term                 | Definition                                                       |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Acid                 | Substance that produces H⁺ ions in aqueous solution.             |
| Base                 | Substance that produces OH⁻ ions in aqueous solution.            |
| Alkali               | A water-soluble base.                                            |
| Indicator            | Substance that changes colour in acidic or basic medium.         |
| Neutralization       | Reaction between an acid and a base forming salt and water.      |
| pH                   | Measure of acidity or basicity of a solution.                    |
| Chlor-alkali Process | Electrolysis of brine to produce NaOH, Cl₂, and H₂.              |
| Bleaching Powder     | Calcium oxychloride used as a bleaching and disinfecting agent.  |
| Baking Soda          | Sodium hydrogen carbonate used in baking and as an antacid.      |
| Washing Soda         | Hydrated sodium carbonate used for cleaning and softening water. |
| Plaster of Paris     | Calcium sulfate hemihydrate used for casts and moulds.           |
| Gypsum               | Calcium sulfate dihydrate used to prepare Plaster of Paris.      |

# Chapter Summary

- **Acids** produce **H⁺ ions**, while **bases** produce **OH⁻ ions** in aqueous solutions.
- Acids react with **metals**, **metal carbonates**, and **metal hydrogen carbonates**, producing characteristic products such as hydrogen gas and carbon dioxide.
- A **neutralization reaction** produces **salt and water**.
- The **pH scale (0–14)** indicates whether a solution is acidic, neutral, or basic.
- The pH of substances plays an important role in **digestion, tooth decay, soil treatment,** and **biological processes**.
- **Sodium hydroxide** is manufactured by the **Chlor-alkali process** through the electrolysis of brine.
- **Bleaching powder** is widely used for bleaching and disinfecting.
- **Baking soda** is used in baking, fire extinguishers, and as an antacid, while **washing soda** is used for cleaning and softening hard water.
- **Plaster of Paris**, prepared from gypsum, is widely used in medicine, construction, and sculpture.
