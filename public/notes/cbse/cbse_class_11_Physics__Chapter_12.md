# Introduction

The **Kinetic Theory of Gases** explains the macroscopic properties of gases such as pressure, temperature, and volume in terms of the microscopic motion of their molecules.

According to this theory, gases consist of a large number of tiny molecules in continuous random motion. Their collisions with the walls of the container produce pressure, while their average kinetic energy determines the temperature.

---

# 1. Kinetic Theory of Gases

## Definition

The kinetic theory explains the behaviour of gases by considering them as collections of rapidly moving molecules.

---

## Objectives

- Explain gas pressure.
- Explain gas laws.
- Relate temperature to molecular motion.
- Calculate molecular speeds.
- Explain internal energy of gases.

---

# 2. Postulates of Kinetic Theory

1. A gas consists of a very large number of tiny molecules.
2. Molecules are in continuous random motion.
3. The volume occupied by molecules is negligible compared to the volume of the gas.
4. Molecules move in straight lines between collisions.
5. Collisions between molecules and with container walls are perfectly elastic.
6. No intermolecular force acts except during collisions.
7. The duration of a collision is negligible.
8. Gas molecules obey Newton's laws of motion.

---

# 3. Pressure of an Ideal Gas

Gas pressure arises due to the continuous collisions of molecules with the walls of the container.

---

# 4. Ideal Gas

## Definition

An **ideal gas** is a hypothetical gas that obeys all gas laws exactly at all temperatures and pressures.

---

## Characteristics

- Molecules are point masses.
- No intermolecular forces.
- Elastic collisions.
- Obeys the ideal gas equation.

---

# 5. Ideal Gas Equation

```
PV = nRT
```

where

- **P** = Pressure
- **V** = Volume
- **n** = Number of moles
- **R** = Universal gas constant
- **T** = Absolute temperature

---

## Universal Gas Constant

```
R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹
```

---

# 6. Gas Laws

---

## Boyle's Law

At constant temperature,

```
PV = Constant
```

```
P₁V₁ = P₂V₂
```

---

## Charles' Law

At constant pressure,

```
V/T = Constant
```

---

## Gay-Lussac's Law

At constant volume,

```
P/T = Constant
```

---

## Avogadro's Law

Equal volumes of all gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules.

---

# 13. Degrees of Freedom

## Definition

The number of independent ways in which a molecule can possess energy.

---

## Monatomic Gas

Degrees of freedom

```
f = 3
```

(Only translational)

---

## Diatomic Gas

At ordinary temperatures,

```
f = 5
```

(3 translational + 2 rotational)

---

## Polyatomic Gas

Generally,

```
f = 6
```

or more, depending on molecular structure and temperature.

# 17. Mean Free Path

## Definition

The average distance travelled by a gas molecule between two successive collisions.

Formula

```
            1
λ = -----------------------
      √2 πd²n
```

where

- **d** = Diameter of molecule
- **n** = Number of molecules per unit volume

---

## Characteristics

- Increases at low pressure.
- Increases with temperature.
- Decreases with increasing density.

---

# 18. Real Gases

Real gases deviate from ideal behaviour due to

- Finite molecular size
- Intermolecular forces

Examples

- Oxygen
- Nitrogen
- Carbon dioxide

---

# 19. Ideal Gas vs Real Gas

Ideal gases obey

```
PV = nRT
```

under all conditions (hypothetically).

Real gases obey it only approximately, especially at

- High temperature
- Low pressure

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Calculate the pressure exerted by an ideal gas of density

```
1.2 kg m⁻³
```

having an RMS speed of

```
500 m s⁻¹
```

**Solution**

Using

```
P = (1/3)ρc²
```

```
=

1/3 × 1.2 × 500²

=

100000 Pa
```

---

### Example 2

One mole of an ideal gas occupies a volume of

```

```

```
300 K
```

Calculate the pressure.

Take

```

```

**Solution**

Using

```
PV = nRT
```

```
P

=

(1 × 8.314 × 300)

──────────────────

0.024

≈ 1.04 × 10⁵ Pa
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **ideal gases** with **real gases**; real gases do not obey the ideal gas equation under all conditions.
- Forgetting to convert the **molar mass** into **kg mol⁻¹** while using the RMS speed formula.
- Assuming **temperature measures total kinetic energy**; it is proportional to the **average translational kinetic energy** of molecules.
- Mixing up **RMS speed**, **average speed**, and **most probable speed**.
- Ignoring the correct **degrees of freedom** while calculating internal energy or specific heat capacities.
- Applying the **law of equipartition of energy** without considering the active degrees of freedom at a given temperature.
- Confusing **mean free path** with the distance between molecules.
- Using **°C** instead of **Kelvin** in gas law calculations.

---

# Chapter Summary

- The **kinetic theory of gases** explains the macroscopic behaviour of gases in terms of the random motion of their molecules.
- Gas pressure results from molecular collisions with the walls of the container.
- An **ideal gas** obeys the equation **PV = nRT**, while real gases deviate from ideal behaviour due to intermolecular forces and finite molecular size.
- The **average translational kinetic energy** of gas molecules is directly proportional to the **absolute temperature**.
- Molecular speeds are described by the **most probable speed**, **average speed**, and **RMS speed**, with **Vrms > Vavg > Vp**.
- The **degrees of freedom** of a molecule determine its internal energy and specific heat capacities through the **law of equipartition of energy**.
- The **internal energy** of an ideal gas depends only on its temperature.
- The **mean free path** is the average distance travelled by a molecule between successive collisions and depends on the density and pressure of the gas.
- The kinetic theory successfully connects microscopic molecular motion with observable properties such as pressure, temperature, and volume.
