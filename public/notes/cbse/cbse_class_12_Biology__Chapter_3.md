# Introduction

**Reproductive health** refers to the complete physical, mental, emotional, and social well-being related to the reproductive system and its functions. It includes safe and responsible sexual behavior, prevention of sexually transmitted diseases (STDs), family planning, maternal and child health care, and treatment of infertility.

The **World Health Organization (WHO)** emphasizes reproductive health as an essential component of overall health and quality of life.

---

# 6. Methods of Birth Control

Birth control methods are broadly classified into:

- Natural methods
- Barrier methods
- Intrauterine devices (IUDs)
- Oral contraceptives
- Injectable and implant methods
- Surgical methods (Sterilization)

---

# 7. Natural Methods

These methods avoid fertilization without using devices or medicines.

---

## (A) Periodic Abstinence

Avoid sexual intercourse during the fertile period.

Fertile period

```
Day 10–17
```

of a 28-day menstrual cycle.

---

## (B) Withdrawal (Coitus Interruptus)

The penis is withdrawn before ejaculation.

---

## (C) Lactational Amenorrhea (LAM)

Pregnancy is naturally prevented during breastfeeding due to the absence of ovulation.

Effective for approximately:

- First **6 months** after childbirth
- Exclusive breastfeeding
- No menstruation

---

# Comparison: Natural Methods

| Method                 | Principle            | Limitation                       |
| ---------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Periodic abstinence    | Avoid fertile period | Requires accurate cycle tracking |
| Withdrawal             | Prevent sperm entry  | Less reliable                    |
| Lactational amenorrhea | Suppresses ovulation | Temporary                        |

---

# 8. Barrier Methods

Prevent sperm from reaching the ovum.

---

## Male Condom

Advantages

- Prevents pregnancy.
- Protects against STDs including HIV.

---

## Female Condom

Placed inside the vagina before intercourse.

---

## Diaphragm

Placed over the cervix.

Usually used with spermicidal cream.

---

## Cervical Cap

Fits tightly over the cervix.

---

## Spermicides

Chemicals that destroy sperms.

Examples

- Creams
- Foams
- Jellies

---

# 9. Intrauterine Devices (IUDs)

Inserted into the uterus by trained doctors.

---

## Types

### A. Non-medicated IUD

Example

- Lippes Loop

---

### B. Copper-Releasing IUD

Examples

- Cu-T
- Cu-7
- Multiload-375

Mechanism

- Release copper ions.
- Reduce sperm motility.
- Increase phagocytosis of sperms.

---

### C. Hormone-Releasing IUD

Examples

- LNG-20
- Progestasert

Mechanism

- Release progesterone.
- Make endometrium unsuitable for implantation.
- Thicken cervical mucus.

---

# Comparison: Types of IUDs

| Type          | Example     | Mechanism                               |
| ------------- | ----------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Non-medicated | Lippes Loop | Prevent implantation                    |
| Copper        | Cu-T        | Decreases sperm motility                |
| Hormonal      | LNG-20      | Prevents implantation and fertilization |

---

# 10. Oral Contraceptive Pills

Contain synthetic hormones.

---

## Examples

# 11. Injectable and Implant Contraceptives

## Tubectomy

# 13. Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP)

## Definition

Intentional termination of pregnancy before full-term development.

---

## Importance

- Prevents unwanted births.
- Protects maternal health.
- Useful in pregnancies due to rape.
- Helps when severe fetal abnormalities are detected.

---

## Medical Significance

MTP is safest during the **first trimester**, especially within the first **12 weeks** of pregnancy.

It should always be performed under qualified medical supervision.

---

# 14. Amniocentesis

## Definition

A prenatal diagnostic technique in which a small amount of amniotic fluid is collected to detect genetic or chromosomal abnormalities in the fetus.

---

## Uses

- Detect chromosomal disorders.
- Detect genetic diseases.
- Diagnose metabolic disorders.

---

## Misuse

Previously misused for **sex determination**, leading to female foeticide.

---

## Legal Status

Sex determination using amniocentesis is **strictly prohibited by law** under the **Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques (PCPNDT) Act** in India.

---

# 15. Sexually Transmitted Diseases (STDs)

## Definition

Diseases transmitted mainly through sexual contact.

Also called:

- Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)
- Venereal Diseases (VDs)

---

## Common STDs

- Gonorrhoea
- Syphilis
- Chlamydiasis
- Genital herpes
- Genital warts
- Hepatitis B
- HIV/AIDS

---

## Symptoms

- Genital itching
- Pain during urination
- Abnormal discharge
- Sores
- Swelling
- Fever

Some infections may remain **asymptomatic** in the early stages.

---

## Prevention

- Safe sexual practices
- Use of condoms
- Avoid multiple sexual partners
- Sterile needles
- Safe blood transfusion
- Early diagnosis and treatment

---

Inability of a couple to conceive despite regular, unprotected intercourse for a prolonged period.

## Causes

---

### Female Factors

# 17. Assisted Reproductive Technologies (ART)

Used to treat infertility.

---

## (A) IVF (In Vitro Fertilization)

Known as the **test-tube baby technique**.

---

### Steps

```
Egg Collection

↓

Fertilization in Laboratory

↓

Embryo Formation

↓

Embryo Transfer
```

---

## (B) ZIFT (Zygote Intra-Fallopian Transfer)

- Fertilization occurs in the laboratory.
- Zygote is transferred into the fallopian tube.

---

## (C) IUT (Intra-Uterine Transfer)

Embryo with more than 8 blastomeres is transferred into the uterus.

---

## (D) GIFT (Gamete Intra-Fallopian Transfer)

Egg and sperm are placed together in the fallopian tube for fertilization inside the body.

Suitable when at least one fallopian tube is functional.

---

## (E) ICSI (Intra-Cytoplasmic Sperm Injection)

A single sperm is directly injected into the cytoplasm of an ovum.

Useful in severe male infertility.

---

## (F) Artificial Insemination (AI)

Semen is introduced into the female reproductive tract artificially.

---

## (G) Intrauterine Insemination (IUI)

Specially prepared semen is introduced directly into the uterus.

---

# Comparison: ART Techniques

| Technique | Fertilization Occurs | Transfer                        |
| --------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| IVF       | Laboratory           | Embryo to uterus                |
| ZIFT      | Laboratory           | Zygote to fallopian tube        |
| GIFT      | Fallopian tube       | Gametes transferred             |
| ICSI      | Laboratory           | Single sperm injected into ovum |
| IUI       | Inside female body   | Sperms placed in uterus         |

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

**Question:** Why are condoms considered one of the best contraceptive methods?

**Solution:**

Condoms act as a physical barrier that prevents sperm from entering the female reproductive tract. They also reduce the transmission of sexually transmitted diseases such as HIV/AIDS, making them one of the safest temporary contraceptive methods.

---

### Example 2

**Question:** Differentiate between vasectomy and tubectomy.

**Solution:**

| Vasectomy                    | Tubectomy                        |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Performed in males           | Performed in females             |
| Vas deferens is cut and tied | Fallopian tubes are cut and tied |
| Prevents sperm transport     | Prevents ovum transport          |

---

### Example 3

**Question:** What is the significance of the PCPNDT Act?

**Solution:**

The PCPNDT Act prohibits prenatal sex determination and prevents the misuse of diagnostic techniques such as amniocentesis for female foeticide. It promotes gender equality and protects the girl child.

---

### Example 4

**Question:** Distinguish between IVF and GIFT.

**Solution:**

In **IVF**, fertilization occurs outside the body in a laboratory, and the embryo is transferred to the uterus. In **GIFT**, sperm and ova are placed directly into the fallopian tube, where fertilization occurs naturally inside the body.

---

### Example 5

**Question:** Why is Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP) performed?

**Solution:**

MTP may be performed to terminate unwanted pregnancies, protect the mother's health, manage pregnancies resulting from sexual assault, or when severe fetal abnormalities are detected. It is safest during the first trimester under medical supervision.

---

### Example 6

**Question:** Name two bacterial and two viral sexually transmitted diseases.

**Solution:**

**Bacterial STDs:**

- Gonorrhoea
- Syphilis

**Viral STDs:**

- HIV/AIDS
- Hepatitis B

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **contraceptive methods** with **infertility treatments**; contraceptives prevent pregnancy, whereas ART techniques help achieve pregnancy.
- Assuming all IUDs work in the same way; non-medicated, copper-releasing, and hormone-releasing IUDs have different mechanisms.
- Believing oral contraceptive pills protect against STDs; **only condoms provide significant protection against most sexually transmitted infections**.
- Confusing **vasectomy** (male sterilization) with **tubectomy** (female sterilization).
- Assuming **IVF**, **ZIFT**, and **GIFT** are identical; they differ mainly in the site of fertilization and embryo or gamete transfer.
- Misunderstanding **amniocentesis** as a sex determination test; its legitimate purpose is prenatal diagnosis of genetic disorders, while sex determination is legally prohibited.
- Assuming all STDs show immediate symptoms; many infections remain asymptomatic during the early stages.
- Forgetting that **Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP)** should be carried out only under qualified medical supervision and in accordance with legal provisions.

# Chapter Summary

- **Reproductive health** ensures physical, mental, emotional, and social well-being related to the reproductive system.
- National reproductive health programmes promote family planning, maternal care, child health, sex education, and prevention of sexually transmitted diseases.
- **Birth control methods** include natural methods, barrier methods, intrauterine devices, hormonal contraceptives, and surgical sterilization.
- **Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP)** is a medically supervised procedure for terminating pregnancy under legally permitted conditions.
- **Amniocentesis** is a valuable prenatal diagnostic technique but cannot legally be used for fetal sex determination in India.
- **Sexually transmitted diseases (STDs)** can be prevented through safe sexual practices, condom use, sterile medical procedures, and early diagnosis.
- **Infertility** may result from male or female factors and can often be managed using **Assisted Reproductive Technologies (ART)** such as IVF, ZIFT, GIFT, ICSI, AI, and IUI.
- Reproductive health awareness, responsible parenthood, and access to modern medical care contribute significantly to healthier individuals, families, and society.
