# Introduction

Objects around us are either at rest or in motion. Their state can change when a **force** acts on them. Force can make an object move, stop, change its speed, or change its direction of motion.

The behavior of moving objects was explained by **Sir Isaac Newton** through his **three laws of motion**, which form the foundation of classical mechanics. This chapter also introduces the concepts of **momentum** and the **Law of Conservation of Momentum**.

---

# 1. Force

A **force** is a push or a pull acting on an object.

### Effects of Force

- Changes the state of rest or motion.
- Changes the speed of an object.
- Changes the direction of motion.
- Changes the shape or size of an object.

### SI Unit

```
newton (N)
```

---

# 2. Types of Force

## Contact Forces

These forces act only when objects are in physical contact.

### Examples

- Muscular force
- Frictional force

---

## Non-contact Forces

These forces act without physical contact.

### Examples

- Gravitational force
- Magnetic force
- Electrostatic force

---

# 3. Balanced and Unbalanced Forces

## Balanced Forces

Balanced forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

### Characteristics

- Resultant force is zero.
- Do not change the state of motion.
- May change the shape of an object.

### Example

Two people pulling a rope with equal force in opposite directions.

---

## Unbalanced Forces

Unbalanced forces do not cancel each other.

### Characteristics

- Resultant force is not zero.
- Cause a change in motion.
- Can change speed or direction.

### Example

Kicking a stationary football.

---

# 4. Newton's First Law of Motion

### Statement

> **An object remains at rest or continues to move with uniform velocity in a straight line unless acted upon by an unbalanced external force.**

This law is also called the **Law of Inertia**.

---

# 5. Inertia

**Inertia** is the property of an object by which it resists any change in its state of rest or motion.

The greater the mass of an object, the greater is its inertia.

### Types of Inertia

#### Inertia of Rest

A stationary object tends to remain at rest.

**Example:**

Passengers fall backward when a bus suddenly starts.

---

#### Inertia of Motion

A moving object tends to continue moving.

**Example:**

Passengers fall forward when a moving bus stops suddenly.

---

#### Inertia of Direction

An object resists changes in its direction of motion.

**Example:**

Passengers are pushed sideways when a vehicle takes a sharp turn.

---

# 6. Momentum

**Momentum** is the quantity of motion possessed by an object.

It depends on both the mass and velocity of the object.

### Formula

```
Momentum (p) = Mass × Velocity
```

```
p = mv
```

### SI Unit

```
kg m/s
```

---

# 7. Newton's Second Law of Motion

### Statement

> **The rate of change of momentum of an object is directly proportional to the applied force and takes place in the direction of the force.**

From this law, the mathematical relation is obtained:

```
F = ma
```

where:

- **F** = Force
- **m** = Mass
- **a** = Acceleration

### SI Unit of Force

```
1 Newton
```

One newton is the force required to produce an acceleration of **1 m/s²** in a body of mass **1 kg**.

---

# 8. Newton's Third Law of Motion

### Statement

> **For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.**

### Characteristics

- Action and reaction forces are equal in magnitude.
- They act in opposite directions.
- They act on different objects.
- They occur simultaneously.

### Examples

- Recoil of a gun.
- Rocket propulsion.
- Walking on the ground.
- Swimming.
- Rowing a boat.

---

# 9. Law of Conservation of Momentum

### Statement

> **When no external force acts on a system, the total momentum before collision is equal to the total momentum after collision.**

### Formula

```
Initial Momentum = Final Momentum
```

For two objects,

```
m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂
```

where:

- **m₁, m₂** = Masses of the objects
- **u₁, u₂** = Initial velocities
- **v₁, v₂** = Final velocities

This law is applicable during collisions and explosions when external forces are negligible.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

A force of **20 N** acts on a body of mass **4 kg**. Find the acceleration.

**Solution**

Using

```
F = ma
```

```
a = F / m
```

```
= 20 / 4
```

```
= 5 m/s²
```

---

### Example 2

Calculate the momentum of a body of mass **5 kg** moving with a velocity of **4 m/s**.

**Solution**

```
p = mv
```

```
= 5 × 4
```

```
= 20 kg·m/s
```

---

### Example 3

A body of mass **2 kg** accelerates at **3 m/s²**. Find the force acting on it.

**Solution**

```
F = ma
```

```
= 2 × 3
```

```
= 6 N
```

---

### Example 4

Why does a passenger fall forward when a moving bus stops suddenly?

**Solution**

Due to the **inertia of motion**, the upper part of the body continues moving forward even after the bus stops.

---

### Example 5

State one example of Newton's Third Law of Motion.

**Solution**

A rocket moves upward because the hot gases are expelled downward with an equal and opposite force.

---

### Example 6

Two objects of masses **2 kg** and **3 kg** move together with a velocity of **4 m/s**. Find their total momentum.

**Solution**

Total mass

```
= 2 + 3 = 5 kg
```

Momentum

```
p = mv
```

```
= 5 × 4
```

```
= 20 kg·m/s
```

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **mass** with **weight**.
- Mixing up **force** and **momentum**.
- Forgetting that action and reaction act on **different objects**.
- Assuming balanced forces produce motion.
- Using **F = ma** without ensuring the units are in SI.
- Confusing inertia with force.
- Ignoring the direction of momentum in numerical problems.

# Chapter Summary

- A **force** is a push or pull that can change the state of motion or shape of an object.
- Forces are classified as **contact** and **non-contact forces**.
- **Balanced forces** do not change the state of motion, whereas **unbalanced forces** cause acceleration.
- **Newton's First Law** explains the concept of **inertia**, the tendency of an object to resist changes in its state of motion.
- **Momentum** is the product of mass and velocity and measures the quantity of motion.
- **Newton's Second Law** establishes the relationship between force, mass, and acceleration through the equation **F = ma**.
- **Newton's Third Law** states that every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
- The **Law of Conservation of Momentum** states that the total momentum of an isolated system remains constant in the absence of external forces.
- Newton's laws explain a wide range of everyday phenomena, from walking and driving to rocket launches and collisions.
