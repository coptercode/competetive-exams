# Introduction

**Anatomy** is the branch of biology that deals with the **internal structure** of organisms. In flowering plants, anatomy involves the study of different tissues and the internal organization of roots, stems, and leaves.

This chapter explains **plant tissues**, the internal anatomy of **dicot and monocot roots**, **dicot and monocot stems**, **dorsiventral and isobilateral leaves**, and the process of **secondary growth** in dicot stems.

---

# 1. Plant Tissues

A **tissue** is a group of similar cells having a common origin and performing a specific function.

Plant tissues are broadly classified into:

```
Plant Tissues
│
├── Meristematic Tissues
└── Permanent Tissues
```

---

# 2. Meristematic Tissues

Meristematic tissues consist of actively dividing cells responsible for plant growth.

## Characteristics

- Cells divide continuously.
- Thin cell walls.
- Dense cytoplasm.
- Large nucleus.
- Vacuoles absent or very small.
- No intercellular spaces.

---

## Types of Meristems

### (A) Apical Meristem

Located at the tips of roots and shoots.

**Function**

- Increases the length of the plant.
- Responsible for **primary growth**.

---

### (B) Intercalary Meristem

Located at the bases of leaves or internodes.

**Function**

- Rapid elongation.
- Regrowth after grazing.

**Examples**

- Grass
- Bamboo

---

### (C) Lateral Meristem

Located along the sides of stems and roots.

**Function**

- Increases girth.
- Responsible for **secondary growth**.

**Examples**

- Vascular cambium
- Cork cambium

---

# 3. Permanent Tissues

Permanent tissues are formed from meristematic tissues after differentiation.

They lose the ability to divide.

---

## Types of Permanent Tissues

### (A) Simple Permanent Tissue

Made up of one type of cells.

Includes

- Parenchyma
- Collenchyma
- Sclerenchyma

---

### Parenchyma

#### Characteristics

- Living cells.
- Thin cell walls.
- Large vacuoles.
- Intercellular spaces present.

#### Functions

- Food storage.
- Photosynthesis (chlorenchyma).
- Buoyancy (aerenchyma).
- Healing.

---

### Collenchyma

#### Characteristics

- Living cells.
- Uneven thickening at corners.
- Little intercellular space.

#### Functions

- Mechanical support.
- Flexibility.

---

### Sclerenchyma

#### Characteristics

- Dead cells.
- Thick lignified walls.
- Narrow lumen.

#### Types

- Fibres
- Sclereids

#### Functions

- Strength.
- Mechanical support.

---

## Comparison of Simple Tissues

| Feature   | Parenchyma | Collenchyma           | Sclerenchyma        |
| --------- | ---------- | --------------------- | ------------------- |
| Cells     | Living     | Living                | Dead                |
| Cell Wall | Thin       | Unevenly thickened    | Thick, lignified    |
| Function  | Storage    | Support & Flexibility | Mechanical Strength |

---

# 4. Complex Permanent Tissues

Complex tissues consist of more than one type of cell.

---

## Xylem

Conducts water and minerals.

### Components

- Tracheids
- Vessels
- Xylem fibres
- Xylem parenchyma

---

## Phloem

Conducts food.

### Components

- Sieve tubes
- Companion cells
- Phloem fibres
- Phloem parenchyma

---

## Comparison

| Xylem             | Phloem                  |
| ----------------- | ----------------------- |
| Conducts water    | Conducts food           |
| Mostly dead cells | Mostly living cells     |
| Upward transport  | Bidirectional transport |

---

# 5. Anatomy of Dicot Root

### Characteristics

- Epiblema (epidermis) with root hairs.
- Cortex made of parenchyma.
- Endodermis with Casparian strips.
- Pericycle present.
- Radial vascular bundles.
- Xylem usually **tetraarch**.
- Large pith absent or very small.

# 6. Anatomy of Monocot Root

### Characteristics

- Similar to dicot root.
- Polyarch xylem.
- Large and well-developed pith.
- Secondary growth absent.

---

# Comparison of Dicot and Monocot Roots

| Character        | Dicot Root | Monocot Root |
| ---------------- | ---------- | ------------ |
| Xylem            | Tetraarch  | Polyarch     |
| Pith             | Small      | Large        |
| Secondary Growth | Present    | Absent       |

---

# 7. Anatomy of Dicot Stem

### Characteristics

- Epidermis with cuticle.
- Cortex differentiated.
- Hypodermis made of collenchyma.
- Endodermis present.
- Vascular bundles arranged in a ring.
- Vascular bundles open (cambium present).
- Large central pith.

---

## Diagram

```
──────── Epidermis ────────
 Cortex
──────── Endodermis ───────
 Vascular Bundles (Ring)
───────────────
      Pith
───────────────
```

# 8. Anatomy of Monocot Stem

### Characteristics

- Epidermis with cuticle.
- Ground tissue undifferentiated.
- Numerous vascular bundles scattered.
- Closed vascular bundles.
- Cambium absent.
- Secondary growth absent.

---

# Comparison of Dicot and Monocot Stems

| Character        | Dicot Stem | Monocot Stem |
| ---------------- | ---------- | ------------ |
| Vascular Bundles | Ring       | Scattered    |
| Cambium          | Present    | Absent       |
| Secondary Growth | Present    | Absent       |

# 9. Anatomy of Leaf

Leaves are specialized organs for photosynthesis.

---

## Dorsiventral Leaf (Dicot Leaf)

### Characteristics

- Upper epidermis.
- Palisade parenchyma beneath upper epidermis.
- Spongy parenchyma below palisade.
- Stomata mainly on lower surface.
- Reticulate venation.

---

## Diagram

```

──────────────

──────────────
```

---

## Isobilateral Leaf (Monocot Leaf)

### Characteristics

- Similar upper and lower surfaces.
- Mesophyll not differentiated.
- Stomata on both surfaces.
- Parallel venation.
- Bulliform cells present.

---

# Comparison of Leaves

| Dorsiventral Leaf            | Isobilateral Leaf          |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Dicot                        | Monocot                    |
| Mesophyll differentiated     | Mesophyll undifferentiated |
| Stomata mainly lower surface | Stomata on both surfaces   |
| Reticulate venation          | Parallel venation          |

# 10. Secondary Growth

Secondary growth is the increase in the **girth (thickness)** of stems and roots.

Occurs mainly in dicot plants.

---

# 11. Vascular Cambium

The vascular cambium is a lateral meristem.

### Functions

Produces

- Secondary xylem (inside)
- Secondary phloem (outside)

```
Secondary Phloem
      │
Cambium
      │
Secondary Xylem
```

---

# 12. Annual Rings

Alternating bands of secondary xylem formed each year are called **annual rings**.

### Importance

- Determine the age of trees.
- Study climatic conditions.

---

# 13. Cork Cambium (Phellogen)

The cork cambium develops in the outer cortex.

Produces

### Outside

- Cork (Phellem)

### Inside

- Secondary Cortex (Phelloderm)

---

## Bark

Bark consists of tissues external to the vascular cambium.

Functions

- Protection
- Prevents water loss
- Mechanical support

---

# 14. Lenticels

Lenticels are small openings in the bark.

### Functions

- Gaseous exchange.
- Transpiration.

# Solved Examples

### Example 1

Differentiate between meristematic and permanent tissues.

**Solution**

Meristematic tissues consist of actively dividing cells responsible for growth, whereas permanent tissues are differentiated cells that have lost the ability to divide and perform specialized functions.

---

### Example 2

Name the four components of xylem.

**Solution**

- Tracheids
- Vessels
- Xylem fibres
- Xylem parenchyma

---

### Example 3

Why does secondary growth occur only in dicot stems?

**Solution**

Dicot stems possess a vascular cambium, which produces secondary xylem and secondary phloem, increasing the girth. Monocot stems lack cambium and therefore generally do not undergo secondary growth.

---

### Example 4

State one anatomical difference between a dicot and a monocot root.

**Solution**

Dicot roots usually have **tetraarch xylem** and a small pith, whereas monocot roots have **polyarch xylem** and a large, well-developed pith.

---

### Example 5

What are bulliform cells?

**Solution**

Bulliform cells are large, thin-walled epidermal cells present in the upper epidermis of monocot leaves. They help in leaf rolling during water stress, reducing water loss.

---

### Example 6

What is the function of lenticels?

**Solution**

Lenticels facilitate the exchange of gases between the internal tissues of woody stems and the external environment. They also permit a small amount of transpiration.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing **parenchyma**, **collenchyma**, and **sclerenchyma** based on cell wall thickness and living status.
- Assuming that all vascular bundles contain cambium; **monocot stems** have **closed vascular bundles** without cambium.
- Mixing up the arrangement of vascular bundles in **dicot** and **monocot** stems.
- Confusing **secondary growth** with **primary growth**.
- Forgetting that **bulliform cells** are characteristic of monocot leaves.
- Assuming that xylem is entirely made of dead cells; **xylem parenchyma** is living.
- Confusing **cork cambium** with **vascular cambium**.
- Believing that monocot roots undergo secondary growth under normal conditions.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Plant tissues are broadly classified into **meristematic** and **permanent** tissues.
- **Meristematic tissues** (apical, intercalary, and lateral) are responsible for plant growth through continuous cell division.
- **Permanent tissues** include **simple tissues** (parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma) and **complex tissues** (xylem and phloem), each performing specialized functions.
- The **dicot root** typically has **tetraarch xylem**, a small pith, and may undergo secondary growth, whereas the **monocot root** has **polyarch xylem**, a large pith, and lacks secondary growth.
- In **dicot stems**, vascular bundles are arranged in a ring and contain cambium, while **monocot stems** have scattered, closed vascular bundles.
- **Dorsiventral (dicot) leaves** have differentiated mesophyll and stomata mainly on the lower surface, whereas **isobilateral (monocot) leaves** have undifferentiated mesophyll, stomata on both surfaces, and bulliform cells.
- **Secondary growth** increases the girth of dicot stems through the activity of the **vascular cambium** and **cork cambium**, resulting in the formation of secondary tissues, bark, annual rings, and lenticels.
- Knowledge of plant anatomy helps explain the internal organization, growth, transport, and adaptation of flowering plants.
