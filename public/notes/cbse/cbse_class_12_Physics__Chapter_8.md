# Introduction

Electromagnetic waves are waves consisting of oscillating electric and magnetic fields that can travel through vacuum.

They were theoretically predicted by:

\[
\boxed{\text{James Clerk Maxwell}}
\]

Experimental verification was done by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Heinrich Hertz}}
\]

---

# 1. Displacement Current

## Need for Displacement Current

According to Ampere's law, current produces magnetic fields.

However, in a charging capacitor, current flows in wires but no conduction current exists between the plates.

Maxwell introduced the concept of **displacement current**.

---

# Definition

The current produced due to a changing electric field is called displacement current.

Symbol:

\[
I_d
\]

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
I_d=\epsilon_0\frac{d\Phi_E}{dt}
}
\]

where:

- \(\epsilon_0\) = permittivity of free space
- \(\Phi_E\) = electric flux

---

# 2. Modified Ampere's Law

Maxwell modified Ampere's law:

\[
\boxed{
\oint B.dl=
\mu_0(I_c+I_d)
}
\]

where:

- \(I_c\) = conduction current
- \(I_d\) = displacement current

---

# 3. Electromagnetic Waves

## Definition

Electromagnetic waves are waves produced by accelerated charges consisting of time-varying electric and magnetic fields.

---

# Production

A changing electric field produces a magnetic field.

A changing magnetic field produces an electric field.

Together they propagate as electromagnetic waves.

---

# 4. Nature of Electromagnetic Waves

Electromagnetic waves are:

- Transverse waves.
- Do not require a medium.
- Can travel through vacuum.

---

# 5. Characteristics of Electromagnetic Waves

---

## 1. Electric and Magnetic Fields

Electric field:

\[
\vec E
\]

and magnetic field:

\[
\vec B
\]

are perpendicular to each other.

---

They are also perpendicular to direction of propagation.

```
        E

        ↑

        |

Wave →→→→→

        |

        ↓

        B
```

---

## 2. Speed

Speed in vacuum:

\[
\boxed{
c=3\times10^8m/s
}
\]

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
c=\frac1{\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}}
}
\]

---

## 3. No Charge Transport

Electromagnetic waves transfer energy but not charge.

---

# 6. Properties of Electromagnetic Waves

1. Produced by accelerated charges.
2. Travel in vacuum.
3. Speed is same for all EM waves in vacuum.
4. Electric and magnetic fields are in phase.
5. Carry energy and momentum.
6. Show reflection, refraction, interference, and diffraction.

---

# 7. Energy in Electromagnetic Waves

Energy is stored in electric and magnetic fields.

---

Energy density of electric field:

\[
u_E=\frac12\epsilon_0E^2
\]

---

Energy density of magnetic field:

\[
u_B=\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}
\]

---

For electromagnetic waves:

\[
\boxed{
u_E=u_B
}
\]

---

# 9. Radio Waves

## Production

Produced by oscillating charges in antennas.

---

## Uses

- Radio broadcasting
- Television transmission
- Wireless communication

---

# 10. Microwaves

## Properties

Shorter wavelength than radio waves.

---

## Uses

### Radar

Used for:

- Aircraft tracking
- Weather monitoring

---

### Satellite Communication

Used because they can pass through the atmosphere.

---

### Microwave Oven

Microwaves heat food by exciting water molecules.

---

# 16. Maxwell's Theory

Maxwell proposed that:

1. Electric and magnetic fields are related.
2. Changing electric fields produce magnetic fields.
3. Changing magnetic fields produce electric fields.
4. Light is an electromagnetic wave.

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

**Question:** Calculate speed of electromagnetic waves in vacuum.

Given:

\[
\mu_0=4\pi\times10^{-7}
\]

\[
\epsilon_0=8.85\times10^{-12}
\]

Formula:

\[
c=\frac1{\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}}
\]

\[
\boxed{
c=3\times10^8m/s
}
\]

---

## Example 2

**Question:** Why can electromagnetic waves travel through vacuum?

### Solution

They consist of changing electric and magnetic fields that generate each other, so no material medium is required.

---

## Example 3

**Question:** What is displacement current?

### Solution

Displacement current is the current associated with a changing electric field.

\[
I_d=\epsilon_0\frac{d\Phi_E}{dt}
\]

---

## Example 4

**Question:** Which electromagnetic waves are used in radar?

### Solution

Microwaves are used in radar because they can travel long distances and reflect from objects.

---

## Example 5

**Question:** Why are gamma rays dangerous?

### Solution

Gamma rays have very high energy and can ionise atoms, damaging living cells.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing electromagnetic waves with mechanical waves.
- Forgetting that EM waves are transverse.
- Mixing up wavelength and frequency order.
- Forgetting that all EM waves travel at the same speed in vacuum.
- Confusing infrared and ultraviolet applications.
- Forgetting that displacement current exists in charging capacitors.
- Writing visible light as the whole electromagnetic spectrum.
- Confusing X-rays and gamma rays.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Maxwell predicted electromagnetic waves and linked electricity with magnetism.
- Displacement current explains magnetic fields in changing electric fields.
- Electromagnetic waves consist of perpendicular electric and magnetic fields.
- They travel through vacuum at:

\[
3\times10^8m/s
\]

- The electromagnetic spectrum includes radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays.
- Different regions have different applications in communication, medicine, astronomy, and technology.
