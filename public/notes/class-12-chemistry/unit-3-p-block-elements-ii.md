# Introduction

p-Block elements include groups 15, 16, 17 and 18 of the periodic table.

This unit covers:

- Nitrogen and phosphorus family
- Oxygen and sulphur family
- Halogens
- Noble gases
- Important compounds and reactions

# 4. Nitrogen

## Unique Properties

Nitrogen differs from other group members because of:

- Small size
- High electronegativity
- Absence of d-orbitals
- Strong N≡N bond

---

# Nitrogen Molecule

Structure:

\[
\boxed{N\equiv N}
\]

Bond energy:

\[
\approx946\,kJ/mol
\]

---

# 5. Ammonia (NH₃)

## Preparation

### Haber Process

\[
\boxed{
N_2+3H_2
\rightleftharpoons
2NH_3
}
\]

Conditions:

- Temperature: 450°C
- Pressure: 200 atm
- Catalyst: Iron

---

# Structure of Ammonia

Shape:

\[
\boxed{\text{Trigonal pyramidal}}
\]

Hybridisation:

\[
\boxed{sp^3}
\]

---

# Properties of Ammonia

## 1. Basic Nature

\[
NH_3+H_2O
\rightarrow
NH_4^++OH^-
\]

---

## 2. Reaction with HCl

\[
NH_3+HCl
\rightarrow
NH_4Cl
\]

White fumes are formed.

---

## 3. Reducing Property

Ammonia reduces metal oxides:

\[
3CuO+2NH_3
\rightarrow
3Cu+N_2+3H_2O
\]

---

# 6. Nitric Acid (HNO₃)

Prepared by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ostwald Process}}
\]

---

# Steps

## Step 1

Oxidation of ammonia:

\[
4NH_3+5O_2
\rightarrow
4NO+6H_2O
\]

Catalyst:

\[
Pt/Rh
\]

---

## Step 2

Oxidation of NO:

\[
2NO+O_2
\rightarrow
2NO_2
\]

---

## Step 3

Formation of nitric acid:

\[
3NO_2+H_2O
\rightarrow
2HNO_3+NO
\]

---

# Properties of Nitric Acid

## Strong Acid

Ionisation:

\[
HNO_3\rightarrow H^++NO_3^-
\]

---

## Oxidising Property

Example:

\[
Cu+4HNO_3
\rightarrow
Cu(NO_3)_2+2NO_2+2H_2O
\]

---

# 12. Sulphuric Acid (H₂SO₄)

Prepared by:

\[

\]

---

# Steps

## Formation of SO₂

\[
S+O_2
\rightarrow
SO_2
\]

---

## Oxidation

\[
2SO_2+O_2
\rightarrow
2SO_3
\]

\[
V_2O_5

---

\[
SO_3+H_2SO_4
\rightarrow
H_2S_2O_7
\]

---

# Properties

## Acidic Nature

\[

\rightarrow
2H^++SO_4^{2-}
\]

---

## Dehydrating Property

Removes water from organic compounds.

---

## Oxidising Property

Oxidises metals and non-metals.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

Why is nitrogen less reactive than phosphorus?

Answer:

Because nitrogen contains a strong triple bond:

\[
\boxed{N\equiv N}
\]

with high bond energy.

---

## Example 2

Which catalyst is used in contact process?

Answer:

\[
\boxed{V_2O_5}
\]

---

## Example 3

Shape of XeF₄:

Using VSEPR theory:

\[
\boxed{\text{Square planar}}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing Haber and Ostwald processes.
- Forgetting catalyst used in sulphuric acid manufacture.
- Mixing phosphorus allotropes.
- Forgetting ammonia is a weak base.
- Confusing halogens with noble gases.
- Forgetting xenon forms compounds.
- Mixing oxidation states of p-block elements.

---

# Unit Summary

- Group 15 elements contain:

\[
ns^2np^3
\]

- Ammonia is manufactured by Haber process.
- Nitric acid is prepared by Ostwald process.
- Sulphuric acid is produced by Contact process.
- Halogens are highly reactive non-metals.
- Noble gases are mostly inert but xenon forms compounds.

\[
\boxed{\text{p-Block elements show wide variation in chemical behaviour.}}
