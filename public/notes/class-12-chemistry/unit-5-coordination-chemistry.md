# Introduction

Coordination chemistry deals with compounds in which a central metal atom or ion is surrounded by molecules or ions called ligands.

These compounds are important in:

- Biological systems
- Industrial catalysts
- Metallurgy
- Medicine

Examples:

\[
[Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}
\]

\[
[Co(NH_3)_6]Cl_3
\]

---

# PART A: BASIC CONCEPTS OF COORDINATION COMPOUNDS

---

# 1. Coordination Compound

## Definition

A compound containing a central metal atom/ion bonded to surrounding ions or molecules through coordinate bonds is called a coordination compound.

---

General formula:

\[
\boxed{
[M(L)_n]
}
\]

where:

- M = central metal ion
- L = ligand
- n = coordination number

---

# 2. Werner's Theory of Coordination Compounds

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Alfred Werner}}
\]

---

# Main Postulates

---

## 1. Primary Valency

- Equal to oxidation state of metal.
- Ionisable.
- Satisfied by negative ions.

Example:

In:

\[
CoCl_3\cdot6NH_3
\]

Primary valency:

\[
\boxed{+3}
\]

---

## 2. Secondary Valency

- Equal to coordination number.
- Non-ionisable.
- Satisfied by ligands.

For:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}
\]

Secondary valency:

\[
\boxed{6}
\]

---

# 3. Difference Between Double Salts and Coordination Compounds

| Double Salts               | Coordination Compounds |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Completely ionise in water | Retain complex ion     |
| Lose individual identity   | Complex ion remains    |
| Example: Mohr's salt       | Example: K₄[Fe(CN)₆]   |

---

# PART B: TERMINOLOGY

---

# 4. Central Metal Atom/Ion

The atom or ion to which ligands are attached is called the central metal ion.

Example:

\[
[Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}
\]

Central ion:

\[
\boxed{Cu^{2+}}
\]

---

# 5. Ligands

## Definition

An ion or molecule that donates an electron pair to the central metal ion is called a ligand.

---

Examples:

Neutral ligands:

\[
NH_3,\ H_2O,\ CO
\]

---

Negative ligands:

\[
Cl^-,CN^-,OH^-
\]

---

# 6. Types of Ligands

---

# Monodentate Ligands

Donate one electron pair.

Examples:

\[
NH_3,\ Cl^-
\]

---

# Bidentate Ligands

Donate two electron pairs.

Examples:

Ethylene diamine:

\[
en
\]

Oxalate:

\[
C_2O_4^{2-}
\]

---

# Polydentate Ligands

Donate multiple electron pairs.

Example:

EDTA

---

# Chelate

A ring structure formed by multidentate ligand with metal ion is called a chelate.

Example:

Metal-EDTA complex.

---

# 7. Coordination Number

## Definition

Number of ligand donor atoms directly attached to central metal ion.

Example:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}
\]

Coordination number:

\[
\boxed{6}
\]

---

# PART C: NOMENCLATURE OF COORDINATION COMPOUNDS

---

# 8. Rules for Naming Complex Compounds

---

## Rule 1

Name the cation first, followed by anion.

Example:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_6]Cl_3
\]

Hexaamminecobalt(III) chloride

---

## Rule 2

Names of ligands are written before metal name.

---

## Rule 3

Number of ligands:

| Number | Prefix |
| ------ | ------ |
| 2      | Di     |
| 3      | Tri    |
| 4      | Tetra  |
| 5      | Penta  |
| 6      | Hexa   |

---

## Rule 4

Anionic ligands end with "o".

Examples:

\[
Cl^- \rightarrow chloro
\]

\[
CN^- \rightarrow cyano
\]

---

## Rule 5

For anionic complexes, metal name ends with:

\[
\boxed{-ate}
\]

Examples:

Iron:

\[
ferrate
\]

Copper:

\[
cuprate
\]

---

# Examples

---

## Example 1

\[
[Cu(NH_3)_4]SO_4
\]

Name:

\[
\boxed{\text{Tetraamminecopper(II) sulphate}}
\]

---

## Example 2

\[
K_4[Fe(CN)_6]
\]

Name:

\[
\boxed{\text{Potassium hexacyanidoferrate(II)}}
\]

# PART D: OXIDATION NUMBER CALCULATION

---

# 9. Finding Oxidation State

Example:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_6]Cl_3
\]

Let oxidation state of Co = x.

NH₃ is neutral.

\[
x+0=+3
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{x=+3}
\]

---

Example:

\[
[Fe(CN)_6]^{4-}
\]

Let Fe = x.

CN = -1

\[
x+6(-1)=-4
\]

\[
x=+2
\]

---

# PART E: ISOMERISM IN COORDINATION COMPOUNDS

---

# 10. Isomerism

Compounds having same molecular formula but different arrangement are called isomers.

---

Types:

1. Structural isomerism
2. Stereoisomerism

---

# Structural Isomerism

---

## 1. Ionisation Isomerism

Different ions are produced in solution.

Example:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_5Br]SO_4
\]

and

\[
[Co(NH_3)_5SO_4]Br
\]

---

## 2. Hydrate Isomerism

Different number of water molecules inside and outside coordination sphere.

---

## 3. Linkage Isomerism

Ligand attaches through different atoms.

Example:

\[
NO_2^-
\]

Nitro:

\[
-Co-NO_2
\]

Nitrito:

\[
-Co-ONO
\]

---

# Stereoisomerism

---

# 1. Geometrical Isomerism

Different spatial arrangement.

Example:

\[
cis-[Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2]
\]

and

\[
trans-[Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2]
\]

---

# 2. Optical Isomerism

Non-superimposable mirror images.

Example:

\[
[Co(en)_3]^{3+}
\]

---

# PART F: VALENCE BOND THEORY (VBT)

---

# 11. Basic Idea

VBT explains:

- Bond formation
- Hybridisation
- Magnetic behaviour

---

# 12. Hybridisation Types

---

## sp³ Hybridisation

Geometry:

\[
\boxed{\text{Tetrahedral}}
\]

Example:

\[
[NiCl_4]^{2-}
\]

---

## dsp² Hybridisation

Geometry:

\[
\boxed{\text{Square planar}}
\]

Example:

\[
[Ni(CN)_4]^{2-}
\]

---

## d²sp³ Hybridisation

Geometry:

\[
\boxed{\text{Octahedral}}
\]

Example:

\[
[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}
\]

---

# 13. Inner and Outer Orbital Complexes

---

# Inner Orbital Complex

Uses inner d-orbitals.

Example:

\[
d^2sp^3
\]

---

# Outer Orbital Complex

Uses outer d-orbitals.

Example:

\[
sp^3d^2
\]

---

# PART G: CRYSTAL FIELD THEORY (CFT)

---

# 14. Basic Concept

CFT explains:

- Colour
- Magnetic properties
- Stability of complexes

---

Ligands are considered as point charges.

They interact with metal d-orbitals.

---

# 15. Splitting of d-Orbitals

In an octahedral field:

Five d-orbitals split into:

---

Lower energy:

\[
t_{2g}
\]

---

Higher energy:

\[
e_g
\]

---

Energy difference:

\[
\boxed{\Delta_o}
\]

---

# 16. High Spin and Low Spin Complexes

---

# High Spin

- Weak field ligands
- More unpaired electrons

Examples:

\[
F^-,Cl^-
\]

---

# Low Spin

- Strong field ligands
- More pairing

Examples:

\[
CN^-,CO
\]

---

# 17. Spectrochemical Series

Increasing ligand strength:

\[
\boxed{
I^-<Br^-<Cl^-<F^-<OH^-<H_2O<CN^-<CO
}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing ligands and coordination number.
- Forgetting anionic complexes use "-ate" ending.
- Mixing primary and secondary valencies.
- Confusing double salts with coordination compounds.
- Forgetting oxidation state calculation rules.
- Mixing high spin and low spin complexes.
- Confusing geometrical and optical isomerism.
