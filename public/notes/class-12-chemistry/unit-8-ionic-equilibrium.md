# Introduction

Ionic equilibrium deals with the equilibrium between ions present in aqueous solutions.

This unit covers:

- Acids and bases
- Acid-base theories
- Ionisation equilibrium
- pH and pOH calculations
- Buffer solutions
- Solubility product

Applications:

- Biological systems
- Water treatment
- Pharmaceutical chemistry
- Analytical chemistry

---

# PART A: ELECTROLYTES AND IONIC EQUILIBRIUM

---

# 1. Electrolytes

## Definition

Substances that produce ions when dissolved in water and conduct electricity are called electrolytes.

---

Examples:

\[
NaCl\rightarrow Na^+ + Cl^-
\]

\[
HCl\rightarrow H^+ + Cl^-
\]

---

# Types of Electrolytes

---

## Strong Electrolytes

Completely ionise in solution.

Examples:

- HCl
- NaOH
- NaCl

---

## Weak Electrolytes

Partially ionise in solution.

Examples:

- CH₃COOH
- NH₄OH

---

# 2. Ionic Equilibrium

## Definition

The equilibrium established between ions and unionised molecules in a weak electrolyte solution is called ionic equilibrium.

---

Example:

\[
CH_3COOH
\rightleftharpoons
H^+ + CH_3COO^-
\]

---

# PART B: THEORIES OF ACIDS AND BASES

---

# 3. Arrhenius Theory

## Acid

A substance that produces:

\[
H^+
\]

ions in water.

Example:

\[
HCl\rightarrow H^++Cl^-
\]

---

## Base

A substance that produces:

\[
OH^-
\]

ions in water.

Example:

\[
NaOH\rightarrow Na^++OH^-
\]

---

# Limitations

- Applicable only in aqueous solution.
- Cannot explain basic nature of NH₃.

---

# 4. Bronsted-Lowry Theory

## Acid

A proton donor.

Example:

\[
HCl\rightarrow H^++Cl^-
\]

---

## Base

A proton acceptor.

Example:

\[
NH_3+H^+
\rightarrow NH_4^+
\]

---

# Conjugate Acid-Base Pair

When an acid loses H⁺, it forms its conjugate base.

Example:

\[
HA\rightleftharpoons H^++A^-
\]

---

# 5. Lewis Theory

## Lewis Acid

Electron pair acceptor.

Examples:

\[
BF_3,\ AlCl_3
\]

---

## Lewis Base

Electron pair donor.

Examples:

\[
NH_3,\ OH^-
\]

---

# PART C: IONISATION CONSTANTS

---

# 6. Acid Dissociation Constant (Ka)

For weak acid:

\[
HA\rightleftharpoons H^++A^-
\]

---

\[
\boxed{
K_a=
\frac{[H^+][A^-]}{[HA]}
}
\]

---

Higher Ka:

\[
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{Stronger acid}}
\]

---

# 7. Base Dissociation Constant (Kb)

For weak base:

\[
BOH\rightleftharpoons B^++OH^-
\]

---

\[
\boxed{
K_b=
\frac{[B^+][OH^-]}{[BOH]}
}
\]

---

Higher Kb:

\[
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{Stronger base}}
\]

---

# 8. Ionic Product of Water

Water undergoes self-ionisation:

\[
H_2O
\rightleftharpoons
H^++OH^-
\]

---

\[
\boxed{
K_w=[H^+][OH^-]
}
\]

At 25°C:

\[
\boxed{
K_w=1.0\times10^{-14}
}
\]

---

# Relationship:

\[
\boxed{
K_aK_b=K_w
}
\]

---

# PART D: pH AND pOH

---

# 9. pH

## Definition

The negative logarithm of hydrogen ion concentration is called pH.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
pH=-\log[H^+]
}
\]

---

# 10. pOH

\[
\boxed{
pOH=-\log[OH^-]
}
\]

---

# Relation Between pH and pOH

At 25°C:

\[
\boxed{
pH+pOH=14
}
\]

---

# Nature of Solution

| pH  | Nature  |
| --- | ------- |
| <7  | Acidic  |
| =7  | Neutral |
| >7  | Basic   |

---

# 11. pH of Strong Acid

For strong acid:

\[
[H^+]=C
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
pH=-\log C
}
\]

---

# 12. pH of Strong Base

\[
[OH^-]=C
\]

\[
pOH=-\log C
\]

Then:

\[
\boxed{
pH=14-pOH
}
\]

---

# PART E: BUFFER SOLUTIONS

---

# 13. Buffer Solution

## Definition

A solution that resists change in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added is called a buffer solution.

---

# Types of Buffers

---

## 1. Acidic Buffer

Contains:

- Weak acid
- Salt of its conjugate base

Example:

\[
CH_3COOH+CH_3COONa
\]

---

## 2. Basic Buffer

Contains:

- Weak base
- Salt of its conjugate acid

Example:

\[
NH_4OH+NH_4Cl
\]

---

# 14. Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation

For acidic buffer:

\[
\boxed{
pH=pK_a+\log\frac{[Salt]}{[Acid]}
}
\]

---

For basic buffer:

\[
\boxed{
pOH=pK_b+\log\frac{[Salt]}{[Base]}
}
\]

---

# Importance of Buffers

- Blood pH regulation
- Pharmaceutical formulations
- Laboratory solutions

---

# PART F: SOLUBILITY PRODUCT

---

# 15. Solubility Equilibrium

For sparingly soluble salt:

\[
AB
\rightleftharpoons
A^++B^-
\]

---

Equilibrium constant:

\[
\boxed{
K_{sp}=[A^+][B^-]
}
\]

---

# 16. Solubility Product (Ksp)

## Definition

The product of ionic concentrations in a saturated solution is called solubility product.

---

Higher Ksp:

\[
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{Greater solubility}}
\]

---

# Examples

For:

\[
AgCl
\]

\[
AgCl
\rightleftharpoons
Ag^++Cl^-
\]

\[
\boxed{
K_{sp}=[Ag^+][Cl^-]
}
\]

---

For:

\[
CaF_2
\]

\[
CaF_2
\rightleftharpoons
Ca^{2+}+2F^-
\]

\[
\boxed{
K_{sp}=[Ca^{2+}][F^-]^2
}
\]

---

# PART G: COMMON ION EFFECT

---

# 17. Common Ion Effect

## Definition

The suppression of ionisation of a weak electrolyte by adding a strong electrolyte containing a common ion is called common ion effect.

---

Example:

Ionisation of acetic acid:

\[
CH_3COOH
\rightleftharpoons
H^++CH_3COO^-
\]

Addition of sodium acetate:

\[
CH_3COONa
\]

increases:

\[
CH_3COO^-
\]

and decreases ionisation.

---

# Important Formula Sheet

## Acid Constant

\[
K_a=
\frac{[H^+][A^-]}{[HA]}
\]

---

## Base Constant

\[
K_b=
\frac{[B^+][OH^-]}{[BOH]}
\]

---

## Water Constant

\[
K_w=[H^+][OH^-]
\]

---

## Relationship

\[
K_aK_b=K_w
\]

---

## pH

\[
pH=-\log[H^+]
\]

---

## pOH

\[
pOH=-\log[OH^-]
\]

---

## pH-pOH Relation

\[
pH+pOH=14
\]

---

## Buffer Equation

\[
pH=pK_a+\log\frac{Salt}{Acid}
\]

---

## Solubility Product

\[
K_{sp}=[ions]
\]

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

Find pH of:

\[
[H^+]=10^{-3}
\]

Solution:

\[
pH=-\log(10^{-3})
\]

\[
\boxed{pH=3}
\]

---

## Example 2

Find pOH if:

\[
pH=5
\]

Using:

\[
pH+pOH=14
\]

\[
5+pOH=14
\]

\[
\boxed{pOH=9}
\]

---

## Example 3

Write Ksp expression for AgCl.

Reaction:

\[
AgCl
\rightarrow
Ag^++Cl^-
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
K_{sp}=[Ag^+][Cl^-]
}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing Arrhenius and Bronsted theories.
- Forgetting pH uses hydrogen ion concentration.
- Using Celsius instead of correct temperature for Kw.
- Mixing Ka and Kb equations.
- Forgetting:

\[
pH+pOH=14
\]

- Writing incorrect Ksp expressions.
- Confusing buffer solution with normal salt solution.

---

# Unit Summary

- Ionic equilibrium explains behaviour of ions in solution.
- Acids donate H⁺ ions and bases accept H⁺ ions.
- pH measures acidity or basicity.
- Buffers maintain constant pH.
- Henderson-Hasselbalch equation calculates buffer pH.
- Ksp explains solubility of sparingly soluble salts.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Equilibrium controls the behaviour of ions in aqueous solutions.}
}
\]
