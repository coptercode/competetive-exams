# PART A: ALCOHOLS

---

# 1. Alcohols

## Definition

Organic compounds containing hydroxyl group attached to a saturated carbon atom are called alcohols.

General formula:

\[
\boxed{R-OH}
\]

Example:

\[
CH_3OH
\]

(Methanol)

---

# 2. Classification of Alcohols

Alcohols are classified based on the carbon attached to -OH group.

---

# Primary Alcohol (1°)

The carbon containing -OH is attached to one carbon atom.

Example:

\[
CH_3CH_2OH
\]

Ethanol

---

# Secondary Alcohol (2°)

The carbon containing -OH is attached to two carbon atoms.

Example:

\[
CH_3CHOHCH_3
\]

Propan-2-ol

---

# Tertiary Alcohol (3°)

The carbon containing -OH is attached to three carbon atoms.

Example:

\[
(CH_3)_3COH
\]

tert-Butanol

---

# 3. Nomenclature of Alcohols

Rules:

1. Select longest carbon chain containing -OH.
2. Replace "e" with "ol".
3. Number chain to give -OH lowest number.

Examples:

\[
CH_3OH
\]

Methanol

\[
CH_3CH_2OH
\]

Ethanol

---

# PART B: PREPARATION OF ALCOHOLS

---

# 4. Preparation from Alkenes

## Hydration of Alkenes

Alkenes react with water in presence of acid catalyst.

Example:

\[
CH_2=CH_2+H_2O
\rightarrow
CH_3CH_2OH
\]

---

# 5. Preparation Using Grignard Reagent

## Grignard Reagent

General formula:

\[
\boxed{RMgX}
\]

where:

- R = alkyl group
- X = halogen

---

Prepared by:

\[
RX+Mg
\rightarrow
RMgX
\]

in dry ether.

---

# Reaction with Carbonyl Compounds

---

## Formaldehyde

Produces primary alcohol.

\[
RMgX+HCHO
\rightarrow
RCH_2OH
\]

---

## Aldehyde

Produces secondary alcohol.

\[
RMgX+R'CHO
\rightarrow
R'CH(OH)R
\]

---

## Ketone

Produces tertiary alcohol.

\[
RMgX+R'COR''
\rightarrow
R'C(OH)(R)R''
\]

---

# 6. Reduction of Carbonyl Compounds

Aldehydes:

\[
RCHO
\rightarrow
RCH_2OH
\]

Ketones:

\[
RCOR'
\rightarrow
RCHOHR'
\]

Reducing agents:

- LiAlH₄
- NaBH₄

---

# PART C: PHYSICAL PROPERTIES OF ALCOHOLS

---

# 7. Hydrogen Bonding

Alcohol molecules form hydrogen bonds.

Effects:

- Higher boiling point
- Greater solubility in water

---

# Boiling Point Order

\[
\boxed{
Alcohols > Ethers > Hydrocarbons
}
\]

---

# PART D: CHEMICAL REACTIONS OF ALCOHOLS

---

# 8. Reaction with Sodium

Alcohols react with sodium to produce hydrogen gas.

\[
\boxed{
2ROH+2Na
\rightarrow
2RONa+H_2
}
\]

---

# 9. Oxidation of Alcohols

---

## Primary Alcohol

Forms aldehyde:

\[
RCH_2OH
\rightarrow
RCHO
\]

Further oxidation:

\[
RCHO
\rightarrow
RCOOH
\]

---

## Secondary Alcohol

Forms ketone:

\[
RCHOHR'
\rightarrow
RCOR'
\]

---

## Tertiary Alcohol

Resistant to oxidation.

---

# PART E: TESTS FOR ALCOHOLS

---

# 10. Lucas Test

Reagent:

\[
\boxed{ZnCl_2+conc.HCl}
\]

---

Used to distinguish:

- Primary
- Secondary
- Tertiary alcohols

---

Observation:

| Alcohol   | Turbidity         |
| --------- | ----------------- |
| Tertiary  | Immediate         |
| Secondary | Few minutes       |
| Primary   | Slow/no turbidity |

---

# 11. Victor Meyer Test

Used to distinguish primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols.

---

Principle:

Alcohol converted into nitro compound and colour change observed.

---

# PART F: PHENOLS

---

# 12. Phenol

## Definition

Compounds containing -OH group directly attached to aromatic ring are called phenols.

Example:

\[
\boxed{
C_6H_5OH
}
\]

---

# 13. Preparation of Phenol

---

## From Chlorobenzene

Dow process:

\[
C_6H_5Cl+NaOH
\rightarrow
C_6H_5ONa
\]

Acidification:

\[
C_6H_5ONa+HCl
\rightarrow
C_6H_5OH+NaCl
\]

---

## From Benzene Diazonium Salt

\[
C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^-
\rightarrow
C_6H_5OH
\]

---

# 14. Acidity of Phenol

Phenol is more acidic than alcohol because phenoxide ion is resonance stabilised.

---

Reaction:

\[
C_6H_5OH
\rightleftharpoons
C_6H_5O^-+H^+
\]

---

# Acid Strength Order

\[
\boxed{
Phenol > Alcohol
}
\]

---

# 15. Reactions of Phenol

---

# Kolbe Reaction

Phenol reacts with CO₂ to form salicylic acid.

\[
C_6H_5ONa+CO_2
\rightarrow
o-HOC_6H_4COOH
\]

---

# Reimer-Tiemann Reaction

Phenol with chloroform gives salicylaldehyde.

\[
\boxed{
Phenol+CHCl_3
\rightarrow
Salicylaldehyde
}
\]

---

# Electrophilic Substitution

Phenol undergoes:

- Nitration
- Halogenation
- Sulphonation

---

# PART G: ETHERS

---

# 16. Ether

## Definition

Organic compounds containing oxygen atom between two alkyl groups are called ethers.

General formula:

\[
\boxed{
R-O-R'
}
\]

---

Examples:

\[
CH_3-O-CH_3
\]

Dimethyl ether

---

# 17. Preparation of Ethers

---

# Williamson Ether Synthesis

## Definition

Preparation of ethers by reaction of sodium alkoxide with alkyl halide.

---

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
RONa+R'X
\rightarrow
ROR'+NaX
}
\]

---

Example:

\[
C_2H_5ONa+C_2H_5Br
\rightarrow
C_2H_5OC_2H_5+NaBr
\]

---

# 18. Cleavage of Ethers

Ethers react with hydrogen halides.

---

Example:

\[
ROR'+HI
\rightarrow
RI+R'OH
\]

---

With excess HI:

\[
ROR'+2HI
\rightarrow
RI+R'I+H_2O
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing alcohols and phenols.
- Forgetting phenol has -OH directly attached to benzene.
- Mixing primary, secondary and tertiary alcohol reactions.
- Forgetting Grignard reagent requires dry ether.
- Confusing Williamson synthesis with ether cleavage.
- Forgetting phenol acidity is due to resonance stabilisation.
- Mixing Lucas test observations.

---

# Unit Summary

- Alcohols contain hydroxyl groups attached to saturated carbon.
- Grignard reagents are important for alcohol synthesis.
- Phenols are more acidic than alcohols due to resonance.
- Williamson synthesis prepares ethers.
- Ethers undergo cleavage with HI/HBr.

\[
\boxed{
R-OH,\ C_6H_5OH,\ R-O-R'
}
\]

represent alcohols, phenols and ethers respectively.
