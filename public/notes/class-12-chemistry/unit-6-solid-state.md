# Introduction

Solid state chemistry deals with the study of the structure, properties and behaviour of solids.

Solids are classified based on:

- Arrangement of particles
- Nature of bonding
- Crystal structure

This unit covers:

- Crystalline and amorphous solids
- Unit cells
- Crystal packing
- Density calculations
- Crystal defects

---

# PART A: CLASSIFICATION OF SOLIDS

---

# 1. Solids

## Definition

A solid is a state of matter having:

- Definite shape
- Definite volume
- Strong intermolecular forces

---

# Types of Solids

Solids are classified into:

1. Crystalline solids
2. Amorphous solids

---

# 2. Crystalline Solids

## Definition

Solids in which atoms, ions or molecules are arranged in a regular repeating pattern are called crystalline solids.

---

Examples:

- NaCl
- Diamond
- Quartz

---

# Properties

- Definite geometrical shape
- Sharp melting point
- Anisotropic nature
- Long-range order

---

# 3. Amorphous Solids

## Definition

Solids without a regular arrangement of particles are called amorphous solids.

---

Examples:

- Glass
- Rubber
- Plastic

---

# Properties

- No definite shape
- Softening over a range of temperature
- Isotropic nature
- Short-range order

---

# Difference Between Crystalline and Amorphous Solids

| Crystalline         | Amorphous          |
| ------------------- | ------------------ |
| Regular arrangement | Random arrangement |
| Sharp melting point | Melt over range    |
| Anisotropic         | Isotropic          |
| Long-range order    | Short-range order  |

# PART C: CRYSTAL LATTICE AND UNIT CELL

---

# 5. Crystal Lattice

## Definition

A regular three-dimensional arrangement of points representing atoms or ions is called crystal lattice.

---

# 6. Unit Cell

## Definition

The smallest repeating unit of a crystal lattice is called unit cell.

---

# Parameters of Unit Cell

A unit cell is described by:

- Edge lengths:

\[
a,b,c
\]

- Angles:

\[
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
\]

---

# 7. Types of Cubic Unit Cells

There are three common cubic structures:

1. Simple cubic
2. Body-centred cubic (BCC)
3. Face-centred cubic (FCC)

---

# PART D: CUBIC STRUCTURES

---

# 8. Simple Cubic Structure

Atoms are present only at corners.

---

Number of atoms per unit cell:

\[
\boxed{1}
\]

Calculation:

8 corners × 1/8

\[
=1
\]

---

# Coordination Number

\[
\boxed{6}
\]

---

# Packing Efficiency

\[
\boxed{52.4\%}
\]

---

# 9. Body Centred Cubic (BCC)

Atoms:

- Eight corners
- One atom at centre

---

Number of atoms:

\[
8(\frac18)+1
\]

\[
\boxed{2}
\]

---

Coordination number:

\[
\boxed{8}
\]

---

Packing efficiency:

\[
\boxed{68\%}
\]

---

Examples:

- Iron
- Sodium
- Potassium

---

# 10. Face Centred Cubic (FCC)

Atoms:

- Eight corners
- Six face centres

---

Number of atoms:

\[
8(\frac18)+6(\frac12)
\]

\[
\boxed{4}
\]

---

Coordination number:

\[
\boxed{12}
\]

---

Packing efficiency:

\[
\boxed{74\%}
\]

---

Examples:

- Copper
- Aluminium
- Silver

---

# Comparison of Cubic Structures

| Structure    | Atoms/unit cell | Coordination number | Packing efficiency |
| ------------ | --------------- | ------------------- | ------------------ |
| Simple cubic | 1               | 6                   | 52.4%              |
| BCC          | 2               | 8                   | 68%                |
| FCC          | 4               | 12                  | 74%                |

# PART F: DENSITY OF UNIT CELL

---

# 13. Density Formula

Density of crystal:

\[
\boxed{
\rho=
\frac{ZM}{a^3N_A}
}
\]

where:

- Z = number of atoms per unit cell
- M = molar mass
- a = edge length
- \(N_A\) = Avogadro number

---

# PART G: CRYSTAL DEFECTS

---

# 14. Crystal Defects

## Definition

Irregularities in the arrangement of particles in a crystal are called crystal defects.

---

Types:

1. Stoichiometric defects
2. Non-stoichiometric defects
3. Impurity defects

---

# PART H: STOICHIOMETRIC DEFECTS

---

# 15. Schottky Defect

## Definition

A defect caused by equal number of cations and anions missing from their lattice positions is called Schottky defect.

---

Properties:

- Density decreases.
- Electrical neutrality maintained.

---

Examples:

\[
NaCl
\]

\[
KCl
\]

\[
CsCl
\]

---

# 16. Frenkel Defect

## Definition

A defect in which an ion leaves its normal position and occupies an interstitial site is called Frenkel defect.

---

Properties:

- Density remains unchanged.
- Usually shown by smaller ions.

---

Examples:

\[
AgCl
\]

\[
ZnS
\]

---

# Difference Between Schottky and Frenkel Defects

| Schottky           | Frenkel             |
| ------------------ | ------------------- |
| Ions leave lattice | Ion shifts position |
| Vacancy defect     | Dislocation defect  |
| Density decreases  | Density unchanged   |
| NaCl               | AgCl                |

---

# PART I: NON-STOICHIOMETRIC DEFECTS

---

# 17. Metal Excess Defect

Metal atoms or electrons are present in excess.

---

Types:

1. Anion vacancy
2. Extra cation occupying interstitial site

---

# F-centres

Electrons trapped in anion vacancies are called F-centres.

They produce colour in crystals.

Example:

NaCl becomes yellow.

---

# 18. Metal Deficiency Defect

Occurs when some metal ions are missing.

Example:

FeO

Find number of atoms in FCC unit cell.

FCC contains:

Corners:

\[
8\times\frac18=1
\]

Faces:

\[
6\times\frac12=3
\]

Total:

\[
1+3
\]

\[
\boxed{4\text{ atoms}}
\]

## Example 2

Which defect decreases density?

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Schottky defect}}
\]

---

Answer:

\[

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing crystalline and amorphous solids.
- Forgetting atoms shared between unit cells.
- Mixing BCC and FCC properties.
- Forgetting FCC has maximum packing efficiency.
- Confusing Schottky and Frenkel defects.
- Forgetting density formula.
- Mixing tetrahedral and octahedral voids.

---

# Unit Summary

- Crystalline solids have ordered structures.
- Unit cells are repeating units of crystal lattices.
- Cubic structures include simple cubic, BCC and FCC.
- FCC has maximum packing efficiency.
- Crystal defects affect physical properties.
- Schottky defect decreases density.
- Frenkel defect maintains density.
