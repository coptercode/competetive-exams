# Introduction

Biomolecules are organic compounds produced by living organisms that are essential for life processes.

Major biomolecules:

1. Carbohydrates
2. Proteins
3. Nucleic acids
4. Vitamins
5. Enzymes

This unit covers:

- Classification of carbohydrates
- Structure of glucose and fructose
- Amino acids and proteins
- Peptide bonds
- DNA and RNA structure

Applications:

- Nutrition
- Genetics
- Medicine
- Biotechnology

---

# PART A: CARBOHYDRATES

---

# 1. Carbohydrates

## Definition

Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or compounds that produce them on hydrolysis.

General formula:

\[
\boxed{C_x(H_2O)_y}
\]

---

Examples:

- Glucose
- Fructose
- Sucrose
- Starch

---

# 2. Classification of Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are classified into:

1. Monosaccharides
2. Oligosaccharides
3. Polysaccharides

---

# 3. Monosaccharides

## Definition

Carbohydrates that cannot be hydrolysed into simpler sugars are called monosaccharides.

---

Examples:

- Glucose
- Fructose
- Ribose

---

# Based on Functional Group

---

## Aldoses

Contain aldehyde group:

\[
\boxed{-CHO}
\]

Example:

Glucose

---

## Ketoses

Contain ketone group:

\[
\boxed{>C=O}
\]

Example:

Fructose

---

# 4. Oligosaccharides

Carbohydrates that produce 2–10 monosaccharide units on hydrolysis.

Examples:

- Sucrose
- Lactose
- Maltose

---

# 5. Polysaccharides

Large carbohydrates containing many monosaccharide units.

Examples:

- Starch
- Cellulose
- Glycogen

---

# PART B: GLUCOSE

---

# 6. Glucose

Formula:

\[
\boxed{C_6H_{12}O_6}
\]

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Dextrose}}
\]

---

# 7. Open Chain Structure of Glucose

Glucose contains:

- One aldehyde group
- Five hydroxyl groups

Structure:

\[
CHO-(CHOH)_4-CH_2OH
\]

---

# 8. Cyclic Structure of Glucose

Glucose exists mainly in cyclic form.

Two forms:

1. α-D-glucose
2. β-D-glucose

---

# Anomeric Carbon

The carbon atom formed during ring closure is called anomeric carbon.

---

Difference:

α-form:

\[
OH
\]

below plane

---

β-form:

\[
OH
\]

above plane

---

# 9. Mutarotation

## Definition

The change in optical rotation due to interconversion of α and β forms of glucose is called mutarotation.

---

# PART C: FRUCTOSE

---

# 10. Fructose

Formula:

\[
\boxed{C_6H_{12}O_6}
\]

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Fruit sugar}}
\]

---

Functional group:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ketone group}}
\]

Therefore fructose is:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ketohexose}}
\]

---

# Cyclic Structure

Fructose forms a five-membered ring structure called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Fructofuranose}}
\]

---

# PART D: DISACCHARIDES

---

# 11. Disaccharides

Two monosaccharide units joined by glycosidic linkage form disaccharides.

---

# 12. Sucrose

Components:

\[
\boxed{
Glucose+Fructose
}
\]

---

Linkage:

\[
\boxed{
\alpha(1\rightarrow2)\beta
}
\]

---

Properties:

- Non-reducing sugar
- Common table sugar

---

# 13. Maltose

Components:

\[
\boxed{
Glucose+Glucose
}
\]

---

Linkage:

\[
\alpha(1\rightarrow4)
\]

---

Properties:

- Reducing sugar

---

# 14. Lactose

Components:

\[
\boxed{
Galactose+Glucose
}
\]

---

Source:

Milk sugar

---

Properties:

- Reducing sugar

---

# Comparison of Disaccharides

| Sugar   | Components          | Reducing nature |
| ------- | ------------------- | --------------- |
| Sucrose | Glucose + Fructose  | Non-reducing    |
| Maltose | Glucose + Glucose   | Reducing        |
| Lactose | Galactose + Glucose | Reducing        |

---

# PART E: POLYSACCHARIDES

---

# 15. Starch

Storage carbohydrate in plants.

Made of:

\[
\boxed{\alpha\text{-glucose}}
\]

Components:

1. Amylose
2. Amylopectin

---

# 16. Cellulose

Structural carbohydrate in plants.

Made of:

\[
\boxed{\beta\text{-glucose}}
\]

---

# 17. Glycogen

Storage carbohydrate in animals.

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Animal starch}}
\]

---

# PART F: AMINO ACIDS

---

# 18. Amino Acids

## Definition

Organic compounds containing amino group and carboxyl group are called amino acids.

General structure:

\[
\boxed{
NH_2-CHR-COOH
}
\]

---

# Types of Amino Acids

---

## Neutral Amino Acids

Equal number of:

- NH₂ groups
- COOH groups

Example:

Glycine

---

## Acidic Amino Acids

More COOH groups.

Example:

Glutamic acid

---

## Basic Amino Acids

More NH₂ groups.

Example:

Lysine

---

# 19. Zwitter Ion

Amino acids exist as dipolar ions containing:

\[
NH_3^+
\]

and

\[
COO^-
\]

groups.

---

# PART G: PROTEINS

---

# 20. Proteins

## Definition

Proteins are polymers of amino acids linked by peptide bonds.

---

# 21. Peptide Bond

The bond formed between amino acids by elimination of water is called peptide bond.

---

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
-COOH+NH_2
\rightarrow
-CONH-+H_2O
}
\]

---

# 22. Classification of Proteins

---

## Fibrous Proteins

Properties:

- Insoluble
- Structural function

Examples:

- Keratin
- Collagen

---

## Globular Proteins

Properties:

- Soluble
- Functional role

Examples:

- Enzymes
- Insulin

---

# PART H: STRUCTURE OF PROTEINS

---

# 23. Primary Structure

Sequence of amino acids in a protein chain.

---

# 24. Secondary Structure

Arrangement of polypeptide chain by hydrogen bonding.

Types:

1. α-helix
2. β-sheet

---

# 25. Tertiary Structure

Three-dimensional folding of protein chain.

---

# 26. Quaternary Structure

Arrangement of multiple polypeptide chains.

Example:

Haemoglobin

---

# 27. Denaturation of Proteins

## Definition

Loss of biological activity due to change in protein structure is called denaturation.

---

Causes:

- Heat
- pH change
- Chemicals

---

Example:

Boiling of egg.

---

# PART I: ENZYMES

---

# 28. Enzymes

## Definition

Enzymes are biological catalysts that increase the rate of biochemical reactions.

---

Properties:

- Highly specific
- Work at mild conditions
- Not consumed during reaction

---

# Lock and Key Model

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Emil Fischer}}
\]

---

Concept:

Enzyme active site fits substrate like:

\[
\boxed{\text{Lock and key}}
\]

---

# PART J: NUCLEIC ACIDS

---

# 29. Nucleic Acids

Biomolecules responsible for storage and transfer of genetic information.

Types:

1. DNA
2. RNA

---

# 30. Nucleotides

Basic units of nucleic acids.

Each nucleotide contains:

1. Nitrogen base
2. Sugar
3. Phosphate group

---

# 31. DNA

Full form:

\[
\boxed{\text{Deoxyribonucleic Acid}}
\]

---

Components:

Sugar:

\[
\text{Deoxyribose}
\]

Bases:

- Adenine (A)
- Thymine (T)
- Guanine (G)
- Cytosine (C)

---

# 32. Double Helix Structure

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{
Watson\ and\ Crick
}
\]

---

Features:

- Two polynucleotide chains
- Hydrogen bonding between bases

---

Base Pairing:

\[
\boxed{
A=T
}
\]

(two hydrogen bonds)

---

\[
\boxed{
G\equiv C
}
\]

(three hydrogen bonds)

---

# 33. RNA

Full form:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ribonucleic Acid}}
\]

---

Sugar:

\[
\text{Ribose}
\]

Bases:

- Adenine
- Uracil
- Guanine
- Cytosine

---

Types:

1. mRNA
2. tRNA
3. rRNA

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Peptide bond}}

\boxed{\text{Uracil}}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing glucose and fructose structures.
- Forgetting sucrose is non-reducing.
- Mixing peptide bond and glycosidic bond.
- Confusing DNA and RNA sugars.
- Forgetting DNA base pairing.
- Mixing primary and secondary protein structures.
- Forgetting enzymes are biological catalysts.

- RNA helps in protein synthesis.
