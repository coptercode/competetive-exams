# Introduction

Carbonyl compounds are organic compounds containing the carbonyl group:

\[
\boxed{C=O}
\]

They include:

- Aldehydes
- Ketones

Carboxylic acids contain:

\[
\boxed{-COOH}
\]

group.

This unit covers:

- Preparation of aldehydes and ketones
- Nucleophilic addition reactions
- Aldol condensation
- Cannizzaro reaction
- Acidity of carboxylic acids
- Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky reaction

Applications:

- Perfumes
- Medicines
- Polymers
- Organic synthesis

# PART A: ALDEHYDES AND KETONES

---

# 1. Carbonyl Compounds

## Definition

Organic compounds containing carbonyl group are called carbonyl compounds.

---

# Types

## Aldehydes

General formula:

\[
\boxed{R-CHO}
\]

Carbonyl carbon is attached to at least one hydrogen.

Example:

\[
CH_3CHO
\]

Ethanal

---

## Ketones

General formula:

\[
\boxed{R-CO-R'}
\]

Carbonyl carbon is attached to two carbon groups.

Example:

\[
CH_3COCH_3
\]

Propanone

---

# 2. Nomenclature

---

## Aldehydes

Suffix:

\[
\boxed{-al}
\]

Examples:

\[
CH_3CHO
\]

Ethanal

---

## Ketones

Suffix:

\[
\boxed{-one}
\]

Examples:

\[
CH_3COCH_3
\]

Propanone

---

# PART B: PREPARATION OF ALDEHYDES AND KETONES

---

# 3. Oxidation of Alcohols

---

## Primary Alcohol

Produces aldehyde:

\[
\boxed{
RCH_2OH
\rightarrow
RCHO
}
\]

---

## Secondary Alcohol

Produces ketone:

\[
\boxed{
RCHOHR'
\rightarrow
RCOR'
}
\]

---

# 4. Rosenmund Reduction

## Definition

Reduction of acid chlorides to aldehydes using poisoned palladium catalyst is called Rosenmund reduction.

---

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
RCOCl+H_2
\rightarrow
RCHO+HCl
}
\]

Catalyst:

\[
Pd/BaSO_4
\]

---

# 5. Stephen Reaction

## Definition

Reduction of nitriles to aldehydes using stannous chloride and HCl is called Stephen reaction.

---

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
RCN
\rightarrow
RCHO
}
\]

Reagents:

\[
SnCl_2+HCl
\]

---

# 6. Etard Reaction

## Definition

Oxidation of methyl benzene to benzaldehyde using chromyl chloride is called Etard reaction.

---

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
C_6H_5CH_3
\rightarrow
C_6H_5CHO
}
\]

Reagent:

\[
CrO_2Cl_2
\]

---

# PART C: NUCLEOPHILIC ADDITION REACTIONS

---

# 7. Nucleophilic Addition

## Definition

The addition of nucleophile to carbonyl carbon is called nucleophilic addition reaction.

---

Reason:

Carbonyl carbon is electrophilic due to:

\[
C^{\delta+}=O^{\delta-}
\]

---

# 8. Addition of Hydrogen Cyanide

Aldehydes and ketones react with HCN to form cyanohydrins.

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
RCHO+HCN
\rightarrow
RCH(OH)CN
}
\]

---

# 9. Addition of Alcohols

Carbonyl compounds react with alcohols to form acetals.

---

Aldehyde:

\[
RCHO+2ROH
\rightarrow
RCH(OR)_2+H_2O
\]

---

# 10. Addition of Ammonia Derivatives

Carbonyl compounds react with:

- Hydroxylamine
- Hydrazine
- Semicarbazide

to form derivatives.

Examples:

Oxime:

\[

> C=NOH
> \]

Hydrazone:

\[

> C=NNH_2
> \]

---

# PART D: IMPORTANT CHEMICAL TESTS

---

# 11. Tollens' Test

## Principle

Aldehydes reduce silver ions to metallic silver.

---

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Silver mirror formation}
}
\]

---

Used to distinguish:

\[
\boxed{\text{Aldehydes}}
\]

from ketones.

---

# 12. Fehling's Test

Aldehydes produce:

\[
\boxed{\text{Brick red precipitate}}
\]

of copper(I) oxide.

---

# 13. Iodoform Test

Compounds containing:

\[
CH_3CO-
\]

group give yellow precipitate of iodoform.

---

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
CHI_3
}
\]

---

Positive for:

- Ethanal
- Methyl ketones
- Ethanol

---

# PART E: ALDOL CONDENSATION

---

# 14. Aldol Condensation

## Definition

Aldehydes or ketones containing alpha hydrogen undergo condensation in presence of dilute base to form aldol.

---

Example:

\[
2CH_3CHO
\rightarrow
CH_3CH(OH)CH_2CHO
\]

---

Product:

\[
\boxed{\text{Aldol}}
\]

---

# Alpha Hydrogen

Hydrogen attached to carbon adjacent to carbonyl group.

---

# PART F: CANNIZZARO REACTION

---

# 15. Cannizzaro Reaction

## Definition

Aldehydes without alpha hydrogen undergo self oxidation and reduction in presence of strong base.

---

Example:

\[
2HCHO+NaOH
\rightarrow
CH_3OH+HCOONa
\]

---

Products:

- Alcohol
- Carboxylate salt

---

# PART G: CARBOXYLIC ACIDS

---

# 16. Carboxylic Acid

## Definition

Organic compounds containing:

\[
\boxed{-COOH}
\]

group are called carboxylic acids.

---

General formula:

\[
\boxed{RCOOH}
\]

---

Examples:

Formic acid:

\[
HCOOH
\]

Acetic acid:

\[
CH_3COOH
\]

---

# 17. Preparation of Carboxylic Acids

---

## Oxidation of Alcohols

Primary alcohol:

\[
RCH_2OH
\rightarrow
RCOOH
\]

---

## Hydrolysis of Nitriles

\[
RCN+H_2O
\rightarrow
RCOOH
\]

---

# 18. Acidity of Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids are acidic because carboxylate ion is resonance stabilised.

---

Reaction:

\[
RCOOH
\rightleftharpoons
RCOO^-+H^+
\]

---

# Acid Strength Order

Electron withdrawing groups increase acidity.

Example:

\[
ClCH_2COOH

>

CH_3COOH
\]

---

# 19. Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky (HVZ) Reaction

## Definition

Alpha halogenation of carboxylic acids in presence of halogen and phosphorus is called HVZ reaction.

---

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
RCH_2COOH
\rightarrow
RCHXCOOH
}
\]

Reagents:

\[
Br_2/P
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing aldehydes and ketones.
- Forgetting aldehydes give Tollens test.
- Mixing Aldol and Cannizzaro reactions.
- Forgetting alpha hydrogen requirement.
- Confusing carboxylic acids with alcohols.
- Forgetting HVZ requires alpha hydrogen.
- Mixing preparation reactions of aldehydes.

---

# Unit Summary

- Aldehydes and ketones contain carbonyl groups.
- Carbonyl compounds undergo nucleophilic addition reactions.
- Aldehydes can be identified by Tollens and Fehling tests.
- Aldol reaction requires alpha hydrogen.
- Cannizzaro reaction occurs in aldehydes without alpha hydrogen.
- Carboxylic acids show acidity due to resonance stabilisation.
