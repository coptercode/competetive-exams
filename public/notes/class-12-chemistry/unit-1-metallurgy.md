# Introduction

Metallurgy is the branch of chemistry that deals with:

- Extraction of metals from ores
- Purification of metals
- Preparation of alloys

The extraction process depends on:

- Nature of ore
- Reactivity of metal
- Physical and chemical properties

---

# PART A: OCCURRENCE OF METALS

---

# 1. Minerals and Ores

## Mineral

A naturally occurring substance containing metal compounds is called a mineral.

---

## Ore

A mineral from which metal can be extracted economically is called an ore.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Ore = Mineral + Economic extraction possibility}
}
\]

---

# 2. Gangue

## Definition

The unwanted earthy impurities present along with ore are called gangue.

Examples:

- Sand
- Clay
- Rock particles

---

# 3. Steps in Metallurgy

The major steps are:

1. Concentration of ore
2. Extraction of crude metal
3. Refining of metal

---

# PART B: CONCENTRATION OF ORES

---

# 4. Concentration of Ore

## Definition

The removal of gangue from ore is called concentration or dressing of ore.

---

Methods:

1. Gravity separation
2. Froth flotation
3. Electromagnetic separation

---

# 5. Gravity Separation Method

## Principle

Based on the difference in density between ore particles and impurities.

---

## Process

- Ore is crushed.
- Powdered ore is washed with water.
- Heavy ore particles settle down.
- Lighter impurities are removed.

---

## Used For

Oxide ores of:

- Tin
- Lead

Example:

Cassiterite:

\[
SnO_2
\]

---

# 6. Froth Flotation Method

## Principle

Based on difference in wettability of ore and gangue particles.

---

Used for:

\[
\boxed{\text{Sulphide ores}}
\]

Examples:

- Zinc blende:

\[
ZnS
\]

- Copper pyrites:

\[
CuFeS_2
\]

---

# Process

1. Ore powder mixed with water.
2. Pine oil added.
3. Air is passed.
4. Ore particles attach to froth.
5. Gangue settles down.

---

# Role of Reagents

## Collector

Increases wettability of ore particles.

Example:

Xanthates

---

## Frother

Produces stable froth.

Example:

Pine oil

---

## Depressant

Prevents unwanted ore from floating.

Example:

NaCN

---

# 7. Electromagnetic Separation

## Principle

Based on difference in magnetic properties of ore and impurities.

---

Used for:

- Magnetic ores

Example:

Magnetite:

\[
Fe_3O_4
\]

---

# PART C: EXTRACTION OF CRUDE METAL

---

# 8. Conversion of Ore into Oxide

Most metals are extracted from oxide ores.

Methods:

1. Calcination
2. Roasting

---

# 9. Calcination

## Definition

Heating ore strongly in limited or absence of air is called calcination.

---

Used for:

- Carbonate ores
- Hydrated ores

---

Example:

Zinc carbonate:

\[
ZnCO_3
\]

On heating:

\[
\boxed{
ZnCO_3
\rightarrow
ZnO+CO_2
}
\]

---

# 10. Roasting

## Definition

Heating sulphide ores in excess air is called roasting.

---

Example:

Zinc sulphide:

\[
ZnS
\]

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
2ZnS+3O_2
\rightarrow
2ZnO+2SO_2
}
\]

---

# Difference Between Calcination and Roasting

| Calcination    | Roasting      |
| -------------- | ------------- |
| Limited/no air | Excess air    |
| Carbonate ores | Sulphide ores |
| CO₂ removed    | SO₂ produced  |

---

# 11. Reduction of Metal Oxides

Metal oxide is converted into metal by removing oxygen.

---

Methods:

1. Carbon reduction
2. Hydrogen reduction
3. Electrolytic reduction

---

# Carbon Reduction

Used for moderately reactive metals.

Example:

\[
ZnO+C
\rightarrow
Zn+CO
\]

---

# Hydrogen Reduction

Used for metals forming stable oxides.

Example:

\[
CuO+H_2
\rightarrow
Cu+H_2O
\]

---

# Electrolytic Reduction

Used for highly reactive metals.

Examples:

- Sodium
- Magnesium
- Aluminium

---

# 12. Ellingham Diagram

## Definition

A graph showing variation of Gibbs energy with temperature for formation of metal oxides is called Ellingham diagram.

---

Applications:

- Predict feasibility of reduction reactions.
- Select reducing agents.

---

# PART D: REFINING OF METALS

---

# 13. Refining

## Definition

The process of purifying crude metal is called refining.

---

Methods:

1. Zone refining
2. Mond process
3. Van Arkel method

---

# 14. Zone Refining

## Principle

Based on difference in solubility of impurities in molten and solid states.

---

Process:

- A circular heater moves along impure metal rod.
- Impurities dissolve in molten zone.
- Pure metal crystallises.

---

Used for:

Highly pure elements:

- Silicon
- Germanium

---

# 15. Mond Process

## Principle

Formation and decomposition of volatile metal carbonyls.

---

Used for:

\[
\boxed{\text{Nickel purification}}
\]

---

Steps:

Formation:

\[
Ni+4CO
\rightarrow
Ni(CO)_4
\]

---

Decomposition:

\[
Ni(CO)_4
\rightarrow
Ni+4CO
\]

---

# 16. Van Arkel Method

## Principle

Formation and decomposition of volatile iodides.

---

Used for purification of:

- Titanium
- Zirconium

---

Reaction:

Formation:

\[
Zr+2I_2
\rightarrow
ZrI_4
\]

---

Decomposition:

\[
ZrI_4
\rightarrow
Zr+2I_2
\]

---

# PART E: EXTRACTION OF IMPORTANT METALS

---

Ore:

\[
Al_2O_3
\]

---

Steps:

1. Purification of bauxite.
2. Electrolysis of alumina.

---

Electrolysis:

Cathode:

\[
Al^{3+}+3e^-
\rightarrow Al
\]

---

# 18. Extraction of Iron

Ore:

\[
Fe_2O_3
\]

---

Blast furnace reactions:

Reduction:

\[
Fe_2O_3+3CO
\rightarrow
2Fe+3CO_2
\]

---

# 19. Extraction of Copper

Ore:

\[
CuFeS_2
\]

Steps:

- Roasting

- Electrolytic refining

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing minerals and ores.
- Using roasting for carbonate ores.
- Using calcination for sulphide ores.
- Forgetting gangue removal before extraction.
- Confusing Mond process and Van Arkel method.
- Forgetting that zone refining gives very high purity.
- Mixing extraction and refining steps.

---

# Unit Summary

- Metallurgy involves extraction and purification of metals.
- Ores are concentrated by physical methods.
- Sulphide ores are concentrated by froth flotation.
- Carbonate ores undergo calcination.
- Sulphide ores undergo roasting.
- Metals are extracted by reduction methods.
- Refining removes impurities from crude metals.

Important concepts:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ore → Concentration → Extraction → Refining → Pure Metal}}
\]
