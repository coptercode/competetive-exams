# Introduction

The elements in which the last electron enters the **p-orbital** are called p-block elements.

They are placed in groups:

- Group 13: Boron family
- Group 14: Carbon family
- Group 15: Nitrogen family
- Group 16: Oxygen family
- Group 17: Halogens
- Group 18: Noble gases

This unit focuses on:

- Group 13 elements
- Group 14 elements
- Their important compounds and properties

---

# PART A: GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS OF p-BLOCK ELEMENTS

---

# 1. Electronic Configuration

General configuration:

\[
\boxed{
ns^2np^{1-6}
}
\]

---

# 2. Variation Across a Group

Moving down a group:

### Atomic size

\[
\boxed{\text{Increases}}
\]

Reason:

Addition of new electron shells.

---

### Ionisation energy

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases}}
\]

Reason:

Increase in atomic size.

---

### Metallic character

\[
\boxed{\text{Increases}}
\]

---

### Electronegativity

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases}}
\]

---

# 4. General Electronic Configuration

\[

ns^2np^1

---

# 5. Oxidation States

Common oxidation states:

\[
\boxed{+3,\ +1}
\]

---

The stability of +1 oxidation state increases down the group due to:

\[
\boxed{\text{Inert pair effect}}
\]

---

# 6. Inert Pair Effect

## Definition

The reluctance of s-electrons to participate in bonding is called inert pair effect.

---

Example:

Thallium:

\[
Tl^{+}
\]

is more stable than:

\[
Tl^{3+}
\]

---

# 7. Anomalous Behaviour of Boron

Boron differs from other group members because:

- Small size
- High ionisation energy
- Absence of d-orbitals

---

Properties of Boron:

- Non-metal
- Hard
- Poor conductor

---

# 8. Aluminium

Properties:

- Metallic nature
- Good conductor
- Forms amphoteric oxide

---

Aluminium oxide:

\[
Al_2O_3
\]

reacts with acids:

\[
Al_2O_3+6HCl
\rightarrow
2AlCl_3+3H_2O
\]

---

With bases:

\[
Al_2O_3+2NaOH
\rightarrow
2NaAlO_2+H_2O
\]

# PART C: IMPORTANT BORON COMPOUNDS

---

# 9. Diborane (B₂H₆)

## Definition

Diborane is an important hydride of boron.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{B_2H_6}
\]

---

# Preparation

From boron halides:

\[
2BF_3+6NaH
\rightarrow
B_2H_6+6NaF
\]

---

# Structure of Diborane

Diborane contains:

- 4 terminal hydrogen atoms
- 2 bridging hydrogen atoms

---

It has:

\[
\boxed{\text{Three-centre two-electron bonds}}
\]

---

# Properties of Diborane

## 1. Hydrolysis

\[
B_2H_6+6H_2O
\rightarrow
2H_3BO_3+6H_2
\]

---

## 2. Reaction with ammonia

Forms:

\[
\boxed{B_3N_3H_6}
\]

(Borazine)

Different forms of the same element are called allotropes.

---

Structure:

- Three-dimensional network
- sp³ hybridisation

- Extremely hard
- Non-conductor

---

Structure:

- Layered structure
- sp² hybridisation

- Conducts electricity

\]

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

What is the oxidation state of boron in boric acid?

Compound:

\[
H_3BO_3
\]

Let oxidation state of B = x.

\[
3(+1)+x+3(-2)=0
\]

\[
3+x-6=0
\]

\[
x=+3
\]

Answer:

\[
\boxed{B=+3}
\]

---

## Example 2

Why does graphite conduct electricity?

Because:

\[
\boxed{\text{It contains delocalised electrons}}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing group 13 and group 14 configurations.
- Forgetting inert pair effect in heavier elements.
- Mixing diamond and graphite properties.
- Forgetting boron is a non-metal.
- Confusing silicate structures.
- Forgetting diborane contains three-centre bonds.
- Mixing silicone and silicon.
