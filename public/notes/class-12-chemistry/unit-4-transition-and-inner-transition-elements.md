# Introduction

The elements placed between the **s-block and p-block elements** in the periodic table are called transition elements.

They include:

- d-block elements
- f-block elements

This unit covers:

- General properties of transition elements
- Variable oxidation states
- Magnetic properties
- Catalytic activity
- Lanthanides
- Actinides
- Lanthanide contraction

---

# PART A: d-BLOCK ELEMENTS

---

# 1. Definition of d-Block Elements

Elements in which the differentiating electron enters the d-orbital are called d-block elements.

They are placed in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Groups 3 to 12}}
\]

---

# 2. General Electronic Configuration

\[
\boxed{
(n-1)d^{1-10}ns^{0-2}
}
\]

---

# 3. Transition Elements

## Definition

Elements having partially filled d-orbitals in their atoms or ions are called transition elements.

Examples:

- Iron (Fe)
- Copper (Cu)
- Chromium (Cr)

---

# 4. Exceptions

Some d-block elements are not transition elements.

Example:

### Zinc

Electronic configuration:

\[
Zn=[Ar]3d^{10}4s^2
\]

Since d-orbitals are completely filled:

\[
\boxed{\text{Zn is not a transition element}}
\]

---

# PART B: GENERAL PROPERTIES OF TRANSITION ELEMENTS

---

# 5. Metallic Character

Transition elements show:

- High melting points
- High boiling points
- High density
- Good electrical conductivity

Reason:

Strong metallic bonding due to participation of d-electrons.

---

# 6. Variable Oxidation States

## Definition

Transition elements show different oxidation states because electrons from both:

- ns orbital
- (n-1)d orbital

participate in bonding.

---

Examples:

## Iron

\[
Fe^{2+},Fe^{3+}
\]

---

## Chromium

\[
Cr^{2+},Cr^{3+},Cr^{6+}
\]

---

# Common Oxidation States

| Element | Oxidation States |
| ------- | ---------------- |
| Sc      | +3               |
| Ti      | +2,+3,+4         |
| V       | +2,+3,+4,+5      |
| Mn      | +2,+3,+4,+6,+7   |
| Fe      | +2,+3            |

---

# 7. Coloured Ions

Transition metal ions are often coloured due to:

\[
\boxed{\text{d-d electronic transitions}}
\]

---

Examples:

\[
Cu^{2+}
\]

Blue colour

---

\[
Fe^{3+}
\]

Yellow/brown colour

---

Elements with completely filled or empty d-orbitals are generally colourless.

Examples:

\[
Zn^{2+}
\]

---

# 8. Magnetic Properties

Transition elements show magnetism due to unpaired electrons.

---

# Paramagnetism

Substances attracted by magnetic field.

Reason:

Presence of unpaired electrons.

---

Magnetic moment:

\[
\boxed{
\mu=\sqrt{n(n+2)}BM
}
\]

where:

n = number of unpaired electrons

---

# Diamagnetism

Substances containing only paired electrons.

---

# 9. Catalytic Activity

Transition metals act as catalysts because of:

1. Variable oxidation states
2. Formation of intermediate compounds
3. Surface adsorption

---

Examples:

### Iron

Used in Haber process:

\[
N_2+3H_2
\rightarrow2NH_3
\]

---

### Vanadium pentoxide

Used in Contact process:

\[
SO_2\rightarrow SO_3
\]

---

# 10. Formation of Complex Compounds

Transition metals form complexes due to:

- Small size
- High charge density
- Availability of vacant orbitals

Examples:

\[
[Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}
\]

\[
[Fe(CN)_6]^{3-}
\]

---

# 11. Alloy Formation

Transition metals form alloys because:

- Similar atomic sizes
- Similar crystal structures

Examples:

## Steel

Iron + Carbon

---

## Brass

Copper + Zinc

---

## Bronze

Copper + Tin

---

# 12. Interstitial Compounds

## Definition

Compounds formed when small atoms occupy spaces between metal atoms are called interstitial compounds.

---

Examples:

\[
TiC
\]

\[
Fe_3C
\]

---

Properties:

- Hard
- High melting point
- Maintain metallic conductivity

---

# PART C: f-BLOCK ELEMENTS

---

# 13. Definition

Elements in which the differentiating electron enters f-orbital are called f-block elements.

They are divided into:

1. Lanthanides
2. Actinides

---

# PART D: LANTHANIDES

---

# 14. Lanthanides

Elements from:

\[
\boxed{Ce(58)\text{ to }Lu(71)}
\]

are called lanthanides.

---

# General Configuration

\[
\boxed{
[Xe]4f^{1-14}5d^{0-1}6s^2
}
\]

---

# 15. Oxidation States

Most common:

\[
\boxed{+3}
\]

---

Some show:

\[
+2,\ +4
\]

---

# 16. Lanthanide Contraction

## Definition

The gradual decrease in atomic and ionic radii of lanthanides with increase in atomic number is called lanthanide contraction.

---

# Cause

Poor shielding effect of:

\[
\boxed{4f\text{ electrons}}
\]

---

# Consequences of Lanthanide Contraction

---

## 1. Similarity Between Elements

Lanthanides have similar properties.

---

## 2. Difficulty in Separation

Due to similar ionic sizes.

---

## 3. Similarity Between Zr and Hf

Because of nearly equal sizes.

---

# 17. Properties of Lanthanides

- Silvery metals
- Electropositive
- Reactive
- Form coloured ions
- Show magnetic behaviour

---

# PART E: ACTINIDES

---

# 18. Actinides

Elements from:

\[
\boxed{Th(90)\text{ to }Lr(103)}
\]

are called actinides.

---

# General Configuration

\[
\boxed{
[Rn]5f^{1-14}6d^{0-1}7s^2
}
\]

---

# 19. Oxidation States

Actinides show:

\[
\boxed{+3\text{ to }+7}
\]

oxidation states.

---

# 20. Properties of Actinides

- All are radioactive.
- More variable oxidation states.
- Form complexes.
- Synthetic elements are included.

---

# Difference Between Lanthanides and Actinides

| Lanthanides               | Actinides              |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| 4f filling                | 5f filling             |
| Mostly +3 oxidation state | +3 to +7 states        |
| Less radioactive          | All radioactive        |
| Less complex formation    | More complex formation |

\boxed{\text{d-d electronic transitions}}

# Common Mistakes

- Considering Zn as a transition element.
- Forgetting the reason for variable oxidation states.
- Confusing paramagnetic and diamagnetic substances.
- Mixing lanthanides and actinides.
- Forgetting lanthanide contraction cause.
- Using wrong magnetic moment formula.
- Confusing d-block and f-block configurations.

---

# Unit Summary

- Transition elements belong to the d-block.
- They show variable oxidation states, coloured ions and magnetic behaviour.
- Transition metals form alloys, complexes and interstitial compounds.
- Lanthanides involve 4f orbitals.
- Actinides involve 5f orbitals.
- Lanthanide contraction occurs due to poor shielding by 4f electrons.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Transition elements show unique properties due to partially filled d and f orbitals.}
}
\]
