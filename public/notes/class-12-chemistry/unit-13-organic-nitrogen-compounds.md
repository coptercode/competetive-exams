# PART A: AMINES

---

# 1. Amines

## Definition

Organic compounds derived from ammonia by replacing one or more hydrogen atoms with alkyl or aryl groups are called amines.

General formula:

\[
\boxed{R-NH_2}
\]

---

# 2. Classification of Amines

Amines are classified based on the number of alkyl/aryl groups attached to nitrogen.

---

# Primary Amine (1°)

One carbon group attached to nitrogen.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{R-NH_2}
\]

Example:

\[
CH_3NH_2
\]

Methylamine

---

# Secondary Amine (2°)

Two carbon groups attached to nitrogen.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{R_2NH}
\]

Example:

\[
(CH_3)_2NH
\]

Dimethylamine

---

# Tertiary Amine (3°)

Three carbon groups attached to nitrogen.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{R_3N}
\]

Example:

\[
(CH_3)_3N
\]

Trimethylamine

---

# 3. Nomenclature of Amines

Rules:

1. Name alkyl groups attached to nitrogen.
2. Add suffix:

\[
\boxed{-amine}
\]

Examples:

\[
CH_3NH_2
\]

Methylamine

---

\[
C_2H_5NH_2
\]

Ethylamine

---

# PART B: PREPARATION OF AMINES

---

# 4. Reduction of Nitro Compounds

Nitro compounds are reduced to primary amines.

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
RNO_2+3H_2
\rightarrow
RNH_2+2H_2O
}
\]

Reducing agents:

- Sn/HCl
- Fe/HCl

---

# 5. Ammonolysis of Alkyl Halides

Alkyl halides react with ammonia to form amines.

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
RX+NH_3
\rightarrow
RNH_2+HX
}
\]

---

# 6. Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis

## Definition

A method used to prepare primary amines using phthalimide is called Gabriel synthesis.

---

Reaction:

Potassium phthalimide reacts with alkyl halide:

\[
C_6H_4(CO)_2NK+RX
\]

forms:

\[
C_6H_4(CO)_2NR
\]

Hydrolysis gives:

\[
\boxed{RNH_2}
\]

---

# Importance

Produces:

\[
\boxed{\text{Pure primary amines}}
\]

---

# 7. Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation

## Definition

Conversion of amide into primary amine with one carbon atom less is called Hoffmann bromamide reaction.

---

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
RCONH_2+Br_2+4NaOH
\rightarrow
RNH_2+Na_2CO_3+NaBr+2H_2O
}
\]

---

Example:

\[
CH_3CONH_2
\rightarrow
CH_3NH_2
\]

---

# PART C: BASICITY OF AMINES

---

# 8. Basic Nature of Amines

Amines are basic because nitrogen has a lone pair of electrons.

Reaction:

\[
RNH_2+H^+
\rightarrow
RNH_3^+
\]

---

# 9. Factors Affecting Basicity

---

## 1. Electron Releasing Groups

Alkyl groups increase electron density on nitrogen.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Basicity increases}}
\]

---

## 2. Resonance Effect

In aniline, lone pair is delocalised into benzene ring.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Basicity decreases}}
\]

---

# Basic Strength Order

---

## Gaseous State

\[
\boxed{
3^\circ >2^\circ>1^\circ>NH_3
}
\]

---

## Aqueous State

Due to solvation:

\[
\boxed{
2^\circ>1^\circ>3^\circ>NH_3
}
\]

---

# 10. Aniline

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
C_6H_5NH_2
}
\]

---

Aniline is less basic than alkyl amines because:

\[
\boxed{\text{Lone pair participates in resonance}}
\]

---

# PART D: CHEMICAL TESTS OF AMINES

---

# 11. Carbylamine Test

## Principle

Primary amines react with chloroform and alcoholic KOH to form isocyanides.

---

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
RNH_2+CHCl_3+3KOH
\rightarrow
RNC+3KCl+3H_2O
}
\]

---

Observation:

Bad smell due to isocyanide.

---

Positive for:

\[
\boxed{\text{Primary amines only}}
\]

---

# 12. Hinsberg Test

Reagent:

\[
\boxed{C_6H_5SO_2Cl}
\]

(Benzenesulphonyl chloride)

---

Used to distinguish:

- Primary amines
- Secondary amines
- Tertiary amines

---

# 13. Azo Dye Test

Primary aromatic amines form coloured azo dyes.

Example:

Aniline gives orange dye.

---

# PART E: DIAZONIUM SALTS

---

# 14. Diazonium Salt

## Definition

Compounds containing:

\[
\boxed{-N_2^+X^-}
\]

group are called diazonium salts.

---

Example:

\[
\boxed{
C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^-
}
\]

Benzene diazonium chloride

---

# 15. Preparation of Diazonium Salts

## Diazotisation

Aniline reacts with nitrous acid at low temperature.

Temperature:

\[
\boxed{0-5^\circ C}
\]

---

Reaction:

\[
\boxed{
C_6H_5NH_2+NaNO_2+2HCl
\rightarrow
C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^-+NaCl+2H_2O
}
\]

---

# PART F: REACTIONS OF DIAZONIUM SALTS

---

# 16. Sandmeyer Reaction

## Definition

Replacement of diazonium group by halogen using copper salts.

---

Example:

\[
C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^-
\rightarrow
C_6H_5Cl
\]

Reagent:

\[
CuCl
\]

---

Products:

- Chlorobenzene
- Bromobenzene
- Cyanobenzene

---

# 17. Gattermann Reaction

Diazonium salt reacts with copper powder and acid.

---

Example:

\[
C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^-
\rightarrow
C_6H_5Cl
\]

---

# 18. Coupling Reaction

## Definition

Diazonium salts react with phenols or aromatic amines to form azo compounds.

---

Example:

\[
Diazonium\ salt+Phenol
\rightarrow
Azo\ dye
\]

---

# Applications of Diazonium Salts

- Preparation of dyes
- Preparation of aromatic compounds
- Organic synthesis

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing primary, secondary and tertiary amines.
- Forgetting Gabriel synthesis gives only primary amines.
- Mixing Hoffmann bromamide with Gabriel reaction.
- Forgetting diazotisation temperature (0–5°C).
- Confusing Sandmeyer and Gattermann reactions.
- Forgetting carbylamine test is only for primary amines.
- Mixing gaseous and aqueous basicity order.

---

# Unit Summary

- Amines are nitrogen-containing organic compounds.
- They are classified as primary, secondary and tertiary amines.
- Gabriel synthesis prepares primary amines.
- Hoffmann bromamide converts amides into amines.
- Amines show basic behaviour due to nitrogen lone pair.
- Diazonium salts are important intermediates in aromatic chemistry.
- Sandmeyer and Gattermann reactions prepare substituted aromatic compounds.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Amines are essential compounds in biological and industrial chemistry.}
}
