# Introduction

Chemistry is the branch of science that studies the composition, structure, properties and transformations of matter.

This chapter introduces the fundamental concepts required for chemical calculations.

Topics covered:

- Classification of matter
- Atomic and molecular concepts
- Mole concept
- Molar mass
- Chemical equations
- Stoichiometry
- Limiting reagent
- Equivalent mass

---

# PART A: CLASSIFICATION OF MATTER

---

# 1. Matter

## Definition

Anything that has mass and occupies space is called matter.

---

Examples:

- Water
- Air
- Metals
- Minerals

---

# 2. Classification of Matter

Matter is classified into:

1. Pure substances
2. Mixtures

---

# PART B: PURE SUBSTANCES

---

# 3. Pure Substance

## Definition

A substance having fixed composition and definite properties is called a pure substance.

---

Types:

1. Elements
2. Compounds

---

# 4. Elements

## Definition

A substance that cannot be broken into simpler substances by chemical methods is called an element.

---

Examples:

- Hydrogen (H)
- Oxygen (O)
- Iron (Fe)

---

Types:

### Metals

Examples:

- Iron
- Copper
- Sodium

---

### Non-metals

Examples:

- Carbon
- Sulphur
- Oxygen

---

### Metalloids

Examples:

- Silicon
- Boron

---

# 5. Compounds

## Definition

A compound is a pure substance formed by chemical combination of two or more elements in a fixed ratio.

---

Examples:

Water:

\[
H_2O
\]

Carbon dioxide:

\[
CO_2
\]

---

Properties:

- Fixed composition
- New properties different from elements
- Can be separated chemically

---

# PART C: MIXTURES

---

# 6. Mixture

## Definition

A mixture is a combination of two or more substances physically mixed together.

---

Properties:

- Variable composition
- Components retain their properties
- Can be separated physically

---

# Types of Mixtures

---

## 1. Homogeneous Mixture

Uniform composition throughout.

Examples:

- Salt solution
- Air

---

## 2. Heterogeneous Mixture

Non-uniform composition.

Examples:

- Sand and water
- Oil and water

---

# PART D: ATOMIC MASS AND MOLECULAR MASS

---

# 7. Atomic Mass

## Definition

The relative mass of an atom compared with 1/12th mass of carbon-12 atom is called atomic mass.

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{u}
\]

---

Examples:

Hydrogen:

\[
1u
\]

Oxygen:

\[
16u
\]

Carbon:

\[
12u
\]

---

# 8. Molecular Mass

## Definition

The sum of atomic masses of all atoms present in a molecule.

---

Example:

Water:

\[
H_2O
\]

\[
=2(1)+16
\]

\[
\boxed{18u}
\]

---

# 9. Formula Mass

Used for ionic compounds.

Example:

NaCl:

\[
=23+35.5
\]

\[
\boxed{58.5u}
\]

---

# PART E: MOLE CONCEPT

---

# 10. Mole

## Definition

A mole is the amount of substance containing \(6.022\times10^{23}\) particles.

---

Avogadro number:

\[
\boxed{
N_A=6.022\times10^{23}
}
\]

---

One mole may contain:

- Atoms
- Molecules
- Ions
- Electrons

---

# 11. Molar Mass

## Definition

The mass of one mole of a substance is called molar mass.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Molar\ mass=
\frac{Mass}{Number\ of\ moles}
}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{g/mol}
\]

---

# 12. Number of Moles

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
n=\frac{Given\ mass}{Molar\ mass}
}
\]

---

# 13. Number of Particles

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Number\ of\ particles
=

n\times N_A
}
\]

---

# PART F: PERCENTAGE COMPOSITION

---

# 14. Percentage Composition

The percentage of each element present in a compound is called percentage composition.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\%\ of\ element=
\frac{Mass\ of\ element}
{Molecular\ mass}
\times100
}
\]

---

Example:

Water:

Molecular mass:

\[
18
\]

Hydrogen mass:

\[
2
\]

Percentage:

\[
\frac2{18}\times100
\]

\[
\boxed{11.11\%}
\]

---

# PART G: CHEMICAL EQUATIONS

---

# 15. Chemical Equation

## Definition

A symbolic representation of a chemical reaction is called a chemical equation.

---

Example:

\[
H_2+O_2\rightarrow H_2O
\]

---

# 16. Balancing Chemical Equations

According to the law of conservation of mass:

Atoms cannot be created or destroyed.

Number of atoms on both sides must be equal.

---

Example:

Unbalanced:

\[
H_2+O_2\rightarrow H_2O
\]

Balanced:

\[
\boxed{
2H_2+O_2\rightarrow2H_2O
}
\]

---

# PART H: STOICHIOMETRY

---

# 17. Stoichiometry

## Definition

The quantitative relationship between reactants and products in a chemical reaction is called stoichiometry.

---

Based on:

- Balanced chemical equation
- Mole ratios

---

Example:

\[
2H_2+O_2\rightarrow2H_2O
\]

Ratio:

\[
2:1:2
\]

---

# 18. Limiting Reagent

## Definition

The reactant that gets completely consumed first and limits product formation is called limiting reagent.

---

Example:

If:

\[
2H_2+O_2\rightarrow2H_2O
\]

If hydrogen is insufficient:

Hydrogen is limiting reagent.

---

# 19. Excess Reagent

The reactant left after completion of reaction is called excess reagent.

---

# PART I: EQUIVALENT MASS

---

# 20. Equivalent Mass

## Definition

Equivalent mass is the mass of a substance that combines with or replaces one equivalent of another substance.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Equivalent\ mass=
\frac{Molar\ mass}{n-factor}
}
\]

---

# 21. Equivalent Mass of Acids

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Eq.\ mass=
\frac{Molecular\ mass}
{Basicity}
}
\]

---

Examples:

HCl:

Basicity = 1

\[
Eq.mass=36.5
\]

---

H₂SO₄:

Basicity = 2

\[
Eq.mass=
\frac{98}{2}
\]

\[
=49
\]

---

# 22. Equivalent Mass of Bases

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Eq.mass=
\frac{Molecular\ mass}
{Acidity}
}
\]

---

Example:

NaOH:

\[
Eq.mass=40
\]

---

# 23. Equivalent Mass of Salts

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Eq.mass=
\frac{Molar\ mass}
{Total\ positive\ charge}
}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing atomic mass and molecular mass.
- Forgetting Avogadro number.
- Not balancing equations before calculations.
- Using incorrect molar mass.
- Confusing limiting and excess reagents.
- Forgetting n-factor in equivalent mass calculations.
- Using Celsius values in chemical calculations.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Matter is classified into pure substances and mixtures.
- Elements and compounds are pure substances.
- Mole concept connects microscopic particles with measurable quantities.
- Stoichiometry helps calculate reactant and product quantities.
- Limiting reagent determines maximum product formation.
- Equivalent mass is useful in acid-base and redox calculations.

\[
\boxed{
\text{The mole concept is the foundation of chemical calculations.}
