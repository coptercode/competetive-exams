# Introduction

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of all living organisms. All organisms are made up of cells, and all life processes occur within cells.

This chapter covers:

- Cell theory
- Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells
- Plant and animal cells
- Cell envelope
- Endomembrane system
- Cell organelles
- Cytoskeleton
- Nucleus

---

# PART A: CELL THEORY

---

# 1. Cell

## Definition

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.

---

Examples:

- Bacteria → Single cell
- Humans → Multicellular organism

---

# 2. Cell Theory

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Matthias Schleiden and Theodor Schwann}}
\]

---

# Statements of Cell Theory

1. All living organisms are made up of cells.
2. Cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
3. All new cells arise from pre-existing cells.

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Rudolf Virchow}}
\]

---

# PART B: TYPES OF CELLS

---

# 3. Prokaryotic Cell

## Definition

Cells without a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles are called prokaryotic cells.

---

Examples:

- Bacteria
- Cyanobacteria

---

Characteristics:

- Nuclear membrane absent.
- DNA present in nucleoid region.
- Ribosomes are present.
- Simple cell structure.

---

# 4. Eukaryotic Cell

## Definition

Cells having a true nucleus surrounded by a nuclear membrane are called eukaryotic cells.

---

Examples:

- Plants
- Animals
- Fungi

---

Characteristics:

- True nucleus present.
- Membrane-bound organelles present.
- Complex organisation.

---

# Difference Between Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

| Prokaryotic Cell             | Eukaryotic Cell             |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| No true nucleus              | True nucleus present        |
| No membrane-bound organelles | Organelles present          |
| Smaller in size              | Larger in size              |
| Example: Bacteria            | Example: Plants and animals |

---

# PART C: PLANT CELL AND ANIMAL CELL

---

# 5. Plant Cell

Characteristics:

- Cell wall present.
- Chloroplast present.
- Large central vacuole.
- Fixed shape.

---

# 6. Animal Cell

Characteristics:

- Cell wall absent.
- Chloroplast absent.
- Small vacuoles.
- Irregular shape.

---

# PART D: CELL ENVELOPE

---

# 7. Cell Envelope

The outer covering of a cell is called the cell envelope.

---

In prokaryotes:

Components:

1. Glycocalyx
2. Cell wall
3. Plasma membrane

---

# 8. Cell Membrane

## Definition

A thin selectively permeable membrane surrounding the cell is called the cell membrane.

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Plasma membrane}}
\]

---

Functions:

- Controls movement of substances.
- Protects cell contents.
- Maintains internal environment.

---

# 9. Fluid Mosaic Model

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Singer and Nicolson}}
\]

---

Features:

- Lipid bilayer.
- Proteins embedded in lipids.
- Flexible structure.

---

# 10. Cell Wall

## Definition

A rigid outer covering present outside the plasma membrane in plants is called cell wall.

---

Main component:

\[
\boxed{\text{Cellulose}}
\]

---

Functions:

- Protection.
- Mechanical support.
- Maintains shape.

---

# PART E: ENDOMEMBRANE SYSTEM

---

# 11. Endomembrane System

A group of membrane-bound organelles working together is called the endomembrane system.

---

Includes:

1. Endoplasmic reticulum
2. Golgi apparatus
3. Lysosomes
4. Vacuoles

---

# 12. Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)

## Definition

A network of membrane-bound tubules and sacs involved in transport and synthesis.

---

Types:

---

## Rough ER

Contains:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ribosomes}}
\]

Function:

- Protein synthesis

---

## Smooth ER

Function:

- Lipid synthesis
- Detoxification

---

# 13. Golgi Apparatus

Discovered by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Camillo Golgi}}
\]

---

Structure:

- Flattened membrane sacs called cisternae.

---

Functions:

- Modification of proteins.
- Packaging and secretion.
- Formation of lysosomes.

---

# 14. Lysosomes

## Definition

Membrane-bound organelles containing digestive enzymes.

---

Called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Suicide bags of the cell}}
\]

---

Functions:

- Intracellular digestion.
- Removal of damaged organelles.

---

# 15. Vacuoles

## Function:

- Storage of water.
- Storage of food.
- Removal of wastes.

---

Plant cells contain:

\[
\boxed{\text{Large central vacuole}}
\]

---

# PART F: CELL ORGANELLES

---

# 16. Mitochondria

## Definition

Double membrane-bound organelle responsible for energy production.

---

Called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Powerhouse of the cell}}
\]

---

Function:

Produces ATP.

---

# 17. Ribosomes

## Definition

Non-membranous organelles responsible for protein synthesis.

---

Types:

- 70S ribosomes → Prokaryotes
- 80S ribosomes → Eukaryotic cytoplasm

---

# 18. Plastids

Present in plant cells.

---

Types:

## Chloroplast

Function:

Photosynthesis

---

## Chromoplast

Function:

Provides colour to flowers and fruits.

---

## Leucoplast

Function:

Storage of food.

---

# 19. Microbodies

Small membrane-bound organelles containing enzymes.

---

Examples:

- Peroxisomes

---

Functions:

- Breakdown of fatty acids.
- Detoxification.

---

# PART G: CYTOSKELETON AND CELL MOVEMENT

---

# 20. Cytoskeleton

## Definition

A network of protein filaments that provides shape and support to the cell.

---

Components:

1. Microtubules
2. Microfilaments
3. Intermediate filaments

---

Functions:

- Maintains cell shape.
- Helps movement of organelles.
- Helps cell division.

---

# 21. Cilia and Flagella

---

## Cilia

Short hair-like structures used for movement.

Example:

Respiratory tract cells

---

## Flagella

Long whip-like structures used for movement.

Example:

Sperm cells

---

# 22. Centrioles

Present mainly in animal cells.

---

Functions:

- Help in spindle formation during cell division.

---

# PART H: NUCLEUS

---

# 23. Nucleus

## Definition

The control centre of the cell containing genetic material is called nucleus.

---

Discovered by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Robert Brown}}
\]

---

# 24. Structure of Nucleus

Parts:

1. Nuclear envelope
2. Nucleoplasm
3. Nucleolus
4. Chromatin

---

# 25. Nuclear Envelope

Double membrane surrounding the nucleus.

---

Function:

Controls exchange of materials between nucleus and cytoplasm.

---

# 26. Nucleolus

Function:

- Formation of ribosomes.

---

# 27. Chromatin

## Definition

A network of DNA and proteins present inside nucleus is called chromatin.

---

During cell division:

Chromatin forms:

\[
\boxed{\text{Chromosomes}}
\]

---

# Important Differences

## Plant Cell vs Animal Cell

| Plant Cell          | Animal Cell        |
| ------------------- | ------------------ |
| Cell wall present   | Cell wall absent   |
| Chloroplast present | Chloroplast absent |
| Large vacuole       | Small vacuoles     |
| Fixed shape         | Irregular shape    |

---

## Rough ER vs Smooth ER

| Rough ER          | Smooth ER        |
| ----------------- | ---------------- |
| Ribosomes present | Ribosomes absent |
| Protein synthesis | Lipid synthesis  |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.
- Forgetting mitochondria produce ATP.
- Mixing ER and Golgi functions.
- Forgetting ribosomes are non-membranous.
- Confusing cell wall with cell membrane.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Cell is the basic unit of life.
- Cells are classified into prokaryotic and eukaryotic types.
- Cell membrane controls movement of substances.
- Endomembrane system includes ER, Golgi bodies, lysosomes and vacuoles.
- Organelles perform specialised functions.
- Nucleus controls cellular activities and contains genetic material.

\[
