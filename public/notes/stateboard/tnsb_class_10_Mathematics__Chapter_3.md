# Introduction

Algebra is the branch of mathematics that uses symbols and variables to represent numbers and relationships.

This chapter covers:

- Simultaneous linear equations
- Polynomial division
- GCD and LCM of polynomials
- Rational expressions
- Quadratic equations
- Matrices and operations

---

# PART A: SIMULTANEOUS LINEAR EQUATIONS

---

# 1. Linear Equation

## Definition

An equation in which the highest power of the variable is 1 is called a linear equation.

---

General form:

\[
\boxed{
ax+by+c=0
}
\]

where:

- a, b, c are constants
- x, y are variables

---

# 2. Linear Equations in Three Variables

General form:

\[
\boxed{
ax+by+cz=d
}
\]

---

Example:

\[
2x+y+3z=10
\]

---

A system of three equations can be solved to find:

\[
x,\ y,\ z
\]

---

# 3. Methods of Solving Linear Equations

---

## Method 1: Elimination Method

Steps:

1. Choose two equations.
2. Eliminate one variable.
3. Repeat for remaining variables.
4. Substitute values back.

---

## Method 2: Substitution Method

Steps:

1. Express one variable in terms of others.
2. Substitute into remaining equations.
3. Solve.

---

## Method 3: Matrix Method

Equations are represented using matrices.

---

# 4. Consistency of Equations

A system may have:

---

## Unique Solution

One solution exists.

---

## No Solution

Lines do not meet.

---

## Infinite Solutions

Same line represented by different equations.

---

# PART B: POLYNOMIALS

---

# 5. Polynomial

## Definition

An algebraic expression containing variables with non-negative integer powers is called a polynomial.

---

Example:

\[
x^3+2x^2-5x+7
\]

---

# 6. Division Algorithm for Polynomials

Similar to division of numbers.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Dividend=Divisor\times Quotient+Remainder
}
\]

---

Example:

\[
p(x)=g(x)q(x)+r(x)
\]

---

# 7. GCD of Polynomials

## Definition

The greatest common divisor of two or more polynomials is the polynomial of highest degree that divides all given polynomials.

---

Example:

Find GCD of:

\[
x^2-1
\]

and

\[
x^2-x
\]

Factor:

\[
x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)
\]

\[
x^2-x=x(x-1)
\]

Common factor:

\[
\boxed{x-1}
\]

---

# 8. LCM of Polynomials

## Definition

The least common multiple of polynomials is the polynomial of lowest degree divisible by all given polynomials.

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
GCD\times LCM=
Product\ of\ polynomials
}
\]

---

# PART C: RATIONAL EXPRESSIONS

---

# 9. Rational Expression

## Definition

An expression written as a ratio of two polynomials is called a rational expression.

---

Form:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}
}
\]

where:

\[
q(x)\neq0
\]

---

Example:

\[
\frac{x+2}{x-3}
\]

---

# 10. Simplification of Rational Expressions

Steps:

1. Factor numerator.
2. Factor denominator.
3. Cancel common factors.
4. Write simplified form.

---

Example:

\[
\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}
\]

Factor:

\[
=\frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2}
\]

Cancel:

\[
\boxed{x+2}
\]

---

# PART D: QUADRATIC EQUATIONS

---

# 11. Quadratic Equation

## Definition

An equation having highest power of variable equal to 2 is called a quadratic equation.

---

General form:

\[
\boxed{
ax^2+bx+c=0
}
\]

where:

\[
a\neq0
\]

---

# 12. Methods of Solving Quadratic Equations

---

## Method 1: Factorisation

Example:

\[
x^2-5x+6=0
\]

Factor:

\[
(x-2)(x-3)=0
\]

Solutions:

\[
\boxed{x=2,3}
\]

---

## Method 2: Quadratic Formula

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}
}
\]

---

# 13. Nature of Roots

Determined by discriminant:

\[
\boxed{
D=b^2-4ac
}
\]

---

## Case 1:

\[
D>0
\]

Two distinct real roots.

---

## Case 2:

\[
D=0
\]

Equal roots.

---

## Case 3:

\[
D<0
\]

No real roots.

---

# 14. Relationship Between Roots

For:

\[
ax^2+bx+c=0
\]

Roots:

\[
\alpha,\beta
\]

---

Sum of roots:

\[
\boxed{
\alpha+\beta=-\frac ba
}
\]

---

Product of roots:

\[
\boxed{
\alpha\beta=\frac ca
}
\]

---

# 15. Graphical Representation

A quadratic equation forms a parabola.

---

Graph:

\[
y=ax^2+bx+c
\]

---

Roots are the points where the graph cuts the x-axis.

---

# PART E: MATRICES

---

# 16. Matrix

## Definition

A rectangular arrangement of numbers in rows and columns is called a matrix.

---

Example:

\[
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2\\
3&4
\end{bmatrix}
\]

---

# 17. Order of Matrix

If a matrix has:

m rows

and

n columns

Order:

\[
\boxed{m\times n}
\]

---

Example:

\[
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2&3\\
4&5&6
\end{bmatrix}
\]

Order:

\[
\boxed{2\times3}
\]

---

# 18. Types of Matrices

---

## Row Matrix

One row.

Example:

\[
[1\ 2\ 3]
\]

---

## Column Matrix

One column.

Example:

\[
\begin{bmatrix}
1\\2\\3
\end{bmatrix}
\]

---

## Square Matrix

Number of rows = number of columns.

---

## Zero Matrix

All elements are zero.

---

## Identity Matrix

Diagonal elements are 1.

Example:

\[
I=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0\\
0&1
\end{bmatrix}
\]

---

# 19. Matrix Operations

---

# Addition

Possible only when matrices have same order.

\[
\boxed{
A+B
}
\]

Add corresponding elements.

---

# Subtraction

Subtract corresponding elements.

---

# Multiplication

Condition:

Number of columns of first matrix = number of rows of second matrix.

---

Example:

\[
(2\times3)(3\times4)
\]

Result:

\[
2\times4
\]

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Solve:

\[
x^2-7x+12=0
\]

Factor:

\[
(x-3)(x-4)=0
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{x=3,4}
\]

---

## Example 2

Find nature of roots:

\[
x^2+4x+5=0
\]

Here:

\[
a=1,b=4,c=5
\]

\[
D=4^2-4(1)(5)
\]

\[
=16-20=-4
\]

Since:

\[
D<0
\]

No real roots.

---

## Example 3

Find order of matrix:

\[
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2&3\\
4&5&6
\end{bmatrix}
\]

Rows = 2

Columns = 3

Order:

\[
\boxed{2\times3}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting quadratic coefficient \(a\neq0\).
- Using wrong sign in quadratic formula.
- Confusing sum and product of roots.
- Adding matrices of different orders.
- Forgetting multiplication order condition.
- Cancelling factors without considering restrictions.
