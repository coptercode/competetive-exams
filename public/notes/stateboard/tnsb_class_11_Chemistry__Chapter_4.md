# Introduction

Hydrogen is the first element in the periodic table and the lightest element known.

It has unique properties because it can behave like:

- Alkali metals
- Halogens

Hydrogen is important in:

- Fuel technology
- Chemical industries
- Hydrogenation processes
- Nuclear reactions

This chapter covers:

- Isotopes of hydrogen
- Ortho and para hydrogen
- Heavy water
- Hydrides
- Properties and uses of hydrogen

---

# PART A: POSITION OF HYDROGEN IN PERIODIC TABLE

---

# 1. Hydrogen

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{H}
\]

Atomic number:

\[
\boxed{1}
\]

Electronic configuration:

\[
\boxed{1s^1}
\]

---

# 2. Unique Position of Hydrogen

Hydrogen resembles:

## Alkali Metals

Because:

- It has one electron in outer shell.
- It can lose one electron.

Reaction:

\[
H\rightarrow H^+ + e^-
\]

---

## Halogens

Because:

- It needs one electron to complete shell.

\[
H+e^-\rightarrow H^-
\]

---

---

## Definition

Atoms of the same element having the same atomic number but different mass numbers are called isotopes.

---

1. Protium
2. Deuterium
3. Tritium

---

- Contains one proton

\]

\[

\]

# PART C: ORTHO AND PARA HYDROGEN

---

# 7. Molecular Hydrogen

Hydrogen exists as:

\[
H_2
\]

The two hydrogen atoms have nuclear spins.

Based on spin arrangement:

1. Ortho hydrogen
2. Para hydrogen

---

# 8. Ortho Hydrogen

## Definition

Hydrogen molecules in which nuclear spins are parallel are called ortho hydrogen.

---

Properties:

- Higher energy state
- More stable at high temperature

---

# 9. Para Hydrogen

## Definition

Hydrogen molecules in which nuclear spins are opposite are called para hydrogen.

---

Properties:

- Lower energy state
- More stable at low temperature

---

# Difference Between Ortho and Para Hydrogen

| Ortho Hydrogen                  | Para Hydrogen                  |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Parallel spins                  | Opposite spins                 |
| Higher energy                   | Lower energy                   |
| More stable at high temperature | More stable at low temperature |

# PART E: HEAVY WATER

---

# 12. Heavy Water

Chemical formula:

\[
\boxed{D_2O}
\]

---

It contains:

- Deuterium
- Oxygen

---

# 13. Preparation of Heavy Water

Prepared by:

- Electrolysis of water
- Fractional distillation

---

- Higher boiling point than ordinary water
- Used as moderator in nuclear reactors

---

- Scientific research

# PART F: HYDRIDES

---

# 15. Hydrides

## Definition

Binary compounds of hydrogen with other elements are called hydrides.

---

Types:

1. Ionic hydrides
2. Covalent hydrides
3. Metallic hydrides

---

# 16. Ionic Hydrides

## Definition

Hydrides formed by highly electropositive elements are called ionic hydrides.

---

Examples:

\[
NaH
\]

\[
CaH_2
\]

---

Characteristics:

- Contain hydride ion:

\[
H^-
\]

- High melting point
- Conduct electricity in molten state

---

# 17. Covalent Hydrides

## Definition

Hydrides formed by sharing electrons between hydrogen and non-metals are covalent hydrides.

---

Examples:

\[
CH_4
\]

\[
NH_3
\]

\[
H_2O
\]

---

Properties:

- Molecular compounds
- Usually low melting points

---

# 18. Metallic Hydrides

## Definition

Hydrides formed by transition metals are called metallic hydrides.

---

Examples:

\[
PdH_x
\]

---

Properties:

- Hydrogen occupies interstitial spaces.
- Used for hydrogen storage.

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Identify the isotope containing one proton and one neutron.

Solution:

Mass number:

\[
1+1=2
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Deuterium}}
\]

---

## Example 2

Write formula of heavy water.

Solution:

Heavy hydrogen = Deuterium (D)

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{D_2O}
\]

---

## Example 3

Classify NaH.

Na is an electropositive metal.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ionic hydride}}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing isotopes with ions.
- Forgetting deuterium symbol is D.
- Confusing heavy water with hydrogen peroxide.
- Mixing ortho and para hydrogen properties.
- Assuming all hydrides are ionic.
- Forgetting tritium is radioactive.

# Chapter Summary

- Hydrogen is the first and lightest element.
- It has three isotopes: protium, deuterium and tritium.
- Ortho and para hydrogen differ in nuclear spin arrangement.
- Heavy water contains deuterium oxide.
- Hydrides are classified as ionic, covalent and metallic.
- Hydrogen has important industrial and energy applications.
