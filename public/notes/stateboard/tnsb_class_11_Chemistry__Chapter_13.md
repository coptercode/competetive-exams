# Introduction

Hydrocarbons are organic compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen atoms.

They are the basic compounds of organic chemistry and are important sources of:

- Fuels
- Petrochemicals
- Polymers
- Industrial chemicals

This chapter covers:

- Alkanes
- Alkenes
- Aromatic hydrocarbons
- Preparation methods
- Chemical properties
- Markovnikov's rule
- Benzene structure
- Huckel's rule

---

# PART A: CLASSIFICATION OF HYDROCARBONS

---

# 1. Hydrocarbons

## Definition

Compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen atoms are called hydrocarbons.

---

Types:

1. Saturated hydrocarbons
2. Unsaturated hydrocarbons
3. Aromatic hydrocarbons

---

# 2. Saturated Hydrocarbons (Alkanes)

Contain only single bonds.

General formula:

\[
\boxed{
C_nH_{2n+2}
}
\]

---

Examples:

Methane:

\[
CH_4
\]

Ethane:

\[
C_2H_6
\]

---

# 3. Unsaturated Hydrocarbons

Contain multiple bonds.

Types:

1. Alkenes
2. Alkynes

---

# 4. Alkenes

Contain carbon-carbon double bond.

General formula:

\[
\boxed{
C_nH_{2n}
}
\]

---

Example:

Ethene:

\[
CH_2=CH_2
\]

---

# 5. Alkynes

Contain carbon-carbon triple bond.

General formula:

\[
\boxed{
C_nH_{2n-2}
}
\]

---

Example:

Ethyne:

\[
HC\equiv CH
\]

---

# PART B: ALKANES

---

# 6. Structure of Alkanes

Alkanes contain:

- C-C single bonds
- sp³ hybridized carbon

Bond angle:

\[
109.5^\circ
\]

---

# 7. Preparation of Alkanes

---

## 1. Hydrogenation of Alkenes

Alkenes react with hydrogen in presence of catalyst.

\[
CH_2=CH_2+H_2
\rightarrow
CH_3-CH_3
\]

Catalyst:

\[
Ni/Pt/Pd
\]

---

## 2. Wurtz Reaction

Alkyl halides react with sodium metal.

General reaction:

\[
\boxed{
2R-X+2Na
\rightarrow
R-R+2NaX
}
\]

---

Example:

\[
2CH_3Cl+2Na
\rightarrow
C_2H_6+2NaCl
\]

---

## 3. Decarboxylation

Sodium salts of carboxylic acids give alkanes.

\[
RCOONa+NaOH
\rightarrow
RH+Na_2CO_3
\]

---

# PART C: PROPERTIES OF ALKANES

---

# 8. Physical Properties

- Insoluble in water
- Soluble in organic solvents
- Boiling point increases with molecular mass
- Lower alkanes are gases

---

# 9. Chemical Reactions of Alkanes

---

## 1. Combustion

Alkanes burn in oxygen.

Example:

\[
CH_4+2O_2
\rightarrow
CO_2+2H_2O
\]

---

## 2. Halogenation

Replacement of hydrogen by halogen.

Example:

\[
CH_4+Cl_2
\rightarrow
CH_3Cl+HCl
\]

Condition:

\[
UV\ light
\]

bond.

Products

# PART F: MARKOVNIKOV'S RULE

---

# 13. Markovnikov's Rule

## Statement

When an unsymmetrical reagent adds to an unsymmetrical alkene, hydrogen attaches to the carbon already having more hydrogen atoms.

---

Example:

Propene + HBr:

\[
CH_3CH=CH_2+HBr
\]

Major product:

\[
\boxed{
CH_3CHBrCH_3
}
\]

---

# 14. Peroxide Effect

In presence of peroxide, addition of HBr occurs opposite to Markovnikov's rule.

---

Called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Anti-Markovnikov addition}}
\]

---

---

# 15. Benzene

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
C_6H_6
}
\]

---

Structure:

- Six carbon ring
- Delocalized π electrons
- Resonance stabilized

---

# 16. Kekule Structure

Benzene was represented by alternating single and double bonds.

---

Modern concept:

Benzene is a resonance hybrid.

---

# PART H: AROMATICITY

---

# 17. Huckel's Rule

## Statement

A cyclic, planar, conjugated compound is aromatic if it contains:

\[
\boxed{
(4n+2)\pi\ electrons
}
\]

---

where:

n = integer

---

Examples:

Benzene:

Number of π electrons:

\[
6
\]

Since:

\[
4(1)+2=6
\]

Benzene is aromatic.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing alkane, alkene and alkyne formulas.
- Forgetting Markovnikov rule applies to unsymmetrical alkenes.
- Mixing aromatic and aliphatic compounds.
- Forgetting benzene has resonance stability.
- Applying Huckel rule without checking cyclic and planar conditions.
- Confusing substitution and addition reactions.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Hydrocarbons contain only carbon and hydrogen.
- Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons.
- Alkenes undergo addition reactions due to double bonds.
- Markovnikov rule predicts addition product orientation.
- Benzene is aromatic due to resonance and Huckel's rule.
- Aromatic hydrocarbons mainly undergo electrophilic substitution reactions.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Hydrocarbons form the foundation of organic chemistry and fuels.}
}
