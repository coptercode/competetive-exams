> **Subject:** Biology

# Introduction

The nervous system controls and coordinates body activities by receiving information from the environment, processing it and producing appropriate responses.

This chapter covers:

- Neuron structure and functions
- Nervous system
- Central nervous system
- Peripheral nervous system
- Autonomic nervous system
- Generation and conduction of nerve impulses

---

# PART A: NERVOUS SYSTEM

---

# 1. Neural Control and Coordination

## Definition

The process by which the nervous system receives information, processes it and controls body responses is called neural control and coordination.

---

Functions:

- Detects changes in surroundings.
- Coordinates body activities.
- Controls movement and responses.

---

# PART B: NEURON

---

# 2. Neuron

## Definition

A neuron is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system.

---

Functions:

- Receives nerve impulses.
- Transmits nerve impulses.

---

# 3. Structure of Neuron

A neuron consists of:

1. Cell body
2. Dendrites
3. Axon

---

# 4. Cell Body (Cyton)

Contains:

- Nucleus
- Cytoplasm
- Cell organelles

---

Function:

- Maintains metabolic activities of neuron.

---

# 5. Dendrites

Characteristics:

- Short branched processes.
- Receive nerve impulses.

---

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Receive signals}}
\]

---

# 6. Axon

Characteristics:

- Long cylindrical process.
- Conducts impulses away from cell body.

---

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Transmit signals}}
\]

---

# 7. Myelin Sheath

## Definition

A protective covering around axons is called myelin sheath.

---

Functions:

- Increases speed of nerve conduction.
- Protects axon.

---

# 8. Types of Neurons

---

## Sensory Neuron

Function:

Carries impulses from receptors to CNS.

---

## Motor Neuron

Function:

Carries impulses from CNS to muscles or glands.

---

## Interneuron

Function:

Connects sensory and motor neurons.

---

# PART C: NERVE IMPULSE

---

# 9. Nerve Impulse

## Definition

A nerve impulse is an electrical signal transmitted along a neuron.

---

# 10. Resting Potential

## Definition

The electrical state of a resting neuron is called resting potential.

---

Characteristics:

- Inside of neuron is negatively charged.
- Maintained by ion distribution.

---

# 11. Action Potential

## Definition

The change in electrical potential during transmission of nerve impulse is called action potential.

---

Steps:

1. Depolarisation
2. Repolarisation

---

# 12. Depolarisation

Occurs due to:

\[
\boxed{\text{Entry of sodium ions (Na⁺)}}
\]

---

# 13. Repolarisation

Occurs due to:

\[
\boxed{\text{Exit of potassium ions (K⁺)}}
\]

---

# PART D: SYNAPSE

---

# 14. Synapse

## Definition

The junction between two neurons is called a synapse.

---

Types:

1. Electrical synapse
2. Chemical synapse

---

# 15. Chemical Synapse

Communication occurs through neurotransmitters.

---

Examples of neurotransmitters:

- Acetylcholine
- Noradrenaline

---

# PART E: HUMAN NERVOUS SYSTEM

---

# 16. Nervous System Divisions

The human nervous system is divided into:

1. Central nervous system (CNS)
2. Peripheral nervous system (PNS)

---

# PART F: CENTRAL NERVOUS SYSTEM (CNS)

---

# 17. CNS

Includes:

1. Brain
2. Spinal cord

---

Function:

- Processing information.
- Controlling body activities.

---

# 18. Brain

## Definition

The brain is the main control centre of the nervous system.

---

Protected by:

- Skull
- Meninges
- Cerebrospinal fluid

---

# 19. Parts of Brain

Major divisions:

1. Forebrain
2. Midbrain
3. Hindbrain

---

# 20. Forebrain

Largest part of brain.

Includes:

- Cerebrum
- Thalamus
- Hypothalamus

---

## Cerebrum

Functions:

- Thinking
- Memory
- Intelligence
- Voluntary actions

---

## Hypothalamus

Functions:

- Temperature regulation.
- Hunger and thirst control.
- Hormone regulation.

---

# 21. Midbrain

Functions:

- Controls visual and auditory reflexes.

---

# 22. Hindbrain

Includes:

1. Cerebellum
2. Pons
3. Medulla oblongata

---

## Cerebellum

Functions:

- Balance.
- Coordination of movement.

---

## Medulla Oblongata

Controls:

- Heartbeat.
- Breathing.
- Blood pressure.

---

# 23. Spinal Cord

## Definition

A long cylindrical structure extending from brain through vertebral column.

---

Functions:

- Conducts impulses between brain and body.
- Controls reflex actions.

---

# PART G: PERIPHERAL NERVOUS SYSTEM (PNS)

---

# 24. PNS

## Definition

The nervous system outside the brain and spinal cord is called peripheral nervous system.

---

Components:

1. Cranial nerves
2. Spinal nerves

---

# 25. Cranial Nerves

Number:

\[
\boxed{12\ pairs}
\]

---

Function:

Connect brain with organs.

---

# 26. Spinal Nerves

Number:

\[
\boxed{31\ pairs}
\]

---

Function:

Connect spinal cord with body parts.

---

# PART H: AUTONOMIC NERVOUS SYSTEM

---

# 27. Autonomic Nervous System

## Definition

The part of nervous system controlling involuntary activities is called autonomic nervous system.

---

Controls:

- Heartbeat
- Digestion
- Gland secretion

---

Divisions:

1. Sympathetic nervous system
2. Parasympathetic nervous system

---

# 28. Sympathetic Nervous System

Function:

Prepares body for emergency situations.

---

Called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Fight or flight response}}
\]

---

Effects:

- Increases heartbeat.
- Dilates pupils.

---

# 29. Parasympathetic Nervous System

Function:

Maintains normal body functions.

---

Called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Rest and digest system}}
\]

---

Effects:

- Slows heartbeat.
- Promotes digestion.

---

# Important Differences

## Sensory Neuron vs Motor Neuron

| Sensory Neuron                | Motor Neuron              |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Carries impulses to CNS       | Carries impulses from CNS |
| Receptor to brain/spinal cord | CNS to muscles/glands     |

---

## Sympathetic vs Parasympathetic

| Sympathetic         | Parasympathetic     |
| ------------------- | ------------------- |
| Emergency response  | Resting state       |
| Increases heartbeat | Decreases heartbeat |
| Fight or flight     | Rest and digest     |

---

# Reflex Action

## Definition

A quick automatic response to a stimulus without conscious thinking is called reflex action.

---

Reflex pathway:

Stimulus

↓

Receptor

↓

Sensory neuron

↓

Spinal cord

↓

Motor neuron

↓

Effector

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing dendrites and axons.
- Mixing CNS and PNS.
- Forgetting medulla controls involuntary activities.
- Confusing sympathetic and parasympathetic systems.
- Forgetting synapse involves neurotransmitters.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Neurons transmit nerve impulses.
- CNS consists of brain and spinal cord.
- PNS connects CNS with body organs.
- Autonomic nervous system controls involuntary functions.
- Sympathetic and parasympathetic systems regulate opposite activities.
- Neural coordination allows rapid responses to stimuli.
