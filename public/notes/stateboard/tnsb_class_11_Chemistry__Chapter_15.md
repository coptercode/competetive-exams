# Introduction

Environmental chemistry is the branch of chemistry that studies chemical processes occurring in the environment and their effects on living organisms.

It deals with:

- Air pollution
- Water pollution
- Soil pollution
- Greenhouse effect
- Acid rain
- Ozone depletion
- Pollution control methods

---

# PART A: ENVIRONMENT

---

# 1. Environment

## Definition

The surroundings in which living organisms exist and interact are called the environment.

---

Components:

1. Biotic components
2. Abiotic components

---

## Biotic Components

Living organisms:

- Plants
- Animals
- Microorganisms

---

## Abiotic Components

Non-living factors:

- Air
- Water
- Soil
- Temperature

---

# PART B: POLLUTION

---

# 2. Pollution

## Definition

The undesirable change in physical, chemical or biological characteristics of the environment is called pollution.

---

# 3. Pollutants

## Definition

Substances that cause pollution are called pollutants.

---

Examples:

- Smoke
- Toxic chemicals
- Plastics
- Industrial waste

---

# Types of Pollution

1. Air pollution
2. Water pollution
3. Soil pollution
4. Noise pollution

---

# PART C: AIR POLLUTION

---

# 4. Air Pollution

## Definition

The contamination of air by harmful substances is called air pollution.

---

# Major Air Pollutants

---

## 1. Carbon Monoxide (CO)

Sources:

- Automobile exhaust
- Incomplete combustion

Effects:

- Reduces oxygen transport in blood
- Causes respiratory problems

---

## 2. Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)

Sources:

- Burning of fossil fuels
- Deforestation

Effects:

- Global warming
- Greenhouse effect

---

## 3. Sulphur Dioxide (SO₂)

Sources:

- Burning coal
- Industrial emissions

Effects:

- Acid rain
- Lung irritation

---

## 4. Nitrogen Oxides (NOx)

Sources:

- Vehicle engines
- Industries

Effects:

- Acid rain
- Smog formation

---

## 5. Particulate Matter

Examples:

- Dust
- Smoke
- Soot

Effects:

- Respiratory diseases

---

# PART D: SMOG

---

# 5. Smog

## Definition

A mixture of smoke and fog is called smog.

---

Types:

1. Classical smog
2. Photochemical smog

---

# 6. Photochemical Smog

Formed by reaction of:

- Nitrogen oxides
- Hydrocarbons
- Sunlight

---

Major components:

- Ozone
- PAN (Peroxyacetyl nitrate)

---

Effects:

- Eye irritation
- Plant damage
- Breathing problems

---

# PART E: GREENHOUSE EFFECT

---

# 7. Greenhouse Effect

## Definition

The warming of Earth's surface due to absorption of infrared radiation by greenhouse gases is called greenhouse effect.

---

# Greenhouse Gases

1. Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
2. Methane (CH₄)
3. Nitrous oxide (N₂O)
4. Water vapour
5. CFCs

---

# 8. Global Warming

## Definition

The gradual increase in Earth's average temperature due to increased greenhouse gases is called global warming.

---

Effects:

- Melting of glaciers
- Sea level rise
- Climate change
- Extreme weather events

---

# PART F: ACID RAIN

---

# 9. Acid Rain

## Definition

Rainwater having pH less than 5.6 due to acidic gases is called acid rain.

---

Main gases:

- SO₂
- NO₂

---

Formation:

Sulphur dioxide:

\[
SO_2+H_2O
\rightarrow
H_2SO_3
\]

---

Nitrogen dioxide:

\[
NO_2+H_2O
\rightarrow
HNO_3
\]

---

Effects:

- Damages monuments
- Harms aquatic life
- Reduces soil fertility

---

# PART G: OZONE LAYER

---

# 10. Ozone Layer

## Definition

A layer of ozone gas present in the stratosphere that protects Earth from ultraviolet radiation.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{O_3}
\]

---

# 11. Ozone Depletion

## Definition

Reduction in thickness of ozone layer is called ozone depletion.

---

Cause:

Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)

---

CFC releases:

\[
Cl\cdot
\]

which destroys ozone.

---

Reaction:

\[
Cl\cdot+O_3
\rightarrow
ClO\cdot+O_2
\]

---

Effects:

- Skin cancer
- Eye damage
- Harm to plants

---

## Definition

Contamination of water resources by harmful substances is called water pollution.

---

# Sources

- Industrial waste
- Sewage

- Plastics

---

# Major Water Pollutants

---

## 1. Heavy Metals

Examples:

- Mercury
- Lead
- Cadmium

Effects:

- Toxicity
- Bioaccumulation

---

## 2. Fertilizers

Contain:

- Nitrates
- Phosphates

Effects:

- Eutrophication

---

# 13. Eutrophication

## Definition

Excess nutrients in water causing excessive growth of algae is called eutrophication.

---

Effects:

- Oxygen depletion
- Death of aquatic organisms

---

# 14. Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD)

## Definition

The amount of oxygen required by microorganisms to decompose organic matter in water is called BOD.

---

High BOD:

\[
\boxed{\text{Highly polluted water}}
\]

---

Low BOD:

\[
\boxed{\text{Cleaner water}}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing greenhouse effect with ozone depletion.
- Forgetting acid rain is caused mainly by SO₂ and NOx.
- Mixing pollutants and pollution types.
- Confusing BOD with oxygen content.
- Forgetting CFCs are responsible for ozone depletion.
- Ignoring the role of human activities in pollution.

# Chapter Summary

- Environmental chemistry studies chemical changes in nature.
- Pollutants disturb environmental balance.
- Air pollution is caused by harmful gases and particles.
- Greenhouse gases cause global warming.
- SO₂ and NOx cause acid rain.
- CFCs destroy the ozone layer.
- Water and soil pollution affect ecosystems.
- Pollution control requires sustainable practices.
