# Introduction

Chemical bonding explains how atoms combine to form molecules and compounds.

Atoms form bonds to achieve:

- Stability
- Lower energy state
- Noble gas electronic configuration

This chapter covers:

- Lewis theory
- Octet rule
- Ionic and covalent bonds
- VSEPR theory
- Valence bond theory
- Hybridization
- Molecular shapes

---

# PART A: CHEMICAL BOND

---

# 1. Chemical Bond

## Definition

The attractive force that holds atoms together in a molecule or compound is called a chemical bond.

---

# 2. Types of Chemical Bonds

Main types:

1. Ionic bond
2. Covalent bond
3. Coordinate bond
4. Metallic bond

---

# PART B: LEWIS THEORY

---

# 3. Lewis Concept

Atoms combine by:

- Losing electrons
- Gaining electrons
- Sharing electrons

to achieve stable electronic configuration.

---

# 4. Lewis Symbols

The valence electrons of an element are represented by dots around its symbol.

Example:

Hydrogen:

\[
H\cdot
\]

---

# PART C: OCTET RULE

---

# 5. Octet Rule

## Statement

Atoms tend to gain, lose or share electrons to achieve eight electrons in their valence shell.

---

Examples:

### Sodium

Electronic configuration:

\[
2,8,1
\]

Loses one electron:

\[
Na\rightarrow Na^+ +e^-
\]

---

### Chlorine

\[
2,8,7
\]

Gains one electron:

\[
Cl+e^-\rightarrow Cl^-
\]

---

# 6. Limitations of Octet Rule

Octet rule fails for:

1. Incomplete octet compounds

Example:

\[
BeCl_2
\]

2. Expanded octet compounds

Example:

\[
SF_6
\]

3. Odd electron molecules

Example:

\[
NO
\]

---

# PART D: IONIC BOND

---

# 7. Ionic Bond

## Definition

A bond formed by transfer of electrons from one atom to another is called ionic bond.

---

Example:

Formation of NaCl:

\[
Na\rightarrow Na^++e^-
\]

\[
Cl+e^-\rightarrow Cl^-
\]

\[
Na^++Cl^-\rightarrow NaCl
\]

---

# 8. Properties of Ionic Compounds

- High melting point
- High boiling point
- Soluble in water
- Conduct electricity in molten state
- Hard crystalline solids

---

# PART E: COVALENT BOND

---

# 9. Covalent Bond

## Definition

A bond formed by sharing of electron pairs between atoms is called covalent bond.

---

Examples:

Hydrogen:

\[
H-H
\]

Oxygen:

\[
O=O
\]

Nitrogen:

\[
N\equiv N
\]

---

# 10. Types of Covalent Bonds

---

## Single Bond

One electron pair shared.

Example:

\[
H-H
\]

---

## Double Bond

Two electron pairs shared.

Example:

\[
O=O
\]

---

## Triple Bond

Three electron pairs shared.

Example:

\[
N\equiv N
\]

---

# PART F: FORMAL CHARGE

---

# 11. Formal Charge

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
FC=V-\frac{N+B}{2}
}
\]

---

where:

V = valence electrons

N = non-bonding electrons

B = bonding electrons

---

# PART G: VSEPR THEORY

---

# 12. VSEPR Theory

## Full form:

Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion Theory

---

## Statement

Electron pairs around central atom repel each other and arrange themselves to minimize repulsion.

---

# 13. Order of Repulsion

\[
\boxed{
LP-LP>LP-BP>BP-BP
}
\]

---

where:

LP = Lone pair

BP = Bond pair

---

# PART I: VALENCE BOND THEORY

---

# 19. Valence Bond Theory (VBT)

## Statement

A covalent bond forms by overlap of atomic orbitals containing unpaired electrons.

---

Types of overlap:

1. Sigma bond (σ)
2. Pi bond (π)

---

# 20. Sigma Bond

## Definition

Bond formed by head-on overlap of orbitals.

---

Properties:

- Strong bond
- Symmetrical
- Allows free rotation

---

# 21. Pi Bond

## Definition

Bond formed by sideways overlap of orbitals.

---

Properties:

- Weaker than sigma bond
- Restricts rotation

---

---

# 26. Molecular Orbital Concept

Atomic orbitals combine to form molecular orbitals.

Types:

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Bond\ order=
\frac{N_b-N_a}{2}
}
\]

---

Nb = bonding electrons

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Find hybridization of carbon in methane.

CH₄ has:

4 sigma bonds

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{sp^3}
\]

---

## Example 2

Shape of BF₃.

Boron has:

3 bond pairs

No lone pairs

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Trigonal planar}}
\]

---

## Example 3

Why is NH₃ pyramidal?

Because nitrogen has:

- 3 bond pairs
- 1 lone pair

Lone pair repulsion changes shape.

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Trigonal pyramidal}}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing ionic and covalent bonds.
- Forgetting lone pairs affect molecular shape.
- Mixing hybridization and geometry.
- Forgetting lone pair repulsion order.
- Confusing sigma and pi bonds.
- Applying octet rule without considering exceptions.
