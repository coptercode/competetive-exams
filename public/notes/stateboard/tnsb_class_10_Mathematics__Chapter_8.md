# PART B: MEASURES OF DISPERSION

---

# 2. Dispersion

## Definition

The measure of how much the data values spread around an average value is called dispersion.

---

Measures of dispersion:

1. Range
2. Mean deviation
3. Variance
4. Standard deviation

---

# 3. Range

## Definition

The difference between the highest value and the lowest value in a data set is called range.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Range=Maximum\ value-Minimum\ value
}
\]

---

Example:

Data:

\[
5,8,12,15,20
\]

Maximum:

\[
20
\]

Minimum:

\[
5
\]

Range:

\[
20-5
\]

\[
\boxed{15}
\]

---

# PART C: MEAN DEVIATION

---

# 4. Mean Deviation

## Definition

Mean deviation is the average of the absolute deviations of observations from a central value.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
MD=
\frac{\sum |x-\bar{x}|}{n}
}
\]

---

Where:

- x = observation
- \(\bar{x}\) = mean
- n = number of observations

---

# Steps to Find Mean Deviation

1. Find mean.
2. Find deviations:

\[
|x-\bar{x}|
\]

3. Add deviations.
4. Divide by number of observations.

---

# PART D: VARIANCE

---

# 5. Variance

## Definition

Variance measures the average of squared deviations from the mean.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Variance=
\frac{\sum(x-\bar{x})^2}{n}
}
\]

---

Properties:

- Always positive.
- Greater variance means greater spread.

---

# PART E: STANDARD DEVIATION

---

# 6. Standard Deviation

## Definition

Standard deviation is the positive square root of variance.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\sigma=
\sqrt{
\frac{\sum(x-\bar{x})^2}{n}
}
}
\]

---

Where:

\[
\sigma=\text{standard deviation}
\]

---

# 7. Importance of Standard Deviation

A smaller standard deviation indicates:

- Data values are closer to mean.
- Less variation.

A larger standard deviation indicates:

- Data values are more spread out.

---

# Example

Find standard deviation of:

\[
2,4,6
\]

Mean:

\[
\bar{x}=\frac{2+4+6}{3}
\]

\[
=4
\]

Deviations:

\[
-2,0,2
\]

Squares:

\[
4,0,4
\]

Variance:

\[
=\frac{8}{3}
\]

Standard deviation:

\[
\boxed{
\sigma=\sqrt{\frac83}
}
\]

---

# PART F: PROBABILITY

---

# 8. Probability

## Definition

Probability is the measure of the chance of occurrence of an event.

---

Probability value lies between:

\[
\boxed{
0\leq P(E)\leq1
}
\]

---

# 9. Random Experiment

## Definition

An experiment whose outcome cannot be predicted exactly is called a random experiment.

---

Examples:

- Tossing a coin
- Rolling a die

---

# 10. Sample Space

## Definition

The set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment is called sample space.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{S}
\]

---

Example:

For tossing a coin:

\[
S=\{H,T\}
\]

---

# 11. Event

## Definition

A collection of one or more outcomes of a sample space is called an event.

---

Example:

Getting an even number on a die:

\[
E=\{2,4,6\}
\]

---

# PART G: CLASSICAL PROBABILITY

---

# 12. Probability of an Event

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
P(E)=
\frac{Number\ of\ favourable\ outcomes}
{Total\ number\ of\ outcomes}
}
\]

---

# Example

A die is rolled.

Find probability of getting 4.

Sample space:

\[
S=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}
\]

Favourable outcomes:

\[
1
\]

Therefore:

\[
P(4)=\frac16
\]

\[
\boxed{\frac16}
\]

---

# 13. Complementary Event

If E is an event, then not occurring of E is called complementary event.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
P(E')=1-P(E)
}
\]

---

Example:

If:

\[
P(E)=0.3
\]

then:

\[
P(E')=1-0.3
\]

\[
\boxed{0.7}
\]

---

# PART H: ADDITION THEOREM OF PROBABILITY

---

# 14. Addition Theorem

For two events A and B:

\[
\boxed{
P(A\cup B)
=

P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)
}
\]

---

Where:

- \(A\cup B\) = A or B occurs
- \(A\cap B\) = Both A and B occur

---

# 15. Mutually Exclusive Events

Two events that cannot occur together are called mutually exclusive events.

---

Condition:

\[
\boxed{
P(A\cap B)=0
}
\]

---

Addition theorem becomes:

\[
\boxed{
P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)
}
\]

---

# Example

A die is rolled.

Find probability of getting 1 or 2.

Events are mutually exclusive.

\[
P(1)=\frac16
\]

\[
P(2)=\frac16
\]

Therefore:

\[
P(1\cup2)
=

\frac16+\frac16
\]

\[
\boxed{\frac13}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing variance with standard deviation.
- Forgetting square root in standard deviation.
- Using incorrect total outcomes in probability.
- Forgetting subtraction term in addition theorem.
- Assuming probability can be greater than 1.
- Confusing mutually exclusive and independent events.

# Chapter Summary

- Statistics helps analyze data variation.
- Range gives the simplest measure of spread.
- Variance and standard deviation measure dispersion more accurately.
- Probability measures the chance of events.
- Addition theorem helps find probability of combined events.
