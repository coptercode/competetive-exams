# PART A: SIMILARITY OF TRIANGLES

---

# 1. Similar Figures

## Definition

Two figures having the same shape but different sizes are called similar figures.

---

Properties:

- Corresponding angles are equal.
- Corresponding sides are proportional.

---

# 2. Similar Triangles

## Definition

Two triangles are similar if their corresponding angles are equal and corresponding sides are proportional.

---

Notation:

\[
\boxed{
\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF
}
\]

---

Meaning:

\[
\angle A=\angle D
\]

\[
\angle B=\angle E
\]

\[
\angle C=\angle F
\]

and

\[
\frac{AB}{DE}
=

\frac{BC}{EF}
=

\frac{AC}{DF}
\]

---

# 3. Criteria for Similarity of Triangles

---

# (i) AAA Criterion

If all three corresponding angles are equal, triangles are similar.

---

Example:

\[
\angle A=\angle D
\]

\[
\angle B=\angle E
\]

\[
\angle C=\angle F
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\triangle ABC\sim\triangle DEF}
\]

---

# (ii) SAS Criterion

If two sides are proportional and included angles are equal, triangles are similar.

---

Condition:

\[
\frac{AB}{DE}
=

\frac{AC}{DF}
\]

and

\[
\angle A=\angle D
\]

---

# (iii) SSS Criterion

If all corresponding sides are proportional, triangles are similar.

---

Condition:

\[
\frac{AB}{DE}
=

\frac{BC}{EF}
=

\frac{AC}{DF}
\]

---

# PART B: THALES THEOREM

---

# 4. Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT)

## Statement

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle and intersects the other two sides, then it divides those two sides in the same ratio.

---

In triangle ABC:

If:

\[
DE\parallel BC
\]

then:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{AD}{DB}
=

\frac{AE}{EC}
}
\]

---

# 5. Converse of BPT

If a line divides two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then it is parallel to the third side.

---

# PART C: ANGLE BISECTOR THEOREM

---

# 6. Angle Bisector Theorem

## Statement

The angle bisector of an angle in a triangle divides the opposite side in the ratio of the adjacent sides.

---

In triangle ABC:

If:

\[
AD
\]

bisects:

\[
\angle A
\]

then:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{BD}{DC}
=

\frac{AB}{AC}
}
\]

---

# PART D: AREAS OF SIMILAR TRIANGLES

---

# 7. Area Theorem

The ratio of areas of two similar triangles is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.

---

If:

\[
\triangle ABC\sim\triangle DEF
\]

then:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Area(ABC)}
{Area(DEF)}
=

\left(
\frac{AB}{DE}
\right)^2
}
\]

---

# PART E: CIRCLES AND TANGENTS

---

# 8. Circle

## Definition

A circle is the set of all points in a plane that are at a fixed distance from a fixed point.

---

Important terms:

- Centre
- Radius
- Diameter
- Chord
- Arc
- Tangent

---

# 9. Tangent to a Circle

## Definition

A line that touches a circle at exactly one point is called a tangent.

---

Point of contact:

The point where tangent touches the circle.

---

# 10. Properties of Tangents

---

## Property 1

The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.

---

If PT is tangent:

\[
\boxed{
OT\perp PT
}
\]

---

## Property 2

Tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.

---

If PA and PB are tangents:

\[
\boxed{
PA=PB
}
\]

---

# PART F: TANGENT-CHORD THEOREM

---

# 11. Tangent-Chord Theorem

## Statement

The angle between a tangent and a chord is equal to the angle in the alternate segment.

---

Example:

If PT is tangent and AB is chord:

\[
\boxed{
\angle PTA=\angle ACB
}
\]

---

# PART G: CYCLIC QUADRILATERALS

---

# 12. Cyclic Quadrilateral

## Definition

A quadrilateral whose all vertices lie on a circle is called a cyclic quadrilateral.

---

Example:

ABCD lies on a circle.

---

# 13. Properties of Cyclic Quadrilaterals

---

## Property 1

Opposite angles are supplementary.

---

\[
\boxed{
\angle A+\angle C=180^\circ
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\angle B+\angle D=180^\circ
}
\]

---

## Property 2

The exterior angle is equal to the opposite interior angle.

---

# PART H: CONSTRUCTIONS

---

# 14. Construction of Tangents from an External Point

Given:

- Circle with centre O
- External point P

Steps:

1. Join OP.
2. Find midpoint M of OP.
3. Draw circle with centre M and radius MO.
4. It cuts original circle at T₁ and T₂.
5. Join PT₁ and PT₂.

These are required tangents.

---

# 15. Construction of Similar Triangles

Steps:

1. Draw the given triangle.
2. Construct required proportional sides.
3. Use parallel lines.
4. Obtain required similar triangle.

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Two similar triangles have side ratio:

\[
2:3
\]

Find area ratio.

Solution:

Area ratio:

\[
=\left(\frac23\right)^2
\]

\[
\boxed{4:9}
\]

---

## Example 2

In triangle ABC:

\[
AD
\]

is angle bisector.

Given:

\[
AB=6cm
\]

\[
AC=8cm
\]

Find:

\[
BD:DC
\]

Using theorem:

\[
BD:DC=AB:AC
\]

\[
=6:8
\]

\[
\boxed{3:4}
\]

---

## Example 3

A tangent is drawn from point P to a circle.

If:

\[
PA=7cm
\]

Find PB.

Since tangents from external point are equal:

\[
PA=PB
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{PB=7cm}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing congruent and similar triangles.
- Forgetting area ratio is square of side ratio.
- Applying BPT without a parallel line condition.
- Forgetting tangent is perpendicular to radius.
- Mixing cyclic quadrilateral angle properties.
- Drawing construction steps incorrectly.
