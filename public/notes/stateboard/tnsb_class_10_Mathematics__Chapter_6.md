# Introduction

Trigonometry is the branch of mathematics that studies the relationship between angles and sides of triangles.

It is used in:

- Engineering
- Physics
- Architecture
- Navigation
- Heights and distances

This chapter covers:

- Trigonometric ratios
- Trigonometric identities
- Relations between ratios
- Heights and distances
- Angle of elevation and depression

# PART A: TRIGONOMETRIC RATIOS

---

# 1. Right-Angled Triangle

Consider a right triangle ABC with:

\[
\angle B=90^\circ
\]

For angle A:

- Opposite side = BC
- Adjacent side = AB
- Hypotenuse = AC

---

# 2. Trigonometric Ratios

There are six trigonometric ratios:

1. Sine
2. Cosine
3. Tangent
4. Cosecant
5. Secant
6. Cotangent

---

# 3. Sine Ratio

\[
\boxed{
\sin\theta=
\frac{Opposite}{Hypotenuse}
}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{
\sin\theta=\frac{P}{H}
}
\]

---

# 4. Cosine Ratio

\[
\boxed{
\cos\theta=
\frac{Adjacent}{Hypotenuse}
}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{
\cos\theta=\frac{B}{H}
}
\]

---

# 5. Tangent Ratio

\[
\boxed{
\tan\theta=
\frac{Opposite}{Adjacent}
}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{
\tan\theta=\frac{P}{B}
}
\]

---

# PART D: PROVING TRIGONOMETRIC IDENTITIES

---

# 11. Method of Proof

Steps:

1. Start with left side (LHS).
2. Convert all ratios into sin and cos.
3. Simplify.
4. Reach right side (RHS).

---

Example:

Prove:

\[
\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=\tan\theta
\]

Solution:

LHS:

\[
\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}
\]

By definition:

\[
=\tan\theta
\]

Hence:

\[
\boxed{LHS=RHS}
\]

# PART E: HEIGHTS AND DISTANCES

---

# 12. Introduction

Heights and distances problems use trigonometric ratios to find unknown heights or distances.

---

Important terms:

- Line of sight
- Angle of elevation
- Angle of depression

---

# 13. Angle of Elevation

## Definition

The angle between horizontal line and the line of sight when an object is above the observer is called angle of elevation.

---

Example:

Looking at the top of a tower.

---

# 14. Angle of Depression

## Definition

The angle between horizontal line and the line of sight when an object is below the observer is called angle of depression.

---

Example:

Looking down from a building.

# PART F: APPLICATION OF TRIGONOMETRY

---

# 15. Basic Height Formula

For a right triangle:

\[
\tan\theta=
\frac{Height}{Distance}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
Height=Distance\times\tan\theta
}
\]

---

# Example

A tower is observed at an angle of elevation 45°. Distance from tower is 20 m.

Find height.

Using:

\[
\tan45^\circ=\frac{h}{20}
\]

Since:

\[
\tan45^\circ=1
\]

\[
1=\frac h{20}
\]

\[
\boxed{h=20m}
\]

If:

Find cos θ.

\[
\boxed{\cos\theta=\frac45}
\]

---

## Example 3

Prove:

\[
1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta
\]

Since:

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}
\]

\[
1+\frac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta}
\]

Taking common denominator:

\[
\frac{\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta}
\]

Using identity:

\[
=\frac1{\cos^2\theta}
\]

\[
=\sec^2\theta
\]

Hence proved.

# Common Mistakes

- Mixing opposite and adjacent sides.
- Forgetting the hypotenuse is always opposite 90°.
- Using wrong standard angle values.
- Starting identity proofs from the wrong side.
- Confusing angle of elevation and depression.
- Forgetting units in height-distance problems.
