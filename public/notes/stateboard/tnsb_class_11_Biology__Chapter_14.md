# Introduction

Breathing and respiration are essential processes that supply oxygen to cells and remove carbon dioxide from the body.

This chapter covers:

- Respiratory organs in animals
- Human respiratory system
- Mechanism of breathing
- Exchange and transport of gases
- Respiratory volumes and capacities
- Regulation of respiration
- Respiratory disorders

---

# PART A: RESPIRATION AND BREATHING

---

# 1. Breathing

## Definition

Breathing is the physical process of taking in oxygen and releasing carbon dioxide.

---

Two stages:

1. Inspiration (inhalation)
2. Expiration (exhalation)

---

# 2. Respiration

## Definition

Respiration is a biochemical process in which food is oxidised to release energy.

---

Occurs inside:

\[
\boxed{\text{Cells}}
\]

---

# Difference Between Breathing and Respiration

| Breathing                    | Respiration            |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Physical process             | Chemical process       |
| Occurs in respiratory organs | Occurs in cells        |
| No energy released           | Energy released as ATP |

---

---

# PART C: HUMAN RESPIRATORY SYSTEM

---

# 4. Human Respiratory System

The respiratory system helps in exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide.

---

Main parts:

1. Nose
2. Nasal cavity
3. Pharynx
4. Larynx
5. Trachea
6. Bronchi
7. Bronchioles
8. Lungs

---

# 5. Nasal Cavity

Functions:

- Filters air.
- Warms air.
- Moistens air.

---

# 6. Pharynx

Common passage for:

- Air
- Food

---

# 7. Larynx

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Voice box}}
\]

Function:

- Produces sound.

---

# 8. Trachea

## Definition

The windpipe carrying air from larynx to bronchi is called trachea.

---

Features:

- Supported by cartilage rings.
- Lined with ciliated epithelium.

---

# 9. Bronchi

The trachea divides into two bronchi:

1. Right bronchus
2. Left bronchus

---

# 10. Lungs

## Definition

Lungs are the main organs of respiration.

---

Located in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Thoracic cavity}}
\]

---

# 11. Alveoli

## Definition

Small air sacs present in lungs where gas exchange occurs are called alveoli.

---

Features:

- Thin walls.
- Large surface area.
- Rich blood supply.

---

# PART D: MECHANISM OF BREATHING

---

# 12. Inspiration (Inhalation)

## Definition

The process of taking air into lungs is called inspiration.

---

Steps:

1. Diaphragm contracts and becomes flat.
2. Ribs move upward and outward.
3. Thoracic volume increases.
4. Air enters lungs.

---

# 13. Expiration (Exhalation)

## Definition

The process of releasing air from lungs is called expiration.

---

Steps:

1. Diaphragm relaxes.
2. Ribs move downward.
3. Thoracic volume decreases.
4. Air moves out.

---

# PART E: EXCHANGE OF GASES

---

# 14. Gas Exchange

Gas exchange occurs by diffusion.

---

Sites:

1. Alveoli
2. Body tissues

---

# 15. Exchange in Alveoli

Oxygen:

\[
\text{Alveoli} \rightarrow \text{Blood}
\]

---

Carbon dioxide:

\[
\text{Blood} \rightarrow \text{Alveoli}
\]

---

# 16. Exchange in Tissues

Oxygen moves from blood to tissues.

Carbon dioxide moves from tissues to blood.

---

# PART F: TRANSPORT OF OXYGEN

---

# 17. Transport of Oxygen

Oxygen is transported mainly by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Haemoglobin}}
\]

in red blood cells.

---

Formation:

\[
Hb + O_2 \rightarrow Oxyhaemoglobin
\]

---

# PART G: TRANSPORT OF CARBON DIOXIDE

---

# 18. Transport of CO₂

CO₂ is transported in three forms:

1. Dissolved in plasma
2. Carbamino-haemoglobin
3. Bicarbonate ions

---

Major form:

\[
\boxed{\text{Bicarbonate ions}}
\]

---

# PART H: RESPIRATORY VOLUMES

---

# 19. Tidal Volume (TV)

## Definition

Amount of air inspired or expired during normal breathing.

---

Normal value:

\[
\boxed{500\ ml}
\]

---

# 20. Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV)

Extra air inhaled after normal inspiration.

---

Approximate value:

\[
2500-3000\ ml
\]

---

# 21. Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV)

Extra air exhaled after normal expiration.

---

Approximate value:

\[
1000-1100\ ml
\]

---

# 22. Residual Volume (RV)

Air remaining in lungs after maximum expiration.

---

Approximate value:

\[
1200\ ml
\]

---

# PART I: RESPIRATORY CAPACITIES

---

# 23. Vital Capacity (VC)

Maximum amount of air exhaled after maximum inspiration.

Formula:

\[
VC = TV + IRV + ERV
\]

---

# 24. Total Lung Capacity (TLC)

Total volume of lungs after maximum inspiration.

Formula:

\[
TLC = VC + RV
\]

---

# PART J: REGULATION OF RESPIRATION

---

# 25. Respiratory Centre

Located in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Medulla oblongata}}
\]

---

Controls:

- Rate of breathing
- Depth of breathing

---

# 26. Chemoreceptors

Detect changes in:

- CO₂ concentration
- H⁺ concentration

---

Help regulate breathing rate.

---

# PART K: RESPIRATORY DISORDERS

---

# 27. Asthma

## Definition

A respiratory disorder caused by narrowing of airways.

---

Symptoms:

- Difficulty breathing.
- Wheezing.
- Coughing.

---

# 28. Emphysema

## Definition

A disease in which alveolar walls are damaged.

---

Effect:

- Reduced surface area for gas exchange.

---

Major cause:

\[
\boxed{\text{Smoking}}
\]

---

# 29. Occupational Respiratory Disorders

Diseases caused due to workplace pollutants are called occupational respiratory disorders.

---

Examples:

- Silicosis
- Asbestosis

---

Caused by:

- Dust
- Chemical particles

---

# Prevention:

- Use protective equipment.
- Reduce exposure.
- Maintain workplace safety.

---

# Important Differences

## Inspiration vs Expiration

| Inspiration               | Expiration                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Air enters lungs          | Air leaves lungs          |
| Diaphragm contracts       | Diaphragm relaxes         |
| Thoracic volume increases | Thoracic volume decreases |

---

## Asthma vs Emphysema

| Asthma                  | Emphysema            |
| ----------------------- | -------------------- |
| Airways become narrow   | Alveoli are damaged  |
| Often reversible        | Permanent damage     |
| Difficulty in breathing | Reduced gas exchange |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing breathing with respiration.
- Forgetting alveoli are the site of gas exchange.
- Mixing oxygen and carbon dioxide transport.
- Forgetting medulla controls respiration.
- Confusing asthma and emphysema.

- Human respiratory system consists of air passages and lungs.
- Gas exchange occurs through alveoli.
- Oxygen is transported by haemoglobin.
- Carbon dioxide is mainly transported as bicarbonate ions.
- Respiratory disorders affect normal breathing.
