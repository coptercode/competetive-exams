# Introduction

The animal kingdom includes multicellular, eukaryotic organisms that are generally heterotrophic and capable of movement.

Animals are classified based on:

- Level of organisation
- Body symmetry
- Germ layers
- Body cavity
- Segmentation
- Presence or absence of notochord

---

# PART A: BASIS OF CLASSIFICATION

---

# 1. Levels of Organisation

Animals show different levels of organisation.

---

## A. Cellular Level

Cells are arranged loosely.

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Porifera}}
\]

---

## B. Tissue Level

Similar cells form tissues.

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Cnidaria}}
\]

---

## C. Organ Level

Different tissues form organs.

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Platyhelminthes}}
\]

---

## D. Organ System Level

Organs form organ systems.

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Higher animals}}
\]

---

# 2. Body Symmetry

---

## Asymmetrical

Body cannot be divided into equal halves.

Example:

Sponges

---

## Radial Symmetry

Body parts arranged around a central axis.

Example:

Hydra

---

## Bilateral Symmetry

Body can be divided into two equal halves.

Example:

Humans

---

# 3. Germ Layers

---

## Diploblastic Animals

Body develops from two germ layers:

1. Ectoderm
2. Endoderm

Example:

Cnidaria

---

## Triploblastic Animals

Body develops from three germ layers:

1. Ectoderm
2. Mesoderm
3. Endoderm

Example:

Platyhelminthes onwards

---

# 4. Body Cavity (Coelom)

---

## Acoelomate

Body cavity absent.

Example:

Platyhelminthes

---

## Pseudocoelomate

False body cavity present.

Example:

Aschelminthes

---

## Coelomate

True body cavity present.

Example:

Annelida onwards

---

# 5. Notochord

## Definition

A flexible rod-like supporting structure present in chordates is called notochord.

---

Animals without notochord:

\[
\boxed{\text{Non-chordates}}
\]

Animals with notochord:

\[
\boxed{\text{Chordates}}
\]

---

# PART B: NON-CHORDATES

---

# 6. Kingdom Animalia Classification

Major non-chordate phyla:

1. Porifera
2. Cnidaria
3. Ctenophora
4. Platyhelminthes
5. Aschelminthes
6. Annelida
7. Arthropoda
8. Mollusca
9. Echinodermata

---

# 1. Porifera

## Characteristics

- Simplest multicellular animals.
- Body has pores.
- Aquatic.
- Cellular level organisation.

---

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Sycon}}
\]

Other examples:

- Spongilla
- Euspongia

---

# 2. Cnidaria (Coelenterata)

## Characteristics

- Aquatic animals.
- Radial symmetry.
- Diploblastic.
- Have cnidoblast cells.

---

Function of cnidoblast:

- Defence
- Capturing prey

---

Examples:

- Hydra
- Jellyfish
- Coral

---

# 3. Ctenophora

## Characteristics

- Marine organisms.
- Have comb plates for movement.
- Bioluminescent.

---

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Pleurobrachia}}
\]

---

# 4. Platyhelminthes

## Characteristics

- Flatworms.
- Bilateral symmetry.
- Triploblastic.
- Acoelomate.

---

Examples:

- Planaria
- Taenia (tapeworm)

---

# 5. Aschelminthes (Nematoda)

## Characteristics

- Roundworms.
- Pseudocoelomate.
- Complete digestive system.

---

Examples:

- Ascaris
- Wuchereria

---

# 6. Annelida

## Characteristics

- Segmented worms.
- True coelom.
- Organ system level.

---

Examples:

- Earthworm
- Leech

---

# 7. Arthropoda

## Characteristics

- Largest animal phylum.
- Jointed appendages.
- Chitinous exoskeleton.

---

Examples:

- Cockroach
- Butterfly
- Crab

---

# 8. Mollusca

## Characteristics

- Soft-bodied animals.
- Muscular foot present.
- Shell usually present.

---

Examples:

- Snail
- Octopus

---

# 9. Echinodermata

## Characteristics

- Marine animals.
- Spiny skin.
- Water vascular system.

---

Examples:

- Starfish
- Sea urchin

---

# PART C: CHORDATES

---

# 10. Chordata

## Characteristics

Chordates possess:

1. Notochord
2. Dorsal hollow nerve cord
3. Pharyngeal gill slits
4. Post-anal tail

---

# Classification of Chordates

Chordates are divided into:

1. Urochordata
2. Cephalochordata
3. Vertebrata

---

# PART D: VERTEBRATES

---

# 11. Vertebrata

## Characteristics

- Backbone present.
- Well-developed nervous system.
- Internal skeleton present.

---

Classes:

1. Pisces
2. Amphibia
3. Reptilia
4. Aves
5. Mammalia

---

# 1. Pisces

## Characteristics

- Aquatic animals.
- Fins for movement.
- Gills for respiration.
- Cold-blooded.

---

Examples:

- Shark
- Rohu

---

# 2. Amphibia

## Characteristics

- Live on land and water.
- Moist skin.
- Respire through lungs and skin.

---

Examples:

- Frog
- Salamander

---

# 3. Reptilia

## Characteristics

- Dry, scaly skin.
- Cold-blooded.
- Internal fertilisation.

---

Examples:

- Snake
- Lizard
- Turtle

---

# 4. Aves

## Characteristics

- Feathers present.
- Forelimbs modified into wings.
- Warm-blooded.
- Eggs with shells.

---

Examples:

- Sparrow
- Pigeon

---

# 5. Mammalia

## Characteristics

- Hair present.
- Mammary glands present.
- Warm-blooded.
- Mostly give birth to young ones.

---

Examples:

- Human
- Cow
- Whale

---

# PART E: DIFFERENCES

---

# Chordates vs Non-Chordates

| Chordates                     | Non-Chordates      |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Notochord present             | Notochord absent   |
| Dorsal nerve cord             | Ventral nerve cord |
| Pharyngeal gill slits present | Usually absent     |
| Backbone usually present      | Backbone absent    |

---

# Cold-blooded vs Warm-blooded Animals

| Cold-blooded               | Warm-blooded                  |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Body temperature changes   | Maintain constant temperature |
| Pisces, Amphibia, Reptilia | Aves, Mammalia                |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing chordates and non-chordates.
- Forgetting notochord is the main chordate feature.
- Mixing body cavity types.
- Confusing Pisces and Amphibia.
- Forgetting arthropods have jointed appendages.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Animals are classified based on body organisation and evolutionary relationships.
- Non-chordates lack notochord.
- Chordates possess notochord and dorsal nerve cord.
- Vertebrates are divided into Pisces, Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves and Mammalia.
- Classification helps understand animal diversity.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Animal classification reveals the evolutionary relationships among organisms.}
