# Introduction

Real numbers are all numbers that can be represented on the number line.

This chapter covers:

- Rational and irrational numbers
- Real number system
- Laws of exponents
- Fractional exponents
- Scientific notation

---

# PART A: NUMBER SYSTEM

---

# 1. Natural Numbers

## Definition

Numbers used for counting are called natural numbers.

---

Set of natural numbers:

\[
\boxed{
N=\{1,2,3,4,5,\dots\}
}
\]

---

# 2. Whole Numbers

## Definition

Natural numbers together with zero are called whole numbers.

---

\[
\boxed{
W=\{0,1,2,3,4,\dots\}
}
\]

---

# 3. Integers

## Definition

Numbers containing positive numbers, negative numbers and zero are called integers.

---

\[
\boxed{
Z=\{\dots,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,\dots\}
}
\]

---

# 4. Rational Numbers

## Definition

A number that can be expressed in the form:

\[
\boxed{
\frac pq
}
\]

where:

- p and q are integers
- q ≠ 0

is called a rational number.

---

Examples:

\[
\frac12,\frac{-3}{5},4
\]

---

Decimal expansion:

Rational numbers have:

- Terminating decimals

Example:

\[
\frac12=0.5
\]

- Non-terminating repeating decimals

Example:

\[
\frac13=0.333...
\]

---

# 5. Irrational Numbers

## Definition

Numbers that cannot be expressed as a ratio of two integers are called irrational numbers.

---

Examples:

\[
\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\pi
\]

---

Decimal expansion:

\[
\boxed{\text{Non-terminating and non-repeating}}
\]

---

# PART B: REAL NUMBERS

---

# 6. Real Numbers

## Definition

The collection of all rational and irrational numbers is called real numbers.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed R
\]

---

Relationship:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Real Numbers}
=

\text{Rational Numbers} +
\text{Irrational Numbers}
}
\]

---

# 7. Hierarchy of Real Numbers

\[
\boxed{
Natural
\subset
Whole
\subset
Integers
\subset
Rational
\subset
Real
}
\]

---

# PART C: REPRESENTATION ON NUMBER LINE

---

# 8. Number Line

A straight line on which numbers are represented according to their values is called a number line.

---

Properties:

- Positive numbers lie on right side of zero.
- Negative numbers lie on left side of zero.

---

# 9. Representation of Irrational Numbers

Example:

Representation of:

\[
\sqrt2
\]

Steps:

1. Draw a right-angled triangle with sides 1 unit.
2. Find hypotenuse using Pythagoras theorem.
3. Use compass to locate √2 on number line.

---

# PART D: LAWS OF EXPONENTS

---

# 10. Exponent

## Definition

An exponent shows how many times a number is multiplied by itself.

---

Example:

\[
2^3=2\times2\times2
\]

---

Here:

2 = base

3 = exponent

---

# 11. Laws of Exponents

---

# Law 1: Product of Powers

\[
\boxed{
a^m\times a^n=a^{m+n}
}
\]

---

Example:

\[
2^3\times2^2
\]

\[
=2^{3+2}
\]

\[
=2^5
\]

---

# Law 2: Division of Powers

\[
\boxed{
\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}
}
\]

---

Example:

\[
\frac{x^5}{x^2}=x^3
\]

---

# Law 3: Power of a Power

\[
\boxed{
(a^m)^n=a^{mn}
}
\]

---

Example:

\[
(2^3)^2=2^6
\]

---

# Law 4: Zero Exponent

\[
\boxed{
a^0=1
}
\]

where:

\[
a\neq0
\]

---

# Law 5: Negative Exponent

\[
\boxed{
a^{-n}=\frac1{a^n}
}
\]

---

Example:

\[
2^{-3}=\frac1{8}
\]

---

# PART E: FRACTIONAL EXPONENTS

---

# 12. Fractional Powers

Fractional exponents represent roots.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
a^{\frac1n}=\sqrt[n]{a}
}
\]

---

Examples:

\[
16^{1/2}=\sqrt{16}=4
\]

---

# 13. General Fractional Exponent

\[
\boxed{
a^{\frac mn}
=

\sqrt[n]{a^m}
}
\]

---

Example:

\[
8^{2/3}
\]

\[
=(\sqrt[3]{8})^2
\]

\[
=2^2
\]

\[
=4
\]

---

# PART F: SCIENTIFIC NOTATION

---

# 14. Scientific Notation

## Definition

A method of writing very large or very small numbers using powers of 10 is called scientific notation.

---

Form:

\[
\boxed{
a\times10^n
}
\]

where:

\[
1\leq a<10
\]

---

# Examples

---

Large number:

\[
5000000
\]

Scientific form:

\[
\boxed{
5\times10^6
}
\]

---

Small number:

\[
0.000003
\]

Scientific form:

\[
\boxed{
3\times10^{-6}
}
\]

---

# 15. Operations in Scientific Notation

---

## Multiplication

\[
(a\times10^m)(b\times10^n)
\]

\[
=ab\times10^{m+n}
\]

---

Example:

\[
(2\times10^3)(3\times10^4)
\]

\[
=6\times10^7
\]

---

## Division

\[
\frac{a\times10^m}{b\times10^n}
\]

\[
=

\frac ab\times10^{m-n}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing rational and irrational numbers.
- Forgetting denominator cannot be zero in rational numbers.
- Adding exponents incorrectly during multiplication.
- Using wrong power sign in scientific notation.
- Confusing fractional powers with multiplication.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Real numbers include rational and irrational numbers.
- Rational numbers have terminating or repeating decimals.
- Irrational numbers have non-terminating, non-repeating decimals.
- Exponent laws simplify powers.
- Fractional exponents represent roots.
- Scientific notation helps write extremely large or small numbers.
