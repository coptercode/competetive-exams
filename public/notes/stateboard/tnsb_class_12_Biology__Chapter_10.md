# PART B: HUMAN INSULIN PRODUCTION

---

# 2. Insulin

## Definition

Insulin is a peptide hormone produced by the pancreas that regulates blood glucose levels.

---

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases blood glucose level}}
\]

---

# 3. Diabetes Mellitus

## Definition

A disease caused due to insufficient insulin production or inability of cells to respond to insulin is called diabetes mellitus.

---

Treatment:

- Insulin injection.
- Lifestyle management.

---

# 4. Recombinant Human Insulin

Human insulin is produced using recombinant DNA technology.

---

Produced by:

\[
\boxed{\textit{E. coli}}
\]

---

# 5. Production of Insulin

Steps:

1. Human insulin gene is isolated.
2. Gene is inserted into bacterial plasmid.
3. Recombinant plasmid is introduced into bacteria.
4. Bacteria produce insulin.
5. Insulin is purified.

---

# 6. Structure of Human Insulin

Insulin consists of:

- A chain
- B chain

Connected by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Disulphide bonds}}
\]

---

# PART C: VACCINE PRODUCTION

---

# 7. Vaccine

## Definition

A vaccine is a biological preparation that stimulates immunity against a specific disease.

---

Functions:

- Produces antibodies.
- Creates immune memory.

---

# 8. Recombinant Vaccines

Produced using biotechnology methods.

---

Examples:

- Hepatitis B vaccine.
- HPV vaccine.

---

# 9. Types of Vaccines

---

## Live Attenuated Vaccines

Contain weakened pathogens.

---

## Inactivated Vaccines

Contain killed pathogens.

---

## Subunit Vaccines

Contain specific antigen components.

---

# PART D: STEM CELL TECHNOLOGY

---

# 10. Stem Cells

## Definition

Undifferentiated cells that can divide and develop into specialized cells are called stem cells.

---

Characteristics:

- Self-renewal ability.
- Differentiation ability.

---

# 11. Types of Stem Cells

---

## Embryonic Stem Cells

Obtained from early embryos.

---

## Adult Stem Cells

Present in adult tissues.

Example:

Bone marrow stem cells.

---

# 12. Applications of Stem Cells

Used in:

- Tissue regeneration.
- Treatment of blood disorders.
- Research on diseases.

---

# PART E: GENE THERAPY

---

# 13. Gene Therapy

## Definition

A technique in which defective genes are replaced or corrected to treat genetic disorders is called gene therapy.

---

# 14. First Gene Therapy Trial

Disease:

\[
\boxed{\text{ADA deficiency}}
\]

---

Cause:

Defect in adenosine deaminase gene.

---

# 15. Steps of Gene Therapy

1. Identification of defective gene.
2. Introduction of normal gene.
3. Transfer into patient cells.
4. Expression of functional protein.

---

# PART F: BIOTECHNOLOGY IN AGRICULTURE

---

# 16. Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs)

## Definition

Organisms whose genetic material has been altered using genetic engineering are called GMOs.

---

Examples:

- GM crops.
- Transgenic animals.

---

# 17. Advantages of GM Crops

- Increased yield.
- Pest resistance.
- Disease resistance.
- Improved nutritional value.
- Stress tolerance.

---

# PART G: Bt CROPS

---

# 18. Bt Cotton

## Definition

A genetically modified cotton variety containing Bt toxin gene is called Bt cotton.

---

Gene source:

\[
\boxed{\textit{Bacillus thuringiensis}}
\]

---

# 19. Bt Toxin

Bt bacteria produce crystalline protein toxic to insects.

---

Mechanism:

1. Insect eats Bt toxin.
2. Toxin becomes active in insect gut.
3. Gut cells are damaged.
4. Insect dies.

---

# 20. Advantages of Bt Crops

- Protection against insect pests.
- Reduced pesticide use.
- Increased productivity.

---

# PART H: TRANSGENIC ANIMALS

---

# 21. Transgenic Animals

## Definition

Animals carrying foreign genes introduced through genetic engineering are called transgenic animals.

---

Examples:

- Transgenic mice.
- Transgenic fish.

---

# 22. Applications of Transgenic Animals

Used for:

1. Studying gene functions.
2. Producing medicines.
3. Testing vaccines.
4. Studying diseases.

---

# PART I: BIOSAFETY ISSUES

---

# 23. Biosafety

## Definition

Measures taken to ensure safe use of biotechnology products are called biosafety measures.

---

Concerns:

- Environmental effects.
- Health risks.
- Effects on biodiversity.

---

# 24. Genetically Modified Food Concerns

Possible issues:

- Allergic reactions.
- Gene transfer to other organisms.
- Impact on ecosystems.

---

# PART J: BIOPIRACY

---

# 25. Biopiracy

## Definition

Unauthorized use of biological resources and traditional knowledge without proper permission or compensation is called biopiracy.

---

Examples:

- Use of medicinal plants without benefit sharing.

---

# 26. Need to Prevent Biopiracy

- Protect biodiversity.
- Protect traditional knowledge.
- Ensure fair benefit sharing.

---

# PART K: PATENTS

---

# 27. Patent

## Definition

A legal right granted for an invention that prevents others from using it without permission is called a patent.

---

# 28. Importance of Patents

- Encourage innovation.
- Protect inventions.
- Provide commercial rights.

---

# Important Differences

## GMO vs Transgenic Organism

| GMO                            | Transgenic Organism              |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| Any organism with modified DNA | Organism containing foreign gene |
| Broad category                 | Specific type of GMO             |

---

## Stem Cells vs Normal Cells

| Stem Cells               | Normal Cells            |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Undifferentiated         | Differentiated          |
| Can form many cell types | Have specific functions |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing Bt toxin and Bt bacteria.
- Forgetting GMOs involve genetic modification.
- Mixing gene therapy and vaccination.
- Confusing patents and biopiracy.
- Forgetting insulin is produced using recombinant technology.
