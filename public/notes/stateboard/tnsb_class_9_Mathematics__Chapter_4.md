> **Subject:** Mathematics

# Introduction

Geometry is the branch of mathematics that deals with shapes, sizes, positions and properties of figures.

This chapter covers:

- Basic concepts of geometry
- Lines and angles
- Triangles
- Quadrilaterals
- Circles

---

# PART A: BASIC GEOMETRIC CONCEPTS

---

# 1. Point

## Definition

A point represents an exact location in space.

---

Properties:

- Has no length
- Has no breadth
- Has no thickness

---

Representation:

\[
\boxed{A}
\]

---

# 2. Line

## Definition

A straight path extending infinitely in both directions is called a line.

---

Symbol:

\[
\overleftrightarrow{AB}
\]

---

Properties:

- Has infinite length
- No endpoints

---

# 3. Line Segment

## Definition

A part of a line having two endpoints is called a line segment.

---

Symbol:

\[
\overline{AB}
\]

---

# 4. Ray

## Definition

A part of a line having one endpoint and extending infinitely in one direction is called a ray.

---

Example:

\[
\overrightarrow{AB}
\]

---

# PART B: ANGLES

---

# 5. Angle

## Definition

An angle is formed when two rays meet at a common endpoint.

---

Parts:

1. Arms
2. Vertex

---

# 6. Types of Angles

---

## Acute Angle

Angle less than:

\[
90^\circ
\]

Example:

\[
45^\circ
\]

---

## Right Angle

Angle equal to:

\[
\boxed{90^\circ}
\]

---

## Obtuse Angle

Angle between:

\[
90^\circ \text{ and }180^\circ
\]

---

## Straight Angle

Angle equal to:

\[
\boxed{180^\circ}
\]

---

## Reflex Angle

Angle greater than:

\[
180^\circ
\]

and less than:

\[
360^\circ
\]

---

# 7. Complementary Angles

Two angles whose sum is:

\[
\boxed{90^\circ}
\]

are called complementary angles.

---

Example:

\[
30^\circ+60^\circ=90^\circ
\]

---

# 8. Supplementary Angles

Two angles whose sum is:

\[
\boxed{180^\circ}
\]

are called supplementary angles.

---

Example:

\[
120^\circ+60^\circ=180^\circ
\]

---

# PART C: PARALLEL LINES AND TRANSVERSALS

---

# 9. Parallel Lines

## Definition

Two lines in the same plane that never meet are called parallel lines.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{l\parallel m}
\]

---

# 10. Transversal

## Definition

A line that intersects two or more lines at different points is called a transversal.

---

# 11. Angles Formed by a Transversal

---

## Corresponding Angles

They are equal when lines are parallel.

\[
\boxed{\angle1=\angle2}
\]

---

## Alternate Interior Angles

They are equal.

---

## Co-interior Angles

Their sum is:

\[
\boxed{180^\circ}
\]

---

# PART D: TRIANGLES

---

# 12. Triangle

## Definition

A polygon with three sides and three angles is called a triangle.

---

Sum of angles:

\[
\boxed{180^\circ}
\]

---

# 13. Types of Triangles

---

## Based on Sides

### Scalene Triangle

All sides unequal.

---

### Isosceles Triangle

Two sides equal.

Properties:

- Base angles are equal.

---

### Equilateral Triangle

All sides equal.

Properties:

Each angle:

\[
\boxed{60^\circ}
\]

---

## Based on Angles

---

### Acute Triangle

All angles less than:

\[
90^\circ
\]

---

### Right Triangle

One angle:

\[
90^\circ
\]

---

### Obtuse Triangle

One angle greater than:

\[
90^\circ
\]

---

# 14. Congruence of Triangles

## Definition

Two triangles are congruent if they have the same shape and size.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{\cong}
\]

---

# 15. Criteria of Congruence

---

## SSS Criterion

Three sides of one triangle equal three sides of another triangle.

---

## SAS Criterion

Two sides and included angle are equal.

---

## ASA Criterion

Two angles and included side are equal.

---

## RHS Criterion

For right triangles:

- Right angle
- Hypotenuse
- One side

are equal.

---

# PART E: QUADRILATERALS

---

# 16. Quadrilateral

## Definition

A polygon with four sides is called a quadrilateral.

---

Sum of interior angles:

\[
\boxed{360^\circ}
\]

---

# 17. Types of Quadrilaterals

---

## Parallelogram

A quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel.

---

Properties:

- Opposite sides equal
- Opposite angles equal
- Diagonals bisect each other

---

# 18. Rectangle

Properties:

- Opposite sides equal
- All angles are 90°
- Diagonals are equal

---

# 19. Square

Properties:

- All sides equal
- All angles 90°
- Diagonals equal and perpendicular

---

# 20. Rhombus

Properties:

- All sides equal
- Opposite angles equal
- Diagonals perpendicular

---

# 21. Trapezium

A quadrilateral having one pair of parallel sides.

---

# 22. Midpoint Theorem

## Statement

The line segment joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and equal to half of it.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
DE=\frac12 BC
}
\]

---

# PART F: CIRCLES

---

# 23. Circle

## Definition

A circle is a set of points in a plane at equal distance from a fixed point.

---

Fixed point:

\[
\boxed{\text{Centre}}
\]

---

# 24. Important Parts of Circle

---

## Radius

Distance from centre to any point on circle.

---

## Diameter

A chord passing through the centre.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
d=2r
}
\]

---

## Chord

A line segment joining two points on a circle.

---

## Arc

A part of circumference.

---

# 25. Properties of Chords

- Equal chords are at equal distances from the centre.
- Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre.
- The perpendicular from centre to a chord bisects the chord.

---

# 26. Cyclic Quadrilateral

## Definition

A quadrilateral whose all vertices lie on a circle is called a cyclic quadrilateral.

---

Property:

Opposite angles are supplementary.

\[
\boxed{
\angle A+\angle C=180^\circ
}
\]

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Find the third angle of a triangle if two angles are:

\[
50^\circ,\ 60^\circ
\]

Solution:

\[
Third\ angle=180^\circ-(50^\circ+60^\circ)
\]

\[
=\boxed{70^\circ}
\]

---

## Example 2

Each angle of an equilateral triangle is:

\[
\boxed{60^\circ}
\]

---

## Example 3

Find diameter if radius is 5 cm.

\[
d=2r
\]

\[
=2(5)
\]

\[
\boxed{10cm}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing line, ray and line segment.
- Forgetting triangle angle sum is 180°.
- Mixing congruence and similarity.
- Forgetting opposite angles of cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.
- Confusing radius and diameter.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Geometry studies shapes and their properties.
- Lines and angles form the foundation of geometry.
- Triangles are classified by sides and angles.
- Congruence proves two triangles have equal shape and size.
- Quadrilaterals have four sides and angle sum 360°.
- Circles contain chords, arcs, radius and diameter.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Geometry helps us understand the shapes and structures around us.}
}
