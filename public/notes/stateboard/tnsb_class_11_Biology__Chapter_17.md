# Chapter 17: Locomotion and Movement

> **Subject:** Biology  
> **Class:** 11  
> **Chapter:** Locomotion and Movement

---

# Introduction

Movement is one of the important characteristics of living organisms. Animals show different types of movements that help them survive, obtain food and reproduce.

This chapter covers:

- Types of movements
- Muscular movement
- Skeletal muscle
- Contractile proteins
- Mechanism of muscle contraction
- Skeletal system
- Joints
- Muscular and skeletal disorders

---

# PART A: TYPES OF MOVEMENT

---

# 1. Movement

## Definition

A change in position or posture of an organism or its body parts is called movement.

---

# 2. Locomotion

## Definition

The movement of an organism from one place to another is called locomotion.

---

Examples:

- Walking
- Swimming
- Flying

---

# 3. Types of Movement

Animals show three major types of movement:

1. Amoeboid movement
2. Ciliary movement
3. Muscular movement

---

# PART B: AMOEBOID MOVEMENT

---

# 4. Amoeboid Movement

## Definition

Movement using temporary projections of cytoplasm called pseudopodia is called amoeboid movement.

---

Examples:

- Amoeba
- White blood cells

---

Structure involved:

\[
\boxed{\text{Pseudopodia}}
\]

---

# PART C: CILIARY MOVEMENT

---

# 5. Ciliary Movement

## Definition

Movement produced by coordinated beating of cilia is called ciliary movement.

---

Examples:

- Respiratory tract
- Oviduct

---

Functions:

- Movement of mucus.
- Movement of ovum.

---

# PART D: FLAGELLAR MOVEMENT

---

# 6. Flagellar Movement

## Definition

Movement produced by flagella is called flagellar movement.

---

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Sperm cells}}
\]

---

Function:

- Helps in movement of sperm.

---

# PART E: MUSCULAR MOVEMENT

---

# 7. Muscular Movement

## Definition

Movement produced by contraction and relaxation of muscles is called muscular movement.

---

Examples:

- Walking
- Running
- Heartbeat

---

# PART F: SKELETAL MUSCLE

---

# 8. Skeletal Muscle

## Definition

Muscles attached to bones that help in body movement are called skeletal muscles.

---

Characteristics:

- Voluntary muscles.
- Striated appearance.
- Multinucleate cells.

---

# 9. Structure of Skeletal Muscle

A skeletal muscle consists of:

1. Muscle fibres
2. Myofibrils
3. Sarcomeres

---

# 10. Sarcomere

## Definition

The functional unit of skeletal muscle is called sarcomere.

---

Contains:

- Actin filaments
- Myosin filaments

---

# PART G: CONTRACTILE PROTEINS

---

# 11. Actin

## Definition

Actin is a thin filament protein involved in muscle contraction.

---

# 12. Myosin

## Definition

Myosin is a thick filament protein responsible for pulling actin during contraction.

---

# 13. Muscle Proteins

Main proteins:

1. Actin
2. Myosin
3. Troponin
4. Tropomyosin

---

# PART H: MECHANISM OF MUSCLE CONTRACTION

---

# 14. Sliding Filament Theory

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{H.E. Huxley and A.F. Huxley}}
\]

---

## Definition

Muscle contraction occurs by sliding of actin filaments over myosin filaments.

---

# 15. Steps of Muscle Contraction

---

## Step 1: Nerve Stimulation

Nerve impulse reaches muscle.

---

## Step 2: Calcium Release

Calcium ions are released from sarcoplasmic reticulum.

---

## Step 3: Actin-Myosin Interaction

Myosin binds with actin forming cross bridges.

---

## Step 4: Filament Sliding

Actin moves over myosin causing contraction.

---

## Step 5: Relaxation

Calcium returns and muscle relaxes.

---

# 16. Role of ATP

ATP provides energy required for:

- Cross bridge formation.
- Muscle contraction.
- Relaxation.

---

# PART I: SKELETAL SYSTEM

---

# 17. Skeletal System

## Definition

The framework of bones and cartilage that supports the body is called skeletal system.

---

Functions:

1. Provides support.
2. Protects internal organs.
3. Helps movement.
4. Stores minerals.
5. Produces blood cells.

# PART L: MUSCULAR AND SKELETAL DISORDERS

---

# 23. Myasthenia Gravis

## Definition

An autoimmune disorder affecting neuromuscular communication is called myasthenia gravis.

---

Symptoms:

- Muscle weakness.
- Fatigue.

---

# 24. Tetany

## Definition

A condition involving involuntary muscle contractions due to low calcium levels is called tetany.

---

Cause:

\[
\boxed{\text{Low calcium concentration}}
\]

---

# 25. Muscular Dystrophy

## Definition

A genetic disorder causing progressive weakening of muscles is called muscular dystrophy.

---

Cause:

- Genetic mutation.

---

# 26. Arthritis

## Definition

Inflammation of joints is called arthritis.

---

Symptoms:

- Joint pain.
- Swelling.
- Stiffness.

---

# 27. Osteoporosis

## Definition

A disease causing reduced bone density and increased fracture risk is called osteoporosis.

---

Cause:

- Loss of calcium from bones.

---

# 28. Gout

## Definition

A disorder caused by accumulation of uric acid crystals in joints is called gout.

---

Symptoms:

- Severe joint pain.
- Inflammation.

---

# Important Differences

## Tendon vs Ligament

| Tendon                  | Ligament              |
| ----------------------- | --------------------- |
| Connects muscle to bone | Connects bone to bone |
| Strong and less elastic | More elastic          |

---

## Skeletal Muscle vs Smooth Muscle

| Skeletal Muscle   | Smooth Muscle     |
| ----------------- | ----------------- |
| Voluntary         | Involuntary       |
| Striated          | Non-striated      |
| Attached to bones | Present in organs |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing locomotion and movement.
- Forgetting sarcomere is the functional unit of muscle.
- Mixing actin and myosin functions.
- Confusing tendons and ligaments.
- Forgetting osteoporosis affects bones.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Movement allows organisms to perform life activities.
- Muscles produce movement through contraction.
- Actin and myosin are important contractile proteins.
- Skeletal system provides support and protection.
- Joints allow different types of movements.
- Disorders can affect muscles, bones and joints.
