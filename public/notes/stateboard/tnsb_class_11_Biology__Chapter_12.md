> **Subject:** Biology  
> **Class:** 11  
> **Chapter:** Respiration in Plants

# Introduction

Respiration is a metabolic process in which food molecules are broken down to release energy in the form of ATP.

This chapter covers:

- Exchange of gases in plants
- Cellular respiration
- Glycolysis
- Fermentation
- Krebs cycle
- Electron transport system
- ATP generation
- Amphibolic pathway
- Respiratory quotient

---

# PART A: RESPIRATION IN PLANTS

---

# 1. Respiration

## Definition

Respiration is the process by which organic food substances are oxidised to release energy.

---

General equation:

\[
C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2
\rightarrow
6CO_2 + 6H_2O + Energy(ATP)
\]

---

# 2. Importance of Respiration

Respiration provides:

- Energy for growth.
- Energy for metabolism.
- ATP for cellular activities.

---

# PART B: EXCHANGE OF GASES IN PLANTS

---

# 3. Gas Exchange

Plants exchange gases through:

- Stomata
- Lenticels
- Root surface

---

Unlike animals, plants do not have specialised respiratory organs.

---

# 4. Stomata

## Definition

Small openings present on leaves that help in gas exchange are called stomata.

---

Functions:

- Exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide.
- Regulation of water loss.

---

# PART C: TYPES OF RESPIRATION

---

# 5. Aerobic Respiration

## Definition

Respiration that occurs in the presence of oxygen is called aerobic respiration.

---

Products:

- Carbon dioxide
- Water
- Large amount of ATP

---

Occurs mainly in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Mitochondria}}
\]

---

# 6. Anaerobic Respiration

## Definition

Respiration occurring without oxygen is called anaerobic respiration.

---

Produces:

- Less ATP

Examples:

- Fermentation in yeast

---

# PART D: CELLULAR RESPIRATION

---

# 7. Steps of Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration occurs in three major stages:

1. Glycolysis
2. Krebs cycle
3. Electron transport system

---

# PART E: GLYCOLYSIS

---

# 8. Glycolysis

## Definition

The breakdown of glucose into pyruvic acid is called glycolysis.

---

Discovered by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Embden, Meyerhof and Parnas}}
\]

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{EMP pathway}}
\]

---

Site:

\[
\boxed{\text{Cytoplasm}}
\]

---

# 9. Steps of Glycolysis

Glucose:

\[
6C
\]

is converted into:

\[
2 \times Pyruvic\ acid
\]

---

Products:

- 2 ATP (net gain)
- 2 NADH
- 2 Pyruvate

---

# PART F: FERMENTATION

---

# 10. Fermentation

## Definition

Incomplete breakdown of glucose without oxygen is called fermentation.

---

Occurs in:

- Yeast
- Some bacteria

---

# 11. Types of Fermentation

---

## Alcoholic Fermentation

Occurs in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Yeast}}
\]

Products:

- Ethanol
- CO₂

---

Equation:

Glucose → Ethanol + CO₂ + Energy

---

## Lactic Acid Fermentation

Occurs in:

- Muscle cells during oxygen shortage
- Bacteria

Product:

\[
\boxed{\text{Lactic acid}}
\]

---

# PART G: KREBS CYCLE

---

# 12. Krebs Cycle

## Definition

The cyclic pathway in which acetyl-CoA is oxidised to release energy is called Krebs cycle.

---

Discovered by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Hans Krebs}}
\]

---

Site:

\[
\boxed{\text{Mitochondrial matrix}}
\]

---

# 13. Steps of Krebs Cycle

Main events:

1. Acetyl-CoA combines with oxaloacetic acid.
2. Series of reactions occur.
3. Oxaloacetic acid is regenerated.

---

Products per glucose molecule:

- ATP
- NADH
- FADH₂
- CO₂

---

# PART H: ELECTRON TRANSPORT SYSTEM (ETS)

---

# 14. Electron Transport System

## Definition

A series of electron carriers present in inner mitochondrial membrane that generate ATP is called ETS.

---

Site:

\[
\boxed{\text{Inner mitochondrial membrane}}
\]

---

# 15. Oxidative Phosphorylation

## Definition

Formation of ATP using energy from electron transfer is called oxidative phosphorylation.

---

Final electron acceptor:

\[
\boxed{\text{Oxygen}}
\]

---

Product:

\[
\boxed{\text{Water}}
\]

---

# PART I: ATP GENERATION

---

# 16. ATP

## Definition

ATP is the energy currency of the cell.

---

Full form:

\[
\boxed{\text{Adenosine Triphosphate}}
\]

---

ATP provides energy for:

- Active transport
- Growth
- Synthesis reactions

---

# 17. Energy Yield in Aerobic Respiration

One glucose molecule produces approximately:

\[
\boxed{36-38\ ATP}
\]

---

# PART J: AMPHIBOLIC PATHWAY

---

# 18. Amphibolic Pathway

## Definition

A metabolic pathway that functions in both breakdown and synthesis of molecules is called an amphibolic pathway.

---

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Krebs cycle}}
\]

---

Importance:

- Provides intermediates for biosynthesis.
- Breaks down carbohydrates, fats and proteins.

---

# PART K: RESPIRATORY QUOTIENT (RQ)

---

# 19. Respiratory Quotient

## Definition

The ratio of volume of carbon dioxide released to volume of oxygen consumed during respiration is called respiratory quotient.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
RQ=
\frac{CO_2\ released}{O_2\ consumed}
}
\]

---

# 20. RQ Values

---

## Carbohydrates

\[
RQ=1
\]

Example:

Glucose

---

## Fats

\[
RQ<1
\]

Approximately:

\[
0.7
\]

---

## Proteins

\[
RQ\approx0.8
\]

---

# PART L: RESPIRATION DURING SEED GERMINATION

---

# 21. Seed Germination

During germination:

- Respiration rate increases.
- Stored food is broken down.
- Energy is produced for growth.

---

Factors affecting respiration:

- Water availability
- Temperature
- Oxygen supply

---

# Important Differences

## Aerobic vs Anaerobic Respiration

| Aerobic Respiration           | Anaerobic Respiration   |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Requires oxygen               | Does not require oxygen |
| More ATP produced             | Less ATP produced       |
| Complete breakdown            | Partial breakdown       |
| Occurs mainly in mitochondria | Occurs in cytoplasm     |

---

## Glycolysis vs Krebs Cycle

| Glycolysis          | Krebs Cycle                      |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Occurs in cytoplasm | Occurs in mitochondria           |
| Glucose breakdown   | Acetyl-CoA oxidation             |
| Produces pyruvate   | Produces CO₂ and energy carriers |

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing glycolysis and Krebs cycle sites.
- Forgetting oxygen is final electron acceptor.
- Mixing aerobic and anaerobic respiration.
- Forgetting fermentation occurs without oxygen.
- Confusing ATP production stages.

- Respiration releases energy from food molecules.
- Glycolysis occurs in cytoplasm.
- Krebs cycle occurs in mitochondrial matrix.
- ETS produces maximum ATP.
- Fermentation occurs without oxygen.
- Respiratory quotient indicates the type of respiratory substrate.
