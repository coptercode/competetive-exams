# Introduction

Statistics is the branch of mathematics that deals with collection, organisation, presentation, analysis and interpretation of data.

This chapter covers:

- Collection of data
- Frequency distribution
- Arithmetic mean
- Median
- Mode

---

# PART A: COLLECTION OF DATA

---

# 1. Data

## Definition

A collection of facts, figures or information is called data.

---

Examples:

- Marks of students
- Population of cities
- Heights of people

---

# 2. Types of Data

Data is classified into:

1. Primary data
2. Secondary data

---

# 3. Primary Data

## Definition

Data collected directly by the investigator for a specific purpose is called primary data.

---

Examples:

- Survey conducted by a teacher
- Direct measurements

---

# 4. Secondary Data

## Definition

Data collected by someone else and used by an investigator is called secondary data.

---

Examples:

- Census reports
- Government records

---

# PART B: ORGANISATION OF DATA

---

# 5. Raw Data

## Definition

Data collected in its original form is called raw data.

---

Example:

\[
5,8,3,7,5,9,2
\]

---

# 6. Frequency

## Definition

The number of times an observation occurs is called its frequency.

---

Example:

Data:

\[
2,3,3,4,4,4
\]

Frequency of 4:

\[
\boxed{3}
\]

A table showing values and their frequencies is called a frequency distribution table.

---

| Value | Frequency |
| ----- | --------- |

# PART C: ARITHMETIC MEAN

---

# 8. Mean

## Definition

The average value of a set of observations is called arithmetic mean.

---

Formula for ungrouped data:

\[
\boxed{
Mean=\frac{\text{Sum of observations}}{\text{Number of observations}}
}
\]

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{\bar{x}}
\]

---

# 9. Mean of Ungrouped Data

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\bar{x}=\frac{\sum x}{n}
}
\]

---

Where:

\[
\sum x=\text{sum of values}
\]

\[
n=\text{number of values}
\]

---

# Example

Find mean of:

\[
5,7,9,11
\]

Solution:

\[
Mean=\frac{5+7+9+11}{4}
\]

\[
=\frac{32}{4}
\]

\[
\boxed{8}
\]

---

# PART D: MEAN OF GROUPED DATA

---

# 10. Mean Using Frequency

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\bar{x}=\frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}
}
\]

---

Where:

f = frequency

x = observation

---

# Example

| x   | f   | fx  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2   | 3   | 6   |
| 4   | 2   | 8   |
| 6   | 5   | 30  |

\[
\sum f=10
\]

\[
\sum fx=44
\]

Mean:

\[
=\frac{44}{10}
\]

\[
\boxed{4.4}
\]

---

# PART E: MEDIAN

---

# 11. Median

## Definition

The middle value of a data set arranged in ascending or descending order is called median.

---

# 12. Finding Median

Steps:

1. Arrange data in order.
2. Find middle observation.

---

# For Odd Number of Observations

Median position:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{n+1}{2}
}
\]

---

Example:

Data:

\[
2,5,7,9,11
\]

Middle value:

\[
\boxed{7}
\]

---

# For Even Number of Observations

Median:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\text{Two middle values}}{2}
}
\]

---

Example:

\[
2,4,6,8
\]

Median:

\[
=\frac{4+6}{2}
\]

\[
\boxed{5}
\]

---

# PART F: MODE

---

# 13. Mode

## Definition

The value that occurs most frequently in a data set is called mode.

---

Example:

\[
2,3,3,4,5
\]

Mode:

\[
\boxed{3}
\]

---

# 14. Importance of Mode

Used in:

- Finding most common size
- Market surveys
- Data analysis

---

# PART G: COMPARISON OF MEASURES

| Mean                       | Median              | Mode                 |
| -------------------------- | ------------------- | -------------------- |
| Average value              | Middle value        | Most repeated value  |
| Uses all observations      | Depends on position | Depends on frequency |
| Affected by extreme values | Less affected       | Not affected much    |

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting to arrange data before finding median.
- Using wrong formula for grouped and ungrouped data.
- Confusing mean with median.
- Choosing largest value instead of most frequent value for mode.
- Forgetting frequency in calculations.

---

- Statistics deals with collection and analysis of data.
- Data can be primary or secondary.
- Mean gives average value.
- Median gives middle value.
- Mode gives most frequently occurring value.
- Frequency tables help organise large data.
