# Chapter 6: Anatomy of Flowering Plants

> **Subject:** Biology  
> **Class:** 11  
> **Chapter:** Anatomy of Flowering Plants

# Introduction

Anatomy is the study of the internal structure of plants. Plant anatomy helps us understand the arrangement and functions of different tissues inside plant organs.

This chapter covers:

- Plant tissues
- Tissue systems
- Anatomy of dicot plants
- Anatomy of monocot plants
- Secondary growth

---

# PART A: PLANT TISSUES

---

# 1. Tissue

## Definition

A group of similar cells performing a specific function is called a tissue.

---

Plant tissues are classified into:

1. Meristematic tissues
2. Permanent tissues

---

# PART B: MERISTEMATIC TISSUES

---

# 2. Meristematic Tissue

## Definition

A tissue containing actively dividing cells is called meristematic tissue.

---

Characteristics:

- Cells divide continuously.
- Dense cytoplasm.
- Large nucleus.
- Vacuoles absent or very small.
- Thin cell wall.

---

# 3. Types of Meristematic Tissue

---

## A. Apical Meristem

Location:

- Root tip
- Shoot tip

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increase in length}}
\]

---

## B. Lateral Meristem

Location:

- Cambium
- Cork cambium

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increase in girth}}
\]

---

## C. Intercalary Meristem

Location:

- Internodes
- Leaf bases

Function:

- Growth of internodes.

---

# PART C: PERMANENT TISSUES

---

# 4. Permanent Tissue

## Definition

Tissues formed from meristematic cells that have lost the ability to divide are called permanent tissues.

---

Types:

1. Simple permanent tissue
2. Complex permanent tissue

---

# PART D: SIMPLE PERMANENT TISSUES

---

# 5. Parenchyma

## Characteristics

- Living cells.
- Thin cell wall.
- Large intercellular spaces.

---

Functions:

- Storage of food.
- Photosynthesis when chloroplasts are present.

---

Types:

## Chlorenchyma

Parenchyma containing chloroplasts.

---

## Aerenchyma

Parenchyma with large air spaces.

---

# 6. Collenchyma

## Characteristics

- Living cells.
- Unevenly thickened cell walls.

---

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Mechanical support and flexibility}}
\]

---

Location:

- Leaf stalk
- Young stems

---

# 7. Sclerenchyma

## Characteristics

- Dead cells.
- Thick lignified walls.

---

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Strength and protection}}
\]

---

Types:

1. Fibres
2. Sclereids

---

# PART E: COMPLEX PERMANENT TISSUES

---

# 8. Complex Tissue

## Definition

A tissue made up of different types of cells working together is called complex tissue.

---

Types:

1. Xylem
2. Phloem

---

# 9. Xylem

## Function

Transports water and minerals from roots to other plant parts.

---

Components:

1. Tracheids
2. Vessels
3. Xylem fibres
4. Xylem parenchyma

---

# 10. Phloem

## Function

Transports food from leaves to other parts.

---

Components:

1. Sieve tubes
2. Companion cells
3. Phloem fibres
4. Phloem parenchyma

---

# PART F: TISSUE SYSTEMS

---

# 11. Tissue System

A group of tissues performing a common function is called a tissue system.

---

Types:

1. Epidermal tissue system
2. Ground tissue system
3. Vascular tissue system

---

# 12. Epidermal Tissue System

Includes:

- Epidermis
- Stomata
- Trichomes

---

Functions:

- Protection
- Prevents water loss
- Gas exchange

---

# 13. Ground Tissue System

Includes:

- Cortex
- Endodermis
- Pericycle
- Pith

---

Functions:

- Storage
- Support

---

# 14. Vascular Tissue System

Includes:

- Xylem
- Phloem

---

Functions:

- Transport of water and food

---

# PART G: ANATOMY OF DICOT ROOT

---

# 15. Dicot Root Structure

Layers:

1. Epidermis
2. Cortex
3. Endodermis
4. Pericycle
5. Vascular tissues
6. Pith

---

## Features

- Xylem is star-shaped.
- Phloem occurs between xylem arms.
- Small pith.

---

# PART H: ANATOMY OF MONOCOT ROOT

---

# 16. Monocot Root Structure

Features:

- Large pith.
- Many xylem bundles.
- Vascular bundles arranged in a ring.

---

Example:

Maize root

---

# PART I: ANATOMY OF DICOT STEM

---

# 17. Dicot Stem Structure

Layers:

1. Epidermis
2. Cortex
3. Endodermis
4. Pericycle
5. Vascular bundles
6. Pith

---

Features:

- Vascular bundles arranged in a ring.
- Open vascular bundles.
- Secondary growth possible.

---

Example:

Sunflower stem

---

# PART J: ANATOMY OF MONOCOT STEM

---

# 18. Monocot Stem Structure

Features:

- Vascular bundles scattered.
- Closed vascular bundles.
- No secondary growth.

---

Example:

Maize stem

---

# PART K: LEAF ANATOMY

---

# 19. Dorsiventral Leaf (Dicot Leaf)

Features:

- Upper epidermis
- Lower epidermis
- Mesophyll differentiated into:
  - Palisade tissue
  - Spongy tissue

---

Stomata:

Mostly present on lower surface.

---

# 20. Isobilateral Leaf (Monocot Leaf)

Features:

- Similar upper and lower surfaces.
- Mesophyll not differentiated.

---

Stomata:

Present on both surfaces.

---

# PART L: SECONDARY GROWTH

---

# 21. Secondary Growth

## Definition

Increase in thickness or girth of stem and root due to lateral meristem activity is called secondary growth.

---

Occurs mainly in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Dicot plants}}
\]

---

# 22. Vascular Cambium

Produces:

- Secondary xylem
- Secondary phloem

---

# 23. Cork Cambium

Produces:

- Cork cells
- Protective outer covering

---

# Important Differences

## Dicot Stem vs Monocot Stem

| Dicot Stem               | Monocot Stem               |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Vascular bundles in ring | Vascular bundles scattered |
| Secondary growth present | Secondary growth absent    |
| Open vascular bundles    | Closed vascular bundles    |

---

## Dicot Root vs Monocot Root

| Dicot Root                | Monocot Root        |
| ------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Few xylem bundles         | Many xylem bundles  |
| Small pith                | Large pith          |
| Secondary growth possible | No secondary growth |

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing xylem and phloem functions.
- Mixing monocot and dicot anatomy.
- Forgetting secondary growth occurs due to cambium.
- Confusing meristem types.
- Forgetting tissue system components.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Plant anatomy explains internal structures of plants.
- Meristematic tissues help in growth.
- Permanent tissues perform specialised functions.
- Xylem transports water and phloem transports food.
- Tissue systems organise plant structures.
- Dicot and monocot plants differ in internal anatomy.
