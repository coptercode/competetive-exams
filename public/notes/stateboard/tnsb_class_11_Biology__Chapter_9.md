# Chapter 9: Biomolecules

> **Subject:** Biology  
> **Class:** 11  
> **Chapter:** Biomolecules

---

# Introduction

Living organisms are made up of various chemical compounds called biomolecules. These molecules are essential for structure, metabolism, growth and reproduction.

This chapter covers:

- Chemical composition of living organisms
- Carbohydrates
- Proteins
- Lipids
- Nucleic acids
- Enzymes

---

# PART A: BIOMOLECULES

---

# 1. Biomolecules

## Definition

Organic compounds present in living organisms that are essential for life processes are called biomolecules.

---

Major biomolecules:

1. Carbohydrates
2. Proteins
3. Lipids
4. Nucleic acids

---

# 2. Chemical Composition of Cells

Cells contain:

- Water
- Minerals
- Organic compounds

---

Organic compounds include:

- Carbohydrates
- Proteins
- Lipids
- Nucleic acids

---

# PART B: CARBOHYDRATES

---

# 3. Carbohydrates

## Definition

Carbohydrates are organic compounds made up of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen that serve mainly as energy sources.

---

General formula:

\[
\boxed{(CH_2O)_n}
\]

---

Functions:

- Provide energy.
- Store food.
- Form structural components.

---

# 4. Classification of Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are classified into:

1. Monosaccharides
2. Disaccharides
3. Polysaccharides

---

# 5. Monosaccharides

## Definition

Simple sugars that cannot be broken down further are called monosaccharides.

---

Examples:

- Glucose
- Fructose
- Ribose

---

Importance:

Glucose is the main source of cellular energy.

---

# 6. Disaccharides

## Definition

Carbohydrates formed by joining two monosaccharides are called disaccharides.

---

Examples:

| Disaccharide | Components          |
| ------------ | ------------------- |
| Sucrose      | Glucose + Fructose  |
| Lactose      | Glucose + Galactose |
| Maltose      | Glucose + Glucose   |

---

# 7. Polysaccharides

## Definition

Complex carbohydrates made up of many monosaccharides are called polysaccharides.

---

Examples:

## Starch

- Storage carbohydrate in plants.

---

## Glycogen

- Storage carbohydrate in animals.

---

## Cellulose

- Structural component of plant cell wall.

---

# PART C: PROTEINS

---

# 8. Proteins

## Definition

Proteins are complex nitrogen-containing biomolecules made up of amino acids.

---

Elements:

- Carbon
- Hydrogen
- Oxygen
- Nitrogen
- Sulphur (sometimes)

---

# 9. Amino Acids

## Definition

The basic units of proteins are called amino acids.

---

General structure:

\[
\boxed{NH_2-CH(R)-COOH}
\]

---

Types:

1. Essential amino acids
2. Non-essential amino acids

---

# 10. Peptide Bond

## Definition

The bond formed between two amino acids is called a peptide bond.

---

Reaction:

Amino acid + Amino acid

↓

Dipeptide

---

# 11. Protein Structure

Proteins have four levels of organisation:

---

## 1. Primary Structure

Sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide chain.

---

## 2. Secondary Structure

Formation of:

- Alpha helix
- Beta pleated sheet

---

## 3. Tertiary Structure

Three-dimensional folding of protein.

---

## 4. Quaternary Structure

Arrangement of multiple polypeptide chains.

---

# 12. Functions of Proteins

| Protein     | Function         |
| ----------- | ---------------- |
| Enzymes     | Catalysis        |
| Haemoglobin | Oxygen transport |
| Antibodies  | Defence          |
| Collagen    | Support          |

---

# PART D: LIPIDS

---

# 13. Lipids

## Definition

Lipids are water-insoluble organic compounds mainly made of fatty acids and glycerol.

---

Examples:

- Fats
- Oils
- Waxes
- Steroids

---

# 14. Structure of Lipids

A triglyceride consists of:

1. Glycerol
2. Three fatty acids

---

# 15. Types of Fatty Acids

---

## Saturated Fatty Acids

- No double bonds.
- Usually solid.

Example:

Butter

---

## Unsaturated Fatty Acids

- One or more double bonds.
- Usually liquid.

Example:

Vegetable oils

---

# 16. Functions of Lipids

- Energy storage.
- Formation of cell membrane.
- Insulation.
- Hormone production.

---

# PART E: NUCLEIC ACIDS

---

# 17. Nucleic Acids

## Definition

Nucleic acids are biomolecules responsible for storing and transmitting genetic information.

---

Types:

1. DNA
2. RNA

---

# 18. Nucleotide

## Definition

The basic unit of nucleic acids is called nucleotide.

---

Components:

1. Nitrogenous base
2. Pentose sugar
3. Phosphate group

---

# 19. DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)

## Function

- Stores genetic information.
- Transfers hereditary characters.

---

Structure:

- Double helix.
- Two polynucleotide chains.

---

Nitrogen bases:

Purines:

- Adenine
- Guanine

Pyrimidines:

- Cytosine
- Thymine

---

# 20. RNA (Ribonucleic Acid)

## Function

- Protein synthesis.
- Gene expression.

---

Types:

1. mRNA
2. tRNA
3. rRNA

---

# Difference Between DNA and RNA

| DNA                        | RNA                     |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Double stranded            | Usually single stranded |
| Contains thymine           | Contains uracil         |
| Stores genetic information | Helps protein synthesis |

---

# PART F: ENZYMES

---

# 21. Enzymes

## Definition

Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up biochemical reactions.

---

Most enzymes are:

\[
\boxed{\text{Proteins}}
\]

---

# 22. Properties of Enzymes

- Highly specific.
- Work in small amounts.
- Not consumed during reaction.
- Affected by temperature and pH.

---

# 23. Enzyme Components

---

## Apoenzyme

Protein part of enzyme.

---

## Cofactor

Non-protein component required for enzyme activity.

---

Complete enzyme:

\[
\boxed{\text{Holoenzyme}}
\]

---

# 24. Mechanism of Enzyme Action

According to lock and key model:

Enzyme + Substrate

↓

Enzyme-substrate complex

↓

Product formation

---

# 25. Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity

---

## 1. Temperature

Very high temperature:

→ Enzyme denaturation

---

## 2. pH

Each enzyme works best at specific pH.

---

## 3. Substrate Concentration

Increasing substrate increases reaction rate up to a limit.

---

# Important Differences

## Carbohydrates vs Proteins

| Carbohydrates      | Proteins                        |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| Made of sugars     | Made of amino acids             |
| Main energy source | Structural and functional roles |

---

## DNA vs RNA

| DNA               | RNA             |
| ----------------- | --------------- |
| Double helix      | Single strand   |
| Deoxyribose sugar | Ribose sugar    |
| Contains thymine  | Contains uracil |

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing starch and glycogen.
- Forgetting amino acids form proteins.
- Mixing DNA and RNA functions.
- Forgetting enzymes are catalysts.
- Confusing nucleoside and nucleotide.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Biomolecules are essential chemical compounds of living organisms.
- Carbohydrates provide energy.
- Proteins perform structural and functional roles.
- Lipids store energy and form membranes.
- Nucleic acids carry genetic information.
- Enzymes regulate biochemical reactions.

\[
