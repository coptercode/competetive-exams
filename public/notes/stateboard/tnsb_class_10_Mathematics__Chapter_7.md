# Introduction

Mensuration is the branch of mathematics that deals with measurement of:

- Length
- Area
- Surface area
- Volume

This chapter covers:

- Surface area of solids
- Volume of solids
- Cylinder
- Cone
- Sphere
- Hemisphere
- Frustum of a cone

---

# PART A: BASIC CONCEPTS

---

# 1. Surface Area

The total area covered by the surface of a three-dimensional object is called surface area.

Types:

1. Curved Surface Area (CSA)
2. Total Surface Area (TSA)

---

# 2. Curved Surface Area (CSA)

Area of only the curved portion of a solid.

---

# 3. Total Surface Area (TSA)

Area of all surfaces of a solid.

\[
\boxed{
TSA=CSA+\text{Area of bases}
}
\]

---

# PART B: CYLINDER

---

# 4. Cylinder

## Definition

A solid having two circular bases connected by a curved surface is called a cylinder.

---

Terms:

- Radius = r
- Height = h

---

# 5. Curved Surface Area of Cylinder

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
CSA=2\pi rh
}
\]

---

# 6. Total Surface Area of Cylinder

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
TSA=2\pi r(h+r)
}
\]

---

# 7. Volume of Cylinder

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
V=\pi r^2h
}
\]

---

# Example

Find volume of cylinder:

Radius:

\[
r=7cm
\]

Height:

\[
h=10cm
\]

Using:

\[
V=\pi r^2h
\]

\[
=\frac{22}{7}\times7\times7\times10
\]

\[
\boxed{1540cm^3}
\]

---

# PART C: CONE

---

# 8. Cone

## Definition

A solid with a circular base and a vertex is called a cone.

---

Terms:

- Radius = r
- Height = h
- Slant height = l

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
l=\sqrt{r^2+h^2}
}
\]

---

# 9. Curved Surface Area of Cone

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
CSA=\pi rl
}
\]

---

# 10. Total Surface Area of Cone

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
TSA=\pi r(l+r)
}
\]

---

# 11. Volume of Cone

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
V=\frac13\pi r^2h
}
\]

---

# Example

Cone with:

\[
r=3cm
\]

\[
h=4cm
\]

Find slant height.

\[
l=\sqrt{r^2+h^2}
\]

\[
=\sqrt{9+16}
\]

\[
=\sqrt{25}
\]

\[
\boxed{5cm}
\]

---

# PART D: SPHERE

---

# 12. Sphere

## Definition

A three-dimensional object in which all points are at equal distance from the centre is called a sphere.

---

Radius:

\[
r
\]

---

# 13. Surface Area of Sphere

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
SA=4\pi r^2
}
\]

---

# 14. Volume of Sphere

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
V=\frac43\pi r^3
}
\]

---

# Example

Find surface area of sphere:

\[
r=7cm
\]

\[
SA=4\pi r^2
\]

\[
=4\times\frac{22}{7}\times49
\]

\[
\boxed{616cm^2}
\]

---

# PART E: HEMISPHERE

---

# 15. Hemisphere

## Definition

Half of a sphere is called a hemisphere.

---

# 16. Curved Surface Area of Hemisphere

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
CSA=2\pi r^2
}
\]

---

# 17. Total Surface Area of Hemisphere

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
TSA=3\pi r^2
}
\]

---

# 18. Volume of Hemisphere

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
V=\frac23\pi r^3
}
\]

---

# PART F: FRUSTUM OF CONE

---

# 19. Frustum

## Definition

A cone obtained by cutting the top portion of a cone parallel to the base is called a frustum.

---

Terms:

- Larger radius = R
- Smaller radius = r
- Height = h
- Slant height = l

---

# 20. Slant Height of Frustum

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
l=\sqrt{h^2+(R-r)^2}
}
\]

---

# 21. Curved Surface Area of Frustum

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
CSA=\pi(R+r)l
}
\]

---

# 22. Total Surface Area of Frustum

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
TSA=\pi(R+r)l+\pi(R^2+r^2)
}
\]

---

# 23. Volume of Frustum

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
V=
\frac13\pi h(R^2+r^2+Rr)
}
\]

---

# PART G: CONVERSION OF SOLIDS

---

# 24. Important Principle

When one solid is melted and converted into another:

\[
\boxed{
Volume\ remains\ constant
}
\]

---

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
V_1=V_2
}
\]

---

# Example

A sphere is melted to form smaller spheres.

Volume of large sphere:

\[
=

\text{Total volume of small spheres}
\]

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Find volume of a cone:

\[
r=6cm,\ h=7cm
\]

Formula:

\[
V=\frac13\pi r^2h
\]

\[
=\frac13\times\frac{22}{7}\times36\times7
\]

\[
\boxed{264cm^3}
\]

---

## Example 2

Find TSA of cylinder:

\[
r=5cm,\ h=10cm
\]

\[
TSA=2\pi r(h+r)
\]

\[
=2\pi(5)(15)
\]

\[
\boxed{150\pi cm^2}
\]

---

## Example 3

Find volume of sphere:

\[
r=3cm
\]

\[
V=\frac43\pi r^3
\]

\[
=\frac43\pi(27)
\]

\[
\boxed{36\pi cm^3}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing CSA and TSA.
- Forgetting cone slant height in surface area.
- Using diameter instead of radius.
- Forgetting volume conversion principle.
- Mixing hemisphere formulas with sphere formulas.
- Using incorrect units.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Mensuration deals with measurement of solid objects.
- Cylinders, cones and spheres have different surface area and volume formulas.
- Frustum is a truncated cone.
- Conversion problems use conservation of volume.
- Correct identification of radius, height and slant height is essential.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Mensuration connects geometry with real-world measurements.}
}
