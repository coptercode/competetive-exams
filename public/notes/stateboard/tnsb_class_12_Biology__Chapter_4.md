# Introduction

Inheritance is the process by which genetic information is transferred from parents to offspring. Variation produces differences among individuals and helps in evolution.

This chapter covers:

- Mendelian genetics
- Laws of inheritance
- Monohybrid and dihybrid crosses
- Incomplete dominance
- Codominance
- Multiple alleles
- Chromosome theory
- Sex determination
- Linkage and crossing over
- Sex-linked inheritance
- Genetic disorders

---

# PART A: HEREDITY AND VARIATION

---

# 1. Heredity

## Definition

The transmission of genetic characters from parents to offspring is called heredity.

---

# 2. Variation

## Definition

Differences among individuals of the same species are called variations.

---

Importance:

- Helps adaptation.
- Provides raw material for evolution.

---

# PART B: MENDELIAN GENETICS

---

# 3. Gregor Johann Mendel

Known as:

\[
\boxed{\text{Father of Genetics}}
\]

---

Experiment:

- Conducted on pea plants (_Pisum sativum_).

---

Reasons for selecting pea plant:

- Short life cycle.
- Clear contrasting characters.
- Self-pollination possible.
- Easy hybridisation.

---

# 4. Mendel's Terms

---

## Gene

Unit of heredity responsible for a character.

---

## Allele

Alternative form of a gene.

Example:

Tallness:

T

Dwarfness:

t

---

## Dominant Character

Character expressed in heterozygous condition.

Example:

T = Tall

---

## Recessive Character

Character hidden in heterozygous condition.

Example:

t = Dwarf

---

# PART C: MENDEL'S LAWS

---

# 5. Law of Dominance

## Definition

When two contrasting alleles are present together, only one allele expresses itself.

---

Example:

Tt → Tall plant

---

# 6. Law of Segregation

## Definition

The two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation.

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Law of purity of gametes}}
\]

---

# 7. Law of Independent Assortment

## Definition

Genes controlling different characters assort independently during gamete formation.

---

# PART D: MONOHYBRID CROSS

---

# 8. Monohybrid Cross

## Definition

A cross involving one pair of contrasting characters is called monohybrid cross.

---

Example:

Tall plant × Dwarf plant

---

Parents:

TT × tt

---

F₁ Generation:

All:

\[
\boxed{Tt\ (Tall)}
\]

---

F₂ Generation:

Tt × Tt

---

Phenotypic ratio:

\[
\boxed{3:1}
\]

Tall : Dwarf

---

Genotypic ratio:

\[
\boxed{1:2:1}
\]

TT : Tt : tt

---

# PART E: DIHYBRID CROSS

---

# 9. Dihybrid Cross

## Definition

A cross involving two pairs of contrasting characters is called dihybrid cross.

---

Example:

Seed colour and seed shape in pea.

---

Phenotypic ratio:

\[
\boxed{9:3:3:1}
\]

---

Shows:

\[
\boxed{\text{Independent assortment}}
\]

---

# PART F: NON-MENDELIAN INHERITANCE

---

# 10. Incomplete Dominance

## Definition

A condition where neither allele is completely dominant and an intermediate phenotype appears.

---

Example:

Flower colour in Snapdragon.

---

Cross:

Red × White

↓

Pink flowers

---

Phenotypic ratio:

\[
1:2:1
\]

---

# 11. Codominance

## Definition

A condition where both alleles express equally in heterozygous condition.

---

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{AB blood group}}
\]

---

Genotype:

IAIB

---

Both A and B antigens are expressed.

---

# 12. Multiple Alleles

## Definition

Presence of more than two alleles controlling a character is called multiple allelism.

---

Example:

ABO blood group system.

---

Alleles:

- IA
- IB
- i

---

# PART G: ABO BLOOD GROUP INHERITANCE

---

# 13. ABO Blood Groups

Controlled by three alleles:

IA, IB and i

---

| Blood Group | Genotype   |
| ----------- | ---------- |
| A           | IAIA / IAi |
| B           | IBIB / IBi |
| AB          | IAIB       |
| O           | ii         |

---

# PART H: PLEIOTROPY

---

# 14. Pleiotropy

## Definition

A single gene controlling multiple traits is called pleiotropy.

---

Example:

Phenylketonuria

---

# PART I: POLYGENIC INHERITANCE

---

# 15. Polygenic Inheritance

## Definition

A single trait controlled by multiple genes is called polygenic inheritance.

---

Example:

---

Characteristics:

- Continuous variation occurs.

# PART L: LINKAGE AND CROSSING OVER

---

# 21. Linkage

## Definition

The tendency of genes located on the same chromosome to be inherited together is called linkage.

---

Discovered by:

\[
\boxed{\text{T.H. Morgan}}
\]

---

# 22. Crossing Over

## Definition

Exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes is called crossing over.

---

Occurs during:

\[
\boxed{\text{Prophase I of meiosis}}
\]

---

Importance:

- Produces variation.

---

# PART M: SEX-LINKED INHERITANCE

---

# 23. Sex-linked Genes

Genes located on sex chromosomes are called sex-linked genes.

---

\[
\boxed{XO}

# Important Differences

## Dominance vs Codominance

| Dominance                | Codominance             |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| One allele masks another | Both alleles express    |
| Example: Tallness        | Example: AB blood group |

---

## Linkage vs Crossing Over

| Linkage                  | Crossing Over                |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| Genes inherited together | Exchange between chromosomes |
| Reduces variation        | Produces variation           |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing genotype and phenotype.
- Mixing dominance and codominance.
- Forgetting male determines sex in humans.
- Confusing linkage with crossing over.
- Forgetting haemophilia is X-linked.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Mendel established laws of inheritance.
- Genes are units of heredity present on chromosomes.
- Variations arise through recombination and mutation.
- Non-Mendelian inheritance includes incomplete dominance and codominance.
- Sex determination differs among organisms.
- Genetic disorders result from gene or chromosome abnormalities.
