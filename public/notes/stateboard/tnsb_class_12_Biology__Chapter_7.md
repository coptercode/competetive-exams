> **Subject:** Biology  
> **Class:** 12

# Introduction

Human health depends on the proper functioning of body systems and protection against diseases. Diseases may be caused by pathogens, genetic factors or lifestyle-related factors.

This chapter covers:

- Vector-borne diseases
- Infectious diseases
- Immunology
- Vaccines
- Cancer
- HIV and AIDS
- Substance abuse

---

# PART A: HEALTH AND DISEASE

---

# 1. Health

## Definition

Health is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease.

---

# 2. Disease

## Definition

A disease is a condition that affects normal functioning of the body.

---

# Types of Diseases

## Infectious Diseases

Caused by pathogens.

Examples:

- Bacteria
- Viruses
- Protozoans
- Fungi

---

## Non-infectious Diseases

Not caused by pathogens.

Examples:

- Cancer
- Diabetes
- Genetic disorders

---

# PART B: VECTOR-BORNE DISEASES

---

# 3. Vector

## Definition

An organism that carries and transmits pathogens from one host to another is called a vector.

---

Examples:

- Mosquito
- Housefly

---

# 4. Dengue

## Causative organism:

\[
\boxed{\text{Dengue virus}}
\]

---

Vector:

\[
\boxed{\textit{Aedes mosquito}}
\]

---

Symptoms:

- High fever.
- Headache.
- Muscle and joint pain.
- Skin rash.

---

Prevention:

- Remove stagnant water.
- Control mosquito breeding.

---

# 5. Chikungunya

## Causative organism:

\[
\boxed{\text{Chikungunya virus}}
\]

---

Vector:

\[
\boxed{\textit{Aedes mosquito}}
\]

---

Symptoms:

- Fever.
- Severe joint pain.
- Body pain.

---

# 6. Filariasis

## Causative organism:

\[

---

Vector:

\[

\[
\boxed{\textit{Ascaris lumbricoides}}

# PART D: IMMUNOLOGY

---

# 13. Immunity

## Definition

The ability of the body to defend itself against pathogens is called immunity.

---

# Types of Immunity

---

# 14. Innate Immunity

## Definition

Non-specific defence mechanisms present from birth are called innate immunity.

---

Examples:

- Skin barrier.
- Mucus.
- Phagocytosis.

---

# 15. Acquired Immunity

## Definition

Specific immunity developed after exposure to pathogens is called acquired immunity.

---

Characteristics:

- Specific.
- Has memory.
- Long-lasting.

---

# 16. Types of Acquired Immunity

---

## Active Immunity

Developed after exposure to antigen.

Example:

Vaccination.

---

## Passive Immunity

Antibodies received from another source.

Example:

Mother's antibodies to baby.

---

# PART E: VACCINES

---

# 17. Vaccine

## Definition

A preparation containing weakened or killed pathogens that stimulates immunity is called a vaccine.

---

Function:

- Produces antibodies.
- Creates immune memory.

---

Examples:

- BCG vaccine.
- Polio vaccine.
- Hepatitis vaccine.

---

# PART F: CANCER

---

# 18. Cancer

## Definition

Uncontrolled and abnormal growth of cells is called cancer.

---

# 19. Causes of Cancer

Factors:

- Genetic mutations.
- Radiation.
- Chemical carcinogens.
- Tobacco use.

---

# 20. Types of Cancer

---

## Carcinoma

Cancer of epithelial tissues.

---

## Sarcoma

Cancer of connective tissues.

---

## Leukemia

Cancer of blood-forming tissues.

---

# 21. Tumours

---

## Benign Tumour

- Slow growth.
- Does not spread.

---

## Malignant Tumour

- Rapid growth.
- Spreads to other tissues.

---

# 22. Cancer Detection

Methods:

- Biopsy.
- Imaging techniques.
- Blood tests.

---

# 23. Cancer Treatment

Methods:

## Surgery

Removal of tumour.

---

## Radiotherapy

Uses radiation to destroy cancer cells.

---

## Chemotherapy

Uses drugs to kill cancer cells.

---

# PART G: HIV AND AIDS

---

# 24. AIDS

## Full Form:

\[
\boxed{\text{Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome}}
\]

---

Cause:

\[
\boxed{\text{HIV}}
\]

(Human Immunodeficiency Virus)

---

# 25. HIV Infection

HIV attacks:

\[
\boxed{\text{CD4+ T lymphocytes}}
\]

---

Effect:

- Weakens immune system.
- Increases infections.

---

# 26. Transmission of HIV

Occurs through:

- Unprotected sexual contact.
- Infected blood transfusion.
- Sharing infected needles.
- Mother to child transmission.

---

# 27. Prevention of AIDS

- Safe sexual practices.
- Screening blood.
- Avoid sharing needles.
- Awareness programmes.

---

# PART H: SUBSTANCE ABUSE

---

# 28. Substance Abuse

## Definition

Excessive and harmful use of drugs or alcohol is called substance abuse.

---

Common substances:

- Alcohol.
- Opioids.
- Cannabinoids.
- Cocaine.

---

# 29. Causes of Substance Abuse

- Peer pressure.
- Stress.
- Curiosity.
- Depression.

---

# 30. Effects of Drug Abuse

Physical effects:

- Organ damage.
- Poor health.

Mental effects:

- Anxiety.
- Depression.
- Addiction.

---

# 31. Prevention of Substance Abuse

- Awareness.
- Counselling.
- Family support.
- Healthy lifestyle.

---

# Important Differences

## Innate vs Acquired Immunity

| Innate Immunity    | Acquired Immunity       |
| ------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Present from birth | Develops after exposure |
| Non-specific       | Specific                |
| No memory          | Has memory              |

---

## Benign vs Malignant Tumour

| Benign          | Malignant                |
| --------------- | ------------------------ |
| Does not spread | Spreads to other tissues |
| Slow growth     | Rapid growth             |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing vectors and pathogens.
- Forgetting HIV attacks CD4 cells.
- Mixing active and passive immunity.
- Confusing benign and malignant tumours.
- Forgetting vaccines create immune memory.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Diseases may be infectious or non-infectious.
- Vectors transmit many diseases.
- Immunity protects the body from pathogens.
- Vaccines help develop protection against diseases.
- Cancer results from uncontrolled cell division.
- HIV weakens immunity and causes AIDS.
- Substance abuse affects physical and mental health.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Good health requires disease prevention, immunity and responsible lifestyle choices.}
}
