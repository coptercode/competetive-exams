# Introduction

Organic reactions involve the breaking and formation of covalent bonds in carbon compounds.

Understanding organic reactions requires knowledge of:

- Electron movement
- Bond polarity
- Reaction intermediates
- Stability of charged species

This chapter covers:

- Electron displacement effects
- Inductive effect
- Electromeric effect
- Resonance
- Hyperconjugation
- Carbocations
- Carbanions
- Free radicals

---

# PART A: ELECTRON DISPLACEMENT EFFECTS

---

# 1. Electron Displacement

## Definition

The movement of electrons within an organic molecule due to the influence of atoms or groups is called electron displacement.

---

Types:

1. Inductive effect
2. Electromeric effect
3. Resonance effect
4. Hyperconjugation

---

# PART B: INDUCTIVE EFFECT

---

# 2. Inductive Effect

## Definition

The permanent displacement of sigma (σ) electrons along a carbon chain due to electronegativity differences is called inductive effect.

---

Characteristics:

- Operates through sigma bonds.
- Decreases with distance.
- Permanent effect.

---

# 3. Types of Inductive Effect

---

## +I Effect

Groups that release electrons through sigma bonds show +I effect.

---

Examples:

\[
-C(CH_3)_3 > -CH(CH_3)_2 > -C_2H_5 > -CH_3
\]

---

Electron donating groups:

- Alkyl groups

---

## -I Effect

Groups that withdraw electrons show -I effect.

---

Examples:

\[
-NO_2,\ -CN,\ -COOH,\ -F
\]

---

Electron withdrawing groups:

- Halogens
- Nitro group
- Carbonyl groups

---

# 4. Applications of Inductive Effect

---

## 1. Stability of Carbocations

+I groups increase carbocation stability.

---

## 2. Acidity of Organic Compounds

Electron withdrawing groups increase acidity.

Example:

\[
ClCH_2COOH > CH_3COOH
\]

---

# PART C: ELECTROMERIC EFFECT

---

# 5. Electromeric Effect

## Definition

The complete transfer of shared pi electrons to one atom in the presence of an attacking reagent is called electromeric effect.

---

Characteristics:

- Temporary effect
- Occurs only in compounds with multiple bonds
- Disappears after removal of reagent

---

# 6. Types of Electromeric Effect

---

## +E Effect

Pi electrons move towards the atom to which the reagent attaches.

---

Example:

Addition of H⁺ to alkene.

---

## -E Effect

Pi electrons move away from the atom where reagent attacks.

---

Example:

Carbonyl compounds.

---

# PART D: RESONANCE

---

# 7. Resonance

## Definition

The delocalization of pi electrons over two or more atoms is called resonance.

---

Characteristics:

- Molecule exists as resonance hybrid.
- Increases stability.
- Electrons are spread over the molecule.

---

# 8. Resonance Structures

Examples:

## Benzene

\[
C_6H_6
\]

has delocalized electrons.

---

## Carbonate Ion

\[
CO_3^{2-}
\]

has resonance structures.

---

# 9. Stability Due to Resonance

Greater resonance:

\[
\boxed{\text{Higher stability}}
\]

---

Examples:

Benzene is stable due to resonance.

---

# PART E: HYPERCONJUGATION

---

# 10. Hyperconjugation

## Definition

The delocalization of sigma electrons of C-H bonds adjacent to a double bond or positive charge is called hyperconjugation.

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{No bond resonance}}
\]

---

# 11. Applications of Hyperconjugation

---

## 1. Stability of Carbocations

More alkyl groups:

Higher stability.

Order:

\[
\boxed{
3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > CH_3^+
}
\]

---

## 2. Stability of Alkenes

More substituted alkenes are more stable.

---

# PART F: REACTION INTERMEDIATES

---

# 12. Reaction Intermediate

## Definition

A short-lived species formed during a reaction mechanism is called a reaction intermediate.

---

Important intermediates:

1. Carbocations
2. Carbanions
3. Free radicals

---

# PART G: CARBOCATIONS

---

# 13. Carbocation

## Definition

A positively charged carbon species is called a carbocation.

---

General formula:

\[
\boxed{
R_3C^+
}
\]

---

Characteristics:

- Electron deficient
- Six electrons around carbon
- Usually sp² hybridized

---

Shape:

\[
\boxed{\text{Trigonal planar}}
\]

---

# 14. Stability of Carbocations

Stability order:

\[
\boxed{
3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > CH_3^+
}
\]

---

Reason:

- +I effect
- Hyperconjugation

---

Example:

\[
(CH_3)_3C^+
\]

is more stable than:

\[
CH_3^+
\]

---

# PART H: CARBANIONS

---

# 15. Carbanion

## Definition

A negatively charged carbon species is called carbanion.

---

General formula:

\[
\boxed{
R_3C^-
}
\]

---

Characteristics:

- Electron rich
- Contains lone pair
- Usually pyramidal structure

---

# 16. Stability of Carbanions

Order:

\[
\boxed{
CH_3^- > 1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ
}
\]

---

Reason:

Alkyl groups destabilize negative charge due to +I effect.

---

Electron withdrawing groups increase stability.

---

# PART I: FREE RADICALS

---

# 17. Free Radical

## Definition

A neutral species containing an unpaired electron is called a free radical.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{R\cdot}
\]

---

Formation:

Homolytic bond cleavage:

\[
A-B
\rightarrow
A\cdot+B\cdot
\]

---

# 18. Stability of Free Radicals

Order:

\[
\boxed{
3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > CH_3\cdot
}
\]

---

Reason:

- Hyperconjugation
- +I effect

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Arrange carbocations in increasing stability:

\[
CH_3^+, C_2H_5^+, (CH_3)_3C^+
\]

Answer:

\[
\boxed{
CH_3^+<C_2H_5^+<(CH_3)_3C^+
}
\]

---

## Example 2

Which group shows -I effect?

Options:

- CH₃
- NO₂
- C₂H₅

Answer:

\[
\boxed{NO_2}
\]

---

## Example 3

Why is benzene stable?

Because of:

\[
\boxed{\text{Resonance}}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing inductive effect and resonance.
- Forgetting inductive effect decreases with distance.
- Mixing carbocation and carbanion stability orders.
- Assuming all electron donating groups increase acidity.
- Forgetting free radicals contain unpaired electrons.
- Confusing temporary electromeric effect with permanent inductive effect.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Organic reactions depend on electron movement.
- Inductive effect operates through sigma bonds.
- Electromeric effect involves temporary electron transfer.
- Resonance increases molecular stability.
- Hyperconjugation explains stability of carbocations and alkenes.
- Carbocations, carbanions and free radicals are important reaction intermediates.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Understanding electron movement is the key to organic reaction mechanisms.}
}
\]
