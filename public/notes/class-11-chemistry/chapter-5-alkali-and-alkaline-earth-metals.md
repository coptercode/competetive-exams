# Introduction

The elements in groups 1 and 2 of the periodic table are called alkali and alkaline earth metals.

They are important because of their:

- High reactivity
- Formation of ionic compounds
- Biological importance
- Industrial applications

This chapter covers:

- Group 1 elements
- Properties of alkali metals
- Anomalous behaviour of lithium
- Group 2 elements
- Important compounds:
  - Quicklime
  - Slaked lime
  - Plaster of Paris

---

# PART A: ALKALI METALS (GROUP 1)

---

# 1. Definition

The elements of group 1 of the periodic table are called alkali metals.

---

Elements:

\[
\boxed{
Li,\ Na,\ K,\ Rb,\ Cs,\ Fr
}
\]

---

Electronic configuration:

\[
\boxed{
ns^1
}
\]

---

# 2. General Properties of Alkali Metals

---

## 1. Soft Metals

Alkali metals are soft and can be cut with a knife.

Example:

Sodium and potassium.

---

## 2. Low Density

Most alkali metals have low density.

---

## 3. Low Ionization Energy

They easily lose their valence electron.

Reaction:

\[
M\rightarrow M^+ + e^-
\]

---

## 4. High Reactivity

Reactivity increases down the group.

\[
\boxed{
Li<Na<K<Rb<Cs
}
\]

---

# 3. Physical Properties of Alkali Metals

| Property          | Trend                |
| ----------------- | -------------------- |
| Atomic size       | Increases down group |
| Ionization energy | Decreases            |
| Reactivity        | Increases            |
| Melting point     | Decreases            |

---

# PART B: CHEMICAL PROPERTIES OF ALKALI METALS

---

# 4. Reaction with Oxygen

Alkali metals form oxides, peroxides and superoxides.

---

Lithium:

\[
4Li+O_2\rightarrow2Li_2O
\]

---

Sodium:

\[
2Na+O_2\rightarrow Na_2O_2
\]

---

Potassium:

\[
K+O_2\rightarrow KO_2
\]

---

# 5. Reaction with Water

Alkali metals react vigorously with water.

General reaction:

\[
\boxed{
2M+2H_2O\rightarrow2MOH+H_2
}
\]

---

Example:

\[
2Na+2H_2O
\rightarrow2NaOH+H_2
\]

---

# 6. Reaction with Halogens

Forms ionic halides.

Example:

\[
2Na+Cl_2\rightarrow2NaCl
\]

---

# PART C: ANOMALOUS BEHAVIOUR OF LITHIUM

---

# 7. Lithium

Lithium differs from other alkali metals due to:

- Small size
- High ionization energy
- High polarising power

---

# 8. Differences of Lithium

---

## 1. Reaction with Oxygen

Lithium forms normal oxide:

\[
Li_2O
\]

while others form peroxides/superoxides.

---

## 2. Reaction with Nitrogen

Lithium directly combines with nitrogen:

\[
\boxed{
6Li+N_2\rightarrow2Li_3N
}
\]

---

## 3. Lithium Carbonate

Lithium carbonate decomposes on heating:

\[
Li_2CO_3
\rightarrow
Li_2O+CO_2
\]

---

# PART D: ALKALINE EARTH METALS (GROUP 2)

---

# 9. Definition

Elements of group 2 are called alkaline earth metals.

---

Elements:

\[
\boxed{
Be,\ Mg,\ Ca,\ Sr,\ Ba,\ Ra
}
\]

---

Electronic configuration:

\[
\boxed{
ns^2
}
\]

---

# 10. General Properties

---

## 1. Harder Than Alkali Metals

Due to stronger metallic bonding.

---

## 2. Higher Melting Points

Compared to group 1 metals.

---

## 3. Formation of Divalent Ions

They lose two electrons.

Reaction:

\[
M\rightarrow M^{2+}+2e^-
\]

---

# PART E: PROPERTIES OF ALKALINE EARTH METALS

---

# 11. Atomic Size

Increases down the group.

\[
\boxed{
Be<Mg<Ca<Sr<Ba
}
\]

---

# 12. Ionization Energy

Decreases down the group.

---

# 13. Reactivity

Increases down the group.

---

# PART F: IMPORTANT COMPOUNDS

---

# 14. Quicklime

Chemical formula:

\[
\boxed{CaO}
\]

---

Preparation:

Heating limestone:

\[
\boxed{
CaCO_3
\rightarrow
CaO+CO_2
}
\]

---

Properties:

- White solid
- Basic oxide
- Reacts with water

Reaction:

\[
CaO+H_2O
\rightarrow
Ca(OH)_2
\]

---

Uses:

- Cement industry
- Manufacture of glass
- Soil treatment

---

# 15. Slaked Lime

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Ca(OH)_2
}
\]

---

Preparation:

\[
CaO+H_2O
\rightarrow
Ca(OH)_2
\]

---

Uses:

- White washing
- Water purification
- Making bleaching powder

---

# 16. Plaster of Paris

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
CaSO_4\cdot\frac12H_2O
}
\]

---

Preparation:

By heating gypsum:

\[
\boxed{
CaSO_4\cdot2H_2O
\rightarrow
CaSO_4\cdot\frac12H_2O
+\frac32H_2O
}
\]

---

Properties:

- White powder
- Sets into hard mass with water

---

Uses:

- Making casts
- Sculptures
- Decorative materials

---

# PART G: IMPORTANT COMPOUNDS OF SODIUM

---

# 17. Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH)

Preparation:

Electrolysis of brine:

\[
2NaCl+2H_2O
\rightarrow
2NaOH+Cl_2+H_2
\]

---

Uses:

- Soap manufacture
- Paper industry
- Chemical industries

---

# 18. Sodium Carbonate

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Na_2CO_3
}
\]

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Washing soda}}
\]

---

Uses:

- Glass production
- Water softening

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing alkali and alkaline earth metals.
- Forgetting group 1 metals form +1 ions.
- Forgetting group 2 metals form +2 ions.
- Mixing quicklime and slaked lime formulas.
- Confusing gypsum with plaster of Paris.
- Ignoring lithium's anomalous behaviour.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Alkali metals belong to group 1 and have configuration \(ns^1\).
- Alkaline earth metals belong to group 2 and have configuration \(ns^2\).
- Reactivity increases down both groups.
- Lithium shows unusual properties due to its small size.
- Calcium compounds like quicklime and plaster of Paris have important uses.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Group 1 and Group 2 elements are highly reactive electropositive metals.}
}
