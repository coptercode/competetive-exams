# Introduction

The gaseous state is one of the three major states of matter.

Gases have:

- No fixed shape
- No fixed volume
- High compressibility
- High molecular motion

This chapter covers:

- Gas laws
- Ideal gas equation
- Dalton's law of partial pressure
- Kinetic theory of gases
- Ideal and real gases
- Compressibility factor
- Van der Waals equation

---

# PART A: GENERAL PROPERTIES OF GASES

---

# 1. Characteristics of Gases

## 1. No Fixed Shape

Gases occupy the shape of their container.

---

## 2. No Fixed Volume

They expand to fill available space.

---

## 3. High Compressibility

Due to large intermolecular spaces.

---

## 4. Low Density

Compared with solids and liquids.

---

## 5. Diffusion

Gases mix rapidly due to molecular motion.

---

# PART B: GAS LAWS

---

# 2. Boyle's Law

## Statement

At constant temperature, the pressure of a fixed mass of gas is inversely proportional to its volume.

---

Mathematically:

\[
P\propto\frac1V
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
PV=constant
}
\]

---

For two conditions:

\[
\boxed{
P_1V_1=P_2V_2
}
\]

---

Graph:

Pressure vs Volume:

- Hyperbolic curve

---

# 3. Charles' Law

## Statement

At constant pressure, volume of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to absolute temperature.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
V\propto T
}
\]

or:

\[
\boxed{
\frac VT=constant
}
\]

---

For two conditions:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{V_1}{T_1}
=

\frac{V_2}{T_2}
}
\]

---

Temperature must be in Kelvin.

---

# 4. Gay-Lussac's Law

## Statement

At constant volume, pressure of a gas is directly proportional to absolute temperature.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
P\propto T
}
\]

---

\[
\boxed{
\frac P T=constant
}
\]

---

# 5. Avogadro's Law

## Statement

Equal volumes of all gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal number of molecules.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
V\propto n
}
\]

---

where:

n = number of moles

---

# PART C: IDEAL GAS EQUATION

---

# 6. Ideal Gas

## Definition

A gas that obeys all gas laws under all conditions is called an ideal gas.

---

Characteristics:

- No intermolecular forces
- Molecules have negligible volume
- Perfect elastic collisions

---

# 7. Ideal Gas Equation

Combining gas laws:

\[
\boxed{
PV=nRT
}
\]

---

where:

P = pressure

V = volume

n = number of moles

R = gas constant

T = temperature

---

# 8. Gas Constant (R)

Value:

\[
\boxed{
R=8.314J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}
}
\]

---

Other value:

\[
\boxed{
R=0.0821L\,atm\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}
}
\]

---

# 9. Density Relation

From:

\[
PV=nRT
\]

Since:

\[
n=\frac mM
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
PV=\frac mMRT
}
\]

---

Density:

\[
d=\frac mV
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
d=\frac{PM}{RT}
}
\]

---

# PART D: DALTON'S LAW OF PARTIAL PRESSURES

---

# 10. Dalton's Law

## Statement

The total pressure of a mixture of non-reacting gases is equal to the sum of individual partial pressures.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
P_{total}=P_1+P_2+P_3+...
}
\]

---

# 11. Partial Pressure

The pressure exerted by an individual gas in a mixture is called partial pressure.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
P_i=X_iP_{total}
}
\]

---

where:

\[
X_i=\text{mole fraction}
\]

---

# 12. Mole Fraction

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
X_i=
\frac{\text{Moles of component}}
{\text{Total moles}}
}
\]

---

Sum of mole fractions:

\[
\boxed{
X_1+X_2+X_3=1
}
\]

---

# PART E: KINETIC THEORY OF GASES

---

# 13. Basic Assumptions

1. Gas contains large number of molecules.

2. Molecules are in continuous random motion.

3. Molecular volume is negligible.

4. Collisions are perfectly elastic.

5. No intermolecular attraction exists.

---

# 14. Gas Pressure Equation

\[
\boxed{
P=\frac13\rho \overline{v^2}
}
\]

---

where:

ρ = density

\(\overline{v^2}\) = mean square velocity

---

# 15. Root Mean Square Velocity

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
v_{rms}
=

\sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}
}
\]

---

Velocity increases with:

- Increase in temperature

Velocity decreases with:

- Increase in molar mass

---

# PART F: REAL GASES

---

# 16. Real Gas

## Definition

A gas that deviates from ideal behaviour is called a real gas.

---

Examples:

- CO₂
- NH₃
- SO₂

---

Reasons for deviation:

1. Molecular volume
2. Intermolecular attraction

---

# 17. Ideal Gas vs Real Gas

| Ideal Gas                | Real Gas                  |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| No intermolecular force  | Forces present            |
| Molecules have no volume | Molecules occupy volume   |
| Obeys PV=nRT always      | Deviates at high pressure |
| Imaginary                | Exists in nature          |

---

# PART G: COMPRESSIBILITY FACTOR

---

# 18. Compressibility Factor (Z)

## Definition

The ratio of actual molar volume to ideal molar volume is called compressibility factor.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Z=\frac{PV}{nRT}
}
\]

---

For ideal gas:

\[
\boxed{
Z=1
}
\]

---

For real gases:

\[
Z\neq1
\]

---

# PART H: VAN DER WAALS EQUATION

---

# 19. Van der Waals Equation

It modifies ideal gas equation for real gases.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\left(P+\frac{an^2}{V^2}\right)(V-nb)=nRT
}
\]

---

Corrections:

---

## Pressure Correction

Due to intermolecular attraction:

\[
\boxed{
P+\frac{an^2}{V^2}
}
\]

---

## Volume Correction

Due to molecular volume:

\[
\boxed{
V-nb
}
\]

---

# 20. Constants a and b

## a

Represents:

- Intermolecular attraction

Higher value of a:

- Stronger attraction

---

## b

Represents:

- Volume occupied by molecules

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

A gas occupies 5 L at 1 atm pressure. Find volume at 2 atm pressure.

Given:

\[
P_1=1atm
\]

\[
V_1=5L
\]

\[
P_2=2atm
\]

Using Boyle's law:

\[
P_1V_1=P_2V_2
\]

\[
1(5)=2V_2
\]

\[
\boxed{V_2=2.5L}
\]

---

## Example 2

Find moles in 22.4 L gas at STP.

At STP:

\[
1mol=22.4L
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{1mol}
\]

---

## Example 3

Calculate total pressure if gases have:

\[
P_1=2atm
\]

\[
P_2=3atm
\]

Using Dalton's law:

\[
P=P_1+P_2
\]

\[
\boxed{5atm}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Using Celsius instead of Kelvin.
- Forgetting units of pressure and volume.
- Confusing ideal and real gases.
- Ignoring intermolecular forces in real gases.
- Using Dalton's law for reacting gases.
- Forgetting compressibility factor of ideal gas is 1.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Gas laws describe the relationship between pressure, volume and temperature.
- Ideal gas equation combines all gas laws.
- Dalton's law explains gas mixtures.
- Real gases deviate due to molecular volume and attraction.
- Van der Waals equation corrects ideal gas behaviour.
- Compressibility factor measures deviation from ideal behaviour.

}
