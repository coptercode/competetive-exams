# Introduction

Organic chemistry is the branch of chemistry that deals with compounds containing carbon.

Organic compounds are important in:

- Fuels
- Medicines
- Polymers
- Biological molecules
- Industrial chemicals

This chapter covers:

- Classification of organic compounds
- IUPAC nomenclature
- Functional groups
- Isomerism
- Structural and stereoisomerism

---

# PART A: ORGANIC COMPOUNDS

---

# 1. Organic Chemistry

## Definition

The branch of chemistry that studies carbon-containing compounds is called organic chemistry.

---

Examples:

- Methane (CH₄)
- Ethanol (C₂H₅OH)
- Benzene (C₆H₆)

---

# 2. Characteristics of Carbon

Carbon forms a large number of compounds due to:

---

## 1. Tetravalency

Carbon has four valence electrons and forms four covalent bonds.

Example:

\[
CH_4
\]

---

## 2. Catenation

The ability of carbon atoms to form chains with each other.

---

Types:

- Straight chains
- Branched chains
- Rings

---

# PART B: CLASSIFICATION OF ORGANIC COMPOUNDS

---

# 3. Open Chain Compounds

Carbon atoms arranged in chains.

---

Types:

## Straight Chain

Example:

\[
CH_3-CH_2-CH_3
\]

---

## Branched Chain

Example:

Isobutane

---

# 4. Closed Chain Compounds

Carbon atoms arranged in rings.

---

Types:

## Alicyclic Compounds

Example:

Cyclohexane

---

## Aromatic Compounds

Example:

Benzene

\[
C_6H_6
\]

---

# PART C: HOMOLOGOUS SERIES

---

# 5. Homologous Series

## Definition

A group of organic compounds having the same functional group and general formula is called a homologous series.

---

Characteristics:

1. Same functional group

2. Similar chemical properties

3. Successive members differ by:

\[
\boxed{-CH_2}
\]

---

Example:

Alkanes:

\[
CH_4,\ C_2H_6,\ C_3H_8
\]

---

# PART D: FUNCTIONAL GROUPS

---

# 6. Functional Group

## Definition

An atom or group of atoms responsible for characteristic properties of organic compounds is called a functional group.

---

# Important Functional Groups

| Functional Group | Formula | Example       |
| ---------------- | ------- | ------------- |
| Alkene           | C=C     | Ethene        |
| Alkyne           | C≡C     | Ethyne        |
| Alcohol          | -OH     | Ethanol       |
| Aldehyde         | -CHO    | Ethanal       |
| Ketone           | >C=O    | Propanone     |
| Carboxylic acid  | -COOH   | Acetic acid   |
| Amine            | -NH₂    | Methylamine   |
| Halo             | -X      | Chloromethane |

# PART E: IUPAC NOMENCLATURE

---

# 7. IUPAC Naming

IUPAC stands for:

\[
\boxed{
\text{International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry}
}
\]

---

# 8. Steps in Naming Organic Compounds

---

## Step 1: Select Longest Carbon Chain

The longest chain is the parent chain.

---

## Step 2: Number the Chain

Number from the end closest to:

- Functional group
- Multiple bond
- Substituent

---

## Step 3: Identify Substituents

Common substituents:

| Group | Name   |
| ----- | ------ |
| CH₃   | Methyl |
| C₂H₅  | Ethyl  |
| Cl    | Chloro |
| Br    | Bromo  |

---

## Step 4: Write Name

Format:

\[
\boxed{
Prefix+Root+Suffix
}
\]

---

# 10. Primary Suffix

Shows type of bond.

| Bond        | Suffix |

# PART F: ISOMERISM

---

# 12. Isomerism

## Definition

The phenomenon in which compounds have the same molecular formula but different structures or properties is called isomerism.

---

Example:

\[
C_4H_{10}
\]

has two structures.

---

# Types of Isomerism

1. Structural isomerism
2. Stereoisomerism

---

# PART G: STRUCTURAL ISOMERISM

---

# 13. Structural Isomerism

Compounds differ in arrangement of atoms.

---

Types:

1. Chain isomerism
2. Position isomerism
3. Functional isomerism

---

# 14. Chain Isomerism

Different carbon skeletons.

---

Example:

Butane:

\[
CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_3
\]

Isobutane:

\[
(CH_3)_3CH
\]

---

# 15. Position Isomerism

Same carbon chain but different position of functional group or multiple bond.

---

Example:

1-Propanol

and

2-Propanol

---

# 16. Functional Isomerism

Same molecular formula but different functional groups.

---

Example:

\[
C_2H_6O
\]

Ethanol:

\[
CH_3CH_2OH
\]

Dimethyl ether:

\[
CH_3OCH_3
\]

---

# PART H: STEREOISOMERISM

---

# 17. Stereoisomerism

Compounds have same connectivity but different spatial arrangement.

---

Types:

1. Geometrical isomerism
2. Optical isomerism

---

# 18. Geometrical Isomerism

Occurs due to restricted rotation around double bonds.

---

Types:

1. Cis-isomer
2. Trans-isomer

---

Example:

But-2-ene

Cis-but-2-ene:

Similar groups on same side.

---

Trans-but-2-ene:

Similar groups on opposite sides.

---

# 19. Optical Isomerism

Occurs due to chirality.

---

# 20. Chiral Carbon

A carbon atom attached to four different groups is called chiral carbon.

---

Example:

Lactic acid

---

Optical isomers:

- Enantiomers
- Mirror images of each other

---

# Common Mistakes

- Choosing incorrect longest carbon chain.
- Wrong numbering of carbon chain.
- Confusing prefix and suffix.
- Forgetting functional group priority.
- Mixing structural and stereoisomerism.
- Confusing cis-trans isomers.

# Chapter Summary

- Carbon forms millions of compounds due to tetravalency and catenation.
- Organic compounds are classified based on carbon skeleton and functional groups.
- IUPAC rules provide systematic naming.
- Functional groups determine chemical properties.
- Isomerism explains compounds with same formula but different structures.
