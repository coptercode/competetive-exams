# Introduction

Periodic classification is the arrangement of elements in a systematic order based on their properties.

The modern periodic table helps us understand:

- Arrangement of elements
- Similarities among elements
- Periodic trends
- Chemical behaviour

This chapter covers:

- Historical classification
- Mendeleev periodic table
- Modern periodic law
- Periods and groups
- Periodic properties

---

# PART A: HISTORICAL CLASSIFICATION OF ELEMENTS

---

# 1. Need for Classification

As the number of discovered elements increased, scientists needed a method to arrange them systematically.

Classification helps to:

- Study properties easily
- Predict properties of unknown elements
- Understand relationships among elements

---

# 2. Dobereiner's Triads

## Definition

Dobereiner grouped elements into sets of three having similar properties called triads.

---

Example:

Lithium (Li)

Sodium (Na)

Potassium (K)

---

Relationship:

Atomic mass of middle element:

\[
\boxed{
=

\frac{
\text{Atomic mass of first + Atomic mass of third}
}{2}
}
\]

---

Limitations:

- Only few elements could be arranged.
- Not applicable to all elements.

---

# 3. Newlands' Law of Octaves

## Statement

When elements are arranged in increasing order of atomic masses, every eighth element has properties similar to the first element.

---

Example:

Li → Be → B → C → N → O → F → Na

---

Limitations:

- Worked only up to calcium.
- Some unlike elements were grouped together.

---

# 4. Mendeleev's Periodic Law

## Statement

The properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic masses.

---

Basis:

Arrangement according to increasing atomic mass.

---

Achievements:

- Left gaps for undiscovered elements.
- Predicted properties of new elements.
- Corrected atomic masses of some elements.

---

# Limitations of Mendeleev Table

1. Position of hydrogen was uncertain.
2. Isotopes could not be explained.
3. Increasing atomic mass order had exceptions.

---

# PART B: MODERN PERIODIC TABLE

---

# 5. Modern Periodic Law

## Statement

The physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.

---

Given by:

\[
\boxed{
Henry Moseley
}
\]

---

Basis:

Increasing atomic number.

---

# 6. Modern Periodic Table

Elements are arranged into:

- 7 periods
- 18 groups

---

# 7. Periods

Horizontal rows are called periods.

---

There are:

\[
\boxed{7\ periods}
\]

---

Period indicates:

\[
\boxed{\text{Number of electron shells}}
\]

---

# 8. Groups

Vertical columns are called groups.

---

There are:

\[
\boxed{18\ groups}
\]

---

Elements in same group have similar chemical properties.

---

# PART C: BLOCK CLASSIFICATION

---

# 9. s-Block Elements

Elements in which the last electron enters s-orbital.

---

Groups:

\[
1,2
\]

---

Examples:

- Na
- Mg
- Ca

---

# 10. p-Block Elements

Last electron enters p-orbital.

---

Groups:

\[
13-18
\]

---

Examples:

- Carbon
- Nitrogen
- Oxygen

---

# 11. d-Block Elements

Last electron enters d-orbital.

---

Groups:

\[
3-12
\]

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Transition elements}}
\]

---

Examples:

- Iron
- Copper
- Nickel

---

# 12. f-Block Elements

Last electron enters f-orbital.

---

Two series:

1. Lanthanides
2. Actinides

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Inner transition elements}}
\]

---

# PART D: PERIODIC PROPERTIES

---

# 13. Atomic Radius

## Definition

The distance from nucleus to the outermost electron shell is called atomic radius.

---

Trend:

Across a period:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases}}
\]

---

Down a group:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increases}}
\]

---

Reason:

Across period:

- Nuclear charge increases
- Electrons are pulled closer

Down group:

- Number of shells increases

---

# 14. Ionic Radius

## Definition

The size of an ion is called ionic radius.

---

Cations:

Smaller than parent atom.

Example:

\[
Na^+<Na
\]

---

Anions:

Larger than parent atom.

Example:

\[
Cl^->Cl
\]

---

# 15. Ionization Energy

## Definition

The minimum energy required to remove an electron from an isolated gaseous atom is called ionization energy.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
X(g)\rightarrow X^+(g)+e^-
}
\]

---

Trend:

Across period:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increases}}
\]

---

Down group:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases}}
\]

---

Factors affecting ionization energy:

- Atomic size
- Nuclear charge
- Shielding effect

---

# 16. Electron Affinity

## Definition

The energy released when an electron is added to a neutral gaseous atom is called electron affinity.

---

Trend:

Generally:

Across period:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increases}}
\]

---

Down group:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases}}
\]

---

# 17. Electronegativity

## Definition

The tendency of an atom to attract shared electrons towards itself is called electronegativity.

---

Given by:

\[
\boxed{
Linus Pauling
}
\]

---

Trend:

Across period:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increases}}
\]

---

Down group:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases}}
\]

---

Most electronegative element:

\[
\boxed{Fluorine(F)}
\]

---

# 18. Metallic Character

## Definition

The tendency of an element to lose electrons and form positive ions is called metallic character.

---

Trend:

Across period:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases}}
\]

---

Down group:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increases}}
\]

---

# PART E: IMPORTANT PERIODIC TRENDS SUMMARY

| Property           | Across Period       | Down Group          |
| ------------------ | ------------------- | ------------------- |
| Atomic radius      | Decreases           | Increases           |
| Ionization energy  | Increases           | Decreases           |
| Electronegativity  | Increases           | Decreases           |
| Metallic character | Decreases           | Increases           |
| Electron affinity  | Generally increases | Generally decreases |

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Which has larger atomic radius?

Na or Cl

Solution:

Both belong to the same period.

Atomic radius decreases across a period.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{Na>Cl}
\]

---

## Example 2

Which is more electronegative?

F or Cl

Solution:

Electronegativity decreases down a group.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{F>Cl}
\]

---

## Example 3

Arrange in increasing atomic size:

Li, Na, K

Size increases down the group.

Answer:

\[
\boxed{
Li<Na<K
}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing atomic number and atomic mass.
- Forgetting modern periodic law is based on atomic number.
- Assuming atomic size always increases.
- Mixing electron affinity and electronegativity.
- Forgetting cations are smaller and anions are larger than atoms.
- Confusing groups and periods.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Elements are classified to study their properties systematically.
- Mendeleev arranged elements based on atomic mass.
- Modern periodic table is based on atomic number.
- Elements are divided into s, p, d and f blocks.
- Periodic properties show regular trends.
- Atomic size decreases across periods and increases down groups.
- Electronegativity and ionization energy generally increase across periods.

\[
\boxed{
\text{The periodic table is the roadmap of chemical behaviour.}
}
\]
