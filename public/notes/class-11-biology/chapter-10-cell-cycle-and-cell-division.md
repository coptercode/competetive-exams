# Introduction

Growth and reproduction of organisms depend on the formation of new cells. Cell division is the process by which a parent cell produces new daughter cells.

This chapter covers:

- Cell cycle
- Interphase
- Mitosis
- Meiosis
- Significance of cell division

---

# PART A: CELL CYCLE

---

# 1. Cell Cycle

## Definition

The sequence of events through which a cell grows, duplicates its DNA and divides into daughter cells is called the cell cycle.

---

The cell cycle consists of:

1. Interphase
2. M phase (Mitotic phase)

---

# PART B: INTERPHASE

---

# 2. Interphase

## Definition

The phase between two successive cell divisions is called interphase.

---

It is the longest phase of the cell cycle.

---

Interphase has three stages:

1. G₁ phase
2. S phase
3. G₂ phase

---

# 3. G₁ Phase (Gap 1 Phase)

Characteristics:

- Cell growth occurs.
- RNA and proteins are synthesised.
- Organelles increase in number.

---

# 4. S Phase (Synthesis Phase)

Main event:

\[
\boxed{\text{DNA replication}}
\]

---

During this phase:

- DNA content doubles.
- Chromosomes are duplicated.

---

# 5. G₂ Phase (Gap 2 Phase)

Characteristics:

- Further growth occurs.
- Proteins required for cell division are produced.

---

# PART C: M PHASE

---

# 6. M Phase

## Definition

The phase in which actual cell division occurs is called M phase.

---

It includes:

1. Nuclear division
2. Cytoplasmic division

---

# PART D: MITOSIS

---

# 7. Mitosis

## Definition

Mitosis is a type of cell division in which one parent cell divides to form two genetically identical daughter cells.

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Equational division}}
\]

---

Occurs in:

- Somatic cells
- Body cells

---

# 8. Stages of Mitosis

Mitosis occurs in four main stages:

1. Prophase
2. Metaphase
3. Anaphase
4. Telophase

---

# 9. Prophase

Events:

- Chromatin condenses into chromosomes.
- Nuclear membrane disappears.
- Spindle fibres appear.

---

# 10. Metaphase

Events:

- Chromosomes arrange at the equatorial plate.
- Spindle fibres attach to centromeres.

---

# 11. Anaphase

Events:

- Centromeres divide.
- Sister chromatids move to opposite poles.

---

# 12. Telophase

Events:

- Nuclear membrane reforms.
- Chromosomes become chromatin.
- Two daughter nuclei form.

---

# 13. Cytokinesis

## Definition

Division of cytoplasm after nuclear division is called cytokinesis.

---

In animal cells:

- Cleavage furrow formation.

---

In plant cells:

- Cell plate formation.

---

# PART E: SIGNIFICANCE OF MITOSIS

---

Mitosis helps in:

1. Growth of organisms.
2. Repair of damaged tissues.
3. Replacement of old cells.
4. Asexual reproduction.

---

# PART F: MEIOSIS

---

# 14. Meiosis

## Definition

Meiosis is a type of cell division in which one diploid cell divides to produce four haploid cells.

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Reduction division}}
\]

---

Occurs in:

- Reproductive cells
- Formation of gametes

---

# 15. Features of Meiosis

- Two successive divisions occur.
- Chromosome number becomes half.
- Genetic variation occurs.

---

# PART G: STAGES OF MEIOSIS

---

Meiosis consists of:

1. Meiosis I
2. Meiosis II

---

# PART H: MEIOSIS I

---

# 16. Prophase I

Longest phase of meiosis.

Sub-stages:

1. Leptotene
2. Zygotene
3. Pachytene
4. Diplotene
5. Diakinesis

---

# 17. Crossing Over

## Definition

Exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes is called crossing over.

---

Occurs during:

\[
\boxed{\text{Pachytene stage}}
\]

---

Importance:

- Produces genetic variation.

---

# 18. Metaphase I

Events:

- Homologous chromosomes arrange at equator.

---

# 19. Anaphase I

Events:

- Homologous chromosomes separate.
- Centromeres do not divide.

---

# 20. Telophase I

Events:

- Two haploid cells are formed.

---

# PART I: MEIOSIS II

---

Meiosis II resembles mitosis.

Stages:

1. Prophase II
2. Metaphase II
3. Anaphase II
4. Telophase II

---

Result:

Four haploid daughter cells are produced.

---

# PART J: DIFFERENCE BETWEEN MITOSIS AND MEIOSIS

| Mitosis                        | Meiosis                        |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Occurs in somatic cells        | Occurs in reproductive cells   |
| One division                   | Two divisions                  |
| Produces two daughter cells    | Produces four daughter cells   |
| Chromosome number remains same | Chromosome number becomes half |
| No crossing over               | Crossing over occurs           |

---

# PART K: IMPORTANCE OF MEIOSIS

---

Meiosis helps in:

1. Formation of gametes.
2. Maintaining chromosome number in species.
3. Producing genetic variation.
4. Evolution.

---

# PART L: CHROMOSOME NUMBER

---

# Important Points

- Cell cycle includes interphase and M phase.
- DNA replication occurs during S phase.
- Mitosis produces identical cells.
- Meiosis produces genetically different cells.
- Crossing over occurs during prophase I.
- Meiosis maintains chromosome number generation after generation.

---

---

DNA replication occurs during:

\]

---

Mitosis is also called:

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Equational division}}
\]

---

## Example 3

Crossing over occurs during:

Answer:

\[

\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing mitosis and meiosis.
- Forgetting S phase is for DNA replication.
- Thinking chromosome number reduces in mitosis.
- Forgetting crossing over occurs only in meiosis.
- Mixing anaphase I and anaphase II events.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Cell cycle controls growth and division of cells.
- Interphase prepares the cell for division.
- Mitosis produces two identical daughter cells.
- Meiosis produces four haploid cells.
- Meiosis creates genetic variation and helps sexual reproduction.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Cell division ensures growth, reproduction and continuity of life.}
}
