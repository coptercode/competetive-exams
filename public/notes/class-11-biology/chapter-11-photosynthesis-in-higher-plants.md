# Introduction

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants prepare food using sunlight, carbon dioxide and water.

This chapter covers:

- Photosynthesis as autotrophic nutrition
- Site of photosynthesis
- Photosynthetic pigments
- Light reaction
- Dark reaction
- Photophosphorylation
- C3 and C4 pathways
- Photorespiration
- Factors affecting photosynthesis

---

# PART A: INTRODUCTION TO PHOTOSYNTHESIS

---

# 1. Photosynthesis

## Definition

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants synthesize carbohydrates from carbon dioxide and water using sunlight energy in the presence of chlorophyll.

---

Equation:

\[
6CO_2 + 12H_2O \xrightarrow[\text{Chlorophyll}]{Light}
C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 + 6H_2O
\]

---

# 2. Autotrophic Nutrition

## Definition

The mode of nutrition in which organisms prepare their own food is called autotrophic nutrition.

---

Examples:

- Green plants
- Algae

---

# 3. Importance of Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis:

- Produces food.
- Releases oxygen.
- Maintains atmospheric balance.
- Provides energy for all organisms.

---

# PART B: SITE OF PHOTOSYNTHESIS

---

# 4. Chloroplast

## Definition

Chloroplasts are specialized cell organelles where photosynthesis occurs.

---

Found in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Green plant cells}}
\]

---

# 5. Structure of Chloroplast

Parts:

1. Outer membrane
2. Inner membrane
3. Stroma
4. Grana
5. Thylakoids

---

# 6. Grana

## Definition

Stacks of thylakoid membranes inside chloroplast are called grana.

---

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Site of light reaction}}
\]

---

# 7. Stroma

## Definition

The fluid-filled region surrounding grana is called stroma.

---

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Site of dark reaction}}
\]

---

# PART C: PHOTOSYNTHETIC PIGMENTS

---

# 8. Photosynthetic Pigments

Pigments absorb light energy required for photosynthesis.

---

Main pigments:

1. Chlorophyll a
2. Chlorophyll b
3. Carotenoids

---

# 9. Chlorophyll a

## Importance

- Primary photosynthetic pigment.
- Directly involved in light reaction.

---

Colour:

Blue-green

---

# 10. Chlorophyll b

Function:

- Helps chlorophyll a by transferring energy.

---

# 11. Carotenoids

Types:

- Carotenes
- Xanthophylls

---

Functions:

- Absorb additional light.
- Protect chlorophyll from damage.

---

# PART D: PHOTOCHEMICAL PHASE (LIGHT REACTION)

---

# 12. Light Reaction

## Definition

The light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis occurring in thylakoid membranes are called light reactions.

---

Requirements:

- Light
- Chlorophyll
- Water

---

Products:

- ATP
- NADPH
- Oxygen

---

# 13. Photosystems

Photosystems are pigment-protein complexes involved in light absorption.

---

Types:

## Photosystem I (PS I)

Reaction centre:

\[
\boxed{P700}
\]

---

## Photosystem II (PS II)

Reaction centre:

\[
\boxed{P680}
\]

---

# 14. Photolysis of Water

## Definition

Splitting of water molecules using light energy is called photolysis.

---

Produces:

- Oxygen
- Hydrogen ions
- Electrons

---

# PART E: PHOTOPHOSPHORYLATION

---

# 15. Photophosphorylation

## Definition

Formation of ATP using light energy is called photophosphorylation.

---

Types:

1. Cyclic photophosphorylation
2. Non-cyclic photophosphorylation

---

# 16. Cyclic Photophosphorylation

Characteristics:

- Involves only Photosystem I.
- Produces ATP only.
- No oxygen release.

---

# 17. Non-Cyclic Photophosphorylation

Characteristics:

- Involves PS I and PS II.
- Produces ATP and NADPH.
- Oxygen is released.

---

# PART F: CHEMIOSMOTIC HYPOTHESIS

---

# 18. Chemiosmotic Hypothesis

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Peter Mitchell}}
\]

---

It explains ATP formation through proton gradient across thylakoid membrane.

---

Steps:

1. Proton accumulation occurs inside thylakoid.
2. Proton gradient develops.
3. ATP synthase produces ATP.

---

# PART G: BIOSYNTHETIC PHASE (DARK REACTION)

---

# 19. Dark Reaction

## Definition

The reactions of photosynthesis that do not directly require light are called dark reactions.

---

Occurs in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Stroma of chloroplast}}
\]

---

Uses:

- ATP
- NADPH
- CO₂

---

# PART H: C3 PATHWAY

---

Discovered by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Melvin Calvin}}
\]

---

First stable product:

\[
\boxed{\text{3-phosphoglyceric acid (PGA)}}
\]

---

---

# PART I: C4 PATHWAY

---

# 21. C4 Plants

Plants having four-carbon compounds as the first product are called C4 plants.

---

Examples:

- Maize
- Sugarcane

---

First stable product:

\[
\boxed{\text{Oxaloacetic acid (OAA)}}
\]

---

# 22. Kranz Anatomy

## Definition

Special arrangement of bundle sheath cells around vascular bundles in C4 plants is called Kranz anatomy.

---

Advantages:

- Reduces photorespiration.
- Increases photosynthetic efficiency.

---

# PART J: PHOTO RESPIRATION

---

# 23. Photorespiration

## Definition

A process in which oxygen competes with carbon dioxide and reduces photosynthetic efficiency is called photorespiration.

---

Occurs in:

- Chloroplast
- Peroxisome
- Mitochondria

---

More common in:

\[
\boxed{\text{C3 plants}}
\]

---

# PART K: FACTORS AFFECTING PHOTOSYNTHESIS

---

# 24. External Factors

---

## 1. Light

Effect:

- Increases photosynthesis up to a limit.

---

## 2. Carbon dioxide Concentration

CO₂ is essential for carbon fixation.

---

## 3. Temperature

Enzyme activity affects photosynthesis rate.

---

## 4. Water

Water shortage reduces photosynthesis.

---

# 25. Internal Factors

Include:

- Chlorophyll content
- Leaf structure
- Enzyme activity
- Age of leaf

---

# Important Differences

## C3 Plants vs C4 Plants

| C3 Plants                 | C4 Plants                                  |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| First product is PGA      | First product is OAA                       |
| Single cell type involved | Mesophyll and bundle sheath cells involved |
| Photorespiration high     | Photorespiration low                       |
| Example: Wheat            | Example: Maize                             |

---

## Cyclic vs Non-Cyclic Photophosphorylation

| Cyclic            | Non-Cyclic             |
| ----------------- | ---------------------- |
| Only PSI involved | PSI and PSII involved  |
| ATP produced      | ATP and NADPH produced |
| No oxygen release | Oxygen released        |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing light and dark reactions.
- Forgetting stroma is the site of Calvin cycle.
- Mixing C3 and C4 products.
- Forgetting oxygen is released during photolysis.
- Confusing cyclic and non-cyclic photophosphorylation.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy.
- Chloroplast is the site of photosynthesis.
- Light reactions produce ATP and NADPH.
- Dark reactions fix carbon dioxide into carbohydrates.
- C3 and C4 pathways differ in their carbon fixation mechanisms.
- Environmental factors influence photosynthetic rate.
