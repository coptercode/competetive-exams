# Introduction

Morphology is the study of the external structure and form of plants. Flowering plants show different modifications in roots, stems, leaves and reproductive structures.

This chapter covers:

- Root
- Stem
- Leaf
- Inflorescence
- Flower
- Fruit
- Seed
- Family Solanaceae

---

# PART A: ROOT

---

# 1. Root

## Definition

The underground part of a plant that develops from the radicle of the embryo is called a root.

---

# 2. Functions of Roots

Roots perform:

1. Absorption of water and minerals.
2. Anchorage of plants.
3. Storage of food.
4. Transport of absorbed substances.

---

# 3. Types of Root Systems

---

## A. Tap Root System

## Definition

A root system with one main primary root and smaller lateral roots is called a tap root system.

---

Found in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Dicot plants}}
\]

Examples:

- Mustard
- Mango

---

## B. Fibrous Root System

## Definition

A root system with many roots arising from the base of the stem is called a fibrous root system.

---

Found in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Monocot plants}}
\]

Examples:

- Grass
- Wheat

---

## C. Adventitious Roots

Roots arising from parts other than the radicle are called adventitious roots.

---

Examples:

- Banyan prop roots
- Maize stilt roots

---

# 4. Root Modifications

Roots may be modified for special functions.

---

## Storage Roots

Store food.

Examples:

- Carrot
- Radish
- Sweet potato

---

## Prop Roots

Provide support.

Example:

Banyan

---

## Stilt Roots

Support weak stems.

Example:

Maize

---

# PART B: STEM

---

# 5. Stem

## Definition

The ascending part of the plant axis that develops from the plumule is called stem.

---

# Functions:

- Supports branches, leaves and flowers.
- Conducts water and food.
- Performs photosynthesis in some plants.
- Stores food.

---

# 6. Stem Modifications

---

## Underground Stem

Functions:

- Storage
- Perennation

Examples:

Potato, Ginger

---

## Sub-aerial Stem

Helps in vegetative propagation.

Example:

Runner in grass

---

## Aerial Stem

Modified for protection or climbing.

Examples:

- Tendrils
- Thorns

---

# PART C: LEAF

---

# 7. Leaf

## Definition

A leaf is a green, flattened structure arising from the node of the stem.

---

Functions:

1. Photosynthesis
2. Transpiration
3. Gas exchange

---

# 8. Parts of Leaf

A typical leaf consists of:

1. Leaf base
2. Petiole
3. Lamina

---

# 9. Venation

## Definition

The arrangement of veins and veinlets in the leaf blade is called venation.

---

Types:

---

## Reticulate Venation

Veins form a network.

Found in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Dicots}}
\]

Example:

Mango

---

## Parallel Venation

Veins run parallel.

Found in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Monocots}}
\]

Example:

Grass

---

# 10. Leaf Modifications

---

## Tendrils

Help in climbing.

Example:

Pea

---

## Spines

Protection.

Example:

Cactus

---

## Storage Leaves

Store food.

Example:

Onion

---

# PART D: INFLORESCENCE

---

# 11. Inflorescence

## Definition

The arrangement of flowers on the floral axis is called inflorescence.

---

Types:

1. Racemose
2. Cymose

---

# 12. Racemose Inflorescence

Characteristics:

- Main axis continues to grow.
- Flowers arranged in acropetal order.

Older flowers:

\[
\text{Lower side}
\]

Younger flowers:

\[
\text{Upper side}
\]

---

Example:

Mustard

---

# 13. Cymose Inflorescence

Characteristics:

- Main axis ends in a flower.
- Flowers arranged in basipetal order.

---

Example:

Jasmine

---

# PART E: FLOWER

---

# 14. Flower

## Definition

A flower is a modified reproductive structure of flowering plants.

---

Functions:

- Sexual reproduction
- Formation of fruits and seeds

---

# 15. Parts of Flower

Four floral whorls:

1. Calyx
2. Corolla
3. Androecium
4. Gynoecium

---

# 16. Calyx

Made of:

\[
\boxed{\text{Sepals}}
\]

Function:

Protects flower bud.

---

# 17. Corolla

Made of:

\[
\boxed{\text{Petals}}
\]

Function:

Attracts pollinators.

---

# 18. Androecium

Male reproductive part.

Contains:

\[
\boxed{\text{Stamens}}
\]

Each stamen has:

- Filament
- Anther

---

# 19. Gynoecium

Female reproductive part.

Contains:

\[
\boxed{\text{Carpels}}
\]

Parts:

- Stigma
- Style
- Ovary

---

# PART F: FRUIT

---

# 20. Fruit

## Definition

A fruit is a mature ovary formed after fertilisation.

---

Parts:

- Pericarp
- Seeds

---

Types:

---

## True Fruit

Develops only from ovary.

Example:

Mango

---

## False Fruit

Develops from ovary and other floral parts.

Example:

Apple

---

# PART G: SEED

---

# 21. Seed

## Definition

A fertilised ovule that develops into a new plant is called a seed.

---

Parts:

1. Seed coat
2. Embryo
3. Endosperm

---

# 22. Types of Seeds

---

## Monocot Seed

Contains one cotyledon.

Example:

Maize

---

## Dicot Seed

Contains two cotyledons.

Example:

Bean

---

# PART H: FAMILY SOLANACEAE

---

# 23. Solanaceae

## Definition

A family of flowering plants commonly known as the nightshade family.

---

Examples:

- Potato
- Tomato
- Brinjal
- Chilli

---

# 24. Characteristics of Solanaceae

---

## Root

Tap root system.

---

## Stem

Usually herbaceous or woody.

---

## Leaves

- Alternate
- Simple
- Reticulate venation

---

## Flower

- Bisexual
- Actinomorphic
- Pentamerous

---

## Fruit

Types:

- Berry
- Capsule

---

Examples:

Tomato → Berry

Datura → Capsule

# Important Differences

## Tap Root vs Fibrous Root

| Tap Root         | Fibrous Root       |
| ---------------- | ------------------ |
| One main root    | Many similar roots |
| Dicot plants     | Monocot plants     |
| Example: Mustard | Example: Wheat     |

---

## Monocot Leaf vs Dicot Leaf

| Monocot Leaf      | Dicot Leaf          |
| ----------------- | ------------------- |
| Parallel venation | Reticulate venation |
| One cotyledon     | Two cotyledons      |

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing root and stem modifications.
- Mixing monocot and dicot features.
- Forgetting fruit develops from ovary.
- Confusing androecium and gynoecium.
- Forgetting leaf functions.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Morphology deals with external structure of plants.
- Roots provide anchorage and absorption.
- Stem supports and transports materials.
- Leaves perform photosynthesis.
- Flowers are reproductive structures.
- Fruits develop from ovaries.
- Solanaceae includes important plants like potato and tomato.
