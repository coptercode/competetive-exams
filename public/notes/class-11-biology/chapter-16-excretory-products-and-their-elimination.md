# Introduction

Metabolism in living organisms produces waste substances that must be removed from the body. The process of removal of metabolic wastes is called excretion.

This chapter covers:

- Modes of excretion
- Human excretory system
- Structure and functions of kidney
- Urine formation
- Osmoregulation
- Hormonal regulation
- Excretory disorders

---

# PART A: EXCRETION

---

# 1. Excretion

## Definition

The process of removal of metabolic waste products from the body is called excretion.

---

Major excretory wastes:

- Carbon dioxide
- Nitrogenous wastes
- Excess salts
- Water

---

# PART B: MODES OF EXCRETION

---

# 2. Types of Nitrogenous Wastes

Organisms remove nitrogenous wastes mainly as:

1. Ammonia
2. Urea
3. Uric acid

---

# 3. Ammonotelism

## Definition

The process of excreting ammonia as the main nitrogenous waste is called ammonotelism.

---

Characteristics:

- Requires large amount of water.
- Common in aquatic animals.

---

Examples:

- Bony fishes
- Aquatic invertebrates

---

# 4. Ureotelism

## Definition

The process of excreting urea as the main nitrogenous waste is called ureotelism.

---

Examples:

- Humans
- Mammals
- Amphibians

---

# 5. Uricotelism

## Definition

The process of excreting uric acid as the main nitrogenous waste is called uricotelism.

---

Examples:

- Birds
- Reptiles
- Insects

---

# PART C: HUMAN EXCRETORY SYSTEM

---

# 6. Human Excretory System

The human excretory system consists of:

1. Two kidneys
2. Two ureters
3. Urinary bladder
4. Urethra

---

# 7. Kidney

## Definition

Kidneys are the main excretory organs responsible for urine formation and regulation of body fluids.

---

Location:

- Behind the abdominal cavity.
- On either side of vertebral column.

---

# 8. Functions of Kidney

Kidneys perform:

1. Removal of nitrogenous wastes.
2. Regulation of water balance.
3. Regulation of electrolyte balance.
4. Maintenance of blood pH.
5. Hormone secretion.

---

# PART D: STRUCTURE OF KIDNEY

---

# 9. Internal Structure of Kidney

Each kidney contains:

1. Cortex
2. Medulla
3. Renal pelvis

---

# 10. Cortex

Outer region of kidney.

Contains:

- Renal corpuscles
- Proximal convoluted tubules

---

# 11. Medulla

Inner region containing:

- Renal pyramids
- Loops of Henle
- Collecting ducts

---

# 12. Nephron

## Definition

The structural and functional unit of kidney is called nephron.

---

Each kidney contains about:

\[
\boxed{1\ million\ nephrons}
\]

---

# 13. Parts of Nephron

1. Renal corpuscle
2. Renal tubule

---

# 14. Renal Corpuscle

Contains:

## Glomerulus

A network of capillaries.

---

## Bowman's Capsule

Surrounds glomerulus.

---

# 15. Renal Tubule

Parts:

1. Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)
2. Loop of Henle
3. Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)
4. Collecting duct

---

# PART E: URINE FORMATION

---

# 16. Urine Formation

Urine formation occurs through three processes:

1. Ultrafiltration
2. Selective reabsorption
3. Tubular secretion

---

# 17. Ultrafiltration

## Definition

Filtration of blood through glomerulus is called ultrafiltration.

---

Occurs at:

\[
\boxed{\text{Glomerulus}}
\]

---

Filtered substances:

- Water
- Glucose
- Amino acids
- Salts
- Urea

---

# 18. Selective Reabsorption

## Definition

Reabsorption of useful substances from filtrate back into blood is called selective reabsorption.

---

Reabsorbed:

- Glucose
- Amino acids
- Water
- Salts

---

# 19. Tubular Secretion

## Definition

The process of secretion of additional wastes into renal tubules is called tubular secretion.

---

Examples:

- Hydrogen ions
- Potassium ions
- Ammonia

---

# PART F: OSMOREGULATION

---

# 20. Osmoregulation

## Definition

Maintenance of water and salt balance in body fluids is called osmoregulation.

---

Kidneys regulate:

- Water content
- Electrolyte concentration

---

# PART G: HORMONAL REGULATION

---

# 21. Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH)

## Source:

\[
\boxed{\text{Posterior pituitary gland}}
\]

---

Function:

- Increases water reabsorption.
- Reduces urine volume.

---

# 22. Renin-Angiotensin Mechanism

## Function:

Maintains blood pressure and water balance.

---

Steps:

1. Reduced blood pressure stimulates renin release.
2. Angiotensin formation occurs.
3. Aldosterone secretion increases.
4. Sodium and water retention occurs.

---

# 23. Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF)

## Source:

\[
\boxed{\text{Heart atria}}
\]

---

Function:

- Promotes sodium loss.
- Reduces blood pressure.

---

# PART H: ROLE OF OTHER ORGANS IN EXCRETION

---

# 24. Lungs

Remove:

- Carbon dioxide
- Water vapour

---

# 25. Skin

Removes:

- Sweat
- Salts
- Small amount of urea

---

# 26. Liver

Functions:

- Detoxification.
- Converts ammonia into urea.
- Removes bile pigments.

---

# PART I: EXCRETORY DISORDERS

---

# 27. Renal Failure

## Definition

Failure of kidneys to perform normal functions is called renal failure.

---

Effects:

- Accumulation of wastes.
- Fluid imbalance.

---

Treatment:

- Dialysis
- Kidney transplant

---

# 28. Kidney Stones (Renal Calculi)

## Definition

Hard deposits formed inside kidneys are called kidney stones.

---

Causes:

- Excess salts.
- Reduced water intake.

---

Symptoms:

- Severe pain.
- Difficulty in urination.

---

# 29. Nephritis

## Definition

Inflammation of kidney tissues is called nephritis.

---

Causes:

- Infection
- Immune disorders

---

# 30. Dialysis

## Definition

Artificial removal of wastes from blood using a dialysis machine is called dialysis.

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Artificial kidney}}
\]

---

# 31. Kidney Transplant

## Definition

Replacement of damaged kidney with a healthy donor kidney is called kidney transplant.

---

# Important Differences

## Ammonotelism vs Ureotelism

| Ammonotelism        | Ureotelism          |
| ------------------- | ------------------- |
| Ammonia excretion   | Urea excretion      |
| Requires more water | Requires less water |
| Aquatic animals     | Humans and mammals  |

---

## Cortex vs Medulla

| Cortex              | Medulla                             |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Outer region        | Inner region                        |
| Contains glomerulus | Contains loops and collecting ducts |

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing excretion and egestion.
- Forgetting nephron is the kidney unit.
- Mixing ADH and ANF functions.
- Confusing ammonotelism and ureotelism.
- Forgetting ultrafiltration occurs in glomerulus.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Excretion removes metabolic wastes from the body.
- Kidneys are the main excretory organs in humans.
- Nephrons filter blood and form urine.
- Urine formation involves filtration, reabsorption and secretion.
- Hormones regulate water and salt balance.
- Kidney disorders affect waste removal.
