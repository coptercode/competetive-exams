# Introduction

Ecology is the study of interactions between organisms and their environment. Organisms live together in populations and interact with other organisms and environmental factors.

This chapter covers:

- Population interactions
- Mutualism
- Competition
- Predation
- Parasitism
- Population attributes
- Population growth
- Birth rate
- Death rate
- Age distribution

---

# PART A: ORGANISMS AND ENVIRONMENT

---

# 1. Ecology

## Definition

Ecology is the branch of biology that studies relationships between organisms and their environment.

---

# 2. Environmental Factors

Environmental factors are divided into:

## Abiotic Factors

Non-living components.

Examples:

- Temperature
- Water
- Light
- Soil

---

## Biotic Factors

Living components.

Examples:

- Plants
- Animals
- Microorganisms

---

# PART B: POPULATION

---

# 3. Population

## Definition

A group of individuals of the same species living in a particular area at a given time is called a population.

---

Examples:

- Deer population in a forest.
- Fish population in a pond.

---

# 4. Population Attributes

Characteristics of populations that cannot be studied in individuals are called population attributes.

---

Main attributes:

1. Population density
2. Birth rate
3. Death rate
4. Age distribution
5. Sex ratio

---

# PART C: POPULATION DENSITY

---

# 5. Population Density

## Definition

The number of individuals of a species present per unit area or volume is called population density.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{\text{Population Density}=\frac{\text{Number of individuals}}{\text{Area or volume}}}
\]

---

# PART D: POPULATION INTERACTIONS

---

# 6. Population Interactions

Organisms interact with each other for:

- Food
- Shelter
- Reproduction
- Survival

---

Types:

1. Mutualism
2. Competition
3. Predation
4. Parasitism

---

# PART E: MUTUALISM

---

# 7. Mutualism

## Definition

An interaction in which both organisms benefit from each other is called mutualism.

---

Relationship:

\[
\boxed{(+/+)}
\]

---

Examples:

---

## Lichen

Association between:

- Algae
- Fungus

Benefits:

Algae:

- Provides food.

Fungus:

- Provides shelter and water.

---

## Mycorrhiza

Association between:

- Fungus
- Plant roots

Benefits:

Plant:

- Better mineral absorption.

Fungus:

- Receives food.

---

# PART F: COMPETITION

---

# 8. Competition

## Definition

Interaction in which organisms compete for limited resources is called competition.

---

Relationship:

\[
\boxed{(-/-)}
\]

---

Resources competed for:

- Food
- Space
- Light
- Water

---

# 9. Competitive Exclusion Principle

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{G.F. Gause}}
\]

---

Statement:

Two species with identical ecological requirements cannot coexist indefinitely.

---

Example:

Paramecium species competition.

---

# PART G: PREDATION

---

# 10. Predation

## Definition

An interaction where one organism kills and consumes another organism is called predation.

---

Relationship:

\[
\boxed{(+/-)}
\]

---

Organism that kills:

\[
\boxed{\text{Predator}}
\]

Organism eaten:

\[
\boxed{\text{Prey}}
\]

---

Examples:

- Tiger and deer.
- Lion and zebra.

---

# 11. Importance of Predation

Predators help:

- Control prey population.
- Maintain ecosystem balance.
- Promote natural selection.

---

# PART H: PARASITISM

---

# 12. Parasitism

## Definition

An interaction where one organism benefits while the other is harmed is called parasitism.

---

Relationship:

\[
\boxed{(+/-)}
\]

---

Organism benefiting:

\[
\boxed{\text{Parasite}}
\]

Host:

Organism affected by parasite.

---

# 13. Types of Parasites

---

## Ectoparasites

Live on the surface of host.

Examples:

- Lice
- Ticks

---

## Endoparasites

Live inside the host body.

Examples:

- Tapeworm
- Ascaris

---

# PART I: POPULATION GROWTH

---

# 14. Population Growth

Population size changes due to:

1. Natality
2. Mortality
3. Immigration
4. Emigration

---

# 15. Natality (Birth Rate)

## Definition

The number of births in a population during a given period is called natality.

---

Effect:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increases population}}
\]

---

# 16. Mortality (Death Rate)

## Definition

The number of deaths in a population during a given period is called mortality.

---

Effect:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases population}}
\]

---

# 17. Immigration

## Definition

Movement of individuals into a population is called immigration.

---

Effect:

Population increases.

---

# 18. Emigration

## Definition

Movement of individuals out of a population is called emigration.

---

Effect:

Population decreases.

---

# PART J: POPULATION GROWTH MODELS

---

# 19. Exponential Growth

Occurs when:

- Resources are unlimited.

---

Equation:

\[
\boxed{\frac{dN}{dt}=rN}
\]

---

Produces:

\[
\boxed{\text{J-shaped growth curve}}
\]

---

# 20. Logistic Growth

Occurs when:

- Resources are limited.

---

Equation:

\[
\boxed{\frac{dN}{dt}=rN\frac{(K-N)}{K}}
\]

---

Produces:

\[
\boxed{\text{S-shaped growth curve}}
\]

---

# 21. Carrying Capacity (K)

## Definition

The maximum population size that an environment can support is called carrying capacity.

---

# PART K: AGE DISTRIBUTION

---

# 22. Age Distribution

The proportion of individuals belonging to different age groups in a population is called age distribution.

---

Age groups:

1. Pre-reproductive
2. Reproductive
3. Post-reproductive

---

# 23. Age Pyramids

Represent age distribution graphically.

---

Types:

---

## Expanding Population

- More young individuals.
- Population increases.

Shape:

Pyramid-shaped

---

## Stable Population

- Similar numbers in age groups.
- Population remains constant.

---

## Declining Population

- Fewer young individuals.
- Population decreases.

---

# Important Differences

## Mutualism vs Parasitism

| Mutualism              | Parasitism                 |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Both organisms benefit | One benefits, other harmed |
| (+/+)                  | (+/-)                      |

---

## Exponential vs Logistic Growth

| Exponential Growth   | Logistic Growth       |
| -------------------- | --------------------- |
| Unlimited resources  | Limited resources     |
| J-shaped curve       | S-shaped curve        |
| No carrying capacity | Has carrying capacity |

---

---

Interaction where both organisms benefit:

Answer:

\[

\]

---

## Example 2

Maximum population size supported by environment:

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Carrying capacity}}
\]

---

## Example 3

Population growth with limited resources:

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Logistic growth}}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing parasitism and predation.
- Forgetting competition affects both organisms negatively.
- Mixing natality and mortality.
- Forgetting carrying capacity is represented by K.
- Confusing immigration and emigration.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Ecology studies organism-environment relationships.
- Populations have unique characteristics like birth rate and death rate.
- Organisms interact through mutualism, competition, predation and parasitism.
- Population size changes through births, deaths and migration.
- Population growth depends on resource availability.
- Age distribution helps predict population trends.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Population ecology explains how organisms survive and interact within ecosystems.}
}
