# Introduction

An ecosystem is a functional unit of nature where living organisms interact with each other and with the physical environment.

This chapter covers:

- Structure of ecosystem
- Components of ecosystem
- Ecosystem patterns
- Productivity
- Decomposition
- Energy flow
- Ecological pyramids

---

# PART A: ECOSYSTEM

---

# 1. Ecosystem

## Definition

An ecosystem is a self-sustaining structural and functional unit of the biosphere where organisms interact with each other and with the environment.

---

Examples:

- Forest ecosystem
- Pond ecosystem
- Grassland ecosystem

---

# 2. Components of Ecosystem

An ecosystem consists of:

1. Abiotic components
2. Biotic components

---

# PART B: ABIOTIC COMPONENTS

---

# 3. Abiotic Components

## Definition

Non-living components of an ecosystem are called abiotic components.

---

Examples:

- Temperature
- Water
- Light
- Soil
- Minerals
- pH

---

# PART C: BIOTIC COMPONENTS

---

# 4. Biotic Components

Living organisms in an ecosystem are called biotic components.

---

They are classified into:

1. Producers
2. Consumers
3. Decomposers

---

# 5. Producers

## Definition

Organisms that prepare their own food by photosynthesis are called producers.

---

Examples:

- Green plants
- Algae

---

Function:

Convert solar energy into chemical energy.

---

# 6. Consumers

## Definition

Organisms that depend on other organisms for food are called consumers.

---

Types:

---

## Primary Consumers

Feed on producers.

Examples:

- Herbivores

---

## Secondary Consumers

Feed on primary consumers.

Examples:

- Small carnivores

---

## Tertiary Consumers

Feed on secondary consumers.

Examples:

- Top predators

---

# 7. Decomposers

## Definition

Organisms that break down dead organic matter are called decomposers.

---

Examples:

- Bacteria
- Fungi

---

Function:

- Recycle nutrients.
- Maintain ecosystem balance.

---

# PART D: ECOSYSTEM STRUCTURE

---

# 8. Food Chain

## Definition

A sequence of organisms through which food and energy pass is called a food chain.

---

Example:

Grass

↓

Grasshopper

↓

Frog

↓

Snake

↓

Eagle

---

# 9. Food Web

## Definition

A network of interconnected food chains is called a food web.

---

Importance:

- Provides ecosystem stability.
- Maintains biodiversity.

---

# 10. Trophic Levels

## Definition

Different feeding levels in a food chain are called trophic levels.

---

Levels:

1. Producers
2. Primary consumers
3. Secondary consumers
4. Tertiary consumers

---

# PART E: PRODUCTIVITY

---

# 11. Ecosystem Productivity

## Definition

The rate at which organic matter is produced in an ecosystem is called productivity.

---

Types:

1. Primary productivity
2. Secondary productivity

---

# 12. Primary Productivity

## Definition

The rate of production of organic matter by producers is called primary productivity.

---

Types:

---

## Gross Primary Productivity (GPP)

Total amount of organic matter produced by photosynthesis.

---

## Net Primary Productivity (NPP)

Organic matter remaining after respiration.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{NPP = GPP - Respiration}
\]

---

# 13. Secondary Productivity

## Definition

The rate of biomass production by consumers is called secondary productivity.

---

# PART F: DECOMPOSITION

---

# 14. Decomposition

## Definition

The breakdown of dead organic matter into simpler substances by decomposers is called decomposition.

---

# 15. Steps of Decomposition

---

## 1. Fragmentation

Breaking of detritus into smaller pieces.

---

## 2. Leaching

Removal of soluble substances into soil.

---

## 3. Catabolism

Breakdown of complex substances by enzymes.

---

## 4. Humification

Formation of dark-coloured humus.

---

## 5. Mineralisation

Release of inorganic nutrients from organic matter.

---

# PART G: ENERGY FLOW

---

# 16. Energy Flow in Ecosystem

Energy enters ecosystem through sunlight and flows from producers to consumers.

---

Flow:

Sun

↓

Producers

↓

Consumers

↓

Decomposers

---

# 17. Ten Percent Law

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Raymond Lindeman}}
\]

---

Statement:

Only about 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next.

---

Example:

Producer:

1000 units

↓

Primary consumer:

100 units

↓

Secondary consumer:

10 units

---

# 18. Energy Pyramid

Characteristics:

- Always upright.
- Energy decreases at higher trophic levels.

---

# PART H: ECOLOGICAL PYRAMIDS

---

# 19. Ecological Pyramid

## Definition

A graphical representation of trophic levels in an ecosystem is called an ecological pyramid.

---

Types:

1. Pyramid of numbers
2. Pyramid of biomass
3. Pyramid of energy

---

# 20. Pyramid of Numbers

Represents:

Number of organisms at each trophic level.

---

Example:

Grassland ecosystem

---

# 21. Pyramid of Biomass

Represents:

Total dry weight of organisms.

---

May be:

- Upright
- Inverted

---

Example:

Aquatic ecosystem may show inverted biomass pyramid.

---

# 22. Pyramid of Energy

Represents:

Energy content at each trophic level.

---

Always:

\[
\boxed{\text{Upright}}
\]

---

# PART I: ECOLOGICAL SUCCESSION

---

# 23. Ecological Succession

## Definition

The gradual change in species composition of an ecosystem over time is called ecological succession.

---

Types:

1. Primary succession
2. Secondary succession

---

# 24. Primary Succession

Occurs in:

- Newly formed areas without life.

Example:

Bare rocks.

---

# 25. Secondary Succession

Occurs in:

- Areas where organisms existed previously.

Example:

Abandoned agricultural land.

---

# Important Differences

## Food Chain vs Food Web

| Food Chain     | Food Web          |
| -------------- | ----------------- |
| Single pathway | Multiple pathways |
| Less stable    | More stable       |

---

## GPP vs NPP

| GPP                             | NPP                               |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Total photosynthetic production | Production after respiration loss |
| Higher value                    | Lower value                       |

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing producers and consumers.
- Forgetting NPP = GPP - respiration.
- Mixing food chain and food web.
- Forgetting energy pyramid is always upright.
- Confusing decomposition steps.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Ecosystems consist of living and non-living components.
- Producers capture solar energy and support food chains.
- Decomposers recycle nutrients.
- Energy decreases at each trophic level.
- Ecological pyramids represent trophic relationships.
- Productivity and decomposition maintain ecosystem functioning.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Ecosystems maintain balance through energy flow and nutrient cycling.}
}
\]
