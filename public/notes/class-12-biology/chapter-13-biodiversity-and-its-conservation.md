# Introduction

Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms present on Earth. It includes diversity at genetic, species and ecosystem levels.

Conservation of biodiversity is essential for maintaining ecological balance and survival of organisms.

This chapter covers:

- Concept of biodiversity
- Patterns of biodiversity
- Importance of biodiversity
- Loss of biodiversity
- Conservation strategies
- Biodiversity hotspots
- Endangered organisms
- Extinction
- Red Data Book
- Sacred groves
- Biosphere reserves

---

# PART A: BIODIVERSITY

---

# 1. Biodiversity

## Definition

The variety and variability of living organisms present in a particular region or on Earth is called biodiversity.

---

# 2. Levels of Biodiversity

Biodiversity exists at three levels:

1. Genetic diversity
2. Species diversity
3. Ecosystem diversity

---

# 3. Genetic Diversity

## Definition

Variation in genes among individuals of the same species is called genetic diversity.

---

Examples:

- Different varieties of rice.
- Different breeds of animals.

---

Importance:

- Helps adaptation.
- Increases survival ability.

---

# 4. Species Diversity

## Definition

The variety of species present in a region is called species diversity.

---

Includes:

- Number of species.
- Relative abundance of species.

---

# 5. Ecosystem Diversity

## Definition

The variety of ecosystems in a geographical area is called ecosystem diversity.

---

Examples:

- Forests.
- Deserts.
- Wetlands.
- Grasslands.

---

# PART B: PATTERNS OF BIODIVERSITY

---

# 6. Global Biodiversity Patterns

Biodiversity is not evenly distributed on Earth.

---

Factors affecting biodiversity:

- Climate.
- Temperature.
- Rainfall.
- Evolutionary history.

---

# 7. Latitudinal Gradient

## Definition

Biodiversity generally increases from poles towards the equator.

---

Reason:

- Tropical regions have stable climate.
- More sunlight.
- Higher productivity.

---

# 8. Species-Area Relationship

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Alexander von Humboldt}}
\]

---

Relationship:

Larger area generally supports more species.

---

Equation:

\[
\boxed{S = CA^Z}
\]

Where:

S = Species richness

A = Area

C and Z = Constants

# PART D: LOSS OF BIODIVERSITY

---

# 13. Biodiversity Loss

## Definition

Reduction in the variety of living organisms due to human and natural causes is called biodiversity loss.

---

# 14. Causes of Biodiversity Loss

Major causes:

\[
\boxed{\text{The Evil Quartet}}
\]

---

# 1. Habitat Loss and Fragmentation

## Definition

Destruction and separation of natural habitats.

---

Effects:

- Loss of species.
- Reduced population size.

---

# 2. Over-exploitation

## Definition

Excessive use of biological resources by humans.

---

Examples:

- Overfishing.
- Hunting.

---

# 3. Invasion by Alien Species

## Definition

Introduction of non-native species that harm local species.

---

Examples:

- Water hyacinth.
- Lantana.

---

# 4. Co-extinction

## Definition

Extinction of one species leading to extinction of another dependent species.

---

Example:

Host-specific parasites disappearing with host extinction.

---

# PART E: ENDANGERED ORGANISMS AND EXTINCTION

---

# 15. Extinction

## Definition

Permanent disappearance of a species from Earth is called extinction.

---

# 16. Causes of Extinction

- Habitat destruction.
- Climate change.
- Pollution.
- Overexploitation.
- Invasive species.

---

# 17. Endangered Species

## Definition

Species facing a high risk of extinction are called endangered species.

---

Examples:

- Tiger.
- Rhinoceros.
- Lion-tailed macaque.

---

# 18. Extinct Species

Examples:

- Dodo.
- Passenger pigeon.

---

# PART F: BIODIVERSITY HOTSPOTS

---

# 19. Biodiversity Hotspot

## Definition

A region with high species richness and a high degree of endemism that is under threat is called a biodiversity hotspot.

---

Identified by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Norman Myers}}
\]

---

# 20. Criteria for Hotspots

A region must have:

1. At least 1500 endemic vascular plant species.
2. Lost more than 70% of original habitat.

---

# 21. Indian Biodiversity Hotspots

India has parts of:

1. Western Ghats
2. Himalayas
3. Indo-Burma region
4. Sundaland

---

# PART G: BIODIVERSITY CONSERVATION

---

# 22. Conservation

## Definition

Protection and management of biodiversity to ensure its survival is called conservation.

---

Methods:

1. In situ conservation
2. Ex situ conservation

---

# PART H: IN SITU CONSERVATION

---

# 23. In Situ Conservation

## Definition

Protection of organisms in their natural habitats is called in situ conservation.

---

Examples:

- National parks.
- Wildlife sanctuaries.
- Biosphere reserves.

---

# 24. National Parks

Protected areas conserving wildlife and habitats.

---

Example:

- Yellowstone National Park.

---

# 25. Wildlife Sanctuaries

Areas where wild animals are protected from hunting and disturbance.

---

# 26. Biosphere Reserves

## Definition

Large protected areas conserving biodiversity along with sustainable use of resources.

---

Zones:

1. Core zone
2. Buffer zone
3. Transition zone

---

# PART I: EX SITU CONSERVATION

---

# 27. Ex Situ Conservation

## Definition

Conservation of organisms outside their natural habitats is called ex situ conservation.

---

Examples:

- Zoos.
- Botanical gardens.
- Seed banks.
- Gene banks.

---

# PART K: SACRED GROVES

---

# 29. Sacred Groves

- Protect biodiversity.

---

Examples in India:

- Khasi hills.
- Western Ghats.

# Important Differences

## In Situ vs Ex Situ Conservation

| In Situ                         | Ex Situ                              |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Conservation in natural habitat | Conservation outside natural habitat |
| Protects whole ecosystem        | Protects selected species            |
| Example: National parks         | Example: Zoos                        |

---

## Genetic Diversity vs Species Diversity

| Genetic Diversity        | Species Diversity  |
| ------------------------ | ------------------ |
| Variation within species | Variety of species |
| Gene level               | Species level      |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing biodiversity levels.
- Mixing in situ and ex situ conservation.
- Forgetting hotspots require high endemism and habitat loss.
- Confusing endangered and extinct species.
- Forgetting Red Data Book is maintained by IUCN.

# Chapter Summary

- Biodiversity includes genetic, species and ecosystem diversity.
- Biodiversity supports ecological balance and human welfare.
- Habitat loss, over-exploitation, invasive species and co-extinction cause biodiversity loss.
- Conservation can be in situ or ex situ.
- Hotspots protect highly diverse threatened regions.
- Biosphere reserves, sacred groves and protected areas help conserve biodiversity.
