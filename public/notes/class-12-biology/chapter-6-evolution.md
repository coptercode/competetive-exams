# Introduction

Evolution is the gradual process of change in organisms over generations that leads to the formation of new species.

It explains the diversity of life on Earth.

This chapter covers:

- Origin of life
- Evidence of evolution
- Evolutionary theories
- Darwin's contribution
- Modern synthetic theory
- Mechanisms of evolution
- Population genetics
- Human evolution
- Pathogens and malaria

---

# PART A: ORIGIN OF LIFE

---

# 1. Origin of Life

## Definition

The process by which life originated on Earth is called origin of life.

---

# 2. Theories of Origin of Life

---

# A. Theory of Special Creation

According to this theory:

- Life was created by supernatural forces.
- Species were created in their present form.

---

# B. Theory of Abiogenesis

## Definition

The theory that life originated from non-living matter is called abiogenesis.

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Spontaneous generation theory}}
\]

---

# C. Biogenesis Theory

## Definition

Life arises only from pre-existing life.

---

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Louis Pasteur}}
\]

---

# D. Chemical Evolution Theory

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Oparin and Haldane}}
\]

---

Main idea:

- Early Earth had simple molecules.
- Organic molecules formed gradually.
- First life forms developed from these molecules.

---

# 3. Miller-Urey Experiment

Scientists:

\[
\boxed{\text{Stanley Miller and Harold Urey}}
\]

---

Experiment:

- Simulated early Earth atmosphere.
- Produced organic molecules like amino acids.

---

Importance:

Supported chemical evolution theory.

---

---

# 4. Biological Evolution

---

# 5. Evidence of Evolution

Evidence comes from:

4. Molecular biology

# PART C: PALEONTOLOGICAL EVIDENCE

---

# 6. Fossils

## Definition

Remains or impressions of ancient organisms preserved in rocks are called fossils.

---

Study of fossils:

\[
\boxed{\text{Palaeontology}}
\]

---

Importance:

- Shows evolutionary history.
- Shows extinct organisms.
- Provides evidence of gradual changes.

---

---

\[

# PART D: COMPARATIVE ANATOMY EVIDENCE

---

# 8. Homologous Organs

## Definition

Organs having similar structural origin but different functions are called homologous organs.

---

Example:

Forelimbs of:

- Humans
- Whales
- Bats

---

Indicate:

\[
\boxed{\text{Common ancestry}}
\]

---

# 9. Analogous Organs

## Definition

Organs having different origins but similar functions are called analogous organs.

---

Example:

- Wings of birds
- Wings of insects

---

Indicate:

\[
\boxed{\text{Convergent evolution}}
\]

---

# 10. Vestigial Organs

## Definition

Reduced organs with little or no function are called vestigial organs.

---

Examples:

- Appendix
- Coccyx

---

# PART E: EMBRYOLOGICAL EVIDENCE

---

# 11. Embryological Evidence

## Definition

Similarity in embryonic development among organisms provides evidence for evolution.

---

Example:

Early embryos of vertebrates show similarities.

---

# PART F: MOLECULAR EVIDENCE

---

# 12. Molecular Evidence

Comparison of:

- DNA sequences
- Protein structures

helps determine evolutionary relationships.

---

Greater similarity:

\[
\boxed{\text{Closer evolutionary relationship}}
\]

# 14. Darwin's Theory of Natural Selection

Main points:

---

## 1. Overproduction

Organisms produce more offspring than can survive.

---

## 2. Variation

Individuals show differences.

---

## 3. Struggle for Existence

Organisms compete for resources.

---

## 4. Survival of the Fittest

Individuals with useful variations survive.

---

## 5. Natural Selection

Favourable traits are passed to future generations.

---

# 15. Example of Natural Selection

## Industrial Melanism

Observed in:

Peppered moth

---

During industrial pollution:

Dark moths survived better.

# PART I: MECHANISM OF EVOLUTION

---

# 17. Mutation

## Definition

A sudden heritable change in genetic material is called mutation.

---

Types:

- Gene mutation
- Chromosomal mutation

---

Importance:

Produces new variations.

---

# 18. Recombination

## Definition

Formation of new gene combinations during sexual reproduction is called recombination.

---

Occurs during:

\[
\boxed{\text{Meiosis}}
\]

---

# 19. Natural Selection

## Definition

Selection of favourable variations by nature is called natural selection.

---

# TYPES OF NATURAL SELECTION

---

# 20. Stabilising Selection

Favours average characteristics.

---

Example:

Human birth weight.

---

# 21. Directional Selection

Favours one extreme variation.

---

Example:

Industrial melanism.

---

# 22. Disruptive Selection

Favours both extreme variations.

---

---

# PART J: POPULATION GENETICS

---

# 23. Gene Flow

## Definition

Movement of genes between populations is called gene flow.

---

Cause:

Migration.

---

# 24. Genetic Drift

## Definition

Random changes in allele frequency in a small population are called genetic drift.

---

Example:

Founder effect.

---

# 25. Hardy-Weinberg Principle

## Definition

Allele frequencies remain constant in a population when evolutionary forces are absent.

---

Equation:

\[
\boxed{p^2+2pq+q^2=1}
\]

---

Conditions:

- No mutation.
- No migration.
- Random mating.
- Large population.
- No natural selection.

---

# Important Differences

## Homologous vs Analogous Organs

| Homologous          | Analogous            |
| ------------------- | -------------------- |
| Same origin         | Different origin     |
| Different functions | Same function        |
| Divergent evolution | Convergent evolution |

---

## Gene Flow vs Genetic Drift

| Gene Flow           | Genetic Drift        |
| ------------------- | -------------------- |
| Due to migration    | Random changes       |
| Increases variation | May reduce variation |

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing homologous and analogous organs.
- Mixing Darwinism and Lamarckism.
- Forgetting Hardy-Weinberg conditions.
- Confusing mutation and recombination.
- Forgetting malaria vector is female Anopheles mosquito.

# Chapter Summary

- Evolution explains the diversity of life.
- Fossils and anatomy provide evidence for evolution.
- Darwin proposed natural selection.
- Mutations and recombination create variations.
- Population genetics explains evolutionary changes.
- Adaptive radiation produces diverse species.
- Human evolution occurred through gradual changes over millions of years.
