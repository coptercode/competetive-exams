# Introduction

Transportation is the process of movement of essential substances from one part of the body to another.

This chapter covers:

- Active transport
- Blood components
- Blood circulation
- Human heart structure
- Double circulation

---

# PART A: TRANSPORTATION IN PLANTS AND ANIMALS

---

# 1. Transportation

## Definition

The movement of water, minerals, nutrients and gases throughout an organism is called transportation.

---

Importance:

- Supplies nutrients
- Removes wastes
- Maintains body functions

---

# PART B: ACTIVE TRANSPORT

---

# 2. Active Transport

## Definition

The movement of substances across a cell membrane against the concentration gradient using energy is called active transport.

---

Characteristics:

- Requires energy (ATP)
- Occurs against concentration gradient
- Uses carrier proteins

---

Example:

Absorption of mineral ions by plant roots.

---

# 3. Difference Between Active and Passive Transport

| Active Transport               | Passive Transport            |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| Requires energy                | Does not require energy      |
| Against concentration gradient | Along concentration gradient |
| Uses carrier proteins          | May occur by diffusion       |

---

# PART C: BLOOD

---

# 4. Blood

## Definition

Blood is a fluid connective tissue that transports substances throughout the body.

---

Functions:

- Transport oxygen
- Transport nutrients
- Remove wastes
- Protect against diseases
- Control body temperature

---

# 5. Components of Blood

Blood consists of:

1. Plasma
2. Red blood cells
3. White blood cells
4. Platelets

---

# 6. Plasma

## Definition

The liquid component of blood is called plasma.

---

Composition:

- Water
- Proteins
- Salts
- Hormones
- Waste materials

---

Function:

Transports dissolved substances.

---

# 7. Red Blood Cells (RBC)

## Definition

Red blood cells are blood cells responsible for oxygen transport.

---

Features:

- Contain haemoglobin
- Red in colour
- No nucleus in humans

---

Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Transport oxygen}}
\]

---

# 8. Haemoglobin

## Definition

Haemoglobin is an iron-containing pigment present in RBCs.

---

Function:

Combines with oxygen to form oxyhaemoglobin.

---

# 9. White Blood Cells (WBC)

## Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Defence against infections}}
\]

---

Types:

- Neutrophils
- Lymphocytes
- Monocytes

---

# 10. Platelets

## Function:

\[
\boxed{\text{Blood clotting}}
\]

---

They prevent excessive blood loss.

---

# PART D: HUMAN HEART

---

# 11. Heart

## Definition

The heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood throughout the body.

---

Location:

\[
\boxed{\text{Chest cavity}}
\]

---

# 12. Chambers of Heart

Human heart has four chambers:

1. Right atrium
2. Right ventricle
3. Left atrium
4. Left ventricle

---

# 13. Functions of Chambers

---

## Right Atrium

Receives deoxygenated blood from body.

---

## Right Ventricle

Pumps blood to lungs.

---

## Left Atrium

Receives oxygenated blood from lungs.

---

## Left Ventricle

Pumps oxygenated blood to body.

---

# 14. Valves of Heart

## Definition

Valves prevent backward flow of blood.

---

Major valves:

- Tricuspid valve
- Bicuspid valve
- Semilunar valves

---

# PART E: BLOOD CIRCULATION

---

# 15. Circulatory System

## Definition

The system responsible for circulation of blood throughout the body is called circulatory system.

---

Components:

- Heart
- Blood vessels
- Blood

---

# 16. Blood Vessels

Types:

1. Arteries
2. Veins
3. Capillaries

---

# 17. Arteries

Characteristics:

- Carry blood away from heart
- Thick muscular walls
- Usually carry oxygenated blood

Example:

Aorta

---

# 18. Veins

Characteristics:

- Carry blood towards heart
- Thin walls
- Have valves

Example:

Vena cava

---

# 19. Capillaries

Characteristics:

- Very thin walls
- Connect arteries and veins
- Allow exchange of substances

---

# PART F: DOUBLE CIRCULATION

---

# 20. Double Circulation

## Definition

The circulation in which blood passes through the heart twice during one complete cycle is called double circulation.

---

Types:

1. Pulmonary circulation
2. Systemic circulation

---

# 21. Pulmonary Circulation

## Definition

Movement of blood between heart and lungs.

---

Path:

Right ventricle

↓

Pulmonary artery

↓

Lungs

↓

Pulmonary vein

↓

Left atrium

---

Function:

Oxygenation of blood.

---

# 22. Systemic Circulation

## Definition

Movement of blood between heart and body parts.

---

Path:

Left ventricle

↓

Aorta

↓

Body tissues

↓

Vena cava

↓

Right atrium

---

Function:

Supply oxygen and nutrients to body.

---

# PART G: CARDIAC CYCLE

---

# 23. Cardiac Cycle

## Definition

The sequence of events that occur during one heartbeat is called cardiac cycle.

---

Stages:

1. Atrial contraction
2. Ventricular contraction
3. Relaxation

---

# 24. Heartbeat

Average human heartbeat:

\[
\boxed{72\ beats/minute}
\]

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Which blood cells transport oxygen?

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Red blood cells}}
\]

---

## Example 2

Which blood component helps clotting?

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Platelets}}
\]

---

## Example 3

Why is the left ventricle thicker than the right ventricle?

Because it pumps blood to the entire body and requires more force.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing arteries and veins.
- Forgetting RBC contains haemoglobin.
- Mixing pulmonary and systemic circulation.
- Forgetting valves prevent backflow.
- Confusing active transport with diffusion.

---

- Transportation moves essential substances through organisms.
- Blood consists of plasma, RBC, WBC and platelets.
- The heart pumps blood through chambers and valves.
- Humans show double circulation.
- Blood vessels perform different transport functions.
