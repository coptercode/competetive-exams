# Introduction

Nuclear physics deals with the study of atomic nuclei, radioactivity and nuclear energy.

This chapter covers:

- Atomic nucleus
- Radioactivity
- Alpha, beta and gamma decay
- Nuclear fission
- Nuclear fusion
- Nuclear reactors

---

# PART A: ATOMIC STRUCTURE

---

# 1. Atom

## Definition

An atom is the smallest unit of an element that retains its chemical properties.

---

An atom consists of:

1. Nucleus
2. Electrons

---

# 2. Nucleus

## Definition

The central dense region of an atom containing protons and neutrons is called the nucleus.

---

Contains:

- Protons
- Neutrons

Together called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Nucleons}}
\]

---

# 3. Atomic Number

## Definition

The number of protons present in the nucleus of an atom is called atomic number.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{Z}
\]

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Z=\text{Number of protons}
}
\]

---

# 4. Mass Number

## Definition

The total number of protons and neutrons present in the nucleus is called mass number.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{A}
\]

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
A=Z+N
}
\]

---

Where:

Z = protons

N = neutrons

---

# PART B: RADIOACTIVITY

---

# 5. Radioactivity

## Definition

The spontaneous emission of radiation from unstable atomic nuclei is called radioactivity.

---

Discovered by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Henri Becquerel}}
\]

---

# 6. Radioactive Substances

Examples:

- Uranium
- Radium
- Thorium

---

# 7. Properties of Radioactive Radiation

- Invisible
- Travel at high speed
- Penetrate matter
- Affect photographic plates
- Produce ionisation

---

# PART C: TYPES OF RADIOACTIVE DECAY

---

# 8. Alpha Decay (α)

## Definition

Emission of an alpha particle from a radioactive nucleus is called alpha decay.

---

Alpha particle:

\[
\boxed{
^4_2He
}
\]

---

Changes:

Mass number:

\[
A-4
\]

Atomic number:

\[
Z-2
\]

---

Example:

\[
^{238}_{92}U
\rightarrow
^{234}_{90}Th+^{4}_{2}He
\]

---

# 9. Beta Decay (β)

## Definition

Emission of beta particles due to conversion of neutron into proton is called beta decay.

---

Beta particle:

\[
\boxed{
^0_{-1}e
}
\]

---

Changes:

Mass number:

\[
\text{No change}
\]

Atomic number:

\[
Z+1
\]

---

Example:

\[
^{14}_{6}C
\rightarrow
^{14}_{7}N+\beta
\]

---

# 10. Gamma Decay (γ)

## Definition

Emission of high energy electromagnetic radiation from an excited nucleus is called gamma decay.

---

Properties:

- No charge
- No mass
- Very high penetration

---

Changes:

Mass number:

\[
\boxed{\text{No change}}
\]

Atomic number:

\[
\boxed{\text{No change}}
\]

---

# PART D: COMPARISON OF RADIOACTIVE RAYS

| Property    | Alpha          | Beta       | Gamma                |
| ----------- | -------------- | ---------- | -------------------- |
| Nature      | Helium nucleus | Electron   | Electromagnetic wave |
| Charge      | +2             | -1         | 0                    |
| Mass        | High           | Very small | No mass              |
| Penetration | Low            | Medium     | High                 |
| Speed       | Low            | High       | Speed of light       |

---

# PART E: HALF-LIFE

---

# 11. Half-Life Period

## Definition

The time required for half of the radioactive nuclei in a sample to decay is called half-life.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{T_{1/2}}
\]

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
N=\frac{N_0}{2^n}
}
\]

---

Where:

N = remaining nuclei

N₀ = initial nuclei

n = number of half-lives

---

# PART F: NUCLEAR ENERGY

---

# 12. Nuclear Fission

## Definition

The splitting of a heavy nucleus into two lighter nuclei with release of energy is called nuclear fission.

---

Example:

Uranium fission:

\[
^{235}U
\]

---

Releases:

- Energy
- Neutrons

---

# 13. Nuclear Fusion

## Definition

The combination of two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus with release of energy is called nuclear fusion.

---

Example:

Hydrogen nuclei combine to form helium.

---

Occurs in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Sun and stars}}
\]

---

# 14. Difference Between Fission and Fusion

| Nuclear Fission            | Nuclear Fusion                 |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Heavy nucleus splits       | Light nuclei combine           |
| Used in reactors           | Occurs in stars                |
| Produces radioactive waste | Less waste                     |
| Requires less temperature  | Requires very high temperature |

---

# PART G: NUCLEAR REACTOR

---

# 15. Nuclear Reactor

## Definition

A device used to produce controlled nuclear energy through fission reactions is called a nuclear reactor.

---

# 16. Components of Nuclear Reactor

---

## 1. Fuel

Material undergoing fission.

Examples:

- Uranium-235
- Plutonium-239

---

## 2. Moderator

Slows down neutrons.

Examples:

- Graphite
- Heavy water

---

## 3. Control Rods

Control the rate of reaction.

Examples:

- Cadmium
- Boron

---

## 4. Coolant

Removes heat produced.

Examples:

- Water
- Liquid sodium

---

# 17. Working of Nuclear Reactor

Steps:

1. Nuclear fuel undergoes fission.
2. Energy is released as heat.
3. Coolant carries heat.
4. Steam is produced.
5. Turbine generates electricity.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing atomic number and mass number.
- Forgetting beta decay changes atomic number only.
- Mixing fission and fusion processes.
- Forgetting gamma rays have no mass and charge.
- Confusing radioactive decay with chemical reactions.
- Forgetting nuclear reactors require controlled fission.
