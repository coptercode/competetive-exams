# Introduction

A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances.

This chapter covers:

- Solute and solvent
- Types of solutions
- Solubility
- Factors affecting solubility
- Concentrated and diluted solutions
- Saturated and unsaturated solutions

---

# PART A: BASIC CONCEPTS OF SOLUTIONS

---

# 1. Solution

## Definition

A solution is a homogeneous mixture of a solute and a solvent.

---

Examples:

- Salt solution
- Sugar solution
- Air

---

# 2. Components of a Solution

A solution contains two parts:

---

## Solute

### Definition

The substance that is dissolved in a solvent is called solute.

Example:

Salt in salt water.

---

## Solvent

### Definition

The substance that dissolves the solute is called solvent.

Example:

Water in salt water.

---

# 3. Difference Between Solute and Solvent

| Solute                      | Solvent                    |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Present in smaller quantity | Present in larger quantity |
| Gets dissolved              | Dissolves solute           |
| Example: Salt               | Example: Water             |

---

# PART B: TYPES OF SOLUTIONS

---

# 4. Based on Physical State

---

## Solid Solution

Solute and solvent are solids.

Example:

Alloys

---

## Liquid Solution

Solvent is liquid.

Examples:

- Salt water
- Sugar solution

---

## Gaseous Solution

Both components are gases.

Example:

Air

---

# 5. Based on Amount of Solute

---

## Dilute Solution

A solution containing a small amount of solute is called a dilute solution.

---

Example:

Small amount of salt in large amount of water.

---

## Concentrated Solution

A solution containing a large amount of solute is called a concentrated solution.

---

Example:

Highly concentrated salt solution.

---

# PART C: SOLUBILITY

---

# 6. Solubility

## Definition

The maximum amount of solute that can dissolve in a given amount of solvent at a particular temperature is called solubility.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Solubility=
\frac{Mass\ of\ solute}
{Mass\ of\ solvent}
\times100
}
\]

---

# 7. Factors Affecting Solubility

Solubility depends on:

1. Nature of solute and solvent
2. Temperature
3. Pressure

---

# PART D: EFFECT OF TEMPERATURE

---

# 8. Solubility of Solids

For most solids:

Increase in temperature:

\[
\boxed{\text{Solubility increases}}
\]

---

Example:

Sugar dissolves better in hot water.

---

# 9. Solubility of Gases

For gases:

Increase in temperature:

\[
\boxed{\text{Solubility decreases}}
\]

---

Example:

Cold drinks lose gas when heated.

---

# PART E: EFFECT OF PRESSURE

---

# 10. Pressure and Gas Solubility

According to Henry's law:

At constant temperature, solubility of a gas increases with pressure.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
S\propto P
}
\]

---

Applications:

- Carbonated drinks
- Scuba diving

---

# PART F: SATURATED AND UNSATURATED SOLUTIONS

---

# 11. Unsaturated Solution

## Definition

A solution that can dissolve more solute at a given temperature is called an unsaturated solution.

---

Example:

Small amount of salt in water.

---

# 12. Saturated Solution

## Definition

A solution that cannot dissolve any more solute at a particular temperature is called a saturated solution.

---

Condition:

\[
\boxed{
Maximum\ solute\ dissolved
}
\]

---

# 13. Supersaturated Solution

## Definition

A solution containing more dissolved solute than a saturated solution is called a supersaturated solution.

---

# PART G: CONCENTRATION OF SOLUTIONS

---

# 14. Concentration

## Definition

The amount of solute present in a given amount of solution is called concentration.

---

# 15. Percentage by Mass

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\%=
\frac{Mass\ of\ solute}
{Mass\ of\ solution}
\times100
}
\]

---

Where:

\[
Mass\ of\ solution
=

Mass\ of\ solute+Mass\ of\ solvent
\]

---

# 16. Percentage by Volume

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\%=
\frac{Volume\ of\ solute}
{Volume\ of\ solution}
\times100
}
\]

---

# PART H: SOLUBILITY CURVE

---

# 17. Solubility Curve

## Definition

A graph showing the variation of solubility with temperature is called a solubility curve.

---

Uses:

- Compare solubility of substances.
- Determine amount of solute required.

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Identify solute and solvent in salt water.

Solute:

\[
\boxed{\text{Salt}}
\]

Solvent:

\[
\boxed{\text{Water}}
\]

---

## Example 2

A solution contains 20 g salt and 80 g water.

Find mass percentage.

Mass of solution:

\[
20+80=100g
\]

Percentage:

\[
=\frac{20}{100}\times100
\]

\[
\boxed{20\%}
\]

---

## Example 3

Why are soft drinks stored under high pressure?

Because pressure increases the solubility of gases.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing solute and solvent.
- Forgetting solution contains both solute and solvent.
- Using wrong temperature effect for gases.
- Mixing concentrated solution with saturated solution.
- Forgetting pressure affects gas solubility.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Solutions are homogeneous mixtures.
- Solute dissolves in solvent.
- Solubility depends on temperature, pressure and nature of substances.
- Solid solubility usually increases with temperature.
- Gas solubility increases with pressure.
- Concentration represents the amount of solute present.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Solutions are essential mixtures used in chemistry and daily life.}
}
\]
