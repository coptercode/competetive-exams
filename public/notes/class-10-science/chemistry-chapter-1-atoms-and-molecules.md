# Introduction

All matter is made up of tiny particles called atoms and molecules.

This chapter covers:

- Atomic mass
- Relative atomic mass
- Mole concept
- Avogadro's hypothesis
- Molecular mass
- Atomicity

---

# PART A: ATOMS

---

# 1. Atom

## Definition

An atom is the smallest particle of an element that takes part in chemical reactions and retains the properties of that element.

---

Examples:

- Hydrogen atom (H)
- Oxygen atom (O)
- Carbon atom (C)

---

# 2. Molecule

## Definition

A molecule is the smallest particle of a substance that can exist independently and shows all properties of that substance.

---

Examples:

\[
H_2,\ O_2,\ H_2O
\]

---

# 3. Difference Between Atom and Molecule

| Atom                            | Molecule                                     |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Smallest particle of an element | Smallest independent particle of a substance |
| May not exist independently     | Can exist independently                      |
| Example: H, O                   | Example: H₂, O₂                              |

---

# PART B: ATOMIC MASS

---

# 4. Atomic Mass

## Definition

The mass of an atom expressed in atomic mass units (u) is called atomic mass.

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{\text{u (atomic mass unit)}}
\]

---

# 5. Atomic Mass Unit

## Definition

One atomic mass unit is defined as:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{1}{12}
}
\]

of the mass of one carbon-12 atom.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{u}
\]

---

# 6. Relative Atomic Mass

## Definition

The ratio of the average mass of an atom to one-twelfth of the mass of carbon-12 atom is called relative atomic mass.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
A_r=
\frac{\text{Average mass of atom}}
{\frac{1}{12}\text{ mass of carbon-12}}
}
\]

---

Examples:

| Element  | Relative Atomic Mass |
| -------- | -------------------- |
| Hydrogen | 1                    |
| Carbon   | 12                   |
| Oxygen   | 16                   |
| Sodium   | 23                   |

---

# PART C: MOLE CONCEPT

---

# 7. Mole

## Definition

A mole is the amount of substance containing \(6.022\times10^{23}\) particles.

---

Number of particles in one mole:

\[
\boxed{
6.022\times10^{23}
}
\]

---

This number is called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Avogadro number}}
\]

---

# 8. Avogadro Number

## Definition

The number of atoms, molecules or ions present in one mole of a substance is called Avogadro number.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{N_A}
\]

---

Value:

\[
\boxed{
6.022\times10^{23}
}
\]

---

# 9. Molar Mass

## Definition

The mass of one mole of a substance is called molar mass.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Molar\ mass=
\frac{Mass}{Number\ of\ moles}
}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{g/mol}
\]

---

# 10. Relationship Between Moles and Mass

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
n=\frac{m}{M}
}
\]

---

Where:

n = number of moles

m = given mass

M = molar mass

---

# Example

Find moles in 18 g of water.

Molar mass of water:

\[
H_2O=2(1)+16
\]

\[
=18g/mol
\]

Using:

\[
n=\frac mM
\]

\[
=\frac{18}{18}
\]

\[
\boxed{1\ mole}
\]

---

# PART D: MOLECULAR MASS

---

# 11. Molecular Mass

## Definition

The sum of atomic masses of all atoms present in one molecule is called molecular mass.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Molecular\ mass=
\text{Sum of atomic masses}
}
\]

---

# Examples

---

## Water (H₂O)

Hydrogen:

\[
2\times1=2
\]

Oxygen:

\[
16
\]

Molecular mass:

\[
\boxed{18u}
\]

---

## Carbon dioxide (CO₂)

Carbon:

\[
12
\]

Oxygen:

\[
2\times16=32
\]

Total:

\[
\boxed{44u}
\]

---

# PART E: ATOMICITY

---

# 12. Atomicity

## Definition

The number of atoms present in one molecule of an element is called atomicity.

---

# Types of Molecules

---

## 1. Monoatomic

Contains one atom.

Examples:

\[
He,\ Ne,\ Ar
\]

Atomicity:

\[
\boxed{1}
\]

---

## 2. Diatomic

Contains two atoms.

Examples:

\[
H_2,\ O_2,\ N_2
\]

Atomicity:

\[
\boxed{2}
\]

---

## 3. Triatomic

Contains three atoms.

Example:

\[
O_3
\]

Atomicity:

\[
\boxed{3}
\]

---

## 4. Polyatomic

Contains more than three atoms.

Examples:

\[
P_4,\ S_8
\]

---

# PART F: AVOGADRO'S HYPOTHESIS

---

# 13. Avogadro's Hypothesis

## Statement

Equal volumes of all gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules.

---

Mathematical form:

\[
\boxed{
V\propto n
}
\]

---

Where:

V = volume

n = number of molecules

---

# Important Formula Sheet

## Number of particles

\[
\boxed{
N=n\times N_A
}
\]

---

## Mole calculation

\[
\boxed{
n=\frac mM
}
\]

---

## Molar mass

\[
\boxed{
M=\frac m n
}
\]

---

## Avogadro Number

\[
\boxed{
6.022\times10^{23}
}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing atomic mass and molecular mass.
- Forgetting Avogadro number value.
- Using grams instead of grams per mole.
- Confusing atom with molecule.
- Forgetting atomicity refers to number of atoms in a molecule.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Matter is made up of atoms and molecules.
- Atomic mass is measured in atomic mass units.
- One mole contains \(6.022\times10^{23}\) particles.
- Molecular mass is the sum of atomic masses.
- Atomicity represents the number of atoms in a molecule.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Atoms combine to form molecules, which build all matter around us.}
}
\]
