# Introduction

Heredity explains the transmission of characteristics from parents to offspring. Evolution explains the gradual changes in organisms over generations.

This chapter covers:

- Mendel's laws
- Genes and DNA
- Chromosomes
- Sex determination
- Evolution

---

# PART A: HEREDITY

---

# 1. Heredity

## Definition

The transmission of genetic characters from parents to offspring is called heredity.

---

Examples of inherited characters:

- Eye colour
- Hair type
- Blood group
- Height

---

# 2. Variation

## Definition

Differences between individuals of the same species are called variations.

---

Importance:

- Helps organisms adapt.
- Provides raw material for evolution.

---

# PART B: MENDEL'S EXPERIMENTS

---

# 3. Gregor Johann Mendel

## Father of Genetics

\[
\boxed{\text{Gregor Mendel}}
\]

---

He studied inheritance using:

\[
\boxed{\text{Pea plants}}
\]

---

Reasons for selecting pea plants:

- Short life cycle
- Clear contrasting traits
- Easy to grow

---

# 4. Mendel's Monohybrid Cross

A cross involving one pair of contrasting characters is called a monohybrid cross.

---

Example:

Tall plant × Dwarf plant

---

First generation:

\[
TT \times tt
\]

Produces:

\[
\boxed{\text{All Tall plants}}
\]

---

# 5. Dominant Character

## Definition

A character that appears in the first generation is called a dominant character.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{\text{Capital letter}}
\]

Example:

\[
T
\]

---

# 6. Recessive Character

## Definition

A character that remains hidden in the first generation is called a recessive character.

---

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{\text{Small letter}}
\]

Example:

\[
t
\]

---

# PART C: MENDEL'S LAWS

---

# 7. Law of Dominance

## Statement

When two contrasting characters are present together, only the dominant character is expressed.

---

# 8. Law of Segregation

## Statement

The two factors of a character separate during gamete formation.

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Law of purity of gametes}}
\]

---

# 9. Law of Independent Assortment

## Statement

Genes controlling different characters are inherited independently.

---

# PART D: GENES AND DNA

---

# 10. Gene

## Definition

A gene is the functional unit of heredity that controls a particular character.

---

Location:

\[
\boxed{\text{Chromosome}}
\]

---

# 11. Chromosome

## Definition

Chromosomes are thread-like structures present in the nucleus that carry genes.

---

Made of:

- DNA
- Proteins

---

# 12. DNA

## Full Form:

\[
\boxed{\text{Deoxyribonucleic Acid}}
\]

---

## Definition

DNA is the hereditary material that carries genetic information.

---

Structure:

- Double helix
- Two nucleotide chains

---

# 13. Nucleotide

Basic unit of DNA.

Components:

1. Sugar
2. Phosphate
3. Nitrogen base

---

Nitrogen bases:

- Adenine (A)
- Thymine (T)
- Guanine (G)
- Cytosine (C)

---

Base pairing:

\[
A=T
\]

\[
G=C
\]

---

# PART E: SEX DETERMINATION

---

# 14. Sex Determination

## Definition

The process by which the sex of an individual is determined is called sex determination.

---

# 15. Human Chromosomes

Humans have:

\[
\boxed{46\ chromosomes}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{23\ pairs}
\]

---

Autosomes:

\[
22\ pairs
\]

Sex chromosomes:

\[
1\ pair
\]

---

# 16. Sex Chromosomes

Female:

\[
\boxed{XX}
\]

Male:

\[
\boxed{XY}
\]

---

# 17. Sex Determination in Humans

Female produces:

\[
X\ eggs
\]

Male produces:

\[
X\ or\ Y\ sperm
\]

---

Combination:

X sperm + X egg:

\[
XX
\]

Female child

---

Y sperm + X egg:

\[
XY
\]

Male child

---

# PART F: EVOLUTION

---

# 18. Evolution

## Definition

The gradual change in organisms over generations is called evolution.

---

# 19. Evidence for Evolution

---

## Fossils

Remains of ancient organisms preserved in rocks.

---

## Comparative Anatomy

Study of similarities and differences in body structures.

---

## Embryology

Study of development of embryos.

---

# 20. Natural Selection

## Definition

The process by which organisms with favourable variations survive and reproduce is called natural selection.

---

Proposed by:

\[
\boxed{\text{Charles Darwin}}
\]

---

# 21. Adaptation

## Definition

Features that help organisms survive in their environment are called adaptations.

---

Examples:

- Camouflage
- Thick fur in polar animals

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing genes and chromosomes.
- Forgetting DNA carries hereditary information.
- Mixing dominant and recessive characters.
- Forgetting males have XY chromosomes.
- Confusing evolution with individual changes during lifetime.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Heredity transfers traits from parents to offspring.
- Mendel discovered laws of inheritance.
- Genes are units of heredity present on chromosomes.
- DNA carries genetic information.
- Sex determination depends on sex chromosomes.
- Evolution occurs through gradual changes over generations.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Heredity explains inheritance, while evolution explains diversity of life.}
}
