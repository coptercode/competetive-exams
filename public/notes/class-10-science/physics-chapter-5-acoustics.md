# Introduction

Acoustics is the branch of physics that deals with the study of sound, its production, propagation and effects.

This chapter covers:

- Sound waves
- Propagation of sound
- Characteristics of sound
- Doppler effect
- Ultrasonic waves
- Applications of ultrasound

---

# PART A: SOUND

---

# 1. Sound

## Definition

Sound is a form of energy produced by vibrating objects that produces the sensation of hearing.

---

Characteristics of sound:

- Requires a medium for propagation.
- Cannot travel through vacuum.
- Travels as a mechanical wave.

---

# 2. Production of Sound

Sound is produced by vibrating objects.

Examples:

- Vibrating tuning fork
- Vocal cords
- Guitar strings

---

# PART B: PROPAGATION OF SOUND

---

# 3. Sound Waves

Sound waves are mechanical waves produced by vibrations.

---

Types:

1. Longitudinal waves
2. Transverse waves

---

# 4. Longitudinal Waves

## Definition

Waves in which particles of the medium vibrate parallel to the direction of wave propagation are called longitudinal waves.

---

Sound waves in air are longitudinal waves.

---

Regions:

## Compression

Region of high pressure and high density.

---

## Rarefaction

Region of low pressure and low density.

---

# 5. Medium Required for Sound

Sound can travel through:

- Solids
- Liquids
- Gases

---

Speed of sound:

\[
\boxed{
Solid > Liquid > Gas
}
\]

---

# 6. Speed of Sound

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
v=f\lambda
}
\]

---

Where:

v = velocity of sound

f = frequency

λ = wavelength

---

# PART C: CHARACTERISTICS OF SOUND

---

# 7. Frequency

## Definition

The number of vibrations produced per second is called frequency.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
f=\frac nt
}
\]

---

SI Unit:

\[
\boxed{Hertz(Hz)}
\]

---

Frequency determines:

\[
\boxed{\text{Pitch}}
\]

---

# 8. Amplitude

## Definition

The maximum displacement of particles from their mean position is called amplitude.

---

Amplitude determines:

\[
\boxed{\text{Loudness}}
\]

---

# 9. Time Period

## Definition

The time taken for one complete vibration is called time period.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
T=\frac1f
}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{second(s)}
\]

---

# 10. Pitch

## Definition

The characteristic of sound that distinguishes high and low sounds is called pitch.

---

Depends on:

\[
\boxed{\text{Frequency}}
\]

---

Example:

- Child's voice → High pitch
- Man's voice → Low pitch

---

# 11. Loudness

## Definition

The characteristic of sound that determines its intensity is called loudness.

---

Depends on:

\[
\boxed{\text{Amplitude}}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decibel (dB)}}
\]

---

# PART D: AUDIBLE RANGE

---

# 12. Human Audible Range

Humans can hear frequencies between:

\[
\boxed{
20Hz\text{ to }20000Hz
}
\]

---

# 13. Infrasonic Waves

Frequency:

\[
\boxed{<20Hz}
\]

---

Examples:

- Earthquakes
- Large animals

---

# 14. Ultrasonic Waves

Frequency:

\[
\boxed{>20000Hz}
\]

---

Examples:

- Bats
- Dolphins

---

# PART E: DOPPLER EFFECT

---

# 15. Doppler Effect

## Definition

The apparent change in frequency of sound due to relative motion between the source and observer is called Doppler effect.

---

# Causes:

1. Source moving
2. Observer moving
3. Both moving

---

# Examples:

- Change in sound of a moving ambulance siren.
- Change in pitch of train horn.

---

# 16. Doppler Effect Formula

Observed frequency:

\[
\boxed{
f'=f\frac{v\pm v_o}{v\mp v_s}
}
\]

---

Where:

f' = observed frequency

f = actual frequency

v = velocity of sound

vo = velocity of observer

vs = velocity of source

---

# PART F: ULTRASONIC WAVES

---

# 17. Ultrasound

## Definition

Sound waves having frequency greater than 20 kHz are called ultrasonic waves.

---

# Properties:

- High frequency
- Short wavelength
- High energy
- Can travel as beams

---

# PART G: APPLICATIONS OF ULTRASOUND

---

# 18. Medical Applications

---

## Ultrasonography

Used to:

- Observe internal organs
- Monitor fetus development

---

## Lithotripsy

Used to break kidney stones.

---

# 19. Industrial Applications

---

## Detecting Cracks

Ultrasound detects defects in metals.

---

## Cleaning

Used to clean:

- Jewellery
- Electronic components

---

# 20. SONAR

## Full form:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Sound Navigation And Ranging}
}
\]

---

Uses:

- Measuring sea depth
- Finding underwater objects

---

Working principle:

Reflection of ultrasonic waves.

---

# PART H: ECHO

---

# 21. Echo

## Definition

The repetition of sound due to reflection from a surface is called echo.

---

Conditions for hearing echo:

1. Reflecting surface should be far away.
2. Time gap should be at least:

\[
\boxed{0.1s}
\]

---

# 22. Echo Formula

Distance of reflecting surface:

\[
\boxed{
d=\frac{vt}{2}
}
\]

---

Where:

v = speed of sound

t = time taken

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

A sound wave has frequency 500 Hz and wavelength 0.68 m.

Find velocity.

Formula:

\[
v=f\lambda
\]

\[
=500\times0.68
\]

\[
\boxed{340m/s}
\]

---

## Example 2

Why can sound not travel in vacuum?

Because sound requires a material medium for particle vibrations.

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Sound is a mechanical wave}}
\]

---

## Example 3

A bat uses which type of waves?

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ultrasonic waves}}
\]

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing frequency and amplitude.
- Forgetting sound cannot travel through vacuum.
- Mixing pitch with loudness.
- Using wrong units for frequency.
- Forgetting ultrasound frequency is above 20 kHz.
- Confusing echo with reverberation.

- Sound waves require a medium for propagation.
- Frequency determines pitch.
- Amplitude determines loudness.
- Doppler effect explains apparent frequency changes.
- Ultrasound has many medical and industrial applications.
