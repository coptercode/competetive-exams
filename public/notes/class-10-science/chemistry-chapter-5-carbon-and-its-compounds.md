# Introduction

Carbon is a unique element that forms a large number of compounds due to its special properties.

This chapter covers:

- Classification of carbon compounds
- Bonding in carbon
- Homologous series
- IUPAC nomenclature
- Ethanol
- Ethanoic acid
- Properties and reactions

---

# PART A: CARBON AND ITS PROPERTIES

---

# 1. Carbon

## Definition

Carbon is a non-metallic element with atomic number 6.

---

Electronic configuration:

\[
\boxed{2,4}
\]

---

Carbon forms four covalent bonds because it has four valence electrons.

---

# 2. Tetravalency

## Definition

The ability of carbon to form four covalent bonds is called tetravalency.

---

Example:

Methane:

\[
CH_4
\]

Carbon forms four bonds with hydrogen.

---

# 3. Catenation

## Definition

The ability of carbon atoms to form bonds with other carbon atoms to create chains is called catenation.

---

Carbon forms:

- Straight chains
- Branched chains
- Ring structures

---

# PART B: COVALENT BONDING

---

# 4. Covalent Bond

## Definition

A chemical bond formed by sharing of electrons between atoms is called a covalent bond.

---

Examples:

\[
H_2,\ O_2,\ CH_4
\]

---

# 5. Properties of Covalent Compounds

- Low melting point
- Low boiling point
- Poor conductors of electricity
- Usually insoluble in water

---

# PART C: CLASSIFICATION OF CARBON COMPOUNDS

---

# 6. Hydrocarbons

## Definition

Compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen are called hydrocarbons.

---

Types:

1. Saturated hydrocarbons
2. Unsaturated hydrocarbons

---

# 7. Saturated Hydrocarbons

## Definition

Hydrocarbons containing only single bonds are called saturated hydrocarbons.

---

Also called:

\[
\boxed{\text{Alkanes}}
\]

---

General formula:

\[
\boxed{
C_nH_{2n+2}
}
\]

---

Example:

Methane:

\[
CH_4
\]

---

# 8. Unsaturated Hydrocarbons

Contain double or triple bonds.

---

Types:

## Alkenes

Contain double bond.

General formula:

\[
\boxed{
C_nH_{2n}
}
\]

Example:

Ethene:

\[
C_2H_4
\]

---

## Alkynes

Contain triple bond.

General formula:

\[
\boxed{
C_nH_{2n-2}
}
\]

Example:

Ethyne:

\[
C_2H_2
\]

---

# PART D: HOMOLOGOUS SERIES

---

# 9. Homologous Series

## Definition

A group of organic compounds having the same functional group and similar chemical properties is called a homologous series.

---

Characteristics:

- Same general formula
- Similar chemical properties
- Successive members differ by:

\[
\boxed{-CH_2}
\]

---

Example:

Alkanes:

\[
CH_4
\]

\[
C_2H_6
\]

\[
C_3H_8
\]

---

# PART E: FUNCTIONAL GROUPS

---

# 10. Functional Group

## Definition

An atom or group of atoms responsible for the characteristic properties of organic compounds is called a functional group.

---

Important functional groups:

| Functional Group | Name            |
| ---------------- | --------------- |
| -OH              | Alcohol         |
| -COOH            | Carboxylic acid |
| -CHO             | Aldehyde        |
| >C=O             | Ketone          |
| -X               | Halo compound   |

---

# PART F: IUPAC NOMENCLATURE

---

# 11. Naming of Organic Compounds

Steps:

1. Identify longest carbon chain.
2. Identify functional group.
3. Number the carbon chain.
4. Write prefix and suffix.

---

| Carbon atoms | Prefix |
| ------------ | ------ |
| 1            | Meth   |
| 2            | Eth    |
| 3            | Prop   |
| 4            | But    |
| 5            | Pent   |

---

# 13. Suffixes

| Compound type | Suffix    |
| ------------- | --------- |
| Alkane        | -ane      |
| Alkene        | -ene      |
| Alkyne        | -yne      |

# PART G: ETHANOL

---

# 14. Ethanol

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
C_2H_5OH
}
\]

---

Functional group:

\[
\boxed{-OH}
\]

---

Properties:

- Colourless liquid
- Soluble in water
- Used as solvent and fuel

---

# 15. Reactions of Ethanol

---

## Reaction with Sodium

\[
2C_2H_5OH+2Na
\rightarrow
2C_2H_5ONa+H_2
\]

---

Hydrogen gas is released.

---

## Oxidation

Ethanol is oxidised to ethanoic acid.

\[
C_2H_5OH
\rightarrow
CH_3COOH
\]

---

# PART H: ETHANOIC ACID

---

# 16. Ethanoic Acid

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
CH_3COOH
}
\]

---

Common name:

\[
\boxed{\text{Acetic acid}}
\]

---

Functional group:

\[
\boxed{-COOH}
\]

---

# 17. Properties of Ethanoic Acid

- Sour taste
- Vinegar contains dilute ethanoic acid
- Acidic nature

---

# 18. Reactions of Ethanoic Acid

---

## Reaction with Sodium Hydroxide

\[
CH_3COOH+NaOH
\rightarrow
CH_3COONa+H_2O
\]

---

## Esterification

Ethanoic acid reacts with ethanol to form ester.

\[
\boxed{
Acid+Alcohol\rightarrow Ester+Water
}
\]

---

# PART I: ESTERS

---

# 19. Ester

## Definition

Esters are sweet-smelling organic compounds formed by reaction between alcohol and carboxylic acid.

---

Example:

Ethyl ethanoate:

\[
CH_3COOC_2H_5
\]

---

Uses:

- Perfumes
- Flavouring agents

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing alkane, alkene and alkyne formulas.
- Forgetting carbon valency is four.
- Mixing alcohol and acid functional groups.
- Incorrect IUPAC numbering.
- Forgetting esterification produces water.

- Organic compounds are classified based on bonds and functional groups.
