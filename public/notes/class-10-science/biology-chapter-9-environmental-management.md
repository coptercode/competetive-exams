# Introduction

Environmental management is the process of protecting and managing natural resources to maintain ecological balance.

This chapter covers:

- Environment and ecosystem
- Renewable energy resources
- Soil erosion
- Conservation of wildlife
- Sustainable development

---

# PART A: ENVIRONMENT

---

# 1. Environment

## Definition

The surroundings in which living organisms exist and interact are called the environment.

---

Components:

1. Biotic components
2. Abiotic components

---

# 2. Biotic Components

## Definition

Living components of the environment are called biotic components.

Examples:

- Plants
- Animals
- Microorganisms

---

# 3. Abiotic Components

## Definition

Non-living components of the environment are called abiotic components.

Examples:

- Air
- Water
- Soil
- Temperature
- Sunlight

---

# PART B: ECOSYSTEM

---

# 4. Ecosystem

## Definition

A functional unit of nature where living organisms interact with each other and with the physical environment is called an ecosystem.

---

Components:

1. Producers
2. Consumers
3. Decomposers

---

# 5. Producers

## Definition

Organisms that prepare their own food by photosynthesis are called producers.

---

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Green plants}}
\]

---

# 6. Consumers

## Definition

Organisms that depend on other organisms for food are called consumers.

---

Types:

- Herbivores
- Carnivores
- Omnivores

---

# 7. Decomposers

## Definition

Organisms that break down dead organic matter are called decomposers.

---

Examples:

- Bacteria
- Fungi

---

# PART C: RENEWABLE ENERGY RESOURCES

---

# 8. Renewable Energy

## Definition

Energy resources that can be naturally replenished are called renewable energy resources.

---

Examples:

- Solar energy
- Wind energy
- Hydroelectric energy
- Biomass energy

---

# 9. Solar Energy

Energy obtained from sunlight is called solar energy.

---

Uses:

- Solar panels
- Solar cookers
- Solar heaters

---

Advantages:

- Pollution-free
- Unlimited source

---

# 10. Wind Energy

Energy obtained from moving air is called wind energy.

---

Uses:

- Wind turbines
- Electricity generation

---

# 11. Hydroelectric Energy

Energy produced using flowing water is called hydroelectric energy.

---

Advantages:

- Renewable
- Produces large amount of electricity

---

# 12. Biomass Energy

Energy obtained from organic materials is called biomass energy.

---

Examples:

- Agricultural waste
- Animal waste

---

# PART D: SOIL EROSION

---

# 13. Soil Erosion

## Definition

The removal of the top fertile layer of soil by wind or water is called soil erosion.

---

# 14. Causes of Soil Erosion

- Deforestation
- Overgrazing
- Heavy rainfall
- Improper farming methods

---

# 15. Effects of Soil Erosion

- Loss of soil fertility
- Reduced agricultural productivity
- Desertification

---

# 16. Prevention of Soil Erosion

Methods:

- Afforestation
- Terrace farming
- Contour ploughing
- Planting trees

---

# PART E: WILDLIFE CONSERVATION

---

# 17. Wildlife Conservation

## Definition

The protection and preservation of wild animals and their habitats is called wildlife conservation.

---

# 18. Importance of Wildlife Conservation

- Maintains biodiversity
- Maintains ecological balance
- Prevents extinction

---

# 19. Threats to Wildlife

- Habitat destruction
- Hunting
- Pollution
- Climate change

---

# 20. Conservation Methods

---

## Wildlife Sanctuaries

Protected areas where wild animals are conserved.

---

## National Parks

Areas protected for conservation of wildlife and ecosystems.

---

## Biosphere Reserves

Large protected areas conserving biodiversity.

---

# PART F: SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT

---

# 21. Sustainable Development

## Definition

Development that meets present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their needs is called sustainable development.

---

# 22. Principles of Sustainable Development

- Conservation of resources
- Reduce pollution
- Use renewable energy
- Protect biodiversity

---

# PART G: POLLUTION CONTROL

---

# 23. Waste Management

Methods:

- Reduce
- Reuse
- Recycle

---

# 24. Three R Principle

## Reduce

Minimise waste production.

---

## Reuse

Use materials repeatedly.

---

## Recycle

Convert waste materials into useful products.

---

# Important Points

- Environment includes living and non-living components.
- Ecosystems maintain balance through interactions.
- Renewable energy reduces pollution.
- Soil erosion reduces land productivity.
- Wildlife conservation protects biodiversity.
- Sustainable development ensures future resource availability.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing renewable and non-renewable resources.
- Forgetting decomposers' role in ecosystems.
- Mixing wildlife sanctuary and national park concepts.
- Ignoring human activities causing environmental damage.
- Forgetting sustainable development balances present and future needs.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Environmental management protects natural resources.
- Ecosystems contain producers, consumers and decomposers.
- Renewable energy provides cleaner alternatives.
- Soil conservation prevents land degradation.
- Wildlife conservation protects biodiversity.
- Sustainable development maintains balance between humans and nature.
