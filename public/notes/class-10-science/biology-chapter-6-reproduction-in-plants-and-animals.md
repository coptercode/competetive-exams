# Introduction

Reproduction is the biological process by which living organisms produce new individuals of their own kind.

This chapter covers:

- Vegetative propagation
- Plant reproduction
- Pollination
- Fertilisation
- Human reproduction

---

# PART A: REPRODUCTION

---

# 1. Reproduction

## Definition

The process by which organisms produce offspring of the same species is called reproduction.

---

Importance:

- Maintains continuity of species
- Transfers genetic information
- Helps evolution

---

# 2. Types of Reproduction

Two main types:

1. Asexual reproduction
2. Sexual reproduction

---

# PART B: ASEXUAL REPRODUCTION IN PLANTS

---

# 3. Asexual Reproduction

## Definition

Reproduction involving only one parent and no fusion of gametes is called asexual reproduction.

---

Characteristics:

- Single parent
- No gamete formation
- Offspring are genetically similar

---

# 4. Vegetative Propagation

## Definition

A method of asexual reproduction in which new plants develop from vegetative parts like roots, stems and leaves is called vegetative propagation.

---

Vegetative parts:

- Root
- Stem
- Leaf

---

# 5. Types of Vegetative Propagation

---

## Natural Vegetative Propagation

Occurs naturally.

---

### 1. By Stem

Examples:

- Potato
- Ginger
- Onion

---

### 2. By Leaf

Example:

- Bryophyllum

---

### 3. By Root

Example:

- Sweet potato

---

## Artificial Vegetative Propagation

Done by humans.

Methods:

1. Cutting
2. Layering
3. Grafting

---

# 6. Cutting

## Definition

A new plant is produced from a cut portion of a plant.

---

Example:

Rose

---

# 7. Grafting

## Definition

Joining two different plant parts to form a new plant is called grafting.

---

Parts:

- Stock
- Scion

---

Example:

Mango

---

# PART C: SEXUAL REPRODUCTION IN PLANTS

---

# 8. Flower

## Definition

The reproductive structure of flowering plants is called a flower.

---

Parts:

1. Sepals
2. Petals
3. Stamens
4. Pistil

---

# 9. Male Reproductive Part

## Stamen

Components:

1. Anther
2. Filament

---

Function:

Produces pollen grains.

---

# 10. Female Reproductive Part

## Pistil

Components:

1. Stigma
2. Style
3. Ovary

---

Function:

Produces ovules.

---

# PART D: POLLINATION

---

# 11. Pollination

## Definition

The transfer of pollen grains from anther to stigma is called pollination.

---

Types:

1. Self-pollination
2. Cross-pollination

---

# 12. Self-Pollination

Transfer of pollen within the same flower or same plant.

---

Advantages:

- No external agents required
- Maintains characteristics

---

# 13. Cross-Pollination

Transfer of pollen from one plant to another plant of the same species.

---

Agents:

- Wind
- Water
- Insects
- Birds

---

# PART E: FERTILISATION IN PLANTS

---

# 14. Fertilisation

## Definition

The fusion of male and female gametes to form a zygote is called fertilisation.

---

Process:

Pollen grain

↓

Pollen tube formation

↓

Male gamete reaches ovule

↓

Fusion occurs

↓

Zygote forms

---

# 15. Seed Formation

After fertilisation:

- Ovule becomes seed
- Ovary becomes fruit

---

# PART F: HUMAN REPRODUCTION

---

# 16. Male Reproductive System

Main organs:

- Testes
- Vas deferens
- Seminal vesicles
- Penis

---

# 17. Testes

Functions:

- Produce sperm
- Produce testosterone hormone

---

# 18. Female Reproductive System

Main organs:

- Ovaries
- Fallopian tubes
- Uterus
- Vagina

---

# 19. Ovaries

Functions:

- Produce ova (eggs)
- Produce female hormones

---

# PART G: FERTILISATION IN HUMANS

---

# 20. Fertilisation

## Definition

Fusion of sperm and egg to form a zygote is called fertilisation.

---

Occurs in:

\[
\boxed{\text{Fallopian tube}}
\]

---

# 21. Development of Embryo

Stages:

Zygote

↓

Embryo

↓

Foetus

↓

Baby

---

# 22. Placenta

## Definition

A temporary organ connecting mother and developing foetus is called placenta.

---

Functions:

- Supplies oxygen and nutrients
- Removes waste materials

---

# PART H: REPRODUCTIVE HEALTH

---

# 23. Menstrual Cycle

## Definition

The monthly cycle of changes in the female reproductive system is called menstrual cycle.

---

Average duration:

\[
\boxed{28\ days}
\]

---

# Important Points

- Vegetative propagation is asexual reproduction.
- Flowers are reproductive organs of plants.
- Pollination occurs before fertilisation.
- Fertilisation produces a zygote.
- Testes produce sperm.
- Ovaries produce eggs.
- Placenta supports foetal development.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing pollination and fertilisation.
- Forgetting anther produces pollen.
- Mixing male and female reproductive organs.
- Confusing ovary and ovule functions.
- Forgetting placenta functions.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Reproduction ensures continuity of life.
- Plants reproduce through vegetative and sexual methods.
- Pollination transfers pollen grains.
- Fertilisation forms a zygote.
- Human reproduction involves male and female reproductive systems.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Reproduction helps maintain life from one generation to the next.}
}
