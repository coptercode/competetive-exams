# PART A: PERIODIC CLASSIFICATION OF ELEMENTS

---

# 1. Periodic Classification

## Definition

The arrangement of elements into groups based on similarities in their properties is called periodic classification.

---

# 2. Early Attempts of Classification

---

## Dobereiner's Triads

### Definition

Groups of three elements having similar properties were called Dobereiner's triads.

---

Example:

Lithium (Li)

Sodium (Na)

Potassium (K)

---

Limitation:

Only a few elements could be arranged.

---

## Newlands' Law of Octaves

### Statement

When elements are arranged in increasing atomic mass, every eighth element shows similar properties.

---

Limitation:

Applicable only for lighter elements.

---

## Mendeleev's Periodic Table

### Basis:

Elements arranged according to increasing atomic mass.

---

Achievements:

- Left gaps for undiscovered elements.
- Predicted properties of new elements.

---

Limitations:

- Position of hydrogen was uncertain.
- Isotopes could not be explained.

---

# PART B: MODERN PERIODIC LAW

---

# 3. Modern Periodic Law

## Statement

The physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Properties depend on atomic number}
}
\]

---

# 4. Modern Periodic Table

Elements are arranged into:

- 7 periods
- 18 groups

---

# 5. Periods

Horizontal rows are called periods.

---

Number of periods:

\[
\boxed{7}
\]

---

# 6. Groups

Vertical columns are called groups.

---

Number of groups:

\[
\boxed{18}
\]

---

# PART C: PERIODIC TRENDS

---

# 7. Atomic Radius

## Definition

The distance between nucleus and outermost electron shell is called atomic radius.

---

Trend:

Across a period:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases}}
\]

Down a group:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increases}}
\]

---

# 8. Ionisation Energy

## Definition

The energy required to remove an electron from an isolated gaseous atom is called ionisation energy.

---

Trend:

Across a period:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increases}}
\]

Down a group:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases}}
\]

---

# 9. Electronegativity

## Definition

The ability of an atom to attract electrons towards itself is called electronegativity.

---

Trend:

Across a period:

\[
\boxed{\text{Increases}}
\]

Down a group:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decreases}}
\]

---

# PART D: METALLURGY

---

# 10. Metallurgy

## Definition

The process of extraction of metals from their ores and purification is called metallurgy.

---

Steps:

1. Concentration of ore
2. Extraction of metal
3. Refining of metal

---

# 11. Minerals and Ores

---

## Mineral

Naturally occurring substances containing metals are called minerals.

---

## Ore

A mineral from which metal can be extracted economically is called ore.

---

# PART E: EXTRACTION OF ALUMINIUM

---

# 12. Aluminium Ore

Main ore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Bauxite}}
\]

Chemical formula:

\[
Al_2O_3.2H_2O
\]

---

# 13. Extraction Process

Steps:

1. Purification of bauxite
2. Formation of alumina
3. Electrolytic reduction

---

# 14. Electrolysis of Alumina

Alumina is dissolved in molten cryolite.

---

At cathode:

\[
Al^{3+}+3e^-\rightarrow Al
\]

---

Aluminium is collected at the cathode.

# 21. Alloy

## Definition

A homogeneous mixture of two or more elements, where at least one is a metal, is called an alloy.

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing atomic mass with atomic number.
- Forgetting modern periodic law is based on atomic number.
- Mixing minerals and ores.
- Confusing extraction methods of different metals.
- Forgetting alloy compositions.
