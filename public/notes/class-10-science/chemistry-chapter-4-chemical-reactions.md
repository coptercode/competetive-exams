# Introduction

Chemical reactions involve the conversion of reactants into new products with different properties.

This chapter covers:

- Chemical equations
- Types of chemical reactions
- Rate of reaction
- Factors affecting reaction rate
- pH scale

---

# PART A: CHEMICAL REACTIONS

---

# 1. Chemical Reaction

## Definition

A chemical reaction is a process in which one or more substances are converted into new substances with different chemical properties.

---

Example:

\[
2H_2+O_2\rightarrow2H_2O
\]

---

# 2. Reactants and Products

## Reactants

Substances that take part in a chemical reaction.

---

## Products

New substances formed after a chemical reaction.

---

Example:

\[
A+B\rightarrow C
\]

A and B:

\[
\boxed{\text{Reactants}}
\]

C:

\[
\boxed{\text{Product}}
\]

---

# 3. Chemical Equation

## Definition

A symbolic representation of a chemical reaction using chemical formulas is called a chemical equation.

---

Example:

Word equation:

Hydrogen + Oxygen → Water

Chemical equation:

\[
\boxed{
2H_2+O_2\rightarrow2H_2O
}
\]

---

# PART B: BALANCED CHEMICAL EQUATIONS

---

# 4. Law of Conservation of Mass

## Statement

Mass can neither be created nor destroyed during a chemical reaction.

---

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Mass of reactants = Mass of products}
}
\]

---

# 5. Balancing Chemical Equations

Steps:

1. Write correct formulas.
2. Count atoms on both sides.
3. Add coefficients to balance atoms.
4. Check the equation.

---

Example:

Unbalanced:

\[
H_2+O_2\rightarrow H_2O
\]

Balanced:

\[
\boxed{
2H_2+O_2\rightarrow2H_2O
}
\]

---

# PART C: TYPES OF CHEMICAL REACTIONS

---

# 6. Combination Reaction

## Definition

Two or more substances combine to form a single product.

---

General form:

\[
A+B\rightarrow AB
\]

---

Example:

\[
CaO+H_2O\rightarrow Ca(OH)_2
\]

---

# 7. Decomposition Reaction

## Definition

A single compound breaks into two or more simpler substances.

---

General form:

\[
AB\rightarrow A+B
\]

---

Example:

\[
CaCO_3
\rightarrow
CaO+CO_2
\]

---

Types:

- Thermal decomposition
- Electrolytic decomposition
- Photolytic decomposition

---

# 8. Displacement Reaction

## Definition

A more reactive element replaces a less reactive element from a compound.

---

General form:

\[
A+BC\rightarrow AC+B
\]

---

Example:

\[
Zn+CuSO_4
\rightarrow
ZnSO_4+Cu
\]

---

# 9. Double Displacement Reaction

## Definition

Two compounds exchange their ions to form new compounds.

---

General form:

\[
AB+CD\rightarrow AD+CB
\]

---

Example:

\[
NaCl+AgNO_3
\rightarrow
AgCl+NaNO_3
\]

---

# 10. Redox Reaction

## Definition

A reaction in which oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously is called a redox reaction.

---

## Oxidation

Addition of oxygen or removal of hydrogen.

---

## Reduction

Removal of oxygen or addition of hydrogen.

---

Example:

\[
CuO+H_2
\rightarrow
Cu+H_2O
\]

CuO is reduced.

Hydrogen is oxidised.

---

# PART D: RATE OF REACTION

---

# 11. Rate of Reaction

## Definition

The speed at which reactants are converted into products is called rate of reaction.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
Rate=
\frac{Change\ in\ concentration}{Time}
}
\]

---

# PART E: FACTORS AFFECTING RATE OF REACTION

---

# 12. Nature of Reactants

Different substances react at different speeds.

Example:

Magnesium reacts faster with acid than copper.

---

# 13. Concentration

Increasing concentration increases collision between particles.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Rate increases}
}
\]

---

# 14. Temperature

Increasing temperature increases kinetic energy of particles.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Rate increases}
}
\]

---

# 15. Surface Area

Powdered substances react faster than larger pieces.

Reason:

More surface area for collision.

---

# 16. Catalyst

## Definition

A substance that changes the rate of reaction without being consumed is called a catalyst.

---

Example:

Manganese dioxide speeds up decomposition of hydrogen peroxide.

---

# PART F: pH SCALE

---

# 17. pH

## Definition

pH is a scale used to measure the acidity or basicity of a solution.

---

Range:

\[
\boxed{0-14}
\]

---

# 18. Acidic Solution

pH:

\[
\boxed{<7}
\]

---

Examples:

- Hydrochloric acid
- Lemon juice

---

# 19. Neutral Solution

pH:

\[
\boxed{7}
\]

---

Example:

Pure water

---

# 20. Basic Solution

pH:

\[
\boxed{>7}
\]

---

Examples:

- Soap solution
- Sodium hydroxide

---

# 21. pH Importance in Daily Life

---

## Digestion

Stomach contains hydrochloric acid.

---

## Tooth Decay

Acids produced by bacteria lower mouth pH.

---

## Agriculture

Soil pH affects plant growth.

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

Identify reaction type:

\[
CaCO_3\rightarrow CaO+CO_2
\]

A single compound breaks down.

Answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{Decomposition reaction}}
\]

---

## Example 2

Why does powdered calcium carbonate react faster than a lump?

Because powder has greater surface area.

---

## Example 3

What is the pH of pure water?

Answer:

\[
\boxed{pH=7}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting to balance equations.
- Confusing oxidation and reduction.
- Mixing displacement and double displacement reactions.
- Assuming catalysts are consumed.
- Forgetting pH scale direction.
- Confusing strong acid with high pH.

---

# Chapter Summary

- Chemical reactions form new substances.
- Equations must obey conservation of mass.
- Reactions are classified into different types.
- Reaction rate depends on several factors.
- pH indicates acidic or basic nature of solutions.

\text{Chemical reactions explain the transformation of substances around us.}
