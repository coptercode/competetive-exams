# Introduction

The motion of a single particle can be described easily, but real objects consist of many particles. This unit studies the motion of systems of particles and rotating rigid bodies.

This unit covers:

- Centre of mass
- Motion of centre of mass
- Rotational motion
- Torque
- Angular momentum
- Conservation of angular momentum
- Moment of inertia
- Radius of gyration
- Axis theorems

Applications:

- Rotating machines
- Planetary motion
- Vehicles
- Engineering structures
- Sports movements

---

# PART A: SYSTEM OF PARTICLES

---

# 1. System of Particles

## Definition

A collection of two or more particles considered together for studying motion is called a system of particles.

---

Examples:

- Solar system
- Molecules
- Human body

---

# 2. Centre of Mass

## Definition

The point at which the entire mass of a system appears to be concentrated is called the centre of mass.

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{C.M.}
\]

---

The centre of mass represents the average position of mass distribution.

---

# 3. Centre of Mass of Two Particles

Consider two particles:

Masses:

\[
m_1,m_2
\]

Positions:

\[
x_1,x_2
\]

Position of centre of mass:

\[
\boxed{
x_{cm}=
\frac{m_1x_1+m_2x_2}
{m_1+m_2}
}
\]

---

In vector form:

\[
\boxed{
\vec R=
\frac{m_1\vec r_1+m_2\vec r_2}
{m_1+m_2}
}
\]

---

# 4. Centre of Mass of Many Particles

For particles:

\[
m_1,m_2,...m_n
\]

position vectors:

\[
r_1,r_2,...r_n
\]

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\vec R=
\frac{\sum m_i\vec r_i}
{\sum m_i}
}
\]

---

# 5. Motion of Centre of Mass

The centre of mass moves as if:

- Entire mass is concentrated at centre of mass
- External force acts on it

---

Equation:

\[
\boxed{
F_{ext}=Ma_{cm}
}
\]

where:

M = total mass

---

# PART B: ROTATIONAL MOTION

---

# 6. Rigid Body

## Definition

A body in which distance between any two particles remains constant during motion is called a rigid body.

---

Examples:

- Wheel
- Fan blade
- Disc

---

# 7. Types of Motion of Rigid Body

---

## Translational Motion

All particles move through equal distances in the same direction.

Example:

Moving car.

---

## Rotational Motion

All particles rotate about a fixed axis.

Example:

Rotating wheel.

---

# PART C: ROTATIONAL KINEMATICS

---

# 8. Angular Displacement

The angle through which a body rotates is called angular displacement.

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{\theta}
\]

---

Relation:

\[
\boxed{
\theta=\frac{s}{r}
}
\]

where:

- s = arc length
- r = radius

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{radian}
\]

---

# 9. Angular Velocity

Rate of change of angular displacement is angular velocity.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\omega=\frac{d\theta}{dt}
}
\]

---

Relation with linear velocity:

\[
\boxed{
v=r\omega
}
\]

---

# 10. Angular Acceleration

Rate of change of angular velocity.

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\alpha=\frac{d\omega}{dt}
}
\]

---

---

# 11. Rotational Equations of Motion

Similar to linear motion:

Initial angular velocity:

\[
\omega_0
\]

Final angular velocity:

\[
\omega
\]

Angular acceleration:

\[
\alpha
\]

Angular displacement:

\[
\theta
\]

---

First equation:

\[
\boxed{
\omega=\omega_0+\alpha t
}
\]

---

Second equation:

\[
\boxed{
\theta=\omega_0t+\frac12\alpha t^2
}
\]

---

Third equation:

\[
\boxed{
\omega^2=\omega_0^2+2\alpha\theta
}
\]

---

# PART D: TORQUE

---

# 12. Torque

## Definition

The turning effect produced by a force about an axis is called torque.

Symbol:

\[
\boxed{\tau}
\]

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
\tau=\vec r\times\vec F
}
\]

---

Magnitude:

\[
\boxed{
\tau=rF\sin\theta
}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
Nm
\]

---

# 13. Relation Between Torque and Angular Acceleration

Newton's second law for rotation:

\[
\boxed{
\tau=I\alpha
}
\]

where:

- I = moment of inertia
- α = angular acceleration

---

# PART E: ANGULAR MOMENTUM

---

# 14. Angular Momentum

## Definition

The rotational equivalent of linear momentum is called angular momentum.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
L=\vec r\times\vec p
}
\]

---

For rotating body:

\[
\boxed{
L=I\omega
}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
kg\,m^2/s
\]

---

# 15. Conservation of Angular Momentum

## Principle

When no external torque acts on a system, angular momentum remains constant.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
I_1\omega_1=I_2\omega_2
}
\]

---

Examples:

- Skater pulling arms inward
- Planetary motion

---

# PART F: MOMENT OF INERTIA

---

# 16. Moment of Inertia

## Definition

The rotational inertia of a body about an axis is called moment of inertia.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
I=\sum mr^2
}
\]

---

where:

- m = mass
- r = distance from axis

---

Unit:

\[
kg\,m^2
\]

---

# 17. Radius of Gyration

## Definition

The distance from the axis at which the entire mass of the body can be assumed concentrated without changing moment of inertia.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
I=Mk^2
}
\]

---

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
k=\sqrt{\frac IM}
}
\]

---

# PART G: THEOREMS OF MOMENT OF INERTIA

---

# 18. Parallel Axis Theorem

## Statement

The moment of inertia about any axis parallel to an axis passing through centre of mass is:

\[
\boxed{
I=I_{cm}+Md^2
}
\]

---

where:

- Icm = moment of inertia through centre of mass
- d = distance between axes

---

# 19. Perpendicular Axis Theorem

## Statement

For a plane lamina:

\[
\boxed{
I_z=I_x+I_y
}
\]

---

where:

- Iz = moment of inertia about perpendicular axis
- Ix, Iy = moments about two perpendicular axes

---

m_1=2kg

at:

\[
x_1=0
\]

and:

\[
m_2=3kg

\[
x_2=5m
\]

\[
x_{cm}

\frac{2(0)+3(5)}

\[

\frac{15}{5}
\]

\boxed{3m}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing centre of mass with centre of gravity.
- Mixing linear and angular quantities.
- Forgetting torque depends on perpendicular distance.
- Using wrong axis for moment of inertia.
- Confusing angular velocity with linear velocity.
- Forgetting angular momentum is conserved only when external torque is zero.
- Applying axis theorems incorrectly.

---

# Unit Summary

- Centre of mass describes average position of mass.
- Rigid bodies can undergo translation and rotation.
- Torque produces rotational motion.
- Angular momentum is the rotational form of momentum.
- Moment of inertia represents resistance to rotational motion.
- Axis theorems help calculate moments of inertia.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Rotational motion is the extension of mechanics to extended bodies.}
}
\]
