# PART A: WORK

---

# 1. Work

## Definition

Work is said to be done when a force acting on a body produces displacement in the direction of the force.

---

Work depends on:

1. Force applied
2. Displacement produced
3. Angle between force and displacement

---

# 2. Work Done by Constant Force

If a force F acts on a body producing displacement s at an angle θ:

\[
\boxed{
W=Fs\cos\theta
}
\]

---

where:

- F = force
- s = displacement
- θ = angle between force and displacement

---

# 3. Work as Scalar Product

Work is the dot product of force and displacement.

\[
\boxed{
W=\vec F\cdot\vec s
}
\]

---

# 4. Cases of Work

---

## Case 1: Force and displacement in same direction

\[
\theta=0^\circ
\]

\[
W=Fs
\]

Work is positive.

Example:

Pushing a box forward.

---

## Case 2: Force opposite to displacement

\[
\theta=180^\circ
\]

\[
W=-Fs
\]

Work is negative.

Example:

Friction acting on moving object.

---

## Case 3: Force perpendicular to displacement

\[
\theta=90^\circ
\]

\[
W=0
\]

Example:

Centripetal force in circular motion.

---

# 5. Work Done by Variable Force

When force changes with displacement:

\[
\boxed{
W=\int F\,dx
}
\]

---

Graphically:

Work = Area under force-displacement graph.

---

# PART B: ENERGY

---

# 6. Energy

## Definition

Energy is the capacity of a body to do work.

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{Joule(J)}
\]

---

Types:

- Kinetic energy
- Potential energy

---

# PART C: KINETIC ENERGY

---

# 7. Kinetic Energy

## Definition

The energy possessed by a body due to its motion is called kinetic energy.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
KE=\frac12mv^2
}
\]

---

where:

- m = mass
- v = velocity

---

# 8. Derivation of Kinetic Energy

Work done:

\[
W=Fs
\]

From Newton's law:

\[
F=ma
\]

Therefore:

\[
W=mas
\]

Using equation:

\[
v^2-u^2=2as
\]

For initial velocity:

\[
u=0
\]

\[
v^2=2as
\]

Therefore:

\[
as=\frac{v^2}{2}
\]

Substituting:

\[
W=m\frac{v^2}{2}
\]

Hence:

\[
\boxed{
KE=\frac12mv^2
}
\]

---

# PART D: WORK-ENERGY THEOREM

---

# 9. Work-Energy Theorem

## Statement

The work done by the net force on a body is equal to the change in its kinetic energy.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
W=K_f-K_i
}
\]

or:

\[
\boxed{
W=\Delta KE
}
\]

---

# PART E: POTENTIAL ENERGY

---

# 10. Potential Energy

## Definition

The energy possessed by a body due to its position or configuration is called potential energy.

---

# 11. Gravitational Potential Energy

For a body of mass m raised to height h:

\[
\boxed{
PE=mgh
}
\]

---

where:

- m = mass
- g = acceleration due to gravity
- h = height

---

# 12. Spring Potential Energy

Energy stored in a stretched or compressed spring:

\[
\boxed{
PE=\frac12kx^2
}
\]

---

where:

- k = spring constant
- x = extension

---

# PART F: CONSERVATION OF ENERGY

---

# 13. Law of Conservation of Energy

## Statement

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It can only be transformed from one form to another.

---

For mechanical energy:

\[
\boxed{
KE+PE=Constant
}
\]

---

# Example

During free fall:

At top:

\[
PE=max
\]

\[
KE=0
\]

At ground:

\[
PE=0
\]

\[
KE=max
\]

---

# PART G: POWER

---

# 14. Power

## Definition

Power is the rate of doing work.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
P=\frac WT
}
\]

---

Unit:

\[
\boxed{Watt(W)}
\]

---

# 15. Instantaneous Power

\[
\boxed{
P=\vec F\cdot\vec v
}
\]

---

where:

- F = force
- v = velocity

---

# PART H: COLLISIONS

---

# 16. Collision

## Definition

A collision is an event in which two bodies interact for a short interval and exchange energy and momentum.

---

Types:

1. Elastic collision
2. Inelastic collision

---

# 17. Elastic Collision

## Definition

A collision in which both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved.

---

Conditions:

Momentum:

\[
\boxed{
P_i=P_f
}
\]

Kinetic energy:

\[
\boxed{
KE_i=KE_f
}
\]

---

Examples:

- Collision of ideal spheres
- Atomic collisions

---

# 18. Inelastic Collision

## Definition

A collision in which momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is not conserved.

---

Examples:

- Clay sticking together
- Vehicle collision

---

# 19. Perfectly Inelastic Collision

Bodies stick together after collision.

Common velocity:

\[
\boxed{
v=
\frac{m_1u_1+m_2u_2}
{m_1+m_2}
}
\]

---

# PART I: COEFFICIENT OF RESTITUTION

---

# 20. Coefficient of Restitution

## Definition

The ratio of relative velocity of separation to relative velocity of approach is called coefficient of restitution.

---

Formula:

\[
\boxed{
e=
\frac{v_2-v_1}
{u_1-u_2}
}
\]

---

# Values of e

---

## Perfectly Elastic Collision

\[
\boxed{e=1}
\]

---

## Inelastic Collision

\[
\boxed{0<e<1}
\]

---

## Perfectly Inelastic Collision

\[
\boxed{e=0}
\]

---

# Solved Examples

---

## Example 1

A force of 20 N moves an object through 5 m in the same direction.

Find work done.

Given:

\[
F=20N
\]

\[
s=5m
\]

\[
W=Fs
\]

\[
=20\times5
\]

\[
\boxed{100J}
\]

---

## Example 2

Find kinetic energy of a body:

Mass:

\[
m=2kg
\]

Velocity:

\[
v=10m/s
\]

\[
KE=\frac12mv^2
\]

\[
=\frac12(2)(100)
\]

\[
\boxed{100J}
\]

---

## Example 3

Find power if 500 J work is done in 10 seconds.

\[
P=\frac WT
\]

\[
=\frac{500}{10}
\]

\[
\boxed{50W}
\]

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting angle in work formula.
- Confusing force and work units.
- Using kinetic energy formula with wrong velocity.
- Forgetting potential energy depends on reference level.
- Assuming kinetic energy is conserved in every collision.
- Mixing power and energy.
- Forgetting work can be negative or zero.

---

# Unit Summary

- Work is energy transferred by force.
- Energy is the ability to perform work.
- Kinetic energy depends on motion.
- Potential energy depends on position or configuration.
- Mechanical energy remains constant when only conservative forces act.
- Power measures the rate of energy transfer.
- Momentum is conserved in all collisions, but kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Energy transforms from one form to another but is always conserved.}
}
\]
